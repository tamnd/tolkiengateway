**Nấm** là một loại sinh vật bao gồm [nấm](../M/Mushrooms.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Cut_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cut-1">[1]</a></sup> Có một số loại nấm phát triển trong [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md) cùng với <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Herbs&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Herbs (page does not exist)">thảo mộc</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-spiders_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiders-2">[2]</a></sup> Những loại nấm và thảo mộc này có "lá nhạt và mùi khó chịu".<sup id="cite_ref-spiders_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiders-2">[2]</a></sup> Trong [cuộc vây hãm Gondor](../S/Siege_of_Gondor.md), Orc hành quân về phía [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) được so sánh với "một loại nấm phát triển hôi hám".<sup id="cite_ref-siege_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Các phiên bản khác của truyền thuyết {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Trong một ghi chú thảo luận về [Drúedain](../D/Drúedain.md), Tolkien đã đề cập đến các loại nấm trong [Kỷ Đệ Nhất](First_Age.md): chúng thường bị [Tiên tộc](../E/Elves.md) và hầu hết [Edain](../E/Edain.md)coi là xấu xí và nguy hiểm, và những người ít khôn ngoan hơn gọi chúng là "cây ork", tin rằng chúng bị [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md)nguyền rủa và tàn lụi. Những con Eldar không ăn chúng và chỉ một số ít Edain biết cách phân biệt những con ăn được và có thể ăn chúng vì cơn đói khủng khiếp khi bị lạc trong tự nhiên. Người Drúedain có truyền thuyết về thực vật và ăn nấm một cách thích thú (trước sự ngạc nhiên của những dân tộc khác), và họ đã nuôi trồng những loại nấm mà họ yêu thích. Họ truyền đạt kiến ​​thức này cho [Haladin](../H/Haladin.md), những người sẽ ăn chúng khi có khách không lịch sự và giải quyết cho họ khi cần. Tolkien sau đó đã loại bỏ ghi chú vì tin rằng đặc điểm này khiến Drúedain "quá giống Hobbit".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Miêu tả trong các tác phẩm chuyển thể {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1984-1997: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>The various books released for the game describe many different kinds of funguses:</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Buttonhead</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Morchwan</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Morihwan</i>), also called <i>Tumbletop</i>, was a small black-headed fungus with a stark white stalk, found in underground caves far from daylight. It grew in clusters of up to a hundred, resembling toadstools but with hard black caps. The white stalk, when eaten, dispelled weariness and granted renewed vigour for twelve hours, though a heavy sleep of equal length followed. The heads themselves were indigestible and caused painful sickness if swallowed whole.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Crowtongue</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Lamdur</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Morilamba</i>), also called <i>Blackfoot</i>, <i>Dardelléz</i>, or <i>Darksight</i>, was a black, tongue-shaped fungus about the size of a finger. It grew underground on damp stone, clinging tightly to its surface by hair-like roots. When ground into a paste, crowtongue produced a cold salve that rendered the wearer invisible to night vision, though it caused shivering when it touches the skin.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Febfendu</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Nenlebed</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Nelleps</i>), also called <i>Durossa</i>, was a white finger-like fungus found along the reeds of cool freshwater. When boiled and poured into the ear, it restored hearing within a week.  Ingestion of the brew caused ringing in the ears. The fungus was also edible raw, tasting pleasant in salads, though it became bitter when cooked.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Flameroot</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Lachthond</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Narsunda</i>) was a short, spiky red fungus growing on rotting wood or dead plants, most often in caves or dark forests. Reaching six inches in height, its bright tufts could be steeped as an infusion to make wounds heal faster. Its flesh was bitter and acidic, leading to a mistaken belief that the fungus was poisonous.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Gulperwash</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Grodwaeth</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Rottovahtë</i>), also known as <i>Trollsnot</i>, was slimy mould found found in underground bodies of water. It could be spread on the forehead to ease fevers and swallowed to treat organ damage.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Hiam Moss</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Neldorhwann</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Fernehwan</i>), also called <i>Corantha</i>, was a parasitic fungus that clung to the beech trees of Rhudaur. It appeared as brown-red, woolly patches of various sizes, and trees that were encircled were doomed to die. Hillmen brewed hiam into a tea that granted heightened strength and endurance, though at the cost of deep exhaustion afterwards. Except for most Hillmen, many Men are allergic to it.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Lenhafn</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> “Way-throne”, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Coimahalma</i>), also called <i>Great-meal</i> or <i>Lemsang</i>, was an edible fungus found in dark forests and the depths of Moria. It grew up to two feet high, with a purple-furred crown three feet across. Cultivated by Dwarves, it was notable for its rich nourishment, akin in repute to <a href="../L/Lembas.md" title="Lembas">lembas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Margath</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Urchar</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Harúrë</i>) was an orange, gelatinous fungus resembling a kidney, found underground. It was used by Orcs in salves to dull pain, replacing it with a steady burning sensation. It also left the user with foul odours and nightmares.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Moonthumb</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Gulgalad</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Calambë</i>), also called <i>Light-tongue</i> or <i>Glow Worm</i>, was a small curved fungus about the size of a thumb. Growing in caverns near running water, it emitted a faint glow too dim to see by. When eaten fresh, it restored vitality, though drying removed its light.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Mustakuu</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Mornithil</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Mornisil</i>), also called <i>Blackmoon</i>, was a globular black fungus found in the sub-arctic wetlands of the Far North. When boiled, it yielded an oily residue that attracted the insect swarms of the tundra, drawing them in frenzied clouds to whatever bore its scent. The Lossoth carried the oil in bone tubes, using it to lure insects away from their camps.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Plant-Like Stone</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Galassondren</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Ondohwan</i>) was a low fungus of Moria’s depths, growing close to the ground and resembling scattered pebbles. When pressed, it exuded a milky sap known only to Dwarves, which sealed wounds, halted bleeding, and warded off infection. The secret was fiercely guarded, and to jest of “blood from a stone” in Dwarvish company was a quick way to cause anger.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Rabbit Thumb</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Helveb</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Helmëa</i>), also called <i>Furry-man</i>, was a tiny, fuzzy brown fungus growing on old animal droppings in deciduous forests. Though unremarkable alone, when brewed with true tarnas it was said to cure the disease <i>igturfas</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Slimy Gorkin</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Mithbaran</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Sindivarnë</i>), also called <i>Slime Grub</i>, was a slimy brown fungus with mucus-like gills. Found in great patches underground, it was edible though unpleasant in both taste and texture, and was a the key ingredient in antidotes to several poisons.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Stout-Heart</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Belegor</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Turcórë</i>) was a small purple fungus found in dry caverns. For Dwarves, it strengthened their constitution when eaten extensively.<sup id="cite_ref-p138_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p138-20">[20]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Strawthumb</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Olgorthad</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Áyolos</i>), also called <i>Dreambreaker</i> or <i>Strengthgiver</i>, was a tiny yellow stalk-fungus found on rotting wood in deciduous forests. When eaten, it briefly increased strength, but repeated use led to nightmares and long-lasting aversion to the fungus.<sup id="cite_ref-p138_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p138-20">[20]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Toad’s Glove</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Gurbeg</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Loicopica</i>), also called <i>Slota-ána</i> or <i>Deathwart</i>, was a small fungus that sprouted rapidly on corpses in rainforests. Appearing within hours of death, it grew and perished in swift succession, thriving until only bone remained. Ground into powder, it was one of the antidotes to the poison slota, and healers kept it alive by storing it with rotting meat.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Viper’s Bane</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Maehern</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Maxar</i>), also called <i>Heal-all</i> or <i>Softstone</i>, was a pebble-sized brown fungus found in deep caverns. Almost indistinguishable from the rocks around it, it was believed to neutralize any toxin when eaten raw.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Zur</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> <i>Durníd</i>, <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Mornenda</i>), also called <i>Strapack</i>, was a pale, slimy fungus growing in cold, lightless caves. Though small, it could be brewed into a foul-smelling tea that heightened the senses of hearing and smell to uncanny levels. Drinkers could overhear conversations from afar or detect poison by scent, but the initial rush often made them vomit, and few dared to repeat the experience.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2005: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Trò chơi nhập vai</a>: <a href="../P/Paths_of_the_Wise%253A_The_Guide_to_Magicians_and_Loremasters.md" title="Paths of the Wise: The Guide to Magicians and Loremasters">Con đường khôn ngoan</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>Two types of funguses are distinguished:</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Hwanestad</b> (<a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">S.</a> “healing fungus”) was a pale green mould that grew only upon the <a href="../E/Ents.md" title="Ents">Ents</a> of <a href="Fangorn_Forest.md" title="Fangorn Forest">Fangorn</a>. Rarely gifted by them, it was a powerful antidote to poisons, both curing recent damage and granting resistance for a day.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Norrog</b> was a puffy, brown cavern fungus with a gritty taste. Even unprepared, a handful could sustain a Man for a day. Properly prepared, it increased vigour and granted resilience. Orcs often used its taste to mask poisons.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Trực tuyến</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the game, many creatures, most commonly <a href="../O/Orcs.md" title="Orcs">Orc-kind</a>, <a href="../S/Spiders.md" title="Spiders">Spiders</a>, and <a href="../T/Trolls.md" title="Trolls">Trolls</a>, possess a fungal infection from the <a class="external text" href="https://lotro-wiki.com/wiki/Foundations_of_Stone" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Foundations of Stone</a>. These creatures are referred to as <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Globsnaga&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Globsnaga (page does not exist)">Globsnaga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth%253A_Shadow_of_Mordor.md" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor">Trung Địa: Bóng Tối trong số Mordor</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>Several funguses can be foraged in the game:<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Lothrond</b>, also known as a cave flower, was considered a delicacy by <a href="../B/Badger-folk.md" title="Badger-folk">Badger-folk</a> and <a href="../H/Hobbits.md" title="Hobbits">Hobbits</a>. Healers could boil a broth with it that cleared airways, or use it in salves instilled a “temporary (and inflated) sense of heroism.”</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Orchamarth</b> was a putrid green fungus. It was used in <a href="../O/Orc-draught.md" title="Orc-draught">Orc-draught</a> and grog, though some Orcs ate it raw because they believed it would increase their bloodlust. Morgai Flies often hovered near Orchamarth, and they likely spread its spores, a process which was mistaken for pollination.</dd></dl>
