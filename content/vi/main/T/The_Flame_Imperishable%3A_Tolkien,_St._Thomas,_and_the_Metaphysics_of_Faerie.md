## Từ nhà xuất bản {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>J. R. R. Tolkien was a profoundly metaphysical thinker, and one of the most formative influences on his imagination, according to this new study of his works, was the great thirteenth-century theologian, <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">St. Thomas Aquinas</a>. Structured around Tolkien’s Middle-earth creation myth, the <i><a href="../A/Ainulindalë.md" title="Ainulindalë">Ainulindalë</a></i>, <i>The Flame Imperishable</i> follows the thought of Aquinas as a guide in laying bare the deeper foundations of many of the more familiar themes from Tolkien’s <a href="../L/Legendarium.md" title="Legendarium">legendarium</a>, including <a href="../S/Sub-creation.md" title="Sub-creation">sub-creation</a>, free will, evil, and <a href="../E/Eucatastrophe.md" title="Eucatastrophe">eucatastrophe</a>. More than merely using Aquinas to illuminate Tolkien, however, this study concludes that, through its appropriation of many of the philosophical and theological insights of Aquinas, what Tolkien’s literary opus achieves is an important and unique landmark in the history of <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomism" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Thomism</a> itself, offering an imaginative and powerful contemporary retrieval, interpretation, and application of Thomistic metaphysics for the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-Angelico_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Angelico-1">[1]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
