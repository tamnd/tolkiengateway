<i><b>Tolkien và Welsh (Tolkien a Chymraeg): Các tiểu luận về việc sử dụng tiếng Wales trong Legendarium của J.R.R. Tolkien</b></i> là tập hợp các bài viết của [Mark T. Hooker](../M/Mark_T._Hooker.md) về việc sử dụng ngôn ngữ và văn hóa [xứ Wales](../W/Welsh.md) của [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).

*Tolkien and Welsh* đã được mời tham gia Cuộc thi năm 2013 để giành Giải thưởng Sách Văn học Wales của Năm. Việc tham gia chỉ khi được mời.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Từ nhà xuất bản {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;Tolkien and Welsh&#39; provides an overview of J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s use of Welsh in his Legendarium, ranging from the obvious (&#39;<a href="../R/Roverandom.md" title="Roverandom">Gwynfa</a>&#39;—the Welsh word for Paradise), to the apparent (&#39;<a href="Took_Family.md" title="Took Family">Took</a>&#39;—a Welsh surname), to the veiled (<i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../G/Gerontius.md" title="Gerontius">Gerontius</a></i>—the Latinizaton of a royal Welsh name), to the hidden (&#39;<a href="../G/Goldberry.md" title="Goldberry">Goldberry</a>&#39;—the English <a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/calque" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">calque</a> of a Welsh <a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/theonym" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">theonym</a>).Though it is a book by a linguist, it was written for the non-linguist with the goal of making the topic accessible. The unavoidable jargon is explained in a glossary, and the narrative presents an overview of how Welsh influenced Tolkien&#39;s story line, as well as his synthetic languages <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Quenya</a> and <a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">Sindarin</a>.The study is based on specific examples of attested names, placed in the context of their linguistic and cultural background, while highlighting the peculiar features of Welsh, &#34;the senior language of the men of Britain&#34; (<a class="mw-redirect" href="The_Monsters_and_the_Critics.md" title="The Monsters and the Critics">MC</a> p. 189), that Tolkien found so intriguing. It supplements, rather than competes with <a href="../C/Carl_Phelpstead.md" title="Carl Phelpstead">Carl Phelpstead</a>&#39;s excellent <i><a href="Tolkien_and_Wales.md" title="Tolkien and Wales">Tolkien and Wales</a></i>, which sidestepped the topic of the Celtic linguistics behind Tolkien&#39;s work. Learn the story behind <i><a href="../L/Lithe.md" title="Lithe">Lithe</a></i>, <i><a href="../B/Buckland.md" title="Buckland">Buckland</a></i>, <i><a href="../A/Anduin.md" title="Anduin">Anduin</a></i>, and <i><a href="../B/Baranduin.md" title="Baranduin">Baranduin</a></i>.
</p>
</blockquote>
