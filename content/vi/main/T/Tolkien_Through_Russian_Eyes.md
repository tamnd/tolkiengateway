<i><b>Tolkien Qua Đôi Mắt Nga</b></i> là cuốn thứ năm trong bộ *[Cormarë Series](../C/Cormarë_Series.md)*của Cây Đi Bộ. Không giống như hầu hết các tập khác, nó không phải là một tập hợp các bài tiểu luận mà là một chuyên khảo của [Mark T. Hooker](../M/Mark_T._Hooker.md).

## Từ nhà xuất bản {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Tolkien Through Russian Eyes examines the sociological impact of the translation and publication of <a href="../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>&#39;s works in post-Soviet Russia. After 70 years of obligatory State atheism, when the Soviet Union collapsed, Russian society began actively seeking new sets of spiritual values. The <a href="../C/Christianity.md" title="Christianity">Christian-like</a> doctrine of Tolkienism has attracted a substantial following. During the Soviet era, <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></i> was a banned book, which was translated independently by a number of underground translators. The result of this is that there are numerous contemporary published translations competing with each other for the reader&#39;s attention. There are 10 translations of <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>; 9 translations of <i>The Hobbit</i> and 6 translations of <i>The Silmarillion</i>. Each translator has a slightly different approach to the text. Each translation has a slightly different interpretation of Tolkien. Each translator has a different story to tell. Most of the existing translations are only Tolkienesque, they are not really Tolkienian. They have been adapted to the Russian mental climate. This book relates the history of the publication of Tolkien&#39;s works; examines the philosophical distortions introduced by the competing translations, attempts to explain their origins and how they will be perceived by the Russian reader. No knowledge of Russian is necessary.
</p>
</blockquote>
