<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Partly because it was a big job, partly because I could only work on it part time.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	The final book came out so long that it&#39;s being split into two volumes, about 350,000 words in all<br/>
&lt;Beren&gt;	if you want another question just tupe &#39;next&#39;<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I also do a mound of research for each point, not all of which makes it into the final product. So, it takes me a while.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	next<br/>
&lt;Beren&gt;	how you got into contact with Tolkien in the first place?<br/>
&lt;Olwe&gt;	My apologies, but obviously I wasn&#39;t meant to ask that question... <br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I first discovered his work in Sept. 1973. I first got in touch with the Estate in 1981. I first worked with the manuscripts when I arrived at Marquette later that year.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	It&#39;s a great regret of mine that I never met Tolkien personally, though I did manage to meet some of the Inklings<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I also got to meet <a href="../C/Christopher_Wiseman.md" title="Christopher Wiseman">Christopher Wiseman</a>, the last surviving member of the TCBS.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Next?<br/>
&lt;Beren&gt;	how you got into the <a href="../M/Marquette_University.md" title="Marquette University">Marquette</a>?<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I applied there for the Ph.D. program and was accepted, teaching freshmen as a T.A. while taking courses and writing the dissertation. All the Tolkien work was done on a volunteer basis, in my spare time, much of it in conjunction with Taum Santoski.<br/>
&lt;Beren&gt;	Taum Santoski must have been a great person? <br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	The dissertation, by the way, was on another fantasy writer, Lord Dunsany<br/>
&lt;Beren&gt;	o thnxs<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Taum was an amazing Tolkien scholar, whose special interest was the invented languages and, later, Tolkien&#39;s illustrations.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	His death was a major loss to Tolkien studies. And, of course, to me personally.<br/>
&lt;Beren&gt;	much too early!<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Yes, age 32.<br/>
&lt;Marcel_Buelles&gt;	I am sorry for repeating the question but something like 25 years for a book - that sounds a lot like Tolkien (which is a compliment, in a sense)?<br/>
&lt;Marcel_Buelles&gt;	Publish! Publish! Publish! <br/>
&lt;Marcel_Buelles&gt;	That&#39;s what you should tell amazing scholars like <a href="../T/Taum_Santoski.md" title="Taum Santoski">Mr. Santoski</a>. <br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Yes, there were complications and delays along the way. First Taum and I were to do it together, with my being Gordon to his Tolkien.<br/>
&lt;QuietDrops&gt;	was there something about The Hobbit that appealled to you more so than LOTR, to make you devote so much time studying it?<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	That is, he&#39;d prepare the text and I&#39;d do all the notes and commentary.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	But I had to withdraw from the project to devote time to the dissertation<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Then, when he became terminally ill before finishing, he asked if I&#39;d take over and see it through<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	But his approach and mine were so different that I essentially had to start over again. So there was a long delay right at the start.<br/>
&lt;Eru_PT&gt;	Beyond official &#34;manuscripts&#34; that you have acess (by Tolkien himself), what other sources have you used in your book(s)<br/>
&lt;Eru_PT&gt;	in your book - The History of the hobbit of course<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Re. THE HOBBIT and LotR: I love both. But Christopher did a splendid job on the LotR manuscripts himself in HME, while H was being rather neglected<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	What other sources? Anything relevant I could get my hands on, basically. Many long hours in libraries researching points.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Lots of time talking to knowledgable friends. Drawing on my own earlier research trips to the Wade and the Bodleian.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	In the final stages, the wonders of the Internet, which made a world of difference tracking down obscure points.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Next?<br/>

<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I think I&#39;ve made a good case that it always was, from the first chapter, part of the legendarium.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	How do I feel about having had the chance to do HISTORY OF THE HOBBIT? Lucky!<br/>
&lt;Tuna&gt;	I have one, if I may. I&#39;m sure many of us are aware of the great many changes Tolkien made to his stories as outlined in HoME. If you don&#39;t mind (I know, you probably wish to save some suspense for your book), How would you rate the making of The Hobbit in comparison to that other great endeavor of Tolkien&#39;s?<br/>

<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Doug gives the final published text and details every change made since first publication. <br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I give the story of the text up till the point of publication. So our two projects are bookends, complementary<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	I also had more space to discuss specific points than is possible in marginal annotations.<br/>
<b>&lt;JohnDRateliff&gt;</b>	Next?<br/>
