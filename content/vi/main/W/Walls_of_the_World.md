<p><br/>
</p><p>The <b>Walls of the World</b> were the walls that surrounded <a href="../A/Arda.md" title="Arda">Arda</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It is not clear what lay beyond them and what they separated.
</p><p><a href="../N/Nienna.md" title="Nienna">Nienna</a>&#39;s <a href="../H/Halls_of_Nienna.md" title="Halls of Nienna">halls</a> that were &#34;west of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="West.md" title="West">West</a>, upon the borders of the world&#34;, had windows that looked outward from the walls of the world.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> 
</p><p>After he was defeated in the <a href="War_of_Wrath.md" title="War of Wrath">War of Wrath</a>, <a href="../M/Morgoth.md" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a> was cast outside the Walls, through the <a href="../D/Door_of_Night.md" title="Door of Night">Door of Night</a>, to the <a href="../V/Void.md" title="Void">Timeless Void</a>, and ever since they are guarded.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>  
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_versions_of_the_legendarium">Other versions of the legendarium</span></h2>
<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>They are as ice and glass and steel, being above all imagination of the <a href="../E/Elves.md" title="Elves">Children of Earth</a> cold, transparent, and hard. They cannot be seen, nor can they be passed, save by the <a href="../D/Door_of_Night.md" title="Door of Night">Door of Night</a>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a class="mw-redirect" href="../R/Rúmil_of_Tirion.md" title="Rúmil of Tirion">Rúmil of Tirion</a>, <i>The <a class="mw-redirect" href="../A/Ambarkanta.md" title="Ambarkanta">Ambarkanta</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-Ambarkanta_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ambarkanta-5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:235</sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>
<p>In earlier versions of the cosmology such as as the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../A/Ambarkanta.md" title="Ambarkanta">Ambarkanta</a>, the Walls of the World were called the <b>Ilurambar</b> (<a href="../Q/Qenya.md" title="Qenya">Q</a>, pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˌiluˈrambar]</span>) (q.v.) in <a href="../Q/Qenya.md" title="Qenya">Qenya</a> and they bounded the layer of <a href="../V/Vaiya.md" title="Vaiya">Vaiya</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bolt_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bolt-6">[6]</a></sup> This was first mentioned in <a href="../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales%253A_Part_One.md" title="The Book of Lost Tales: Part One">The Book of Lost Tales: Part One</a>. Ilurambar derives from <i><a href="../I/Ilu.md" title="Ilu">ilu</a>-</i> meaning &#34;all, the universe&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-etymil_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-etymil-7">[7]</a></sup> and <i>rambar</i> (plural of <i><a href="../R/Ramba.md" title="Ramba">ramba</a></i>) meaning &#34;walls&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-etymra_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-etymra-8">[8]</a></sup> Earlier it was called the <b>Wall of Things</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> 
</p><p>The Walls were a shell that fence <a href="../V/Vaiya.md" title="Vaiya">Vaiya</a> and the world from emptiness and darkness of the <a href="../V/Void.md" title="Void">Outer Void</a>. The World and all its airs were globed inside of them. They were totally impervious and their only opening was the <a href="../D/Door_of_Night.md" title="Door of Night">Door of Night</a> which was guarded by <a href="../E/Eärendil.md" title="Eärendil">Eärendil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ambarkanta_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ambarkanta-5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:237–238</sup>
</p><p>Tolkien later changed the conception of Ilurambar to <b>Eärambar</b> (<a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Q.</a> <i>Walls of <b>Eä</b></i>).<sup id="cite_ref-Earambar_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earambar-10">[10]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:62</sup> Presumably, this means he conceived <b>two</b> sets of Walls, the <a href="Walls_of_the_Night.md" title="Walls of the Night">first of which</a> separated Arda from the wide space of Eä, while the other separated Eä itself from the Timeless Void.<sup id="cite_ref-Earambar_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earambar-10">[10]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:64</sup>
</p>
