<i><b>Tolkien&#39;s Oxford</b></i> is a [2008](/wiki/2008) book by [Robert S. Blackham](/wiki/Robert_S._Blackham). It shows images, maps and drawings of [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford) as [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") lived it.

## From the Publisher {#From_the_Publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The buildings and people of Oxford were a valuable source of information for <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a> and <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a>. This absorbing book charts Tolkien&#39;s life in Oxford from <a href="/wiki/1911" title="1911">1911</a> to <a href="/wiki/1973" title="1973">1973</a>, using old postcards, maps and photographs to paint a picture of the places and times that relate to one of the leading authors of the twentieth century. The book covers such places as <a href="/wiki/The_Eagle_and_Child" title="The Eagle and Child">The Bird and Baby</a>, where the <a href="/wiki/Inklings" title="Inklings">Inklings</a> met to read passages from their work; the Old Ashmolean Building on Broad Street where Tolkien worked on the <a href="/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary" title="Oxford English Dictionary">Oxford English Dictionary</a>; the University Botanical Gardens opposite Magdalen College, which contained Tolkiens favourite tree under which to ponder; and the <a href="/wiki/Wolvercote_Cemetery" title="Wolvercote Cemetery">Wolvercote Cemetery</a> where Tolkien was finally buried, with <a href="/wiki/Edith_Tolkien" title="Edith Tolkien">his wife</a>, in <a href="/wiki/1973" title="1973">1973</a>. Presented in a handy guide-book format, this is an essential companion for anyone wanting to find the places that influenced Tolkien and his work.
</p>
</blockquote>
