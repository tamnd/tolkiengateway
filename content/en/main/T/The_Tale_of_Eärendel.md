**The Tale of Eärendel** is the fifth chapter of *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two.md)*. Unlike the previous tales in the book, it does not contain a full narrative, but contains [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md)'s exposition of the various notes and "condensed and often contradictory outlines" of the uncompleted Tale of [Eärendel](../E/Eärendil.md). Christopher also includes [four poems](#Early_poems) written before the Lost Tales.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

This narrative was suppoused to pick up where *[The Fall of Gondolin](<The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter).md>)* and the *[Tale of the Nauglafring](The_Nauglafring.md)* left off, with the upbringing of Eärendel and [Elwing](../E/Elwing.md) amongst the [Lothlim](../L/Lothlim.md) at the mouth of [Sirion](../S/Sirion.md), and the love between them. The outlines then sketch Eärendel's rescue from a wreck by [Ulmo](../U/Ulmo.md), his voyages in search of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md), the [binding of Melko](../W/War_of_Wrath.md) and finally his departure to the [firmament](../F/Firmament.md).

There are two major discrepancies between this outline of the Eärendel narrative and that of the published *[Silmarillion](The_Silmarillion.md)*. Firstly, in the *Lost Tale*, Eärendel arrives in Valinor too late: a message borne by the birds of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md) has already roused the Eldar, who decided to go to war despite the wishes of the Gods, departing to the [Great Lands](../M/Middle-earth.md). According to [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) the "extraordinary" conclusion that Eärendel's quest was fruitless survived to the early versions of the *Silmarillion*, but leaves a serious unresolved question regarding Ulmo's purpose in guiding the fates of Tuor and Eärendel. The image of Eärendel arriving in [Kôr](../K/Kôr.md) only to find it deserted is indeed preserved in the published *Silmarillion*, but in the later conception it is because the inhabitants are attending a feast in [Valmar](../V/Valmar.md).

Secondly, it does not appear that Elwing was ever to be found by Eärendel after the sack of Eärendel's home. In contrast to *The Silmarillion*, the earlier narrative has Eärendel driven through the [Door of Night](../D/Door_of_Night.md) in search of her, not at the design of the Valar and not bearing a [Silmaril](../S/Silmaril.md). His brightness is instead attributed to having trodden in the 'diamond dust' of Kôr and increased by his own sadness.

As Christopher notices, the tale was thought to be divided in seven parts, each one having a comparable length to the tale of *[The Nauglafring](The_Nauglafring.md)*; so if ever acomplished, the *Tale of Eärendel* would have been half of the length of all *The Book of Lost Tales*.

## Outlines compared {#Outlines_compared}

Here are presented the four outlines given by Christopher Tolkien throughout the chapter. These schemes were not dated, so it is impossible to know what were Tolkien's last plans for the Tale. Schemes B and C are the longest, and Christopher compares them with *The Silmarillion*. He also provides a couple of short prefaces attached to two of the poems, but they are not considered here, as they are too early. The division here has only be created to help the comparison, sometimes breaking the paragraphs, but the text of each scheme is not edited beyond that. The scattered notes belong to the Notebook of Scheme C, keeping the same numeration made by Christopher, but not always following the same order.
