In 1981 BBC Radio 4 broadcast a dramatisation of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s <i><b><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></b></i> in 26 half-hour stereo instalments.

It followed [a previous 12-part BBC Radio version](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1955_radio_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (1955 radio series)") from 1955 and 1956, of which no recordings are known to have survived.

## Broadcast history {#Broadcast_history}

Each of the original 26 episodes received two broadcasts per week - standard practice for many BBC radio serials even today. The first broadcast of Episode 2 was blacked out across a large part of south east England because of a transmitter failure (a very rare occurrence even then).

<br> The 26-part series was subsequently edited into 13 hour-long episodes, restoring some dialogue originally cut for timing (since each hour-long episode is actually around 57 minutes, as opposed to 54 minutes for two half-hour episodes), rearranging some scenes for dramatic impact and adding linking narration and music cues. It was broadcast in 13 episodes in [1982](/wiki/1982).

The re-edited version was released on both cassette tape and CD sets which also included the soundtrack album (noticeably taken from a vinyl copy).

A soundtrack album featuring a completely re-recorded and in some cases expanded, suite of [Stephen Oliver](/wiki/Stephen_Oliver)'s music was released in 1981.

## Differences from the book {#Differences_from_the_book}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d9/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg/300px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="295" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d9/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg/450px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d9/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg/600px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1981_Music_by_Stephen_Oliver.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1480" data-file-height="1454"/></a><figcaption>Vinyl with the soundtrack by <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Oliver" title="Stephen Oliver">Stephen Oliver</a></figcaption></figure>

The script by [Brian Sibley](/wiki/Brian_Sibley) and [Michael Bakewell](/wiki/Michael_Bakewell) attempted to be as faithful as possible to the original books, but there are some errors and alterations. They include:

- Scenes are added at the beginning featuring [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum)'s torture in [Barad-Dur](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr "Barad-dûr") and adapting the riddle-game from [The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit).
- The old miller [Sandyman](/wiki/Sandyman) is omitted with his lines given to his son.
- [Fredegar Bolger](/wiki/Fredegar_Bolger) is entirely omitted
- Gandalf's imprisonment at [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard), escape via [Gwaihir](/wiki/Gwaihir), and his visit to [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras) are incorporated into the narrative rather then being related at the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond).
- The [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl)'s visit to Isengard in search of information on the Shire and their encounter with [Gríma](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma) is adapted from [Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales).
- [Gildor Inglorion](/wiki/Gildor "Gildor") does not appear in the story.
- The radio serial omits the sequence in the book in which the hobbits visit with [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil). However, in doing so, the Hobbits are never shown to receive their [swords](/wiki/Barrow-blade "Barrow-blade") from the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs), which are enchanted with spells that are proof against the [Lord of the Nazgûl](/wiki/Witch-king "Witch-king"). This leaves later references to Frodo having a sword before [Sting](/wiki/Sting) and where the swords of the other Hobbits came from unexplained. Sibley acknowledges this later, when Merry stabs him during the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields). Instead of being weakened as in the book, he merely says Merry stings like a gnat. However the Mouth of Sauron still describes Sam's blade as a "Sword of the Downfallen West".
- [Gloin](/wiki/Gl%C3%B3in "Glóin")'s appearance at Rivendell is cut.
- Any mention of [Dáin Ironfoot](/wiki/D%C3%A1in_Ironfoot) is omitted with [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) stating his father Gloin was the one who met with Sauron's messenger.
- [Durin's Bane](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane) is mistakenly stated to be a "servant of Sauron"
- [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) is oddly referred to by his original name Melkor when [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) is discussing the origin of Orcs and when Gandalf refers to Durin's Bane as a "Spawn of Melkor".
- [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)'s physical death is omitted from his account of his battle with Durin's Bane with no mention of his being "sent back back", with the script implying he was merely wounded. Though later when confronting Saruman, Gandalf claims he returned from death.
- [Quickbeam](/wiki/Quickbeam) the Ent does not appear in the story.

- At one point when Gandalf is listing the locations of the [Palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri), [Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Anor) and [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) are referred to as though they were separate cities; these are merely alternate names for the same city. Later Gandalf does refer correctly to the Palantír of Minas Tirith as the "Stone of Anor."
- [Beregond](/wiki/Beregond) has his role from the book entirely cut and only appears as the anonymous messenger who brings news of the battle to [Denethor](/wiki/Denethor).
- Prince [Imrahil](/wiki/Imrahil) does not appear in the story.
- Part of the [Battle of Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields "Battle of the Pelennor Fields") sequence is described in song by an opera-style vocalist rather than acted, which may distance some listeners from the action. Others may find the kinship between the sequence and Eddic style of storytelling interesting.

- The journey through [Drúadan Forest](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAadan_Forest) and the subsequent alliance with [Ghân-buri-Ghân](/wiki/Gh%C3%A2n-buri-Gh%C3%A2n)'s wild men, or "woses" is removed from the radio drama.
- The journey of the army from Minas Tirith to the Morannon is cut including the restoration of the statue at the crossroads and departure of the faint-hearted
- The [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon) is almost entirely cut, including Pippin slaying a troll.
- [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) and Celeborn do not attend the wedding of Aragorn and Arwen
- Legolas and Gimli's visit to the [Glittering Caves](/wiki/Glittering_Caves) is omitted
- The encounter with Saruman and Wormtongue on the way back to Rivendell is omitted.
- [The Scouring of the Shire](/wiki/The_Scouring_of_the_Shire) is significantly cut down. The journey from the [Brandywine Bridge](/wiki/Brandywine_Bridge) to Hobbiton is skipped over and the Sheriffs are cut. The Hobbiton and Bywater segments are conflated so the [Battle of Bywater](/wiki/Battle_of_Bywater) takes place in Hobbiton.
- [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan) is omitted

## Re-release in 2002 {#Re-release_in_2002}

In 2002, to coincide with the release of Peter Jackson's [*The Lord of the Rings* (film series)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)"), the BBC reissued the series in three sets corresponding to the three original volumes (*[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*, *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)* and *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)*).

This version omitted the original episode divisions, and included a new opening and closing narration for the first two sets, and an opening narration only for the last, recorded by Ian Holm in character as Frodo Baggins - Frodo's narrations deal with his efforts to write his historical account of the War of the Ring in the [Red Book](/wiki/Red_Book), as well as his own personal reflections and musings on the story's events.

The re-edited version also included some additional music cues, which had to be taken from the soundtrack album because the original master tapes for the series music had been lost.

The soundtrack, now digitally remastered, was also included with *The Return of the King* set, with a demo of John Le Mesurier singing *[Bilbo's Last Song](/wiki/Bilbo%27s_Last_Song)* included as a bonus track.

The 13-episode series was also rerun on Radio 4 in 2002.
