The **Took** family was the pre-eminent clan of [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), being wealthy and having held the office of the [Thain](/wiki/Thain) for centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-ordering_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ordering-1">[1]</a></sup>

The Took family was still, indeed, accorded a special respect, for it remained both numerous and exceedingly wealthy, and was liable to produce in every generation strong characters of peculiar habits and even adventurous temperament. The latter qualities, however, were now rather tolerated (in the rich) than generally approved.

## Territory, origins and customs {#Territory,_origins_and_customs}

Tooks were mainly of [Fallohide](/wiki/Fallohides "Fallohides") Hobbit stock, and had quite a reputation for unusual behavior (among other things being more adventurous than the other Hobbits<sup id="cite_ref-hobbits_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup>), a quality not valued in the Shire. For this they would be seen as less respectable, but those traits were "tolerated" thanks to their large numbers and wealth.<sup id="cite_ref-ordering_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ordering-1">[1]</a></sup> An absurd legend among other families, was that one of the Took ancestors married a [fairy](/wiki/Fairies "Fairies"). The [Wizard](/wiki/Istari "Istari") [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) was a known, if disreputable, associate.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

The majority of the Tooks lived in the [Great Smials](/wiki/Great_Smials) of [Tuckborough](/wiki/Tuckborough) and in the surrounding area of [Tookland](/wiki/Tookland), which lay in the [Westfarthing](/wiki/Westfarthing).<sup id="cite_ref-ordering_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ordering-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> A smaller clan called the **[North-tooks](/wiki/North-took_Family "North-took Family")** who lived far up in [Long Cleeve](/wiki/Long_Cleeve); these were descendants of the legendary hero [Bandobras Took](/wiki/Bandobras_Took).<sup id="cite_ref-Took_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Took-5">[5]</a></sup>

As was customary in traditional hobbit families, the head of the Took family ("The Took") ruled the clan with his wife, thus upon his death, she continued to rule as the matriarch of the family. Such was the case with [Lalia Clayhanger](/wiki/Lalia_Clayhanger) who succeeded her husband, [Fortinbras](/wiki/Fortinbras_Took_II "Fortinbras Took II"), to clan leadership. However, the Thainship passed only through male descent, so her son, [Ferumbras III](/wiki/Ferumbras_Took_III "Ferumbras Took III"), was the Thain immediately after his father’s death.<sup id="cite_ref-letter_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-6">[6]</a></sup>

Took men usually were given high-sounding names which evoked battles, weapons or figures of legend, like Fortimbras or Isembold. [Razanur Tûk](/wiki/Razanur_T%C3%BBk) (Pippin) was named after <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Razanur&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Razanur (page does not exist)">Razanur</a>, a mythical traveller.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Took women had names of jewels or flowers.<sup id="cite_ref-Trans_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trans-8">[8]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The first recorded Took was [Isumbras Took](/wiki/Isumbras_Took_I "Isumbras Took I"), who in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [740](/wiki/Third_Age_2340 "Third Age 2340") became the 13th [Thain](/wiki/Thain) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") after [Gorhendad Oldbuck](/wiki/Gorhendad_Oldbuck) crossed into [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and became the Master there.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-9">[9]</a></sup> Thereafter Isumbras Took was known as Isumbras I and the office of the Thain became hereditary within the Took family.

Excavation of the seat of the Took family, the Great Smials, was begun in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1083](/wiki/Third_Age_2683 "Third Age 2683") by Thain [Isengrim II](/wiki/Isengrim_Took_II "Isengrim Took II").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-9">[9]</a></sup>

[Bandobras Took](/wiki/Bandobras_Took), the "Bullroarer", defeated an Orc-band in the Northfarthing in [1147](/wiki/Third_Age_2747 "Third Age 2747").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-9">[9]</a></sup>

[Gerontius Took](/wiki/Gerontius_Took), noted for his friendship with Gandalf, held the record of the oldest Hobbit (living up to 130) and of most offspring (twelve) although those records were bested after the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring). Two of his sons, [Hildifons](/wiki/Hildifons_Took "Hildifons Took") and [Isengar](/wiki/Isengar_Took "Isengar Took"), were a few of the Hobbits who "went off" for adventures, sometimes inspired by Gandalf. Young [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins), Gerontius's grandson, used to pester Gandalf for information about his uncles and other Hobbits who sought adventures.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

Hobbit heroes of the later Third Age, Bilbo Baggins and [Meriadoc Brandybuck](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck) both had Took mothers. [Peregrin "Pippin" Took](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took"), son of Thain [Paladin II](/wiki/Paladin_II), participated in the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) and the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).

After the War Peregrin became the Thain; in [1484](/wiki/Fourth_Age_63 "Fourth Age 63") he handed his office to his son, [Faramir Took I](/wiki/Faramir_Took_I) and rode away from the Shire (later it is said that he and Meriadoc were laid to rest beside the bed of [King Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") after his passing).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age) the Great Smials became an important repository of historic books and records.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

<i><b>Tûk</b></i> was an older name of unknown meaning in Hobbitish [Westron](/wiki/Westron).<sup id="cite_ref-Trans_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trans-8">[8]</a></sup>

*Took* is a [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) surname, referring to a type of sword. One of the earliest spellings of the Hobbitish form of the name in *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)* (*Tūca*) matches the Welsh spelling for this name/sword,<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[14]</a></sup> which suggests that Tolkien apparently was aware of the Welsh meaning.

"Took" was pronounced like "Two-k", not like "book" or "took" (as in the past tense of "take)".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[15]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The inspiration for the name Took may have come from Tollkühn, meaning "foolhardy".

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395753">.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider{margin-top:1rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem;color:var(--tg-color-subtle);display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:space-between}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider:not(:empty){gap:1rem;white-space:nowrap}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::before,.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::after{content:"";height:1px;width:100%;background-color:var(--tg-border-color-base)}</style><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>She [Tolkien&#39;s aunt Grace] alleged that the family name [&#39;Tolkien&#39;] had originally been &#39;von Hohenzollern&#39;, for they had emanated from the Hohenzollern district of the Holy Roman Empire. A certain George von Hohenzollern had, she said, fought on the side of Archduke Ferdinand of Austria at the Siege of Vienna in 1529. He had shown great daring in leading an unofficial raid against the Turks and capturing the Sultan&#39;s standard. This (said Aunt Grace) was why he was given the nickname &#39;Tollkühn,&#39; &#39;foolhardy&#39;; and the name stuck.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_A_Biography" title="J.R.R. Tolkien: A Biography">J.R.R. Tolkien: A Biography</a></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

[Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter), in his attempt to draw an analogy between Bilbo and Tolkien, noted that Tolkien's mother, [Mabel Suffield](/wiki/Mabel_Suffield), was one of three remarkable daughters of [John Suffield](/wiki/John_Suffield), who (like Gerontius) was centennial and had three remarkable daughters.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[16]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[17]</a></sup>

The inspiration for [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) blood amongst the Tooks may have possibly come from the Tollkühn family intermarrying with nobility:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The family [Tollkühn] was also supposed to have connections with France and to have <i>intermarried with the nobility in that country&#39;</i>, where they acquired a French version of their nickname, du Temeraire.
</p>
