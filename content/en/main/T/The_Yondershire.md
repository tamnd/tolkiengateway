The **Yondershire** is a division of [the Shire](The_Shire.md) invented for the game *[The Lord of the Rings Online](The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md)*. Located north-west of [Needlehole](../N/Needlehole.md), it connects the rest of the Shire to the [Ered Luin](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) region.<sup id="cite_ref-U33_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-U33-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is described as 'a sparsely populated region of moor, thicket, and fen that has long been home to Hobbit recluses and troublemakers'.<sup id="cite_ref-U33_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-U33-1">[1]</a></sup> It encompasses the northern part of the [Westfarthing](../W/Westfarthing.md) and the western part of the [Northfarthing](../N/Northfarthing.md), and also includes the wilderness between the Bounds and the [River Lune](../L/Lhûn.md) in the west and the [Twilight Hills](../H/Hills_of_Evendim.md) in the north.

The inhabitants are called "Yonder-hobbits" by the inhabitants of the Shire proper, which folk of the Yondershire in turn call the "Hithershire".<sup id="cite_ref-Quests_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quests-2">[2]</a></sup>

It was settled by [Blanco](../B/Blanco.md), who continued to wander while his brother [Marcho](../M/Marcho.md) became fat and comfortable in Michel Delving. Blanco built his home at [Long Cleeve](../L/Long_Cleeve.md), near the source of [the Water](The_Water.md). Since the time of [Bandobras Took](../B/Bandobras_Took.md), Long Cleeve has been the home of the [North-tooks](../N/North-took_Family.md) and the seat of their leader, the Reeve of the Yondershire.<sup id="cite_ref-Sites_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sites-3">[3]</a></sup>

Another important village is [Nobottle](../N/Nobottle.md), founded in the time of [Thain](Thain.md) [Ferumbras Took I](../F/Ferumbras_Took_I.md) and located near Needlehole on the edge of the Hithershire. The other two settlements are the hunting village of [Gamwich](../G/Gamwich.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Sites_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sites-3">[3]</a></sup> and [Tighfield](Tighfield.md), known for its rope-making.<sup id="cite_ref-FTtG_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FTtG-4">[4]</a></sup>
