"**Tree-men**" was an enigmatic term used by [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) in [The Shire](/wiki/The_Shire) for some unspecified creatures.<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On [12 April](/wiki/12_April) in the year [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018") of the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), Samwise Gamgee told [Ted Sandyman](/wiki/Ted_Sandyman) in the [Green Dragon](/wiki/Green_Dragon) about rumours regarding sightings of giant Tree-men beyond the [North Moors](/wiki/North_Moors). When Ted questioned Sam's credibility, Sam stated that his cousin [Halfast Gamgee](/wiki/Halfast_Gamgee) actually saw one of these [giants](/wiki/Giants "Giants") which apparently "was as big as an elm tree, and walking - walking seven yards to a stride". Ted still questioned the credibility of the rumour.<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the first iteration of the conversation at the Green Dragon, Samwise never names the creature, and only used the word "giants", describing them as being "as big as a tower or leastways a tree".<sup id="cite_ref-first_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien changed this phrase soon afterwards, adding the term "**Tree-Men**" and briefly dropping "or leastways a tree" from the sentence.<sup id="cite_ref-first_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-2">[2]</a></sup> In the second draft of the conversation, Tolkien revised the spelling to **Tree-men**,<sup id="cite_ref-second_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-3">[3]</a></sup> yet in a later draft, Tolkien spelled the term as **Treemen**.<sup id="cite_ref-last_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-4">[4]</a></sup> Sometime later, Tolkien changed the term back to Tree-men and the comparison from a tower to an elm tree.

Assuming that Halfast's story is not a fanciful fabrication and he did see something strange, [fans](/wiki/Fandom "Fandom") have connected the "tree-man" to the [Ents](/wiki/Ents) (or [Entwives](/wiki/Entwives)) that appear later in the story. However [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") specifically mentioned that he had not invented the Ents before the moment he reached the chapter *[Treebeard](</wiki/Treebeard_(chapter)> "Treebeard (chapter)")*<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and the "tree-man" reference was written before it.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It was unknown whether Tolkien himself consciously considered such a connection, revisited the matter subsequently,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> or decided that Halfast should not be taken at his word.

On [28 February](/wiki/28_February) in [1964](/wiki/1964), Tolkien stated in a letter to James Heaf that he did "not think it was an [Entwife](/wiki/Entwives "Entwives") which Hal saw on the North Moors" and suggested that the Tree-man may have been "a [troll](/wiki/Trolls "Trolls")" of some kind.<sup id="cite_ref-unpublished_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-unpublished-8">[8]</a></sup> While trolls were never said to be capable of "walking seven yards to a stride"<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup>, it is possible that some details of the sighting may have been exaggerated by Halfast.

However, on 28 February in [1966](/wiki/1966), Tolkien stated in a [letter to Jenny Hall](</wiki/Letter_to_Jenny_Hall_(28_February_1966)> "Letter to Jenny Hall (28 February 1966)") that "the walking elms were meant to be ents (but not entwives)" and that [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) "had asked one or two of them to keep a watch on the Shire, but he did not tell anybody about it".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In "[The Tale of Eärendel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_E%C3%A4rendel)" in *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two)*, [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) set sail in [Vingilot](/wiki/Vingilot) and had many voyages in his quest to reach [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor). During one of these voyages, Vingilot was driven south by the wind into [dark regions](/wiki/Far_Harad "Far Harad") and fire-mountains where he encountered "Tree-men" alongside [Sun-dwellers](/wiki/Haradrim "Haradrim"), [Pygmies](/wiki/Pygmies), and *[Sarqindi](/wiki/Sarqindi)*.<sup id="cite_ref-south_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-south-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

### *The Lord of the Rings Online* {#The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online}

In the video game *[The Lord of the Rings Online](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online)*, there are many different types of trolls, one of these groups are **Wood-trolls**. These Wood-trolls are often mistaken for [Ents](/wiki/Ents) and Giants. There is also a quest in the Shire wherein a Hobbit requests that the player investigate sightings of a walking tree in the North Moors. The **Walking Tree** in question turns out to be a [Huorn](/wiki/Huorns "Huorns") that is trapped by [Spiders](/wiki/Spiders).

### *The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power* {#The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power}

In the [fourth episode](/wiki/The_Great_Wave "The Great Wave") of the first season of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power)*, [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) tells [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) of the stone-giants of the [North Moors](/wiki/North_Moors).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> While the term "Tree-men" is not mentioned, it can be inferred that Galadriel is referring to the Tree-men rather than the giants of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) since in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, the Tree-men are described as possibly being akin to giants,<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup> and that giants were never said to dwell in the North Moors.
