"**Tree-men**" was an enigmatic term used by [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) in [The Shire](The_Shire.md) for some unspecified creatures.<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On [12 April](../_/12_April.md) in the year [3018](Third_Age_3018.md) of the late [Third Age](Third_Age.md), Samwise Gamgee told [Ted Sandyman](Ted_Sandyman.md) in the [Green Dragon](../G/Green_Dragon~72852.md) about rumours regarding sightings of giant Tree-men beyond the [North Moors](../N/North_Moors.md). When Ted questioned Sam's credibility, Sam stated that his cousin [Halfast Gamgee](../H/Halfast_Gamgee.md) actually saw one of these [giants](../G/Giants.md) which apparently "was as big as an elm tree, and walking - walking seven yards to a stride". Ted still questioned the credibility of the rumour.<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the first iteration of the conversation at the Green Dragon, Samwise never names the creature, and only used the word "giants", describing them as being "as big as a tower or leastways a tree".<sup id="cite_ref-first_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien changed this phrase soon afterwards, adding the term "**Tree-Men**" and briefly dropping "or leastways a tree" from the sentence.<sup id="cite_ref-first_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-2">[2]</a></sup> In the second draft of the conversation, Tolkien revised the spelling to **Tree-men**,<sup id="cite_ref-second_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-3">[3]</a></sup> yet in a later draft, Tolkien spelled the term as **Treemen**.<sup id="cite_ref-last_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-4">[4]</a></sup> Sometime later, Tolkien changed the term back to Tree-men and the comparison from a tower to an elm tree.

Assuming that Halfast's story is not a fanciful fabrication and he did see something strange, [fans](../F/Fandom.md) have connected the "tree-man" to the [Ents](../E/Ents.md) (or [Entwives](../E/Entwives.md)) that appear later in the story. However [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) specifically mentioned that he had not invented the Ents before the moment he reached the chapter *[Treebeard](<Treebeard_(chapter).md>)*<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and the "tree-man" reference was written before it.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It was unknown whether Tolkien himself consciously considered such a connection, revisited the matter subsequently,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> or decided that Halfast should not be taken at his word.

On [28 February](../_/28_February.md) in [1964](../_/1964.md), Tolkien stated in a letter to James Heaf that he did "not think it was an [Entwife](../E/Entwives.md) which Hal saw on the North Moors" and suggested that the Tree-man may have been "a [troll](Trolls.md)" of some kind.<sup id="cite_ref-unpublished_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-unpublished-8">[8]</a></sup> While trolls were never said to be capable of "walking seven yards to a stride"<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup>, it is possible that some details of the sighting may have been exaggerated by Halfast.

However, on 28 February in [1966](../_/1966.md), Tolkien stated in a [letter to Jenny Hall](<../L/Letter_to_Jenny_Hall_(28_February_1966).md>) that "the walking elms were meant to be ents (but not entwives)" and that [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) "had asked one or two of them to keep a watch on the Shire, but he did not tell anybody about it".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In "[The Tale of Eärendel](The_Tale_of_Eärendel.md)" in *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two.md)*, [Eärendil](../E/Eärendil.md) set sail in [Vingilot](../V/Vingilot.md) and had many voyages in his quest to reach [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md). During one of these voyages, Vingilot was driven south by the wind into [dark regions](../F/Far_Harad.md) and fire-mountains where he encountered "Tree-men" alongside [Sun-dwellers](../H/Haradrim.md), [Pygmies](../P/Pygmies.md), and *[Sarqindi](../S/Sarqindi.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-south_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-south-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

### *The Lord of the Rings Online* {#The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online}

In the video game *[The Lord of the Rings Online](The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md)*, there are many different types of trolls, one of these groups are **Wood-trolls**. These Wood-trolls are often mistaken for [Ents](../E/Ents.md) and Giants. There is also a quest in the Shire wherein a Hobbit requests that the player investigate sightings of a walking tree in the North Moors. The **Walking Tree** in question turns out to be a [Huorn](../H/Huorns.md) that is trapped by [Spiders](../S/Spiders.md).

### *The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power* {#The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power}

In the [fourth episode](The_Great_Wave.md) of the first season of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power](The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Rings_of_Power.md)*, [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) tells [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) of the stone-giants of the [North Moors](../N/North_Moors.md).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> While the term "Tree-men" is not mentioned, it can be inferred that Galadriel is referring to the Tree-men rather than the giants of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) since in *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, the Tree-men are described as possibly being akin to giants,<sup id="cite_ref-hobbit_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbit-1">[1]</a></sup> and that giants were never said to dwell in the North Moors.
