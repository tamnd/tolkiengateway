<i><b>The Golden Key</b></i> is fairy-tale written by [George MacDonald](../G/George_MacDonald.md) that was published in *Dealings with the Fairies* in [1867](../_/1867.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) was once sought to write a preface to *The Golden Key*, but abandoned it after it began to grow into *[Smith of Wootton Major](../S/Smith_of_Wootton_Major.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Overview {#Overview}

In 1964, Tolkien was asked to write a preface to an American edition of MacDonald's short story *The Golden Key*. While accepting the commission, for the reason that he thought "well of this story of his", Tolkien never finished the preface, and wrote in a letter to the publishers that "I am not as warm an admirer of George MacDonald as C.S. Lewis was".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In "An Interview with Tolkien", Henry Resnick (Niekas Issue 18, 1967) Tolkien said:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>As a matter of fact, insofar as... without harping or preaching on the side of various rather old-fashioned things like humility, valor, and so on... and courage, you can carry [on] those over and I think it has rather an effect on people — young folks are ready in their attitudes to rather be changed. But I didn&#39;t intend these things, because I didn&#39;t write it for children. That&#39;s why I don&#39;t like George MacDonald very much; he&#39;s a horrible old grandmother. That&#39;s a very find woman figure, of course, really — the Queen is rather a mother.
</p>
</blockquote>

[Wayne G. Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) have argued that Tolkien, when re-reading *The Golden Key* and other of MacDonald's works for the preparation of the foreword, apparently reconsidered his former admiration on the grounds that the stories often contained a narrator's voice and too much of moral lecturing reminding of the literary style of *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)* (a style which Tolkien had regretted later in life). Hammond and Scull further quote from a manuscript, held at the [Bodleian Library](../B/Bodleian_Library.md), where Tolkien writes that "a highly selective memory had retained only a few impressions of things that moved me, and re-reading G\[eorge\] M\[acDonald\] critically filled with me distaste".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
