<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

**Tirion** was the city of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) in [Eldamar](/wiki/Eldamar). It was here that [Ingwë](/wiki/Ingw%C3%AB) and later [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB) ruled.

## Description {#Description}

The green hill of [Túna](/wiki/T%C3%BAna) was located in the steep-walled valley of [Calacirya](/wiki/Calacirya) ("The Cleft of Light" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya)), the only pass through the mountains of the [Pelóri](/wiki/Pel%C3%B3ri). Upon the crown of the hill the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) raised their largest settlement west of the sea, Tirion.<sup id="cite_ref-S_Unrest_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Unrest-3">[3]</a></sup>

The walls and terraces were white, and the sand in the streets was said to be of grains of diamond, and white crystal stairs climbed from the fertile land beneath to the great gates.

The centre of the city was dominated by the High King of all Elves [Ingwë](/wiki/Ingw%C3%AB)'s tower [Mindon Eldaliéva](/wiki/Mindon_Eldali%C3%A9va), whose silver lantern shone far out to sea. Beneath the tower was the house of Finwë, first King of the Noldor. Here also was the [Great Square](/wiki/Great_Square), where the white tree [Galathilion](/wiki/Galathilion) flourished, and later the site of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor)'s infamous [Oath](/wiki/Oath_of_F%C3%ABanor "Oath of Fëanor").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When the city was built, it was occupied by both the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar) and the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor), and was ruled by Ingwë. When most of the Vanyar resettled to the base of [Mount Taniquetil](/wiki/Mount_Taniquetil), the rule of Tirion was given to Finwë. Many years of bliss followed, until Tirion was shaken by the King's eldest son, Fëanor who was banished from Tirion, and was exiled (with many Noldor) to [Formenos](/wiki/Formenos).<sup id="cite_ref-S_Unrest_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Unrest-3">[3]</a></sup>

After the murder of his father at the hands of the dark Lord [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) and the theft of his most precious gems, the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils), Fëanor assembled the Noldor at the Great Square. There he urged them to go back to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) to avenge their king and reclaim the Silmarils, and to see that their lives in Tirion were simply a prison brought upon them by the Valar. In the end only a tenth of the population remained in Tirion when Fëanor and his people departed, though some followed their new king only reluctantly, and some would soon abandon Fëanor and follow [Finarfin](/wiki/Finarfin) back to Tirion.

Nearly 600 years later, when all the elven kingdoms in [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) were in ruins, the [half-elf](/wiki/Half-elves "Half-elves") [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) sailed into the West in search of Valinor to ask for the assistance of the Valar in the war against Morgoth. Eärendil arrived in Tirion on a day of festival when the city was all but empty, and only when he had turned his back on the city and began to return was he approached by a herald of the Valar. His coming led to the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) and the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).

More than 3,000 years followed before Tirion was for the first time seen by mortal eyes. Soldiers of the king of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), deceived by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), landed in on the shores of Eldamar and camped around Túna, which the fleeing elves emptied. When the men of Númenor were buried under falling hills, Tirion, along with all the [Undying Lands](/wiki/Undying_Lands), was taken out of mortal reach forever.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), *[tirion](</wiki/Tirion_(Quenya)> "Tirion (Quenya)")* means "watch-tower, tower", deriving from root [TIR](/wiki/TIR).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Tirion was also known as "the Watchful City", and "Tirion the Hallowed".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§67</sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the early [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") of the *[Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, the city was called **[Kortirion](/wiki/Kortirion)**.

## In other stories {#In_other_stories}

Tirion is referenced in Tolkien's *[Roverandom](/wiki/Roverandom)*. In the story, written down in [1927](/wiki/1927), the great whale [Uin](/wiki/Uin) takes the enchanted dog [Roverandom](/wiki/Rover "Rover") (formerly known as Rover) on adventures through the seas: Uin takes Roverandom through the [Shadowy Seas](/wiki/Shadowy_Seas) to the [Bay of Fairyland](/wiki/Bay_of_Eldamar "Bay of Eldamar") beyond the [Magic Isles](/wiki/Enchanted_Isles "Enchanted Isles") where Rover saw the [Mountains of Elvenhome](/wiki/Pel%C3%B3ri "Pelóri") and the [light of Faery](/wiki/Light_of_Valinor "Light of Valinor"). Roverandom thought he could see the white glint of a city of Elves on a green hill far away in the distance.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-7">[7]</a></sup>
