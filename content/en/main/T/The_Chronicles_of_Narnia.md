<i><b>The Chronicles of Narnia</b></i> is a series of fantasy novels for children by [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis), published during the 1950s. The seven books in the series are <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/The_Lion,_the_Witch_and_the_Wardrobe" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe</a></i> ([1950](/wiki/1950)), <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/Prince_Caspian" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Prince Caspian</a></i> ([1951](/wiki/1951)), <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/The_Voyage_of_the_Dawn_Treader" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Voyage of the Dawn Treader</a></i> ([1952](/wiki/1952)), <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/The_Silver_Chair" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Silver Chair</a></i> ([1953](/wiki/1953)), <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/The_Horse_and_His_Boy" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Horse and His Boy</a></i> ([1954](/wiki/1954)), <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/The_Magician&#39;s_Nephew" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Magician&#39;s Nephew</a></i> ([1955](/wiki/1955)), and <i><a class="external text" href="http://narnia.wikia.com/wiki/The_Last_Battle" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Last Battle</a></i> ([1956](/wiki/1956)).

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) once admitted "I seldom find any modern books that hold my attention"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> including some of Lewis's works.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Likewise, he has been quoted and described at several occasions to express his dislike of the *Narnia* books for various reasons<sup id="cite_ref-long_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-long-3">[3]</a></sup> which he attributed to "*merely a difference in taste*".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Specifically he said to <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Eileen_Elgar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Eileen Elgar (page does not exist)">Eileen Elgar</a> "I do not like 'allegory', and least of all religious allegory of this kind",<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> considering its Christian message too blatant and obvious.<sup id="cite_ref-long_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-long-3">[3]</a></sup> [George Sayer](/wiki/George_Sayer) has remarked that Tolkien's dislike of the novels derived from incongruously "*assembling figures from various mythologies*" and that the books "*were carelessly and superficially written*."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

On the other hand, his granddaughter <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Joan_Anne_Tolkien&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Joan Anne Tolkien (page does not exist)">Joan Anne Tolkien</a> remembers that her grandfather directed her to reading his copies of *The Chronicles of Narnia* before *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, suggesting that it was "*perhaps an indication of his humility*".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

[Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes), an artist Tolkien appreciated and frequently used,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> made the original cover and interior illustrations for the books in the *The Chronicles of Narnia* series.

[Daniel Reeve](/wiki/Daniel_Reeve), an artist who worked in the production of [Peter Jackson's *The Lord of the Rings*](/wiki/Peter_Jackson%27s_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings"), also worked for the *Narnia* movies.

*The Magician's Nephew* shows Aslan creating Narnia while singing, like [Arda](/wiki/Arda) and [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4). Lewis was perhaps inspired or influenced by the *[Ainulindalë](/wiki/Ainulindal%C3%AB)*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
