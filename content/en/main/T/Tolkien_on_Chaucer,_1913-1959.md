<i><b>Tolkien on Chaucer, 1913-1959</b></i> is a scholarly book by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=John_M._Bowers&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="John M. Bowers (page does not exist)">John M. Bowers</a> and <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Steffensen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Peter Steffensen (page does not exist)">Peter Steffensen</a>, published in [2024](/wiki/2024) by the [Oxford University](/wiki/Oxford_University) Press.

It traces [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s critical engagements with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="wikipedia:Geoffrey Chaucer">Geoffrey Chaucer</a> from his undergraduate Oxford essays in 1913 to remarks in his retirement lecture in 1959. It reprints and analyses Tolkien's major articles such as "[Chaucer as a Philologist: The Reeve's Tale](/wiki/Chaucer_as_a_Philologist:_The_Reeve%27s_Tale)", along with his unpublished edition of the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reeve%27s_Tale" title="wikipedia:The Reeve&#39;s Tale">Reeve&#39;s Tale</a></i> and his lectures on the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clerk%27s_Tale" title="wikipedia:The Clerk&#39;s Tale">Clerk&#39;s Tale</a></i> and the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pardoner%27s_Tale" title="wikipedia:The Pardoner&#39;s Tale">Pardoner&#39;s Tale</a></i>.

For a guide on the book's content, [see below](#Guide_to_the_content).

## Guide to the content {#Guide_to_the_content}

The book's 300-more pages are packed with [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s primary materials, printing (and reprinting) a wide selection of writings that reveal Tolkien's scholarly involvement in [Middle English](/wiki/Middle_English) and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="wikipedia:Geoffrey Chaucer">Chaucer</a>.

Chapter 1 focuses on Tolkien's undergraduate period in Oxford and the early years of his career, included here are: excerpts from Tolkien's tutorial essay "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Language_of_Chaucer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Language of Chaucer (page does not exist)">The Language of Chaucer</a>"; notes taken from a lecture series "Chaucer and his Contemporaries"; private study notes on <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troilus_and_Criseyde" title="wikipedia:Troilus and Criseyde">Troilus and Criseyde</a></i>; some detail of Tolkien's working on the *[Oxford English Dictionary](/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary)*; and quotes from some of his early essays published in the *[Times Literary Supplement](/wiki/Times_Literary_Supplement)* and *[The Year's Work in English Studies](/wiki/The_Year%27s_Work_in_English_Studies)*.

Chapter 2 publishes much material from the abandoned "[Clarendon Chaucer](/wiki/Clarendon_Chaucer)" project, which Bowers' previous book *[Tolkien's Lost Chaucer](/wiki/Tolkien%27s_Lost_Chaucer)* didn't do. These are: the full eight letters by Tolkien that bear on the project's progress; some of Tolkien's editing work on Chaucer's texts; samples from the project's "Glossary"; and samples from the project's "Notes".

Chapter 3 reprints, first Tolkien's essay "[Chaucer as a Philologist: The Reeve's Tale](/wiki/Chaucer_as_a_Philologist:_The_Reeve%27s_Tale)", then his edition of the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reeve%27s_Tale" title="wikipedia:The Reeve&#39;s Tale">Reeve&#39;s Tale</a></i>. Both of these have been reprinted before in the *[Tolkien Studies 5](/wiki/Tolkien_Studies_5)*, but the latter in this book incorporates some newly discovered materials.

Chapter 4 contains Tolkien's lecture notes on three works by Chaucer: the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clerk%27s_Tale" title="wikipedia:The Clerk&#39;s Tale">Clerk&#39;s Tale</a></i>, the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pardoner%27s_Tale" title="wikipedia:The Pardoner&#39;s Tale">Pardoner&#39;s Tale</a></i>, and the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parlement_of_Foules" title="wikipedia:Parlement of Foules">Parlement of Foules</a></i>.

Chapter 5 includes a talk by Tolkien titled "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=A_Neck_Verse&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="A Neck Verse (page does not exist)">A Neck Verse</a>", a letter that discusses the word *faynights*, and reprints the essay "[Middle English 'Losenger'](/wiki/Middle_English_%27Losenger%27)".

Chapter 6 publishes an entire lecture series by Tolkien on the *Pardoner's Tale*, titled "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Pardoner%27s_Tale:_The_Story_and_its_Form&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Pardoner&#39;s Tale: The Story and its Form (page does not exist)">The Pardoner&#39;s Tale: The Story and its Form</a>".

Chapter 7 reprints some excerpts from Tolkien's "[Valedictory Address](/wiki/Valedictory_Address_to_the_University_of_Oxford "Valedictory Address to the University of Oxford")".

The book's Coda includes a letter in which Tolkien shares his thoughts on the *Retracciouns*, a brief text found at the end of the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canterbury_Tales" title="wikipedia:The Canterbury Tales">Canterbury Tales</a></i>.

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#34;Tolkien on Chaucer, 1913-59&#34; traces J. R. R. Tolkien&#39;s critical engagements with Geoffrey Chaucer from his undergraduate Oxford essays in 1913 to remarks in his retirement lecture in 1959. Reprinted with both Tolkien&#39;s own annotations and new notes from the authors, this book analyses his major articles such as &#34;Chaucer as a Philologist: The Reeve&#39;s Tale&#34;, as well as his unpublished edition of the Reeve&#39;s Tale and his lectures on the Clerk&#39;s Tale and the Pardoner&#39;s Tale.<br/><br/>
Though his scholarship was best known for his work on Beowulf, Tolkien was also an expert on Geoffrey Chaucer. He lectured on Chaucer, edited Chaucer, and published essays on Chaucer. &#34;Tolkien on Chaucer, 1913-59&#34; reprints many of these works for the first time, and documents Tolkien&#39;s career-long engagement with the poet and traces his influence in Tolkien&#39;s own works. Bowers and Steffensen reveal how the Reeve&#39;s Tale was a source for Tolkien&#39;s description of Merry and Pippin&#39;s battle with Saruman, and how the Pardoner&#39;s Tale influenced Tolkien&#39;s own story of men fighting to the death over a gold treasure. Chaucer emerges as a major source of inspiration for Tolkien&#39;s creative writings and profoundly formative in the creation of The Lord of the Rings.
</p>
</blockquote>
