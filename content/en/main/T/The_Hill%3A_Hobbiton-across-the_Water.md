**The Hill: Hobbiton-across-the Water** is the title of an illustration by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Description {#Description}

The illustration shows the road leading through [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) to [Bagshot Row](/wiki/Bagshot_Row) and the [Hobbiton Hill](/wiki/Hobbiton_Hill). Just below the Hill is seen the [Party Tree](/wiki/Party_Tree). In the foreground is depicted [The Water](/wiki/The_Water) and the [Old Mill](/wiki/Old_Mill). In the centre stands the [Old Grange](/wiki/Old_Grange). In the distant, to the left of the Hill, one can glimpse what likely is the [Bindbale Wood](/wiki/Bindbale_Wood) of the [North Farthing](/wiki/North_Farthing).<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/a0/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="390" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/a0/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg/450px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/a/a0/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg/600px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Hill_-_Hobbiton-across-the-Water.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="1952"/></a><figcaption>The tracing of the first, black-and-white version.</figcaption></figure>

Tolkien made several sketches — at least five — before creating the final composition, first rendered as a drawing in black and white. This drawing was used as a frontispiece to the [first British printing](/wiki/The_Hobbit_1st_edition "The Hobbit 1st edition") of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* in [1937](/wiki/1937). The second version (with some minor changes<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup>), rendered in water colour by Tolkien, appeared as frontispiece to the second British impression of *The Hobbit* (also in 1937), and in the first American edition ([1938](/wiki/1938)).<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>

The coloured version has since been published several times. Early calendars reproducing the painting include *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Calendar 1973](/wiki/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Calendar_1973)*, *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Calendar 1974](/wiki/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Calendar_1974)*, and *[The Hobbit Calendar 1976](/wiki/The_Hobbit_Calendar_1976)*.

In the first edition of *[Pictures by J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/Pictures_by_J.R.R._Tolkien)*, a black-and white drawing depicting the same scene was published, said to be the ink frontispiece of the first printing of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*. However, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) wrote in his foreword to the second edition of *Pictures by J.R.R. Tolkien* that this was an error; the drawing was, as has been noted by [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull), "a tracing Tolkien had made of the ink frontispiece \[of the first printing of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*\] to transfer its outlines to a fresh sheet, on which he made the watercolour version".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

A passage in the last chapter of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, describing Bilbo's return to Bag-end, suggests that Tolkien was looking at some version of the picture when it was written: “And so they crossed the bridge and passed the mill by the river and came right back to Bilbo’s own door.”

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") have speculated that Tolkien might have been inspired by the picture "German Planes visiting Cassel" (see Gallery below), painted by Sir <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Orpen" title="wikipedia:William Orpen">William Orpen</a> in 1917.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>
