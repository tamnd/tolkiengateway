**Tolkien studies** is the scholarly research of the works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md). This includes his fictional literature and languages and his philological work.

## History {#History}

<h3><span id="Tolkien.27s_Scholarship"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Tolkien&#39;s_Scholarship">Tolkien&#39;s Scholarship</span></h3>

Tolkien's own academic research has had a great impact within the field of philology.

Tolkien wrote many of the entries under "W" in the *[Oxford English Dictionary](../O/Oxford_English_Dictionary.md)* and many of those entries still survive this day as he originally wrote them.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> He also wrote the groundbreaking *[A Middle English Vocabulary](../A/A_Middle_English_Vocabulary.md)* which looked at common words instead of exotic words.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

His lecture titled *[Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics](../B/Beowulf%253A_The_Monsters_and_the_Critics.md)* changed scholars' understanding of the epic poem. During his time it was largely viewed as a historical document, but Tolkien argued that it should be examined as a literary work of art. This method of studying Beowulf is now popular today.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

### Age of Innocence {#Age_of_Innocence}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I shouldn&#39;t call it a fad; I wouldn&#39;t call it underground. I&#39;d call it a game [...] because there is a whole lot of stuff that amuses people -- alphabets, history, etc. [...] I don&#39;t mind it, as long as it doesn&#39;t become obsessive. It doesn&#39;t obsess <i>me</i>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien to Henry Resnick, &#34;An Interview with Tolkien&#34; published in <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Niekas_18&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Niekas 18 (page does not exist)"><i>Niekas</i> 18</a> (Spring <a href="../_/1967.md" title="1967">1967</a>)</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Generally, the history of Tolkien scholarship is divided into four time periods:

- the book reviews
- the cult period and the fierce reactions to it
- the acceptance of Tolkien as a literary agent
- and lastly, the post-[movie](<The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md>) phase, featuring expanded volumes, reprints, and a wide variety of subjects.<sup id="cite_ref-ScholOverview_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ScholOverview-5">[5]</a></sup>

The first two periods have been summarized by [Tom Shippey](Tom_Shippey.md) as the "Age of Innocence": the time before the publishing of *[The Silmarillion](The_Silmarillion.md)*, *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)*, *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)* and *[The History of Middle-earth](The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)*. After the publishing of at least some of those, many musings, theories and guesses were flattened by additional information.<sup id="cite_ref-CompFore_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CompFore-6">[6]</a></sup> The field of literary critics taking interest was still thin, and serious academic research was rare. The first conference on Tolkien's literature was held in [1966](../_/1966.md); before that, only collected works on children's literature had picked up serious attention for *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-ScholFirst_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ScholFirst-7">[7]</a></sup>

### 1,800 New Entries {#1,800_New_Entries}

A major turn in scholarship was the publishing of *[The Silmarillion](The_Silmarillion.md)*, edited by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md). The book included the history of things that were previously no more than a "background-word", such as [Beren](../B/Beren.md)<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> or [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> Apart from many expanded entries, the second edition of [J.E.A. Tyler](../J/J.E.A._Tyler.md)'s *[The Tolkien Companion](The_Tolkien_Companion.md)* included some 1,800 new entries.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> A landmark publication of this time was Shippey's *[The Road to Middle-earth](The_Road_to_Middle-earth.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-ScholOverview_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ScholOverview-5">[5]</a></sup>

There are a number of universities and colleges that offer course work in Tolkien studies. This is a listing of some of the courses devoted solely to Tolkien studies. There are some institutions that offer Tolkien studies as part of another course.

- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethel_University" title="wikipedia:Bethel University">Bethel University</a> offers a course on the theology of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_University" title="wikipedia:Bradley University">Bradley Unviersity</a> offers a course taught by J.R.R. Tolkien scholar [Mike Foster](../M/Mike_Foster.md) on *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.
- Cardiff Centre for Lifelong Learning offers a course on the literature of Tolkien and how it relates to his understanding of earlier fantasy literature.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University" title="wikipedia:Rice University">Rice University</a> offers a course on *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* and *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)* as a mythology for England.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University" title="wikipedia:Rutgers University">Rutgers University</a> offers a course on Christian elements found in *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington" title="wikipedia:Victoria University of Wellington">Victoria University of Wellington</a> offers a course on *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* and how it relates to medieval literature.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
