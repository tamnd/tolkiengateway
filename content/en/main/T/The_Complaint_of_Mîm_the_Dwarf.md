<i><b>The Complaint of Mîm the Dwarf</b></i> is a short poem and prose text that was written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) and edited by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) about the [petty-dwarf](../P/Petty-dwarves.md) [Mîm](../M/Mîm.md) and how he struggles with not being able to forgive. The poem contains twenty-six lines while the prose text consists of seven paragraphs.<sup id="cite_ref-Guide_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guide-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Poem excerpt {#Poem_excerpt}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p><span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">Under a mountain in a wild land</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">a cave opened paved with sand.</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">One evening Mîm stood by its door:</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">his back was bent and his beard was hoar;</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">long ways he had wandered homeless and cold,</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">the little dwarf Mîm, two hundred years old.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup></span>
</p>
</div>

## Prose synopsis {#Prose_synopsis}

One day, Mîm stopped for an hour and beheld the works he had created. He marvelled at them, that they had grown out of him, but were of him no more. He felt a great pride in his hard work, and the fire that was once within him, and that he had poured nearly all of himself into these works. They were a part of Mîm, and without them there would be little left of him.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-3">[3]</a></sup>

Mîm further noticed that they were strewn all about—laying on the floor, piled in corners, or hanging on pegs—and devised a proper way to hoard them. He created a magnificent chest, filled with compartments and secret drawers, detailed with intricate [dragons](../D/Dragons.md) and ancient [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) with axes, and locked with a key bound by [magic](../M/Magic.md). With the closing of lid on the chest, which now contained his hoard of memories and bygone years, Mîm too closed his eyes and slept with his head upon it.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-3">[3]</a></sup>

Unaware of how long he slept, Mîm was awakened by choking smoke, even though his forge was cold. [Men](../M/Men.md) had found him, and set fire to his dwelling. They carried away all his ore, gems, and his great chest. Chased out with the jeering sounds of their mocking songs, Mîm fled his home, only able to save a sack of hand-tools, and a secret [knife](../K/Knife.md) with [poisoned](../P/Poisoned_weapons.md) [runes](../R/Runes.md) on its blade which he since frequently sharped, spitting on the cutting edge until it shone under the cruel [stars](../S/Stars.md). He begrudged those that took his work, making of them <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../R/Rings.md" title="Rings">rings</a> and artless ornaments, trading them for petty kingdoms and feigned friendships, and noting that there was power in the works of old [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) that drives people to madness.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-3">[3]</a></sup>

Mîm, now old and bitter, came to a refuge in the wild hills where he had to begin his work anew, seeking to catch an echo of his memories before they were lost forever. While his work was still good, the freshness was gone, and he could only glimpse the work he had once created, not that which he had once seen. Mîm understood that others thought of him as full of hate and malice, that he would bite and stab if approached, and for that he was shot at with arrows if he dared show his face to the [Sun](../S/Sun.md). He laments that if he could forgive, then perhaps he could still create shapes such as a leaf or a flower with dew, as it once shone at [Tarn Aeluin](Tarn_Aeluin.md) in his youth, but that he cannot as embers still smouldered in his heart.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In either [1961](../_/1961.md) or [1962](../_/1962.md), [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) wrote an early version of the first and second stanzas of the poem "on the reverse sides of"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> a draft of *[The Mewlips](The_Mewlips.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup> In that version, Mîm is three-hundred years old instead of two-hundred and mentions "[gold](../G/Gold.md) and <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Garnet&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Garnet (page does not exist)">garnet</a>" instead of "graven [silver](../S/Silver.md) and twisted gold".<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup> Sometime after finishing a first draft of the poem and prose text, Tolkien decided to revise it and made comments on changes to be made.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup> After Tolkien's death in [1973](../_/1973.md), his son, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), made a typescript and revised the poem and prose text to include Tolkien's intended revisions, adding the title, *The Complaint of Mîm the Dwarf*, in his private notes.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup> After which, he provided it to [Klett-Cotta](../K/Klett-Cotta.md) in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart" title="wikipedia:Stuttgart">Stuttgart</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> to be translated.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup>

On [1 January](../_/1_January.md)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> of [1987](../_/1987.md), the original text was translated from English into German by [Hans J. Schütz](../H/Hans_J._Schütz.md) and first published as <i><b>Mîms Klage</b></i> on pages 302-5 of *[Klett-Cotta: Das erste Jahrzehnt 1977-1987: Ein Almanach](../K/Klett-Cotta%253A_Das_erste_Jahrzehnt_1977-1987%253A_Ein_Almanach.md)*, the anniversary volume of the publishing firm Klett-Cotta.<sup id="cite_ref-Guide_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guide-1">[1]</a></sup> As a result a translation being published before the original text, there had been multiple unofficial attempts to reverse translate the text "back to English".<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-8">[8]</a></sup> In [2017](../_/2017.md), [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) and [Wayne G. Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) included a one-paragraph summary of the poem and prose text in the second edition of *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Companion and Guide](The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Companion_and_Guide.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Guide_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guide-1">[1]</a></sup> Hammond and Scull likely did not know the dating and the location of the manuscripts of the poem and prose text until at least after 2017.<sup id="cite_ref-Guide_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guide-1">[1]</a></sup> Before then, it was believed to possibly have been written in or after the [1950s](../_/1950.md) because [Tarn Aeluin](Tarn_Aeluin.md) first appeared as a term in *[The Grey Annals](The_Grey_Annals.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and *[The Lay of Leithian Recommenced](The_Lay_of_Leithian_Recommenced.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

On [21 April](../_/21_April.md) of [2024](../_/2024.md), it was hinted by Hammond and Scull in a blog post that the original poem would be included in *[The Collected Poems of J.R.R. Tolkien](The_Collected_Poems_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> On [24 August](../_/24_August.md), the poem's inclusion was eventually confirmed to be the case when Hammond and Scull leaked a PDF of their own book's table of contents.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> In an early review by the <a class="external text" href="https://www.tolkienguide.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tolkien Collector&#39;s Guide</a> on [11 September](../_/11_September.md), *The Complaint of Mîm the Dwarf* was listed as one of the "poems which" would "please readers".<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-8">[8]</a></sup> On [12 September](../_/12_September.md), the original English text was first published as entry 185 in *The Collected Poems of J.R.R. Tolkien*.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup> In their commentary, Hammond and Scull suggested that Tolkien may have been inspired to write a poem about Mîm while working on *[The Hoard](The_Hoard.md)* and the "[Preface](../P/Preface.md)" for *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil and Other Verses from the Red Book](The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-5">[5]</a></sup> In a note to line 17 of the poem, they suggest that the phrase "Tink-tink-tink tink-donk" probably refers to "the sound of Mîm hammering".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> Hammond and Scull also observed that some of the words referring to Mîm's smithing were originally used by Tolkien "to suggest the sounds of motorcycles" in an earlier poem <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=The_Motor-cyclists&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Motor-cyclists (page does not exist)">The Motor-cyclists</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> In their commentary to *[The Hoard](The_Hoard.md)*, they wrote "see also poem no. 185" in connection with a passage they quote from page 132 in *[The Shaping of Middle-earth](The_Shaping_of_Middle-earth.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> In a review of *The Collected Poems of J.R.R. Tolkien* in the *[Journal of Tolkien Research](../J/Journal_of_Tolkien_Research.md)*, John R. Holmes notes that the text *The Complaint of Mîm the Dwarf* uses a medieval genre known as a complaint, called *planctus* in neo-[Latin](../L/Latin.md) and *planh* in Occitan, which "were usually laments for lost love, but…found any number of subjects".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>
