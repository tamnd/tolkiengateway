The **Tolkien Family** is a family of Saxon descent, arriving in England six generations before [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name “Tolkien” (pron.: Tol-keen; equal stress on both syllables) is believed to be of German origin; Toll-kühn: foolishly brave, or stupidly clever; a cognate in [Modern English](/wiki/Modern_English) would be "dull-keen".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Tolkien tranlated his name in [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic) as <i>Ruginwaldus <b>Dwalakōneis</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) as <i>Rægnold <b>Hrædmóding</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> (<i><a class="external text" href="https://bosworthtoller.com/53089" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">hræd-mód</a></i> "hasty, quick tempered"). One time he signed [a poem](/wiki/Knocking_at_the_Door "Knocking at the Door") under the pseudonym **Oxymore** (<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/oxymoron" title="wiktionary:oxymoron">oxymoron</a></i> being greek for "sharp-dull", "keen-stupid"). He also created the character [Professor **Rashbold**](/wiki/Professor_Rashbold "Professor Rashbold"), loosely translating the meaning of his surname.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> He possibly translated it in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) as **[Arcastar](/wiki/Arcastar)**.

Research conducted by [Ryszard Derdziński](/wiki/Ryszard_Derdzi%C5%84ski) suggests another etymology of the Tolkien family name, deriving it from <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_languages" title="wikipedia:Baltic languages">Baltic</a> *Tolk-īn*, "a descendant of Tolk", *tolk* being a Baltic term for "translator, interpreter".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Tolkien himself had always been dismissive of this connection.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Origins {#Origins}

Research conducted by [Ryszard Derdziński](/wiki/Ryszard_Derdzi%C5%84ski) shows that the Tolkien family actually had its roots in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk" title="wikipedia:Gdańsk">Gdańsk</a> (German form *Danzig*) in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth and earlier in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavskoye,_Russia" title="wikipedia:Slavskoye, Russia">Kreuzburg, East Prussia</a>, but had been living in England since 1770.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> The Tolkien name is attested there since the 16th century.

The verified paternal line of J. R. R. Tolkien starts with **Michel Tolkien**, who was born around <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=1620&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="1620 (page does not exist)">1620</a> in Kreuzburg. Michel's son **Christianus Tolkien** (<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=1663&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="1663 (page does not exist)">1663</a>-[1746](/wiki/1746)) was a wealthy miller in Kreuzburg. His son **Christian Tolkien** (<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=1706&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="1706 (page does not exist)">1706</a>-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=1791&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="1791 (page does not exist)">1791</a>) moved from Kreuzburg to nearby <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk" title="wikipedia:Gdańsk">Danzig</a>, and his two sons Daniel Gottlieb Tolkien (<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=1747&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="1747 (page does not exist)">1747</a>-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=1813&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="1813 (page does not exist)">1813</a>) and **[Johann (later known as John) Benjamin Tolkien](</wiki/John_Benjamin_Tolkien_(George%27s_father)> "John Benjamin Tolkien (George's father)")** ([1752](/wiki/1752)-[1819](/wiki/1819)) emigrated to London in the 1770s, and became the ancestors of the English family. The family first appears in English records in 1777. In 1792 John Benjamin Tolkien and William Gravell took over the Erdley Norton manufacture in London, which from then on sold clocks and watches under the name *Gravell &amp; Tolkien*. Daniel Gottlieb obtained British citizenship in 1794, but John Benjamin apparently never became a British citizen. Their German nephew Daniel Gottlieb Bergmann also joined them in London. Johann (John) Benjamin Tolkien, who died in London in 1819, had a son named **[George Tolkien](/wiki/George_Tolkien)**, who was the grandfather of **[Arthur Tolkien](/wiki/Arthur_Tolkien)**, J. R. R. Tolkien's own father.
