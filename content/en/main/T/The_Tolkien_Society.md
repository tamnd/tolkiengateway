**The Tolkien Society** is an international fan club and educational charity based in the United Kingdom, devoted to the study and promotion of the life and works of the author and academic J.R.R. Tolkien. Founded in [1969](/wiki/1969) with the blessing of J.R.R. Tolkien, it has expanded its membership across the United Kingdom, Europe and the world. Its President is Tolkien, the Vice-president is [Priscilla Tolkien](/wiki/Priscilla_Tolkien); since [2025](/wiki/2025), [Shaun Gunner](/wiki/Shaun_Gunner) has been the Society's Chief Executive Officer.

## History {#History}

The Tolkien Society was formed in 1969 by [Vera Chapman](/wiki/Vera_Chapman), an [Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford")-educated retired teacher/civil servant, who, horrified at the annexation of Tolkien's works by 'the drugs-orientated writings of hippiedom',<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> decided to form a group dedicated to the true appreciation of Tolkien's works.

On [6 November](/wiki/6_November) [1969](/wiki/1969), Chapman announced the formation of the Tolkien Society in the United Kingdom in the *New Statesman*.<sup id="cite_ref-CGII_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGII-6">[6]</a></sup> Initially London-based, the Society would meet in Chapman's North London flat; they kept in communication via a photocopied newsletter called *[Belladonna's Broadsheet](/wiki/Belladonna%27s_Broadsheet)* (Chapman had adopted the name "[Belladonna Took](/wiki/Belladonna_Took)" as her Middle-earth alias), which was first published in December 1969.<sup id="cite_ref-CGII_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGII-6">[6]</a></sup> The Society's inaugural meeting was held in January [1970](/wiki/1970),<sup id="cite_ref-CGII_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGII-6">[6]</a></sup> and its constitution was ratified on [15 January](/wiki/15_January) [1972](/wiki/1972).

The Tolkien Society grew steadily, and Chapman willingly passed over responsibility for the bulletin to other hands whilst a more scholarly publication, "[Mallorn](</wiki/Mallorn_(journal)> "Mallorn (journal)")" also appeared. *Belladonna's Broadsheet* went through a number of identity changes, being "Henneth Annun" for one issue before its then editor, John Martin, adopted its current name: "[Amon Hen](</wiki/Amon_Hen_(journal)> "Amon Hen (journal)")".

The Society also undertook a pilgrimage to [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford), exploring the colleges and sites relevant to J.R.R. Tolkien's life. Visit was made to [Wolvercote Cemetery](/wiki/Wolvercote_Cemetery) where wreaths in tribute were laid. At that first visit, it was found that a wild bramble had rooted in the topsoil of the grave. This was rapidly uprooted, and the Society offered to buy a rose plant, the offer of which [Priscilla Tolkien](/wiki/Priscilla_Tolkien) - Tolkien's only daughter - accepted. The weekend event culminated with a visit to the home of Priscilla Tolkien.

In [2014](/wiki/2014), the Society inaugurated its annual **[Awards](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Society_Awards "The Tolkien Society Awards")**.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [2025](/wiki/2025) the Society was restructured as a <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charitable_incorporated_organisation" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Charitable Incorporated Organisation</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> with Shaun Gunner as the first CEO of the Society.<sup id="cite_ref-CEO_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CEO-3">[3]</a></sup> One of the main benefits of this change was the ability for the charity to be able to enter into contracts, employ staff, own/lease property in its own name whilst minimising the trustees financial liability for the charity's debts or legal actions.

## Relations with J.R.R. Tolkien {#Relations_with_J.R.R._Tolkien}

In 1972 <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_Honours" title="wikipedia:New Year Honours">New Year Honours</a>, Tolkien was made a [CBE](/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire") (Commander of the Order of the British Empire) just two days before his birthday. [Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_and_Unwin "Allen and Unwin") had encouraged the Tolkien Society to write to Tolkien on the occasion, and they sent the following telegram:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>CONGRATULATIONS ON 80TH BIRTHDAY AND HONOUR. ANOTHER 50 YEARS TO BEAT THE OLD TOOK.<br/>THE TOLKIEN SOCIETY (IN BRITAIN)
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>The Tolkien Society&#39;s telegram to J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-Mallorn50-21_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mallorn50-21-9">[9]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

Congratulations on your 80th birthday. 'Elen sila lumenn' omentielmo.' The Tolkien Society.*" As a birthday present, the Society also sent him a green china tobacco jar - designed with a huntsman and a white deer - which included best Latakia Mixture tobacco; attached was the following note:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>FROM all Hobbits, Elves, Elf-friends, Dwarves, Ents, Numenoreans, Rohirrim, etc. etc. etc. of the TOLKIEN SOCIETY (in Britain) with love and honour and hearty congratulations, to the creator of so much wonder. </p>

### President {#President}

On the [27 June](/wiki/27_June) [1972](/wiki/1972), Vera Chapman was invited to a sherry party by [Rayner Unwin](/wiki/Rayner_Unwin), where she met Tolkien himself. After exchanging a few words with him about the [belladonna](/wiki/Belladonna_Took "Belladonna Took") plant, she asked if he would would be willing to be the Society's honorary president; he said "*Certainly*" before concluding, "*If I can help your society in any way, I will*."<sup id="cite_ref-Mallorn50-22_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mallorn50-22-10">[10]</a></sup>

Following the death of J.R.R. Tolkien on [2 September](/wiki/2_September) [1973](/wiki/1973), the Tolkien Society offered the post of Honorary President to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), Tolkien's son, but Christopher declined saying he thought the his father should remain president forever. At the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/AGM" title="wiktionary:AGM">AGM</a> on [16 February](/wiki/16_February) [1974](/wiki/1974) the motion was passed that J.R.R. Tolkien should remain the Honorary President *in perpetuo*; he remains the Society's president to this day.<sup id="cite_ref-Mallorn50-23_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mallorn50-23-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Leadership {#Leadership}

The Society is primarily volunteer-run organisation, governed by a Board of Trustees with the President and Vice-president as purely honorary positions. Historically, the Chair of the Trustees led the Board and represented the Society both internally and externally; however, since [1 June](/wiki/1_June) [2025](/wiki/2025), [Shaun Gunner](/wiki/Shaun_Gunner) has served as the Society's Chief Executive Officer in leading the organisation, responsible to the Board.<sup id="cite_ref-CEO_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CEO-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Smials {#Smials}

Smials are the local groups of the Tolkien Society, formed by members who meet in a particular town, region, or country to share their interest in the life and works of J.R.R. Tolkien. The name derives from Tolkien’s own term [smial](/wiki/Smials "Smials") for a hobbit‑hole.

Smials operate semi‑independently and vary widely in size and activity. Many hold regular meetings, while others organise reading groups, lectures, themed walks, or celebrations of Tolkien‑related anniversaries such as [Tolkien Reading Day](/wiki/Tolkien_Reading_Day). Although they are recognised by the Tolkien Society, they are run by volunteers and set their own programmes, providing a local complement to the Society’s events. Smials exist in the United Kingdom and in several countries worldwide, and new groups may be founded by members with the Society’s approval.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[12]</a></sup>

## Publications {#Publications}

- *[Belladonna's Broadsheet](/wiki/Belladonna%27s_Broadsheet)*, the first bulletin of The Tolkien Society.
- *[Amon Hen](</wiki/Amon_Hen_(journal)> "Amon Hen (journal)")* is the bulletin of the Society and is published six times a year.<sup id="cite_ref-Amon_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amon-16">[13]</a></sup>
- *[Mallorn](</wiki/Mallorn_(journal)> "Mallorn (journal)")* is the journal of the Society and is currently published once a year, in the Spring.<sup id="cite_ref-Mallorn_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mallorn-17">[14]</a></sup>
- *[Quettar](/wiki/Quettar)* was the journal of the Linguistic Fellowship of The Tolkien Society.

## Events {#Events}

The Tolkien Society organises the following events each year:

- **[The Birthday Toast](/wiki/Birthday_Toast "Birthday Toast")** is held on [3 January](/wiki/3_January) (Tolkien's birthday) every year. The Society encourages Tolkien fans from across the world to toast the Professor at 9pm wherever they are.
- **[Tolkien Reading Day](/wiki/Tolkien_Reading_Day)**, held annually on [25 March](/wiki/25_March), is an international event which promotes the reading of Tolkien's works.
- **The Annual General Meeting and Springmoot** involves a meeting where formal business (with regard to the running of the Tolkien Society) is discussed, followed by a semi-formal dinner with a guest speaker. It is held over a weekend each April.<sup id="cite_ref-AGM_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AGM-18">[15]</a></sup> The **[Tolkien Society Awards](/wiki/Tolkien_Society_Awards)** are announced during the dinner of this event.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[16]</a></sup>
- **The Seminar** is an event which consists of a programme of talks on a Tolkien-related theme. It is held most summers (around June or July).<sup id="cite_ref-Seminar_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seminar-20">[17]</a></sup>
- **[Oxonmoot](/wiki/Oxonmoot)** is a convention held at an Oxford college on the weekend closest to [22 September](/wiki/22_September), [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") and [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins")'s birthday.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxonmoot_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxonmoot-21">[18]</a></sup>
- **[Westmoot](/wiki/Westmoot)** is a new convention aimed at US members of the Society. The inaugural event took place in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri" title="wikipedia:Kansas City, Missouri">Kansas City, Missouri</a> on Friday [May 9](/wiki/May_9) - Sunday [May 11](/wiki/May_11) [2025](/wiki/2025) at the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_World_War_I_Museum_and_Memorial" title="wikipedia:National World War I Museum and Memorial">National World War I Museum and Memorial</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[19]</a></sup>

The Tolkien Society also organises large conferences in order to celebrate an anniversary around J.R.R. Tolkien and/or his works. Past events:

- **[The J.R.R. Tolkien Centenary Conference](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien_Centenary_Conference "J.R.R. Tolkien Centenary Conference")** was held at Keble College (in [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford), England) in [1992](/wiki/1992). It celebrated the centenary of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s birth. It was organised in conjunction with the [Mythopoeic Society](/wiki/Mythopoeic_Society) and was a combination of Oxonmoot 1992 and Mythcon 23.<sup id="cite_ref-Centenary_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Centenary-23">[20]</a></sup>
- **[Tolkien 2005: The Ring Goes Ever On](/wiki/Tolkien_2005 "Tolkien 2005")** was held at Aston University (in Birmingham, England) in [2005](/wiki/2005). It celebrated the fiftieth anniversary of the complete publication of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Tolkien2005_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolkien2005-24">[21]</a></sup>
- **[The Return of the Ring: Celebrating Tolkien in 2012](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_Ring "The Return of the Ring")** was held at Loughborough University (in Leicestershire, England) in [2012](/wiki/2012). It celebrated the seventy-fifth anniversary of the publication of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-25">[22]</a></sup>
