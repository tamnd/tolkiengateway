**The King of the Green Dozen** is the title of an incomplete story written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-firstmention_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-firstmention-1">[1]</a></sup> [Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter) explained in a footnote to [Letter 33](/wiki/Letter_33) in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien)* that the story is set in Wales and is about the King of Iwerddon who, along with the twelve sons of his descendant, has green hair.<sup id="cite_ref-letternote_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letternote-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

On [31 August](/wiki/31_August) [1938](/wiki/1938), Tolkien wrote a letter to <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_A._Furth&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Charles A. Furth (page does not exist)">Charles A. Furth</a> describing the story as a "pseudo-Celtic fairy-story" with a little satire in a similar genre<sup id="cite_ref-secondmention_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secondmention-3">[3]</a></sup> to *[Farmer Giles of Ham](/wiki/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham)*.<sup id="cite_ref-firstmention_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-firstmention-1">[1]</a></sup> On [18 March](/wiki/18_March) [1945](/wiki/1945), Tolkien wrote a letter to [Stanley Unwin](/wiki/Stanley_Unwin), offering to send him the story along with other texts, stating that the story "is half-written, and could be finished" if he approved of *[Farmer Giles of Ham](/wiki/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham)*.<sup id="cite_ref-secondmention_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secondmention-3">[3]</a></sup> On [22 June](/wiki/22_June) of [2025](/wiki/2025), [Jason Fisher](/wiki/Jason_Fisher) speculated that it is likely that, regardless of whether the story holds "interest for readers", it will likely "end up in print someday".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In a footnote to [Letter 33](/wiki/Letter_33) in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien)*, [Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter) explained that Tolkien wrote the story to be a parody of "the ‘high’ style of narrative".<sup id="cite_ref-letternote_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letternote-2">[2]</a></sup>
