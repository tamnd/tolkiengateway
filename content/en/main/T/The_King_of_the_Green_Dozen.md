**The King of the Green Dozen** is the title of an incomplete story written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-firstmention_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-firstmention-1">[1]</a></sup> [Humphrey Carpenter](../H/Humphrey_Carpenter.md) explained in a footnote to [Letter 33](../L/Letter_33.md) in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)* that the story is set in Wales and is about the King of Iwerddon who, along with the twelve sons of his descendant, has green hair.<sup id="cite_ref-letternote_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letternote-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

On [31 August](../_/31_August.md) [1938](../_/1938.md), Tolkien wrote a letter to <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Charles_A._Furth&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Charles A. Furth (page does not exist)">Charles A. Furth</a> describing the story as a "pseudo-Celtic fairy-story" with a little satire in a similar genre<sup id="cite_ref-secondmention_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secondmention-3">[3]</a></sup> to *[Farmer Giles of Ham](../F/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-firstmention_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-firstmention-1">[1]</a></sup> On [18 March](../_/18_March.md) [1945](../_/1945.md), Tolkien wrote a letter to [Stanley Unwin](../S/Stanley_Unwin.md), offering to send him the story along with other texts, stating that the story "is half-written, and could be finished" if he approved of *[Farmer Giles of Ham](../F/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-secondmention_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secondmention-3">[3]</a></sup> On [22 June](../_/22_June.md) of [2025](../_/2025.md), [Jason Fisher](../J/Jason_Fisher.md) speculated that it is likely that, regardless of whether the story holds "interest for readers", it will likely "end up in print someday".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In a footnote to [Letter 33](../L/Letter_33.md) in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)*, [Humphrey Carpenter](../H/Humphrey_Carpenter.md) explained that Tolkien wrote the story to be a parody of "the ‘high’ style of narrative".<sup id="cite_ref-letternote_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letternote-2">[2]</a></sup>
