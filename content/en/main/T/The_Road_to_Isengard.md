**The Road to Isengard** is the eighth chapter of the first book in *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)*.

## Summary {#Summary}

King [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden), [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) the white rider, [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas), [Erkenbrand](/wiki/Erkenbrand), [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer), [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) and the men of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) all gathered beside a stream. When Théoden asked Gandalf about his wizardry over the trees, Gandalf laughed and told them that it had not been his doing. He asked them to come with him to [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard) if they wished to know more about the trees.

After resting, the [men](/wiki/Men "Men") set forth again. They came to [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc), the citadel of [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) at Isengard. They were surprised to find that [Treebeard](/wiki/Treebeard) and his [Ents](/wiki/Ents) had destroyed the citadel, and that Saruman and [Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma") had hidden themselves away in [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc), guarded by none other than [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took"). Théoden, who was anxious to see Treebeard, said good-bye to the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) and accompanied Gandalf to the Northern wall.

## Composition {#Composition}

Tolkien wrote this chapter, along with the five final chapters of Book III were written as a whole (not sequentially) during his spare time in summer and autumn [1942](/wiki/1942). He wrote seven different versions of the dialogue between Gandalf and Théoden. Eventually, after making a fair copy, a lot of that version, its chronology and route to Orthanc, was rejected and rewritten.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> <span></span>
