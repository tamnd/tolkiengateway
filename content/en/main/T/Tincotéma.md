**Tincotéma** was one of the four series of the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) writing system. As with all of the series it consisted of six characters in each of six *[tyeller](/wiki/Tyell%C3%AB "Tyellë")* or 'grades'. This particular series took its name from the first of its signs, *[tinco](/wiki/Tinco "Tinco")* (literally meaning 'metal', but here simply representing the character 't').

## Shapes {#Shapes}

Half of the symbols in this series had one bow or *[lúva](/wiki/L%C3%BAva "Lúva")* while the other half had two bows. All bows in this series were open, curving downwards and to the right from a stem, or *[telco](/wiki/Telco "Telco")*, which was either lowered, raised, or shortened.

## Values {#Values}

In principle, the characters in this series could represent a variety of different sounds, though in practice their use had become essentially standardised by the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveolar_consonant" title="wikipedia:Alveolar consonant">alveolar</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_consonant" title="wikipedia:Dental consonant">dental</a> consonants.

The first four signs in the series, *[tinco](/wiki/Tinco "Tinco")*, *[ando](/wiki/Ando "Ando")*, <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%BAle&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Thúle (page does not exist)">thúle</a></i> and *[anto](/wiki/Anto "Anto")*, each represented a variant of the basic 't' sound: 't', 'd', 'th' and 'dh', respectively. The fifth sign, *[númen](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen "Númen")*, represented 'n', and finally *[óre](/wiki/%C3%93re "Óre")* indicated a weak 'r' sound.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
