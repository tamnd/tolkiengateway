The <b>First Map of <i>The Lord of the Rings</i></b> refers to the earliest map of the western regions of Middle-earth that was drawn by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The map has squares of 2 centimetre side and a map scale where 2 centimetres are the equivalent of 100 miles.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> It was redrawn and commented on by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) in *[The Treason of Isengard](/wiki/The_Treason_of_Isengard)* (1989; chapter 15).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

The First Map of the Lord of the Rings covers a larger area of Middle-earth than the [General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth) or [The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age](/wiki/The_West_of_Middle-earth_at_the_End_of_the_Third_Age) map. It also shows the lands to the north of the [Icebay of Forochel](/wiki/Icebay_of_Forochel) and the island of [Tol Fuin](/wiki/Tol_Fuin) west of the island of [Himling](/wiki/Himling) off the coast of [Forlindon](/wiki/Forlindon) in the [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer) ocean. The sea west of the [Icebay of Forochel](/wiki/Icebay_of_Forochel) was labeled as *[North Sea](/wiki/North_Sea)* in pencil.

## Early concepts {#Early_concepts}

A label "Forodwaith (Northerland)" is written in the area between the river [Baranduin](/wiki/Baranduin) and the river [Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3) and extends from south of the [Sarn Ford](/wiki/Sarn_Ford) to beyond the northern end of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains). There is no label "Eriador" on the map. However, J.R.R. Tolkien added "(or Eriador)" after "Forodwaith" on The 1943 Map of The Lord of the Rings.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> This and the absence of the name Eriador from earlier manuscripts suggest that Forodwaith was initially used as a name of the region that was later called Eriador.

A label "[Westfold](/wiki/Westfold)" was written to the south of a label "[Dunland](/wiki/Dunland)" west of the southern end of the Misty Mountains and north of the river [Isen](/wiki/Isen), but was struck through<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and reinserted west of [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras), north of the northern foothills of the ["Black Mountains"](/wiki/White_Mountains "White Mountains").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> This and the use of the name Westfold for the land of the Westfolders, the invaders of Rohan in earlier drafts of *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)*<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> suggest that Westfold was initially used as a name of the region that was later called Dunland.
