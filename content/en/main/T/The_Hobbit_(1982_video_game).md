<i><b>The Hobbit</b></i> (also known as **The Hobbit Software Adventure** in North America and [Australia](/wiki/Australia)) was the first licensed video game based on Tolkien's work, being the first part of *[The Tolkien Trilogy](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Trilogy)* (also known as *[The Tolkien Software Adventure Series](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Software_Adventure_Series)*). The game was designed by [Philip Mitchell](/wiki/Philip_Mitchell) and [Veronika Megler](/wiki/Veronika_Megler),<sup id="cite_ref-world_spectrum_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-world_spectrum-1">[1]</a></sup> and illustrated by Kent Rees.<sup id="cite_ref-Review_p._76_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Review_p._76-3">[3]</a></sup> The game was considered large at the time of its release and included 80 locations, of which 30 were illustrated.<sup id="cite_ref-Review_p._76_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Review_p._76-3">[3]</a></sup> The game was released by [Melbourne House](/wiki/Melbourne_House) for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstrad_CPC" title="wikipedia:Amstrad CPC">Amstrad CPC</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_B" title="wikipedia:BBC B">BBC B</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64" title="wikipedia:Commodore 64">Commodore 64</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_32/64" title="wikipedia:Dragon 32/64">Dragon 32</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSX" title="wikipedia:MSX">MSX</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oric-1" title="wikipedia:Oric-1">Oric-1</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZX-Spectrum" title="wikipedia:ZX-Spectrum">ZX-Spectrum</a> in most of the world,<sup id="cite_ref-Tolkien_Games_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolkien_Games-2">[2]</a></sup><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r403243">.mw-parser-output .cite-web-dated,.mw-parser-output .cite-web-accessed{color:var(--tg-color-subtle)}</style><cite class="citation cite-web"><span class="cite-web-author">Fredrik Ekman</span>. <span class="cite-web-article"><a class="external text" href="http://www.lysator.liu.se/tolkien-games/entry/hobbit.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">&#34;The Hobbit / The Hobbit Software Adventure&#34;</a></span>. <span class="cite-web-website"><a class="external text" href="http://www.lysator.liu.se" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tolkien computer games</a></span>. <span class="cite-web-accessed">Retrieved 30 December 2014</span></cite>&lt;/ref&gt; whilst [Addison-Wesley](/wiki/Addison-Wesley) released it as *The Hobbit Software Adventure* for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_II" title="wikipedia:Apple II">Apple II</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64" title="wikipedia:Commodore 64">Commodore 64</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macintosh" title="wikipedia:Macintosh">Macintosh</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-DOS" title="wikipedia:MS-DOS">MS-DOS</a> in North America and Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-Tolkien_Games_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolkien_Games-2">[2]</a></sup> After its initial success, it was re-released by Sinclair Research Ltd.<sup id="cite_ref-world_spectrum_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-world_spectrum-1">[1]</a></sup> Beam Software also developed editions for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiga" title="wikipedia:Amiga">Amiga</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRS-80" title="wikipedia:TRS-80">TRS-80</a>, but were never released.<sup id="cite_ref-Tolkien_Games_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolkien_Games-2">[2]</a></sup> Every game also included a copy of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and an instruction book.<sup id="cite_ref-Review_p._76_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Review_p._76-3">[3]</a></sup>

In [1984](/wiki/1984) [David Elkan](/wiki/David_Elkan) published a book, *[A Guide to Playing The Hobbit](/wiki/A_Guide_to_Playing_The_Hobbit)*, to help players complete the game.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Beam Software originally planned a sequel, *[Where Hobbits Dare](/wiki/Where_Hobbits_Dare)*, but was cancelled.<sup id="cite_ref-FAQ_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FAQ-5">[5]</a></sup> Instead, it was succeeded by [Lord of the Rings: Game One](/wiki/Lord_of_the_Rings:_Game_One).

In 2012 the Hobbit environment "Wilderland" was published (see external link below). It runs the original ZX Spectrum game code in an emulator and reveals what is going on in the background of the game.

## Plot {#Plot}

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Hobbit_(1982_video_game)_-_Dale_ruins.gif"><img src="/w/images/d/d3/The_Hobbit_%281982_video_game%29_-_Dale_ruins.gif" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="288" height="224" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="288" data-file-height="224"/></a><figcaption>The Ruins of <a href="/wiki/Dale" title="Dale">Dale</a>.</figcaption></figure>

The player controls [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) - the main character - on his quest to [the Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"), in company with the [Dwarf](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin). On their way, they meet characters like [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) and [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) and are confronted by [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls), [Wargs](/wiki/Wargs) and [Goblins](/wiki/Goblins). Eventually Bilbo and Thorin reach the Lonely Mountain and plunder the lair of [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug).<sup id="cite_ref-Review_p._76_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Review_p._76-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Review_p._78_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Review_p._78-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Reception {#Reception}

With a retail price of £14.95,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> *The Hobbit*, sold over 100,000 copies in the first two years,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> and had sold over a million copies by the end of the decade.

In [1983](/wiki/1983)'s <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Joystick_Awards" title="wikipedia:Golden Joystick Awards">Golden Joystick Awards</a> it was the winner of the category *Best Strategy Game* and came second in the category *Game of the Year*.<sup id="cite_ref-world_spectrum_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-world_spectrum-1">[1]</a></sup>
