**The Beatles** were a legendary band, made up of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon" title="wikipedia:John Lennon">John Lennon</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney" title="wikipedia:Paul McCartney">Paul McCartney</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison" title="wikipedia:George Harrison">George Harrison</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr" title="wikipedia:Ringo Starr">Ringo Starr</a>, who were in talks to star in an adaptation of *The Lord of the Rings* in 1969.

## History {#History}

In 1969, The Beatles were due one more film on their three-picture deal with United Artists. Their agent Denis O'Dell thought an adaptation of The Lord of the Rings with the four band members as the four Hobbits "and <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donovan" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Donovan</a>, too"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup> would be a good match. By chance, United Artists were just closing negotiations for the cinema rights to *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. The band, who were currently in India, did not read the book until urged to do so by Donovan and O'Dell, who brought copies in his suitcase.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup>

According to O'Dell, John Lennon fancied the role of Gandalf,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup> but George Harrison recalled that Lennon then wanted to swap for Frodo. Ringo Starr wanted to play Sam, while Paul McCartney coveted Frodo.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">[2]</a></sup> He told [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson) that Lennon would have been Gollum, Ringo Sam, and Harrison Gandalf.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Donovan was keen on Merry, and they wanted <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twiggy" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Twiggy</a> for Galadriel.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup>

Meanwhile, in conversations with [United Artists](/wiki/United_Artists), it was decided to court a star director in place of the Beatles' previous director Richard Lester: First, <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lean" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">David Lean</a> (brother of Tangye Lean, one of the original [Inklings](/wiki/Inklings)) was approached and called it an "interesting idea"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup> but was busy preparing <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan%27s_Daughter" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><i>Ryan&#39;s Daughter</i></a>. Stanley Kubrick was next, reading the book at the urging of his daughter while on a ferry back to the UK from doing publicity for <i><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001:_A_Space_Odyssey_(film)" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">2001: A Space Odyssey</a></i>. Interested but busy preparing a to-be-aborted biopic of Napoleon's, Kubrick declined twice, before telling O'Dell its "unfilmable."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup> Heinz Edelmann, a fan of the book and art director on *Yellow Submarine*, pitched his own idea for an adaptation to United Artists, but was summarily rejected.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">[2]</a></sup>

O'Dell courted <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo_Antonioni" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Michaelangelo Antonioni</a>, but by that point the project ran aground: accounts vary as to whether Tolkien threatened to pull out of negotiations for the rights with United Artists if the Beatles were involved,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> or whether the band members simply lost interest.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">[2]</a></sup> When UA did finally land the rights, they contracted [John Boorman](/wiki/John_Boorman) to write and direct, still with the Beatles attached to star,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> not knowing the band was breaking up.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

In 2021, [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson), who directed the [Lord of the Rings film series](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)"), directed a three-part documentary about the band called Get Back.
