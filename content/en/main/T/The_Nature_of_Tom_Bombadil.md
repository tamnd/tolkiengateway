[Tom Bombadil](Tom_Bombadil.md)'s mythological origins in the internal cosmology of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) have puzzled even erudite fans. For example, the fact that the Ring had no power on Tom would either suggest that Tom existed in both the [Seen](../S/Seen.md) and the [Unseen](../U/Unseen.md) realms or that the Unseen had no effect on him since "He is his own [master](../M/Master.md)".

Speculative ideas about his true nature range from simply a wise [Elven](../E/Elves.md) hermit to a [Maia](../M/Maiar.md) or [Vala](../V/Valar.md), to even [Ilúvatar](../I/Ilúvatar.md) himself.

<h2><span id="Tolkien.27s_words"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Tolkien&#39;s_words">Tolkien&#39;s words</span></h2>

Even during his lifetime, [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) was asked directly about Tom, but he dismissed these questions, clearly indicating that Tom is a living enigma, part of a wider, incomprehensible world:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Even in a mythical Age there must be some enigmas, as there always are. Tom Bombadil is one (intentionally).
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I don&#39;t think Tom needs philosophizing about, and is not improved by it. But many have found him an odd or indeed discordant ingredient.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-L153_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L153-2">[2]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Bombadil is just as he is. Just an odd ‘fact’ of that world. He won&#39;t be explained, because as long as you are [...] concentrated on the Ring, he is inexplicable. [A reminder] that the world is so large and manifold that if you take one facet and fix your mind and heart on it, there is always something that does not come in to that story [...] and seems to belong to a larger story.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-coghill_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coghill-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Tolkien suggests that even in the [secondary world](../S/Secondary_World~65496.md), the characters, primarily Hobbits, who interacted with him were not fully aware of his nature.

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The <a href="../B/Bucklanders.md" title="Bucklanders">Bucklanders</a> knew Bombadil, though, no doubt, they had as little understanding of his powers as the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../S/Shire-folk.md" title="Shire-folk">Shire-folk</a> had of <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a>&#39;s: both were regarded as benevolent persons, mysterious maybe and unpredictable but nonetheless comic.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil.md" title="The Adventures of Tom Bombadil">The Adventures of Tom Bombadil</a></i>, Preface</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

## Tom as Eru Ilúvatar {#Tom_as_Eru_Ilúvatar}

When Goldberry was asked by [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) who he was, she simply said "*He is*" which parallels the scriptural name of God in Christianity, "*ὁ ὤν*" (the Being, the One who is). Goldberry also said that Tom is simply "the [Master](../M/Master.md)",<sup id="cite_ref-Council_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-4">[4]</a></sup> and the theory that Tom is an incarnation, embodiment or "avatar" of Ilúvatar has been favored by the fans.<sup id="cite_ref-FAQ_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FAQ-5">[5]</a></sup>

Tolkien, however, later remarked that this dialogue was not a reference to God, in the same way that the title of "Father" in reference to a priest does not allude to God.<sup id="cite_ref-L153_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L153-2">[2]</a></sup>

In the [Council of Elrond](../C/Council_of_Elrond.md), it was mentioned that Tom has limited knowledge and understanding of the powers of the Ring, which would suggest that he is not Ilúvatar.

Elrond calls Bombadil "a strange *creature*" \[emphasis added\] which would not be a correct description of an uncreated Creator. It is possible, however, that unless Elrond also was ignorant about Bombadil's nature.

Tolkien was a devout Roman Catholic, and he specifically stated that the idea of God's incarnation was too large to fit in his work.<sup id="cite_ref-L181_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L181-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-L211_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L211-7">[7]</a></sup> Tolkien would later go on to experiment with this idea in the *[Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth](../A/Athrabeth_Finrod_ah_Andreth.md),* which is about a future incarnation of Ilúvatar.

Tolkien specifically hinted that Tom, unlike Ilúvatarr, is a part of this world, and he would be destroyed along with everything else if evil triumphed.<sup id="cite_ref-coghill_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coghill-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Tom as an Ainu {#Tom_as_an_Ainu}

Another possible explanation is that Tom is an [Ainu](../A/Ainur.md).<sup id="cite_ref-FAQ_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FAQ-5">[5]</a></sup>

Since all seven Valar are named and accounted for,<sup id="cite_ref-Val_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Val-8">[8]</a></sup> then one would have to be manifesting as Tom. For instance, a theory identifies Tom with [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) and Goldberry with [Yavanna](../Y/Yavanna.md).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>In contrast to the seven Valar, the Maiar are of an unknown number,<sup id="cite_ref-Val_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Val-8">[8]</a></sup> so it is easier to group Tom among them. Importantly, however, Tom was in Arda before the [Valar](../V/Valar.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Days_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Days-10">[10]</a></sup> making it harder to support Tom's identity as an Ainu.

Tom remembers himself to be the *"[Eldest](../E/Eldest.md), that's what I am...Tom remembers the first raindrop and the first acorn...He knew the dark under the stars when it was fearless – before the Dark Lord came from [Outside](Timeless_Halls.md)"*. "Eldest", incidentally, is also a title given to [Treebeard](Treebeard.md).

After the destruction of the One Ring, Gandalf goes to talk with Tom, suggesting a deep bond between the two. Gandalf's visit to Tom, at the very least, shows an important association with the Maiar. The Ring, however, had no effect on him, although it had some effect on [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md) and [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md), both of whom are Maia. Also, Gandalf in the Council of Elrond said that Tom is "neutral" and would not understand the cause of the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md), while all other known Maiar had taken sides.

## Tom as the embodiment of Arda {#Tom_as_the_embodiment_of_Arda}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The spirit of this earth made aware of itself.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien explaining Tom to <a href="../N/Nevill_Coghill.md" title="Nevill Coghill">Nevill Coghill</a><sup id="cite_ref-coghill_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coghill-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

A more radical and abstract theory is that he is possibly the embodiment of [Arda](../A/Arda.md) itself, a "Father Nature." Tom, though close to nature, at times separates himself from it. One such instance is in the Old Forest while battling against Old Man Willow and by having a different disposition than that of the Old Forest which is described as dark and full of hate for everything that goes about freely. Tom on the other hand, sings constantly even when fighting the barrow-wight: *"None has ever caught him yet, for Tom, he is the Master: His songs are stronger songs, and his feet are faster"*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Beings like him {#Beings_like_him}

Whatever his nature, another question about Bombadil is whether he is one-of-a-kind being, or if he has other colleagues in other parts of Arda. Bombadil could be for example the one and single "spirit of all Arda", or just the "spirit of the Old Forest" with other such beings in other forests.

The nature of Goldberry (and [River-woman](../R/River-woman.md)) is also obscure. They could be the same kind of being like himself, or his female counterpart.

Gandalf, when narrating his fight with [Durin's Bane](../D/Durin's_Bane.md), mentions tunnels made by [nameless things](../N/Nameless_things.md) whose existence Sauron knows not, since they are "older than he".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
