<i><b>Tolkien and the Classical World</b></i> is a collection of essays edited by Hamish Williams which looks at the classical sources of inspiration behind [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s works. It was published as No. 45 in the *[Cormarë Series](/wiki/Cormar%C3%AB_Series)*.

## Contents {#Contents}

<dl><dt>Introduction</dt></dl>

- "Classical Tradition, Modern Fantasy, and the Generic Contracts of Readers" by Hamish Williams

<dl><dt>Section 1 - Classical Lives and Histories</dt></dl>

- "Tolkien the Classicist: Scholar and Thinker" by Hamish Williams
- "Greek and Roman Historiographies in Tolkien's Númenor" by Ross Clare

<dl><dt>Section 2 - Ancient Epic and Myth</dt></dl>

- "The Gods in (Tolkien's) Epic: Classical Patterns of Divine Interaction" by Giuseppe Pezzini
- "Middle-earth as Underworld: From Katabasis to Eucatastrophe" by Benjamin Eldon Stevens
- "Pietas and the Fall of the City: A Neglected Virgilian Influence on Middle-earth's Chief Virtue" by Austin M. Freeman
- "The Love Story of Orpheus and Eurydice in Tolkien's Orphic Middle-earth" by Peter Astrup Sundt

<dl><dt>Section 3 - In Dialogue with the Greek Philosophers</dt></dl>

- "Plato's Atlantis and the Post-Platonic Tradition in Tolkien's Downfall of Númenor" by Michael Kleu
- Less Consciously at First but More Consciously in the Revision: Plato's Ring of Gyges as a Putative Source of Inspiration for Tolkien's Ring of Power" by [Łukasz Neubauer](/wiki/%C5%81ukasz_Neubauer)
- "Horror and Fury: J.R.R. Tolkien's The Children of Húrin and the Aristotelian Theory of Tragedy" by Julian Eilmann

<dl><dt>Section 4 - Around the Borders of the Classical World</dt></dl>

- "'Eastwards and Southwards': Philological and Historical Perspectives on Tolkien and Classicism" by Philip Burton
- "The Noldorization of the Edain: The Roman-Germani Paradigm for the Noldor and Edain in Tolkien's Migration Era" by Richard Z. Gallant
- "'Escape and Consolation': Gondor as the Ancient Mediterranean and Rohan as the Germanic World in The Lord of the Rings" by Juliette Harrisson

<dl><dt>Section 5 - Shorter Remarks and Observations</dt></dl>

- "Shepherds and the Shire: Classical Pastoralism in Middle-earth" by Alley Marie Jordan
- "Classical Influences on the Role of Music in Tolkien's Legendarium" by Oleksandra Filonenko and Vitalii Shchepanskyi

<dl><dt>Afterword</dt></dl>

- "Afterword: Tolkien's Response to Classics in Its Wider Context" by D. Graham J. Shipley

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>While scholars have often cited the influence of medieval texts and society on J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s seminal fantasy creations, the role of the classical world – the literature and thought of ancient Greece and Rome – has received far less attention.<br/><br/>This volume of essays explores various ways in which Tolkien&#39;s literary creations were shaped by classical epic, myth, poetry, history, philosophy, drama, and language. In making such connections, the contributors to this volume are interested not simply in source-hunting but in how a reception of the classical world can shape the meaning we derive from Tolkien&#39;s masterworks.<br/><br/>The contributions to this volume by Philip Burton, <a href="/wiki/%C5%81ukasz_Neubauer" title="Łukasz Neubauer">Łukasz Neubauer</a>, Giuseppe Pezzini, Benjamin Eldon Stevens, Graham Shipley, and several other scholars should pave the way for further discussions between classical studies and fantasy studies.
</p>
</blockquote>
