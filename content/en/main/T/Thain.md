The **Thain** was an office of great respect in [the Shire](The_Shire.md). The Thain was master of the [Shire-moot](../S/Shire-moot.md) and captain of the [Shire-muster](../S/Shire-muster.md) and [Hobbitry-in-arms](../H/Hobbitry-in-arms.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Order_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Order-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

For nearly four hundred years after its foundation, the Shire had been part of the lands of [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md), and under the rule of that land's [King](../K/Kings_of_Arthedain.md). Ultimately, Arthedain fell to the forces of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md), and its last King, [Arvedui](../A/Arvedui.md), was lost in [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [1975](Third_Age_1975.md),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">[2]</a></sup> leaving the [Shire-hobbits](../S/Shire-hobbits.md) without a ruler. They remedied this by choosing a new leader from among themselves, [Bucca of the Marish](../B/Bucca_of_the_Marish.md), who was given the title *thain*, a word simply meaning "chief" in [their dialect](../H/Hobbitish.md).

In origin a military office, Thainship passed strictly through the male line.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter-3">[3]</a></sup>

Bucca and his descendants, a family known as the [Oldbucks](../O/Oldbucks.md), served as Shire-thain for twelve generations. The twelfth Thain, one [Gorhendad Oldbuck](../G/Gorhendad_Oldbuck.md), left the Shire and crossed the River [Brandywine](../B/Brandywine.md) to found [Buckland](../B/Buckland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Conspiracy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conspiracy-4">[4]</a></sup> With his departure, the Thainship passed to a new line, the preeminent [Tooks](Took_Family.md), and specifically to [Isumbras Took I](../I/Isumbras_Took_I.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Order_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Order-1">[1]</a></sup>

As a rule, the Thain was also the head of the Took clan (known as "The Took") and the [Great Smials](../G/Great_Smials.md), but the two offices were not identical, as if the Thain was survived by a widow, she remained the head of the Tooks; at least this was the case of [Lalia Clayhanger](../L/Lalia_Clayhanger.md), widow of Thain [Fortinbras II](../F/Fortinbras_Took_II.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Letter_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter-3">[3]</a></sup>

But as the times of emergency in the Shire were rare, as the need for the muster and the moot (the [only battle](../B/Battle_of_Greenfields.md) was fought in [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2747](Third_Age_2747.md) and the [last emergency](<../F/Fell_Winter_(Third_Age).md>) in [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2912](Third_Age_2912.md)), the title ended up as nothing more than nominal. Indeed, there was no "government" in the Shire as the clans managed their own affairs. By the end of the Age, the [Mayor of Michel Delving](../M/Mayor_of_Michel_Delving.md) was "the only real official in the Shire".<sup id="cite_ref-Order_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Order-1">[1]</a></sup>

From Isumbras I, the line of the Took Shire-thains continued unbroken to the time of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) and beyond with Peregrin I when thainship became also a Counsellor office of the [North Kingdom](../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Later_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Later-5">[5]</a></sup>

The last recorded Thain was his son, [Faramir Took I](../F/Faramir_Took_I.md), the thirty-fourth to hold the title of Shire-thain.<sup id="cite_ref-Later_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Later-5">[5]</a></sup> The fact that both he and his father [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) are recorded as the first of that name shows that there must have been more Thains after this time, and the office clearly lasted well into the [Fourth Age](../F/Fourth_Age.md).

Peregrin's successors continued the custom of gathering many [Gondorian](../G/Gondor.md) manuscripts mainly copies or summaries of legends relating to [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) and his heirs, and the libraries of [Great Smials](../G/Great_Smials.md) were the only place in the Shire with sources of [Númenorean](../N/Númenor.md) interest. Pippin's great-grandson (not known if he was a Thain himself) requested a copy of the [Thain's Book](Thain's_Book.md), completed for him by [Findegil](../F/Findegil.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## List of Thains {#List_of_Thains}

It is known that there were at least thirty-three Shire-thains, from [Bucca of the Marish](../B/Bucca_of_the_Marish.md), who became Thain in [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [1979](Third_Age_1979.md), to [Faramir I](../F/Faramir_Took_I.md), who took on the title in [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [63](../F/Fourth_Age_63.md), more than a thousand years later.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Thain is an alternative spelling, found in Middle English texts, of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_English" title="wikipedia:Early Modern English">Early Modern English</a> word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thegn" title="wikipedia:Thegn">thane</a></i>. The word is derived from [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *þegn*, which meant a person holding lands from the king in exchange for military service. The spelling "thane" appears in the verse from “The Muster of Rohan” which begins "From dark Dunharrow in the dim morning/with thane and captain rode Thengel’s son." <span></span>
