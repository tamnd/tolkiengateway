<i><b>Tolkien and the Study of His Sources: Critical Essays</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> edited by [Jason Fisher](../J/Jason_Fisher.md), is a collection of essays — dealing with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_criticism" title="wikipedia:Source criticism">source criticism</a> — on [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s works. The book was awarded the [Mythopoeic Society](../M/Mythopoeic_Society.md)'s 2014 Mythopoeic Scholarship Award for Inklings Studies.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

*Tolkien and the Study of His Sources* includes "new primary material: quotations from a handful of previously unpublished letters<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 2]</a></sup> as well as from Tolkien’s unpublished lecture notes on the '[Legends of the Goths](The_Goths.md)'".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[2]</a></sup>

## Contents {#Contents}

- "Introduction: Why Source Criticism?" ([Tom Shippey](Tom_Shippey.md))
- "Source Criticism: Background and Applications" (E.L. Risden)
- "Tolkien and Source Criticism: Remarking and Remaking" (Jason Fisher)
- "The Stones and the Book: Tolkien, Mesopotamia, and Biblical Mythopoeia" (Nicholas Birns)
- "Sea Birds and Morning Stars: Ceyx, Alcyone, and the Many Metamorphoses of Eärendil and Elwing" ([Kristine Larsen](../K/Kristine_Larsen.md))
- "'Byzantium, New Rome!': Goths, Langobards, and Byzantium in *The Lord of the Rings*" (Miryam Librán-Moreno)
- "The Rohirrim: 'Anglo-Saxons on Horseback'? An Inquiry into Tolkien's Use of Sources" ([Thomas Honegger](Thomas_Honegger.md))
- "William Caxton's *The Golden Legend* as a Source for J.R.R. Tolkien's *The Lord of the Rings*" (Judy Ann Ford)
- "*She* and Tolkien, Revisited" ([John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md))
- "Reading John Buchan in Search of J.R.R. Tolkien" ([Mark T. Hooker](../M/Mark_T._Hooker.md))
- "Biography as Source: Niggles and Notions" (Diana Pavlac Glyer and [Josh B. Long](../J/Josh_B._Long.md))

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Over the past four decades, source criticism--the analysis of a writer&#39;s source material--has emerged as one of the most popular approaches in exploring the work of J.R.R. Tolkien. Since Tolkien drew from a wide range of disparate sources in the construction of his legendarium--<i>The Book of Lost Tales</i>, <i>The Hobbit</i>, <i>The Lord of the Rings</i> trilogy and beyond--an understanding of the sources Tolkien used, as well as how and why he incorporated them, can enhance readers&#39; appreciation of his works. This compendium by leading Tolkien scholars describes the theory and methodology for proper source criticism of Tolkien&#39;s works and then provides practical demonstrations of the approach. Ranging widely across Tolkien&#39;s writings, as well as across the periods and genres from which he took inspiration, the essays provide the most balanced and comprehensive demonstration of Tolkien source criticism available.
</p>
