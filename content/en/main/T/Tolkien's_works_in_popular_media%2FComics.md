**[Tolkien's works in popular media](/wiki/Tolkien%27s_works_in_popular_media)** include a multitide of illustrations in the style of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comics" title="wikipedia:Comics">comics</a> or <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoons" title="wikipedia:Cartoons">cartoons</a>. This article lists [notable](/wiki/Tolkien_Gateway:Notability_policy "Tolkien Gateway:Notability policy") comics based on, or including references to, [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and/or [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

## Early comics/cartoons {#Early_comics/cartoons}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/c9/20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg/300px-20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="202" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/c9/20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg/450px-20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/c9/20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg/600px-20_November_1954_Newsweek_cartoon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1093" data-file-height="735"/></a><figcaption>Single frame cartoon in <i>Newsweek</i> from 1954</figcaption></figure>

The first known published illustration in the comical vein is a single frame cartoon appearing in the [29 November](/wiki/29_November) [1954](/wiki/1954) edition of *Newsweek* (which contains a review of the newly published *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*). The cartoon, drawn by "M. Simont" (apparently being <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Simont" title="wikipedia:Marc Simont">Marc Simont</a>), portrays the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) of [Moria](/wiki/Moria) as hairy creatures with large heads featuring beaks.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) wears a robe adorned with what appears to be magical symbols.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

- [1964](/wiki/1964)-[1965](/wiki/1965): *[Princess](/wiki/Princess)*

- [1979](/wiki/1979)-[1992](/wiki/1992): *[Duga](/wiki/Duga)*

## References in popular comics {#References_in_popular_comics}

### DC Comics {#DC_Comics}

- In the 2006 <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Beetle" title="wikipedia:Blue Beetle">Blue Beetle</a></i> series, the eponymous hero's "hideout" is called [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton). His minor enemy, the Diviner, summons Ents.
- In <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Origins" title="wikipedia:Secret Origins">Secret Origins</a></i> #20, Barbara Gordon reads *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* on the plane from Chicago to Gotham.
- The <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Super-Heroes" title="wikipedia:Legion of Super-Heroes">Legion of Super-Heroes</a> villain Tseln has a "[Mithril](/wiki/Mithril) silver" knife.
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasha_Bordeaux" title="wikipedia:Sasha Bordeaux">Sasha Bordeaux</a> mentions [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) in *Checkmate* #4
- The story in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amethyst,_Princess_of_Gemworld" title="wikipedia:Amethyst, Princess of Gemworld"><i>Amethyst</i></a> (1985) #4 is called "There and Back Again".
- In <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_Man" title="wikipedia:Plastic Man">Plastic Man</a></i> (2004) #12, FBI Agent Morgan tracks down an illegal downloader and notices *The Lord of the Rings*, which she heard was good.
- Kyle Rayner carries a copy of *The Lord of the Rings* in <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern" title="wikipedia:Green Lantern">Green Lantern</a></i> (1990) #97
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainiac_5" title="wikipedia:Brainiac 5">Brainiac 5</a> mentions the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring) in *Legion of Super-Heroes* (2010) #1
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beast_Boy" title="wikipedia:Beast Boy">Changeling</a> calls burrowing aliens [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) in *DC Special: The Return of Donna Troy* #2
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_League" title="wikipedia:Justice League"><i>Justice League of America</i></a> #43 (2010): Josiah Power quotes Numbers 20:18, and Doctor Impossible is surprised it's from the Bible and not Gandalf (You shall not pass)

### Marvel {#Marvel}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Captain_America_reading_Tolkien_-_Avengers_46.jpg"><img src="/w/images/1/15/Captain_America_reading_Tolkien_-_Avengers_46.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="256" height="382" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="256" data-file-height="382"/></a><figcaption>Captain America reading Tolkien in <i>Avengers</i> #46 (1967)</figcaption></figure>

- Marvel's got characters named [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), [Numenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor "Númenor") and [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) (a winged horse). Sauron actually named himself after the character.
- Spidey calls his pals the Fellowship in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Tiger_(comics)" title="wikipedia:White Tiger (comics)"><i>White Tiger</i></a> #6
- Billy Kaplan's bedroom door has "Speak Friend and Enter", in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Avengers" title="wikipedia:Young Avengers"><i>Young Avengers Presents</i></a> #1
- A boxing match between Slammer Sauron and Freddy Frodo in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(comics)" title="wikipedia:Avengers (comics)"><i>Avengers</i></a> #43
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America" title="wikipedia:Captain America">Captain America</a> is reading a "chapter of Tolkien" in *Avengers* #46 (1967)<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup>

### IDW {#IDW}

- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Little_Pony_(IDW_Publishing)#Friendship_Is_Magic_.28November_2012.E2.80.93Present.29" title="wikipedia:My Little Pony (IDW Publishing)"><i>My Little Pony: Friendship is Magic</i></a> #16 (2014) featured numerous reference to Tolkien's work. The first involved Gandalf appearing in Ponyville, after it had been overran by literary characters. The second being in a brief story called "One Worm to Fool Them All", which parodied *The Lord of The Rings*. Each character dressed as a different *Lord of the Rings* character, with Twilight Sparkle as Boromir, Rainbow Dash as Bilbo Baggins and Pinkie Pie as Gandalf. The story began to end after the characters grew tired of walking for "25 pages" and transitioned into a *Star Trek* parody.
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Little_Pony_(IDW_Publishing)#Friends_Forever_.28January_2014.E2.80.93April_2017.29" title="wikipedia:My Little Pony (IDW Publishing)"><i>My Little Pony: Friends Forever</i></a> #2 (2017) featured a brief sequence where Discord used his magic to transport the Cutie Mark Crusaders across historical and fictional locations. One of which included them appearing in the Shire where they told Starswirl the Bearded that they lost "that ring" and wondered if it was important.
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_My_Little_Pony_comics_issued_by_IDW_Publishing#Special_editions" title="wikipedia:List of My Little Pony comics issued by IDW Publishing"><i>My Little Pony: Holiday Special</i></a> (2017) had Night Light read a book titled the "Lord of the Reigns".
