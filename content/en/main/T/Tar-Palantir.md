**Tar-Palantir** was the twenty-fourth [King of Númenor](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor),<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Palantir_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Palantir-1">[1]</a></sup> who tried to restore the [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor "Númenor") traditions during the last dark years of the realm.

## History {#History}

Tar-Palantir received the [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name *Inziladûn* from his father after his birth.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Palantir_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Palantir-1">[1]</a></sup> His father [Ar-Gimilzôr](/wiki/Ar-Gimilz%C3%B4r) was a bitter opponent of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), and had banished the speaking of the [Elven tongues](/wiki/Elvish "Elvish") in his realm. However, Inziladûn's mother, [Inzilbêth](/wiki/Inzilb%C3%AAth), was secretly a member of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful) and taught her son to be an [Elf-friend](/wiki/Elf-friend).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Gimilzor_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Gimilzor-2">[2]</a></sup>

When Inziladûn took the [Sceptre of Númenor](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor) it was a time of great darkness in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor). Ever since the time of [Tar-Atanamir](/wiki/Tar-Atanamir), every King had spoken against the Valar and questioned the [Ban](/wiki/Ban_of_the_Valar "Ban of the Valar") they had put upon the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) against sailing into the [West](/wiki/Aman "Aman"). By this time, the Númenóreans's riches and power grew, but also did their fear for Death, and their joy and years lessened.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup>

Inziladûn sought to repent of these actions of his predecessors and took a royal name in the Elvish tongue [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) in the tradition of his ancestors: Tar-Palantir. As a consequence, he let the Faithful live in peace. Once again the [White Tree](/wiki/Nimloth_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Nimloth of Númenor") was properly tended, for Tar-Palantir's gift of foresight allowed him to ascertain that if the White Tree perished, then the line of Kings would also come to an end. The King also faithfully observed the ancient [religious ceremonies](/wiki/Three_Prayers "Three Prayers") of the Númenóreans at due times.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup>

The Valar did not respond to Tar-Palantir's repentance because of the insolence of the Kings before him, and because the greater part of the Númenóreans were still hostile to the [Lords of the West](/wiki/Valar "Valar"). Nor did Elven ships ever come from [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) to Númenor again. Tar-Palantir became filled with sorrow over the continuing rebellion of his people, and began to spend more of his time at the tower of [Tar-Minastir](/wiki/Tar-Minastir) in the west of the isle, looking to the West with nostalgia, maybe hoping to see a ship from there. But even the sight of it was withheld from him. His brother Gimilkhâd followed the policies of his father Ar-Gimilzôr and became the leader of the party that had been called the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men) and opposed the will of Tar-Palantir both openly and in secret. After the death of Gimilkhâd, his son [Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n "Ar-Pharazôn") probably became the leader of the King's Men, because he was even more restless and eager for power and wealth than his father and used his renown as a captain both by land and sea and his great wealth to turn the hearts of the people to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup> As a consequence, there was strife and rebellion in Númenor during the reign of Tar-Palantir.<sup id="cite_ref-Rebellion_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rebellion-4">[4]</a></sup>

Tar-Palantir married late and had only one child, a daughter named [Míriel](/wiki/Tar-M%C3%ADriel "Tar-Míriel"). According to the [law of succession](/wiki/Law_of_Succession_in_N%C3%BAmenor "Law of Succession in Númenor"), Míriel would have succeeded her father as [Queen of Númenor](/wiki/Queen_of_N%C3%BAmenor) with the royal name Tar-Míriel. However, when Tar-Palantir died, Pharazôn, the leader of the rebellion,<sup id="cite_ref-Rebellion_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rebellion-4">[4]</a></sup> married Míriel against her will and against the laws of Númenor, because she was his first cousin, usurped the sceptre and took the title Ar-Pharazôn (Tar-Calion). His wife Míriel was named Ar-Zimraphel.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Palantir_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Palantir-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Palantir* is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name. It means "Farsighted"<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> or "He who looks afar".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It is a compound of *palan* ("far") and a form of *tir-* ("to watch").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Like for all the rulers of Númenor whose name was inscribed in the Scroll of Kings, the prefix *tar-* ("high") was added to his name.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Inziladûn is the name given to Tar-Palantir at birth by his father. It is [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) for "Flower of the West".<sup id="cite_ref-Flower_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flower-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> It is a compound of *inzil* ("flower") and *adûn* ("west").<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> The meaning of the Adûnaic prefix *Ar-* is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "king" and is a prefixal form of *ârû* ("king") or derived from the same root.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

The secret [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) version of *Inziladûn* was <i><b>Númellótë</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Flower_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flower-9">[9]</a></sup>

<b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>, Season 1:</b>

<dl><dd><b>Episode 3: <i><a href="/wiki/Adar_(episode)" title="Adar (episode)">Adar</a></i>:</b>
<dl><dd>Tar-Palantir appears off-camera at the end of the final scene, when his daughter ascends the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=King%27s_house&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="King&#39;s house (page does not exist)">King&#39;s Tower</a> to inform him that his warning of the appearance of <a href="/wiki/Galadriel" title="Galadriel">Galadriel</a> was accurate.</dd></dl></dd>
<dd><b>Episode 4: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Great_Wave" title="The Great Wave">The Great Wave</a></i>:</b>
<dl><dd>When Galadriel breaks into the King&#39;s Tower, it is revealed by Míriel that he is ill and not fully aware of himself or his surroundings.</dd></dl></dd>
<dd><b>Episode 5: <i><a href="/wiki/Partings" title="Partings">Partings</a></i>:</b>
<dl><dd>When Míriel informs him of her plan to aid the <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Southlands</a> in <a href="/wiki/Middle-earth" title="Middle-earth">Middle-earth</a>, he warns her not to go as all that awaits her there is darkness, foreshadowing Míriel&#39;s blindness later on.</dd></dl></dd>
<dd><b>Episode 8: <i><a href="/wiki/Alloyed" title="Alloyed">Alloyed</a></i>:</b>
<dl><dd>Upon his deathbed, he speaks with <a href="/wiki/E%C3%A4rien" title="Eärien">Eärien</a>, mistaking her for Míriel. He instructs her to look into the <i><a href="/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri" title="Palantíri">palantír</a></i>, but warns her not to look for too long as he did - now he cannot tell the difference between what is and what was and what will be. Tar-Palantir dies shortly afterwards.</dd></dl></dd></dl>
