<i><b>There and Back Again</b></i> was a book written by [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) recounting his adventures with [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) and the quest to reclaim the [Dwarf](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves")-realm of [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"). At one time Bilbo thought of subtitling the book "a Hobbit's Holiday". <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Conceived as his memoirs, the book was begun by Bilbo upon his return to [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) but not completed until his retirement in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell).

The diary contained the story of Bilbo's finding of the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring) as he originally told it: thus, Gollum willingly gives the Ring to Bilbo, and there is no trace of the Ring's hold over Gollum, something that he never corrected, even when the truth was known.<sup id="cite_ref-ring_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ring-2">[2]</a></sup>

Only to [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) had he shown his journal, as much as he had written (and only he knew about his secret magic ring)<sup id="cite_ref-ring_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ring-2">[2]</a></sup> and also [Merry Brandybuck](/wiki/Merry_Brandybuck) who took a peek secretly.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Bilbo kept the diary, along with a bundle of notes and papers wrapped in old cloths, in a strongbox in his study. He took these with him to [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3001](/wiki/Third_Age_3001 "Third Age 3001").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019") Frodo brought it back to [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") (along with [three volumes](/wiki/Translations_from_the_Elvish "Translations from the Elvish") bound in red leather, a parting gift from Bilbo). During [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3020](/wiki/Third_Age_3020 "Third Age 3020")-[3021](/wiki/Third_Age_3021 "Third Age 3021") he nearly filled the diary's pages adding his own memoirs of the War: <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Downfall_of_the_Lord_of_the_Rings_and_the_Return_of_the_King&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Downfall of the Lord of the Rings and the Return of the King (page does not exist)">The Downfall of the Lord of the Rings and the Return of the King</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-records_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-records-5">[5]</a></sup>

The additional three large volumes containing the *[Translations from the Elvish](/wiki/Translations_from_the_Elvish)* and [historical and cultural writings](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") were annexed to the diary, and bound together in a red case with a fifth volume added later. The volumes became known as the [Red Book of Westmarch](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch), the primary source for the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) and other matters of the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). The first volume containing Bilbo's journals however was copied most by the record-keepers,<sup id="cite_ref-records_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-records-5">[5]</a></sup> who avoided making corrections out of respect.<sup id="cite_ref-ring_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ring-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the Legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_Legendarium}

In the [Foreword](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Foreword "The Lord of the Rings Foreword") of the first edition, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) poses as the "translator", explaining that a version of *There and Back Again* (being the first chapters of the Red Book), was translated from its native language of [Westron](/wiki/Westron) and was the basis for the modern novel published as *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*. The quote above is an "explanation" from Tolkien for the difference in tone between the two books.

The Foreword, including the reference to Bilbo's journal, was removed for the [1965](/wiki/1965) American [Ballantine Books](/wiki/Ballantine_Books) edition, and the [1966](/wiki/1966) British second edition, replaced by a new Foreword ever since.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> However the concept that the two books are "translations" persiste throughout, especially in [Appendix F](/wiki/Appendix_F).
