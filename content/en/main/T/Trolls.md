**Trolls** were a race of large humanoid creatures of limited intellect. They were strong and vicious, but some of them could not endure [sunlight](../S/Sun.md) and turned to stone when exposed to it, which rendered them permanently lifeless.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

#### Origins {#Origins}

[Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md) devised or corrupted Trolls during the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) ("*twilight of the [Elder Days](../E/Elder_Days.md)*").<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup> Their exact origins are unknown, though Treebeard believed that Trolls were made by the [Dark Lord](../D/Dark_Lord.md) "*in mockery of [Ents](../E/Ents.md)*", similar to the belief that [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) were a twisted parody of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup> Whatever their exact origins were, Trolls were dull and lumpish, and at least in their beginning, were as inarticulate as animals.<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup>

#### Nirnaeth Arnoediad {#Nirnaeth_Arnoediad}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/13/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg/300px-Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="160" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/13/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg/450px-Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/13/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg/600px-Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Troll_guard.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="800"/></a><figcaption><i>Troll guard</i> by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Joel_Kilpatrick.md" title="Category:Images by Joel Kilpatrick">Joel Kilpatrick</a></figcaption></figure>

The participation of Trolls in the [War of the Jewels](../W/Wars_of_Beleriand.md) was not recorded in the tales of the Elder Days until [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [472](../F/First_Age_472.md),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> when the [Battle of Unnumbered Tears](../N/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad.md) took place.

The hosts of [Angband](../A/Angband.md) attacked and killed all of the rearguard of the brothers [Húrin](../H/Húrin.md) and [Huor](../H/Huor.md) and the remnant of the [Men](../E/Edain.md) of the [House of Hador](../H/House_of_Hador.md) from [Dor-lómin](../D/Dor-lómin.md) except Húrin at the river [Rivil](../R/Rivil.md).

When Húrin was the last man standing, he threw away his shield and gripped his axe with both hands. A [song](<../N/Narn_i_Chîn_Húrin_(tale).md>) tells that his axe smoked in the black blood of the Troll-guard of [Gothmog](<../G/Gothmog_(balrog).md>), the Lord of [Balrogs](../B/Balrogs.md) and the High-captain of Angband until at last he was taken alive by Orcs by Morgoth's command, being bound by Gothmog and dragged to Angband.<sup id="cite_ref-Fifth_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fifth-7">[6]</a></sup>

### Second and Third Ages {#Second_and_Third_Ages}

Later on, [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) taught them the little that they were able to learn and increased their intelligence with wickedness. As a consequence, the Trolls learned as much language as they could from the [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md). In the Westlands Stone-trolls spoke a debased form of Westron. The Olog-hai only spoke the [Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md) of [Barad-dûr](../B/Barad-dûr.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup>

Since days before memory Trolls settled in the north of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md), in particular near the [Ettenmoors](../E/Ettenmoors.md). The realm of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) was founded in their area, and while it lasted (c. [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [1300](Third_Age_1300.md) – [1975](Third_Age_1975.md)) the Trolls' numbers increased, as did their wickedness. After the fall of Angmar, the Trolls retreated to the east of the Misty Mountains, but 300 years before the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) they returned to [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md). They managed to make dens in the hills as far west as the [North Downs](../N/North_Downs.md) despite the efforts of the [Rangers of the North](../R/Rangers_of_the_North.md) to watch the area.

In the time of [Arador](../A/Arador.md), the 14th [Chieftain of the Dúnedain](../C/Chieftain_of_the_Dúnedain.md), a band of Trolls threatened his house in the woods near the river [Hoarwell](../H/Hoarwell.md) north of the [Trollshaws](Trollshaws.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> In [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2930](Third_Age_2930.md)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup> hill-trolls captured Arador in the north of Rivendell and killed him.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>

In the [Third Age](Third_Age.md), Sauron bred a new race of Trolls, which were called *[Olog-hai](../O/Olog-hai.md)* in the Black Speech. In contrast to the older race of Trolls from the First Age the Olog-hai could bear exposure to sunlight as long as they were controlled by the will of Sauron. They were cunning, strong, agile, fierce, harder than stone and spoke little. The Olog-hai were first seen at the end of the Third Age in southern [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md) and in the mountain borders of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup>

#### The Quest for Erebor {#The_Quest_for_Erebor}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/60/Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg/200px-Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="249" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/60/Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg/300px-Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/60/Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg/400px-Justin_Gerard_-_Bilbo_and_the_Three_Trolls.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1528" data-file-height="1900"/></a><figcaption><i>Bilbo and the Three Trolls</i> by <a href="../J/Justin_Gerard.md" title="Justin Gerard">Justin Gerard</a></figcaption></figure>

In [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2941](Third_Age_2941.md),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> [Thorin and Company](Thorin_and_Company.md) encountered [Bert](../B/Bert.md), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="Tom.md" title="Tom">Tom</a> and [Bill](../W/William.md), three Trolls who had come down from the mountains and eaten a number of [Men](../M/Men.md) equivalent to village and a half, who probably stopped there on their way through the area, as well as sheep.

The trolls captured [Bilbo](../B/Bilbo.md) and the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md), but [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) returned in time to keep the trolls go on arguing how to cook the Dwarves by impersonating the voices of the Trolls from a place of hiding until the first lights of the morning sun came over the hill and turned the trolls to stone. Afterwards Thorin and Company discovered the cave of the trolls and took the swords [Glamdring](../G/Glamdring.md) and [Orcrist](../O/Orcrist.md), the knife [Sting](../S/Sting.md), gold coins and food from the cave.<sup id="cite_ref-Mutton_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mutton-12">[11]</a></sup>

It is not known how the trolls had gotten the swords, but [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md) guessed that the Trolls had robbed other robbers or had found them in a hold in the mountains as remnants of old robberies.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup>

#### The War of the Ring {#The_War_of_the_Ring}

When the fiftieth birthday of [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) was getting near, probably early in the year [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3018](Third_Age_3018.md)<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup> rumors had reached [the Shire](The_Shire.md) that Trolls were abroad, more cunning than normal and armed with dreadful weapons.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> By [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3018](Third_Age_3018.md) when [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) and the [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) were attacked on [Weathertop](../W/Weathertop.md)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> the land east of Weathertop was uninhabited and had been deserted, because Trolls sometimes came down from the northern valleys of the Misty Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> At that time Trolls still lived in the [Ettendales](../E/Ettendales.md),<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> the valleys of the Ettenmoors, which ran up to the foothills of the Misty Mountains far north of [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>

On 18 October [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3018](Third_Age_3018.md), Aragorn and the Hobbits found a path in the [Trollshaws](Trollshaws.md), which had probably been made by the three Trolls, followed it and discovered that it led to the cave of the trolls and then on to the clearing in the woods not far below the cave where the three trolls that had been turned to stone still stood.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/Jan_Pospíšil_-_Troll_slayer.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/4e/Jan_Posp%C3%AD%C5%A1il_-_Troll_slayer.jpg/250px-Jan_Posp%C3%AD%C5%A1il_-_Troll_slayer.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="374" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/4e/Jan_Posp%C3%AD%C5%A1il_-_Troll_slayer.jpg/375px-Jan_Posp%C3%AD%C5%A1il_-_Troll_slayer.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/4/4e/Jan_Posp%C3%AD%C5%A1il_-_Troll_slayer.jpg 2x" data-file-width="477" data-file-height="714"/></a><figcaption><i>Troll slayer</i> by <a href="../J/Jan_Pospíšil.md" title="Jan Pospíšil">Jan Pospíšil</a></figcaption></figure>

On 15 January [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3019](Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> the [Fellowship of the Ring](../F/Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md) was attacked in the [Chamber of Mazarbul](../C/Chamber_of_Mazarbul.md) by a cave-troll and Orcs. When [Boromir](../B/Boromir.md) hewed at the arm of the troll which came through a gap in the door with his sword, his sword was notched, glanced aside and fell from his hand. However the troll retreated when Frodo stabbed it in the foot with his sword [Sting](../S/Sting.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-23">[22]</a></sup>

In the early hours of 15 March [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3019](Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup> in the [siege of Minas Tirith](../S/Siege_of_Gondor.md) mountain-trolls walked behind the great hundred-foot long battering ram [Grond](<../G/Grond_(battering_ram).md>) to the [Great Gate of Minas Tirith](../G/Great_Gate_of_Minas_Tirith.md) and swung Grond four times against the gate until the gate broke.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-25">[24]</a></sup>

On 25 March [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3019](Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> at the beginning of the [Battle of the Morannon](../B/Battle_of_the_Morannon.md) the [Army of the West](../A/Army_of_the_West.md) consisting of forces of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md) was attacked on the [Slag-hills](../S/Slag-hills.md) before the [Black Gate](../B/Black_Gate.md) of Mordor by a great company of hill-trolls from [Gorgoroth](../G/Gorgoroth.md) with huge round bucklers and heavy hammers. Pippin managed to fell the chief of the Trolls by stabbing him with his [Barrow-blade](../D/Daggers_of_Westernesse.md) when the troll tried to bite the throat of [Beregond](../B/Beregond.md) who had fallen down after having been stunned by the monster.<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-27">[26]</a></sup>

### Legacy {#Legacy}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/M/Matěj_Čadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/46/Mat%C4%9Bj_%C4%8Cadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg/180px-Mat%C4%9Bj_%C4%8Cadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="180" height="125" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/46/Mat%C4%9Bj_%C4%8Cadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg/270px-Mat%C4%9Bj_%C4%8Cadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/46/Mat%C4%9Bj_%C4%8Cadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg/360px-Mat%C4%9Bj_%C4%8Cadil_-_Troll_sat_alone_on_his_seat_of_stone.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="694"/></a><figcaption><i>Troll sat alone on his seat of stone</i> by <a href="../M/Matěj_Čadil.md" title="Matěj Čadil">Matěj Čadil</a></figcaption></figure>

While it is unknown what happened to Trolls after the [Downfall of Barad-dûr](../D/Downfall_of_Barad-dûr.md), the [hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) composed two poems that had Trolls in them.<sup id="cite_ref-Face_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Face-28">[27]</a></sup> The first one, called *[The Stone Troll](The_Stone_Troll.md)*,<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup> was recited by [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) to [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md), [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md), [Merry](../M/Merry.md), and [Pippin](../P/Pippin.md) during their stay near the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Troll_Ridge&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Troll Ridge (page does not exist)">Troll Ridge</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup> The second poem, called *[Perry-the-Winkle](../P/Perry-the-Winkle.md)*, also composed by Sam,<sup id="cite_ref-Face_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Face-28">[27]</a></sup> featured a [Lonely Troll](../L/Lonely_Troll.md) as one of the main characters. However, unlike other trolls, the Lonely Troll was friendly and good-natured.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Yes, I am afraid trolls do behave like that, even those with only one head each.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../R/Roast_Mutton.md" title="Roast Mutton">Roast Mutton</a></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Trolls were taller and broader than men<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-27">[26]</a></sup> with a height of twelve feet or more.<sup id="cite_ref-feet_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-feet-1">[1]</a></sup> They had dark skin with greenish scales, large flat toeless feet<sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-23">[22]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[32]</a></sup> and knotted hands with claws.<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-27">[26]</a></sup> Their blood was black.<sup id="cite_ref-Fifth_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fifth-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-23">[22]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Gate_27-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-27">[26]</a></sup>

Some Trolls might have had more than one head, which is fleetingly suggested in *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)*, commenting on [William](../W/William.md) wiping his lips on his sleeve.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup> If this statement was not merely a way of speaking, perhaps there were indeed Trolls in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) that, unlike the [Stone-trolls](../S/Stone-trolls.md), had more than one head and behaved even worse.

Regular Trolls turned back to stone from which they had been created when exposed to the sunlight,<sup id="cite_ref-Mutton_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mutton-12">[11]</a></sup> but the Troll race called Olog-hai that appeared at the end of the [Third Age](Third_Age.md) could tolerate sunglight as long as they were controlled by the will of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup>

Trolls lived in different terrains and were known as cave-trolls,<sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_23-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-23">[22]</a></sup> hill-trolls<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_27-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-27">[26]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup> or mountain-trolls.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-25">[24]</a></sup> They did not build buildings,<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[35]</a></sup> and slept in caves or holes.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-2">[2]</a></sup> It is possible that the term Stone-trolls<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup> was used for regular trolls who turned back to stone when exposed to the sunlight.<sup id="cite_ref-L153_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L153-37">[36]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[note 2]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) word for a "troll" is *[torog](Torog.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md) equivalent is *[olog](../O/Olog.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[38]</a></sup>

The name *[Rhudaur](../R/Rhudaur.md)* is translated by Tolkien as "Troll shaw", with <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Rh%C3%BB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rhû (page does not exist)">rhû</a></i> translated as "evil, wicked".<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[39]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Early legendarium {#Early_legendarium}

In an early chart of different creatures, Trolls are given the [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) name *maulir*. [Patrick H. Wynne](../P/Patrick_H._Wynne.md) and [Christopher Gilson](../C/Christopher_Gilson.md) have suggested that *maulir* is possibly related to Qenya *maule* ("crying, weeping"), thus perhaps "referring to cries made by these monsters or to the weeping of their victims".<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[40]</a></sup>

In the *[Lay of Leithian Canto XIII (Beren and Lúthien in Angband)](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_XIII.md)*, as [Beren](../B/Beren.md) and [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md) descend into the depths of Angband, among other horrors "huge shapes there stood like carven trolls enormous hewn of blasted rock to forms that mortal likeness mock; monstrous and menacing, entombed, at every turn they silent loomed in fitful glares that leaped and died."<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[41]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

In the drafts of *The Lord of the Rings*, Tolkien considered trolls to be living stones that were possessed by the spirits of [goblins](../O/Orcs.md).<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[42]</a></sup>

In an earlier version of what would become the [Appendix F](../A/Appendix_F.md) it was also mentioned that the evil Power had crossed their breed with that of larger Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[43]</a></sup>

In an unpublished note on the origin of the Orcs without any indication of the date when the note was written, Tolkien stated that the Elves would have considered the Trolls to be Orcs in character and origin, though they were larger and slower, and that it would seem evident they were corruptions of primitive human types.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[44]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolls" title="wikipedia:Trolls">Trolls</a> were originally a part of the [Norse mythology](../N/Norse_mythology.md) (as a negative synonym for *jötunn*, "[giants](../G/Giants.md)") and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_folklore" title="wikipedia:Scandinavian folklore">Scandinavian folklore</a> (as ugly, large creatures of remote wildlife areas). [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) used elements of old barbarous mythmaking that Trolls returned to stone when they were not in the dark.<sup id="cite_ref-L153_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L153-37">[36]</a></sup>

Helen Buckhurst, a friend and colleague of J.R.R. Tolkien presented a paper titled *Icelandic Folklore* in [1926](../_/1926.md) in which she stated that Icelandic Trolls in the Sagas and in more recent tales were huge, mishappen and ugly creatures who generally lived in caves in the mountains, were almost always evil, often raided outlying farms at night to take sheep, horses, children, men and women and ate them in their homes in the mountains. She also mentioned that some kinds of Trolls must stay in caves during the day, because the light of the sun turns them to stone.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[45]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[46]</a></sup>

Commenting on the possibility of Trolls having more than one head, [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) has noted that trolls "with multiple heads appear in many stories", especially pointing to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Webbe_Dasent" title="wikipedia:George Webbe Dasent">George Webbe Dasent</a>'s "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soria_Moria_Castle" title="wikipedia:Soria Moria Castle">Soria Moria Castle</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[47]</a></sup> [C.S. Lewis](../C/C.S._Lewis.md) mentioned the <a class="external text" href="https://narnia.fandom.com/wiki/Ettins" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Ettins</a> of <a class="external text" href="https://narnia.fandom.com/wiki/Ettinsmoor" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Ettinsmoor</a> in [Narnia](../N/Narnia.md), some of which have two heads.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[48]</a></sup> In many later fantasy settings, but also some adaptations of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), there are two-headed monsters, which usually are called "[ettens](../E/Etten.md)" (q.v.).

## Other fiction {#Other_fiction}

In *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight](../S/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight,_Pearl_and_Sir_Orfeo.md)* there are mentions of the "wood-trolls" (translated by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) from the [manuscript](<../S/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight_(poem).md>) word <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Wodwo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Wodwo (page does not exist)">wodwos</a></i>).<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">[49]</a></sup> The latter word is related to the [Woses](../W/Woses.md).<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="../../help/R/References.md" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

### Films {#Films}

<b><a href="../_/1967.md" title="1967">1967</a>: <a href="The_Hobbit_(1967_film).md" title="The Hobbit (1967 film)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1967 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The three Trolls&#39; position in the narrative was taken by two creatures called &#34;Groans&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[50]</a></sup> They had wooden, bark-like skin, and, instead of stone, turned into dead trees when exposed to sunlight.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/1978.md" title="1978">1978</a>: <a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The troll that attempts to enter Balin&#39;s Tomb is depicted as a shadowy creature with dark green fur. Unlike in the novel, its feet have toes. It is the only troll seen in the film.</dd></dl>

### Games {#Games}

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>An individual monster called Skessa, a cross-breed between a troll and <a href="../G/Giants.md" title="Giants">giant</a>, has two heads.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">[51]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1995-8: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game.md" title="Middle-earth Collectible Card Game">Middle-earth Collectible Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>&#34;&#39;Two-headed&#39; Troll&#34; is a Warrior Ally for evil players.</dd></dl>

<b>1998-2004: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../M/Middle-earth_Online.md" title="Middle-earth Online">Middle-earth Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ettins&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ettins (page does not exist)">Ettins</a></i>, a race of two-headed trolls, was intended to be included in the game.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[52]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2002-5: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><i>Ettens</i> resemble the <a href="../H/Hill-trolls.md" title="Hill-trolls">Hill-trolls</a>, but they possess &#34;<i>not one but two long heads</i>&#34;. The solitary creatures, a result of an ancient experiment by Morgoth, live almost exclusively in the Ettenmoors.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">[53]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2002.md" title="2002">2002</a>: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Two_Towers_(video_game).md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (video game)">The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Like in the film adaptation of <i>The Fellowship of the Ring</i>, a Cave-troll serves as the boss of the &#34;Balin&#39;s Tomb&#34; level. A pair of Cave-trolls also serve as the bosses of the &#34;Hornburg Courtyard&#34; level. Also unique to this game are a new breed of troll known as &#34;Forest-trolls&#34;, which appear in several levels including &#34;Fangorn Forest&#34; and &#34;Breached Wall&#34;.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2003.md" title="2003">2003</a>: <i><a href="The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game).md" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)">The Hobbit</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In addition to the three Stone-trolls from the book, a Cave-troll was intended to appear during the &#34;Over Hill and Under Hill&#34; level in the PC and console versions, where it would have served as the level&#39;s boss. Even though the encounter was cut from the final version of the game, Bilbo still mentions the troll to Gandalf when he reunites with <a href="Thorin_and_Company.md" title="Thorin and Company">Thorin and Company</a>, and <a class="mw-redirect" href="../K/Kili.md" title="Kili">Kili</a> informs Bilbo that Trolls are known to live in the Misty Mountains, and hopes Bilbo didn&#39;t encounter any while he was separated.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[54]</a></sup></dd>
<dd>Third-party trainer programs for the PC version allow the boss battle to be reintegrated into the game. Like the three Stone-trolls, the Cave-troll would have been capable of speaking <a href="../W/Westron.md" title="Westron">Westron</a>, as shown during its introductory cutscene where it complains about having a toothache. Defeating it would have yielded the Troll Key which, in the final version of the game, instead lays on the floor for Bilbo to take without having to kill any of the creatures that guard it.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2004.md" title="2004">2004</a>: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Trolls are a playable unit in the game. You can play as either a mountain troll or drummer troll. When the mountain troll levels up, it receives armor. Their designs are based off of the Peter Jackson film series.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2006.md" title="2006">2006</a>: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Trolls are a playable unit like in the previous game. In the expansion, <a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II%253A_The_Rise_of_the_Witch-king.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king</a> additional units of trolls can be played called the snow trolls and hill trolls. The campaign features a named troll called Rhogash which helps the Witch-king lead his assaults on Arnor.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2011.md" title="2011">2011</a>: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_War_in_the_North.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Compared to other enemies, Trolls are much stronger. Trolls first appear in <a class="mw-redirect" href="../F/Fornost.md" title="Fornost">Fornost</a>, where one traps <a href="../E/Eradan_(video_game_character).md" title="Eradan (video game character)">Eradan</a>, <a href="../A/Andriel.md" title="Andriel">Andriel</a> and <a href="../F/Farin_(video_game_character).md" title="Farin (video game character)">Farin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">[55]</a></sup> Another troll appears at the end of the level, where Eradan, Andriel and Farin have to protect <a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Elladan.md" title="Elladan">Elladan</a> and <a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Elrohir.md" title="Elrohir">Elrohir</a>. When this troll is killed, the player is able to enter the Citadel to confront <a href="Tharzog.md" title="Tharzog">Tharzog</a> and <a href="../A/Agandaûr.md" title="Agandaûr">Agandaûr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">[56]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2014.md" title="2014">2014</a>: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth%253A_Shadow_of_Mordor.md" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor">Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Udûn and Sea of Nûrnen regions of Mordor are inhabited by large troll-like creatures known as &#34;Olog Graugs&#34;. According to their Nature article in the Appendices menu, Sauron intends to use the Graug to crossbreed with ordinary Trolls, and create a new breed of &#34;hill-trolls&#34; that can withstand direct sunlight. Graugs are very rare in the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 versions of the game due to technical limitations, but are more abundant in the PS4, PC, and Xbox One versions.</dd></dl>

<b>2017: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth%253A_Shadow_of_War.md" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of War">Middle-earth: Shadow of War</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Overlord of the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../N/Núrnen.md" title="Núrnen">Núrnen</a> region of Mordor is an <a href="../O/Olog-hai.md" title="Olog-hai">Olog</a> named Az-Harto, who poses as a two-headed <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Troll</a>. In reality, the second head is that of the <a href="../O/Orcs.md" title="Orcs">Uruk</a> <a href="../R/Ratbag.md" title="Ratbag">Ratbag the Coward</a>, who is riding on Az-Harto&#39;s back and wearing a large helmet to conceal his identity.</dd></dl>

<b>2018: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Two-headed Trolls are among the evil creatures of <a href="../G/Gundabad.md" title="Gundabad">Gundabad</a> and can be found among the <a href="../G/Grey_Mountains.md" title="Grey Mountains">Grey Mountains</a>.</dd></dl>
