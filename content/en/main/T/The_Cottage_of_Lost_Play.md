**The Cottage of Lost Play** is the first chapter of *[The Book of Lost Tales Part One](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One.md)*.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

We are introduced to [Eriol](../E/Eriol.md) the mariner, described as a *"man of great curiosity"* who has journeyed to the Lonely Island, [Tol Eressëa](Tol_Eressëa.md) from the [Great Lands](../M/Middle-earth.md#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium).

Whilst travelling through the centre of the island, he reaches the region later named as [Alalminórë](../A/Alalminórë.md). Eriol decides it is time to seek lodging just as the sun has begun to set and the first stars appear in the sky. At the summit of a hill he turns down a winding lane and on the western slope he spots a tiny dwelling, this is the [Cottage of Lost Play](../C/Cottage_of_Lost_Play.md).

There he meets [Lindo](../L/Lindo.md) and [Vairë](../V/Vairë.md), who built the cottage long years ago, and now care for the many lost children of men that have remain in [Kôr](../K/Kôr.md) with the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md) for ever. From them Eriol learns of the foundation of [Kortirion](../K/Kortirion.md) and some small parts of the history of the Eldar.

Many great events are mentioned here but not explained in detail; the [Faring Forth](../F/Faring_Forth.md), the Exiles of Kôr, the [Valar](../V/Valar.md) and [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md). The story ends with Vairë suggesting that Eriol may be a descendant of one that had *"found the rocks of Eldamar in those old days"*.

## Commentary {#Commentary}

The story is recorded in a High School Exercise Book labelled *The Cottage of Lost Play, which intoduceth \[the\] Book of Lost Tales* and was written out by [Edith Tolkien](../E/Edith_Tolkien.md), being a fair copy of the original pencil manuscript likely written in the winter of [1916](../_/1916.md)-[17](../_/1917.md).

Eriol is described as a *"Son of Eärendel"* but this is not to say that he is a descendant of [Eärendel](../E/Eärendel.md); if a beam from the star of Eärendel falls on a new-born child he is considered a “child of Eärendel“ and becomes a restless wanderer.

Alongside this notebook is a small pocket-book labelled as *Story of Eriol’s life* which amongst other things explains his genealogy. He traces his descent from the god Woden. At the time of visiting Tol Eressëa has two sons, named [Hengest and Horsa](../H/Hengest_and_Horsa.md), they are from his first wife Cwén who had died of unknown cause shortly before Eriol’s journey to find the Lonely Island.

Eriol is said to have come from *"the lands East of the North Sea"* which certainly refers to an area of the Danish peninsula according to [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md). It also indicates that Eriol appears to belong to a period preceding the Anglo-Saxon invasion of Britain.

## Poems {#Poems}

The four poems published at the end of this chapter are gradual revisions of the same work over a period of almost half a century.

- *[You and Me / and the Cottage of Lost Play](The_Little_House_of_Lost_Play%253A_Mar_Vanwa_Tyaliéva.md)* (first version written in [April](../A/April.md) [1915](../_/1915.md))
- *The Little House of Lost Play / Mar Vanwa Tyaliéva* (the final revision from [April](../A/April.md) [1915](../_/1915.md))
- *Kortirion among the Trees* (written sometime pre-[1937](../_/1937.md))
- *[The Trees of Kortirion](The_Trees_of_Kortirion.md)* (written in [1962](../_/1962.md))
