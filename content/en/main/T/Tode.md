**Tode** was a marshy location mentioned in the [Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")-poem *[The Mewlips](/wiki/The_Mewlips)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is mentioned to be after a long road "beyond the [Merlock Mountains](/wiki/Merlock_Mountains)", and "through the [spider](/wiki/Spider "Spider")-shadows"; other than that it is unknown if Tode refers to an actual marsh in the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands), or if it was just an invention by the poet.

As the poem describes a location similar to [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> it's possible that Tode refers to the [Long Marshes](/wiki/Long_Marshes) at the end of the [Old Forest Road](/wiki/Old_Forest_Road).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tode#Middle_English" title="wiktionary:tode">tode</a></i> is the Middle English spelling for a "[toad](/wiki/Frog "Frog")"; it was also a word with evil connotations, as the toad/frog was considered a demonic creature, according to Wiktionary (cf. abovementioned link). Furthermore, <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Tode#German" title="wiktionary:Tode">Tode</a></i> is also the [German](/wiki/Germanic "Germanic") word for "death". Although the two words are unrelated, and [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) didn't specifically explain his choice for the name, both explanations are fitting for an ominous swamp. <span></span>
