<i><b>The Real Middle Earth</b></i> is a [2003](/wiki/2003) documentary about the locations and landscapes of [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s works, and the potential sources behind them. The documentary is narrated by [Ian Holm](/wiki/Ian_Holm), who played [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) in [*The Lord of the Rings* films](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").

## Summary {#Summary}

The documentary explores the locations and events in Tolkien's life that influenced his works, and his languages. Looking at Tolkien's upbringing in [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire) and [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), the documentary looks into the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_(region)" title="wikipedia:West Midlands (region)">West Midlands</a> region of England - the former kingdom of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia" title="wikipedia:Mercia">Mercia</a> - and how locations inspired his vision of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"). The documentary also looks into Tolkien's time at [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford), his experience in the [First World War](/wiki/First_World_War), and his visits to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonyhurst_College" title="wikipedia:Stonyhurst College">Stonyhurst College</a>.

## Original press release {#Original_press_release}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The Real Middle-Earth is a brand new documentary that uncovers the real places that inspired The Lord of the Rings.<br/><br/>Sir Ian Holm (Fellowship of the Ring, Alien, The Borrowers) narrates a fascinating exploration into a world that, although imaginary, seems so solid we pore over its maps and contemplate journeys made from one place to another.<br/><br/>Is this because the foundations of fantastic <a href="/wiki/Middle-earth" title="Middle-earth">Middle-Earth</a> were laid on the bedrock of factual places?<br/><br/>Was there a shire to inspire the Shire? What shaped the darker and more troubled lands beyond its borders? How did European languages fuse in Tolkien&#39;s mind to create a vocabulary that evokes extraordinary places and heroic adventure?<br/><br/>The quest for The Real Middle-Earth takes us to England&#39;s west midlands, to Warwick and Oxford, to an ancient Lancashire school, to Saxon burial sites and many other places, including the real <a href="/wiki/Dead_Marshes" title="Dead Marshes">Dead Marshes</a>, otherwise known as the Great War battlefields of <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme" title="Battle of the Somme">the Somme</a>.<br/><br/>The Real Middle-Earth is 58 minutes long. There are also 50 minutes of extras on the DVD, including a profile of the official The Lord of the Rings mapmaker and a look behind the scenes at the prosthetics teams behind some of the LOTR movies&#39; special effects.
</p>
</blockquote>
