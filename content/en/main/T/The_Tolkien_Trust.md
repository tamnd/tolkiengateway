**The Tolkien Trust** is a British charity founded in [1977](/wiki/1977) by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s children. As a charitable trust, it makes grants to a wide variety of organisations at the discretion of the current trustees.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It does not manage Tolkien's literary estate,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> even though its source of income is via the copyright it owns to some of Tolkien's works that were formerly owned by The [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate).

## Activities {#Activities}

Created on [1 April](/wiki/1_April) [1977](/wiki/1977), and registered with the Charity Commission from [2 June](/wiki/2_June) [1977](/wiki/1977), the Trust was founded by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s four children: [John](/wiki/John_Tolkien "John Tolkien"), [Michael](/wiki/Michael_Tolkien "Michael Tolkien"), [Christopher](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien "Christopher Tolkien") and [Priscilla](/wiki/Priscilla_Tolkien "Priscilla Tolkien"). Today, the trustees of the charity are Christopher, Priscilla, [Michael George Tolkien](/wiki/Michael_George_Tolkien) (son of Michael, succeeding his sister Joan as a trustee in 2008),<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2009_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2009-3">[3]</a></sup> and Christopher's wife [Baillie Tolkien](/wiki/Baillie_Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Trust makes grants to other charitable causes at the discretion of the trustees. It currently does not accept unsolicited applications for funding,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> but does list a number of specific areas it supports:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>1. emergency and disaster relief<br/>2. overseas aid and development<br/>3. the homeless and refugees<br/>4. healthcare charities, especially those focusing on illnesses of childhood and old age, the needs of disadvantaged communities and medical research<br/>5. religious causes promoting peace and reconciliation and work with impoverished communities<br/>6. environmental causes<br/>7. education and the arts
</p>

### Litigation {#Litigation}

From [2003](/wiki/2003) and [2006](/wiki/2006) the Trust and The [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate) demanded royalties from [*The Lord of the Rings* films](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Following claims from [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema) that the films failed to make a profit, on [11 February](/wiki/11_February) [2008](/wiki/2008) The Tolkien Trust, The [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate) and [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins) filed a lawsuit seeking at least $150 million (7.5% of gross revenue) in damages. They accused New Line Cinema of "unabashed and insatiable greed" and the "infamous practice of creative 'Hollywood accounting'."<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> The claim, which also attempted to stop production of the [forthcoming *Hobbit* films](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)> "The Hobbit (film series)"), was concluded on [8 September](/wiki/8_September) [2009](/wiki/2009) with an out-of-court settlement.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) said that they "regret that legal action was necessary, but are glad that this dispute has been settled on satisfactory terms"<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> and that "New Line may now proceed with its proposed films of 'The Hobbit'", whilst their lawyer said that they "feel vindicated and are entirely satisfied with the terms of the settlement." Alan Horn, president of [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) (owners of New Line Cinema) said "we all look forward to a mutually productive and beneficial relationship in the future".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> Although both sides refused to reveal the size of settlement - simply saying that the Tolkien Trust would receive a "significant portion" - the Trust's accounts reveal that in [2009](/wiki/2009) and [2010](/wiki/2010) it received £24 million and £1.6 million respectively in film rights,<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2009_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2009-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2010_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2010-6">[6]</a></sup> and in [2011](/wiki/2011) it received £603,515 in merchandising rights.<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2011_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2011-7">[7]</a></sup>

On the [19 November](/wiki/19_November) [2012](/wiki/2012), Priscilla Tolkien as a trustee of The Tolkien Trust, [The Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate "Tolkien Estate") and [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins) ("the plaintiffs") filed a lawsuit against [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.), [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema) and the [Saul Zaentz Company](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz_Company) ("the defendants") seeking damages of $80 million.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> They claimed that Tolkien's copyright had been breached and that the defendants had no right to sell themed slot machines and downloadable video games; in the claim the plaintiffs state: "Not only does the production of gambling games patently exceed the scope of defendants' rights, but this infringing conduct has outraged Tolkien's devoted fan base, causing irreparable harm to Tolkien's legacy and reputation and the valuable goodwill generated by his works."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> They assert that the merchandising rights only extend to physical, and not electronic, products.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> On [11 March](/wiki/11_March) [2013](/wiki/2013), Saul Zaentz and Warner Bros. filed counter-claims against the plaintiffs (including The Tolkien Trust) arguing that as a result of the Estate's actions the defendants had lost out on merchandising income, and that there has been a breach of good-faith by the Estate and the Trust.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup>

## Copyrights {#Copyrights}

The primary assets of The Tolkien Trust are the copyrights to certain works by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) which continue to provide income to the Trust. The assets held by The Tolkien Trust are a number of (many posthumous) publications by J.R.R. Tolkien:<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2009_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2009-3">[3]</a></sup>

- *[Beowulf: A Translation and Commentary](/wiki/Beowulf:_A_Translation_and_Commentary)*<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2013_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2013-24">[24]</a></sup>
- *[The Fall of Arthur](/wiki/The_Fall_of_Arthur)*<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2013_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2013-24">[24]</a></sup>
- *[The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún](/wiki/The_Legend_of_Sigurd_and_Gudr%C3%BAn)*
- Renewal copyrights in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* in the USA
- *[Mythopoeia](/wiki/Mythopoeia)*
- *[Roverandom](/wiki/Roverandom)*
- *[Smith of Wootton Major](/wiki/Smith_of_Wootton_Major)*
- Author royalties from *[The Tolkien Family Album](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Family_Album)*
- 40% of the renewal copyrights in *[The Tolkien Reader](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Reader)* in the USA
- *[Tree and Leaf](/wiki/Tree_and_Leaf)*
- "published editions of various philological writings and fragments"<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2013_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2013-24">[24]</a></sup>

The Trust also holds a number of sundry assets:

- Letters from Tolkien to A.B.M. Ronald and C. Martin (see "[Letter to Amy Ronald (15 March 1969)](</wiki/Letter_to_Amy_Ronald_(15_March_1969)>)" and "[Letter to Amy Ronald (20 March 1969)](</wiki/Letter_to_Amy_Ronald_(20_March_1969)>)")
- Posters drawn by [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes)
- "Certain unpublished papers, letters, artistic works, pictures, photographs and other documents originally forming part of the Estate of J R R Tolkien, and the copyright in such materials formerly owned by the Estate."<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2013_24-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2013-24">[24]</a></sup>

## Finances {#Finances}

The Trust does not partake in any fund-raising activity: all income received constitute royalties from copyrights and subsequent investments. Although the Trust holds literary assets no value is provided for them on the grounds that it would not be "practical and cost-effective"; as such, the primary assets of the Trust are cash and investments (<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance)" title="wikipedia:Bond (finance)">bonds</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_(finance)" title="wikipedia:Share (finance)">shares</a>). The Trust has an <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socially_responsible_investing" title="wikipedia:Socially responsible investing">ethical investment policy</a> so has no investment in tobacco, or the manufacture of armaments.<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2009_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2009-3">[3]</a></sup> In [2012](/wiki/2012), gambling services was added to the list of investments the Trust would not hold.<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2012_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2012-8">[8]</a></sup>

The Trust was founded on [1 April](/wiki/1_April) [1977](/wiki/1977) and its financial year was the same as the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year#United_Kingdom" title="wikipedia:Fiscal year">United Kingdom&#39;s fiscal year</a> for individuals ([6 April](/wiki/6_April) - [5 April](/wiki/5_April)). In [2013](/wiki/2013) the existed unincorporated charity was closed and its assets transferred to a new <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charitable_incorporated_organisation" title="wikipedia:Charitable incorporated organisation">charitable incorporated organisation</a>; the new charity's financial year is the same as the calendar year.<sup id="cite_ref-Accounts2013_24-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Accounts2013-24">[24]</a></sup>
