**Tram Nybol**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> was a grey, arched bridge crossing the rivers [Afros](/wiki/Afros) and [Gruir](/wiki/Gruir) at their confluence near the [House of the Hundred Chimneys](/wiki/House_of_the_Hundred_Chimneys) in [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel), according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Nyb_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nyb-2">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

After the disastrous [Battle of the Heath of the Sky-roof](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Heath_of_the_Sky-roof) during the [Faring Forth](/wiki/Faring_Forth), the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and, according to one outline, [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol), crossed Afros and Gruir in an attempt to escape the invading [Men](/wiki/Men), presumably at Tram Nybol.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Tram Nybol* is in [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish). The first element of the name is <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Tram&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tram (page does not exist)">tram</a></i> ("bridge"), but the second element *Nybol* is unclear, possibly being an adjectival form of <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Nib&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nib (page does not exist)">nib</a></i> ("snowflake") - therefore, the name might mean "Snowy Bridge", although it is not obvious why such a name might be appropriate.<sup id="cite_ref-Nyb_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nyb-2">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tram Nybol might have been inspired by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_Bridge,_Staffordshire" title="wikipedia:Essex Bridge, Staffordshire">Essex Bridge</a>, crossing the river <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent" title="wikipedia:River Trent">Trent</a> near the village of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Haywood" title="wikipedia:Great Haywood">Great Haywood</a> (which was the inspiration for Tavrobel).<sup id="cite_ref-Nyb_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nyb-2">[1]</a></sup>
