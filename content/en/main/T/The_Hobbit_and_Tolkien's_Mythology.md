<b>The Hobbit <i>and Tolkien&#39;s Mythology: Essays on Revisions and Influences</i></b> is a collection of essays edited by [Bradford Lee Eden](/wiki/Bradford_Lee_Eden) examining how *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* fits into - and influenced - [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s wider legendarium. It features contributions from [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) and [Verlyn Flieger](/wiki/Verlyn_Flieger), amongst others.

## Contents {#Contents}

- "Introduction" by [Bradford Lee Eden](/wiki/Bradford_Lee_Eden)

<dl><dt>Part I - The Evolution of the Dwarven Race</dt></dl>

- "Anchoring the Myth: The Impact of *The Hobbit* on Tolkien's Legendarium" by [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff)
- "From Nauglath to [Durin's Folk](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk): *The Hobbit* and Tolkien's Dwarves" by Gerard Hynes

<dl><dt>Part II - Durin&#39;s Day</dt></dl>

- "'It passes our skill in these days': Primary World Influences on the Evolution of [Durin's Day](/wiki/Durin%27s_Day)" by [Kristine Larsen](/wiki/Kristine_Larsen)
- "A Scientific Examination of Durin's Day" by [Sumner Gary Hunnewell](/wiki/Sumner_Gary_Hunnewell)

<dl><dt>Part III - Themes</dt></dl>

- "Tolkien's French Connection" by [Verlyn Flieger](/wiki/Verlyn_Flieger)
- "Tolkien’s Hybrid Mythology: *The Hobbit* as Old Norse 'Fairy-Story'" by [Jane Chance](/wiki/Jane_Chance)
- "From '[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)' to *The Hobbit* and Back Again: An Onomastic Foray" by Damien Bador
- "Civilized Goblins and Talking Animals: How *The Hobbit* Created Problems of Sentience for Tolkien" by Gregory Hartley
- "Seeing in the Dark, Seeing by the Dark: How Bilbo's Invisibility Defined Tolkien’s Vision" by Michael A. Wodzak
- "A Victorian in Valhalla: [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) as the Link Between England and [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth)" by William Christian Klarner
- "[Beorn](/wiki/Beorn) and [Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil "Tom Bombadil"): Mythology, Place and Landscape in Middle-earth" by Justin T. Noetzel
- "Travel, Redemption and Peacemaking: [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits), [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) and [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and the Transformative Power of Pilgrimage" by Vickie L. Holtz-Wodzak
- "A Baggins Back Yard: Environmentalism, Authorship and the Elves in Tolkien's Legendarium" by David Thiessen
- "Polytemporalith and Epic Characterization in *[The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey](/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey)*: Reflecting *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)'*s Modernism and Medievalism" by Judy Ann Ford and Robin Anne Reid
- "The Wisdom of the Crowd: Internet Memes and *The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey*" by Michelle Markey Butler

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>At the 2013 &#34;Celebrating The Hobbit&#34; conference at Valparaiso University - marking the 75th anniversary of the book&#39;s publication and the first installment of Peter Jackson&#39;s Hobbit movies - two plenary papers were presented: &#34;Anchoring the Myth: The Impact of The Hobbit on Tolkien&#39;s Legendarium&#34; by John D. Rateliff provided numerous examples of The Hobbit&#39;s influence on Tolkien&#39;s legendarium; and &#34;Tolkien&#39;s French connections” by Verlyn Flieger discussed French influences on the development of Bilbo Baggins and his adventures. In discussions with the plenary speakers and other presenters, it became apparent that a book focusing on how The Hobbit influenced the subsequent development of Tolkien&#39;s legendarium; was sorely needed.<br/><br/>
This collection of essays fills that need. With Rateliff&#39;s and Flieger&#39;s papers included, the book presents two chapters on the Evolution of the Dwarven Race, two chapters on Durin&#39;s Day examining the Dwarven lunar calendar, and 11 chapters on themes exploring various topics on influences and revisions between The Hobbit.
</p>
</blockquote>
