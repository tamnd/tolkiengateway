<i><b>The Monsters and the Critics, and Other Essays</b></i> is a collection of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s scholarly linguistic essays and lectures. The book was edited by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) and published posthumously in [1983](../_/1983.md).

All of them were initially delivered as lectures to academics, with the exception of *On Translating Beowulf*, which Christopher Tolkien notes in his foreword is not addressed to an academic audience.

## Contents {#Contents}

Seven essays are included in the book, some of which had been published before, while others were presented for the first time in print.

- Foreword
- "[Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics](../B/Beowulf%253A_The_Monsters_and_the_Critics.md)" — lecture delivered in [1936](../_/1936.md), looks at the critics' understanding of *[Beowulf](<../B/Beowulf_(poem).md>)*, and proposes a fresh take on the poem
- "[On Translating Beowulf](../P/Prefatory_Remarks_on_Prose_Translation_of_'Beowulf'.md)" — published in [1940](../_/1940.md), looks at the difficulties in translating the poem from Old English
- "[On Fairy-stories](../O/On_Fairy-stories~23735.md)" — lecture delivered in [1939](../_/1939.md), a defence of the fantasy genre
- "[A Secret Vice](../A/A_Secret_Vice.md)" — lecture delivered in [1931](../_/1931.md), talks about creating imaginary languages, giving background to Tolkien's [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md)
- "<a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../S/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight.md" title="Sir Gawain and the Green Knight">Sir Gawain and the Green Knight</a>" — [lecture](../W/W.P._Ker_Lecture.md) delivered in [1953](../_/1953.md), a study of the medieval poem of the same name
- "[English and Welsh](../E/English_and_Welsh.md)" — lecture delivered in [1955](../_/1955.md), a survey of the historical relationship between the two tongues, including an analysis of the word *Welsh*
- "[Valedictory Address to the University of Oxford](../V/Valedictory_Address_to_the_University_of_Oxford.md)" — lecture delivered in [1959](../_/1959.md), given upon Tolkien's retirement from Oxford

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The seven &#39;essays&#39; by J.R.R. Tolkien assembled in this new paperback edition were with one exception delivered as general lectures on particular occasions; and while they mostly arose out of Tolkien&#39;s work in medieval literature, they are accessible to all. Two of them are concerned with <i>Beowulf</i>, including the well-known lecture whose title is taken for this book, and one with <i>Sir Gawain and the Green Knight</i>, given in the University of Glasgow in 1953.<br/>
Also included in this volume is the lecture <i>English and Welsh</i>; the <i>Valedictory Address to the University of Oxford</i> in 1959; and a paper on Invented Languages delivered in 1931, with exemplification from poems in the Elvish tongues. Most famous of all is <i>On Fairy-Stories</i>, a discussion of the nature of fairy-tales and fantasy, which gives insight into Tolkien&#39;s approach to the whole genre.<br/>
The pieces in this collection cover a period of nearly thirty years, beginning six years before the publication of <i>The Hobbit</i>, with a unique &#39;academic&#39; lecture on his invention (calling it <i>A Secret Vice</i>) and concluding with his farewell to professorship, five years after the publication of <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>.
</p>
</blockquote>

## Relation to the legendarium {#Relation_to_the_legendarium}

As these essays are of a scholarly nature, there are scarcely any direct references to the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) of Tolkien. However, the essay "A Secret Vice" contains a final section of *Notes* by Christopher Tolkien, where he points to references to *[The Book of Lost Tales](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two.md)* and also reprints a later version of one of the Elvish poems, being "one of the major pieces of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
