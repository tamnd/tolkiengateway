**Tom Bombadil** was an enigmatic figure who lived throughout the history of [Arda](/wiki/Arda). Living a short distance east of the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest), he seemed to possess unequaled power in the land around his dwelling. Although seemingly benevolent, he took no stance against the [Dark Lords](/wiki/Dark_Lord "Dark Lord").

## Appearance and traits {#Appearance_and_traits}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Old Tom Bombadil is a merry fellow;<br/>Bright blue his jacket is, and his boots are yellow.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Tom Bombadil</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Tom looked like an old man who was taller and heavier than a [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits"), but not quite tall enough for a [man](/wiki/Men "Men").<sup id="cite_ref-OldF_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldF-2">[2]</a></sup>

His face was creased and red as a ripe apple with blue and bright eyes.<sup id="cite_ref-OldF_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldF-2">[2]</a></sup> His hands were brown-skinned.<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup> He had thick brown hair<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup> and a long brown beard.<sup id="cite_ref-OldF_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldF-2">[2]</a></sup>

Tom wore a blue coat and an old tall hat with a long blue feather and yellow boots.<sup id="cite_ref-OldF_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldF-2">[2]</a></sup> Earlier he wore a [swan](/wiki/Swans "Swans") feather, which he later replaced with a blue one from a [kingfisher](/wiki/Kingfishers "Kingfishers").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

He lived in a [house](</wiki/Underhill_(house)> "Underhill (house)") just outside the eastern edge of the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest) by the river [Withywindle](/wiki/Withywindle),<sup id="cite_ref-OldF_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldF-2">[2]</a></sup> together with his lovely wife [Goldberry](/wiki/Goldberry).<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup> According to him<sup id="cite_ref-FR|Barrow_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FR|Barrow-5">[5]</a></sup> and to [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) as well,<sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup> he would not leave his country. The only known location on the border of his country is where he makes that statement, on the south side of the [East Road](/wiki/East_Road), north of the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs) and about four miles northwest of [Bree](/wiki/Bree). His country must have included the Barrow-downs and the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest), and according to the poem "[Bombadil Goes Boating](/wiki/Bombadil_Goes_Boating)," he visited villages in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and [Farmer Maggot](/wiki/Farmer_Maggot)'s farm in the [Marish](/wiki/Marish), which would mean that his country included them as well.

The [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders) had little understanding of his powers and nature. They saw him as mysterious and unpredictable, but benevolent and comic, more or less as the [Shire-folk](/wiki/Shire-folk) thought of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf).<sup id="cite_ref-Adv_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adv-7">[7]</a></sup>

The origins and [nature of Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil/Nature "Tom Bombadil/Nature") are unknown. He claims to have existed when the [Dark Lord](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth") came "from the Outside",<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:131</sup> perhaps meaning he was alive at least as far back as the [Spring of Arda](/wiki/Spring_of_Arda).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Bombadil calls himself the "Eldest", [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel) calls him the "First", and his Sindarin name *Iarwain Ben-adar* means "Oldest and fatherless."

His role and nature in the [Elder Days](/wiki/Elder_Days) and later is unknown. He was in Arda "before the river and the trees" and before the first rain. He "made paths" before the [Great March](/wiki/Great_March) of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar), and saw the [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men) and their [tombs](/wiki/Barrow_Downs "Barrow Downs"). He witnessed the [Changing of the World](/wiki/Changing_of_the_World), the reducing of the great forests that covered all [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), the arrival of the [Exiles of Númenor](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and of the [Barrow-wights](/wiki/Barrow-wights).<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup>

The nature of his interactions with the outside world is unclear. He had contact with the [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders) throughout the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Adv_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adv-7">[7]</a></sup> He was familiar with Frodo, his companions and their families when they visit him. He attributed most of this knowledge to [Farmer Maggot](/wiki/Farmer_Maggot).<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup> He had names among the Elves, Dwarves and Northern Men. [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) knew of him when he ventured in his lands. However, most of the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond) were unfamiliar with him, and Elrond admitted to having forgotten about him from long ago.<sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Adv_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adv-7">[7]</a></sup>

### War of the Ring {#War_of_the_Ring}

On [26 September](/wiki/26_September), [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), he encountered [four hobbits](/wiki/Travellers "Travellers") while he was searching for water-lilies for his wife. Two of those Hobbits, [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took"), had been captured by [Old Man Willow](/wiki/Old_Man_Willow). Tom, who was the master of the Old Forest, rescued them, and took all four of them to his house.<sup id="cite_ref-OldF_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldF-2">[2]</a></sup>

The four hobbits stayed two nights, and he told them many tales and songs. With cunning questions, he made [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") tell him of the [Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). When Tom tried it on, nothing happened, but he then took it off and flipped it in the air and made the ring itself disappear, showing that indeed within his realm Tom was master. However, when Frodo put the ring on, Tom could still see him. He bade the Hobbit to come back and sit down; his hand was fairer without the ring.

Before his guests went to sleep, Tom warned them of the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs), and advised them to pass any barrow on the western side. He also taught them a song, should they come to peril.<sup id="cite_ref-house_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-house-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:134</sup>

And they did come to peril. Tom chased off a [wight](/wiki/Barrow-wights "Barrow-wights") with song, and broke the spells on the barrow in which the four hobbits were captured. While he sent the Hobbits to calm down, he went for provisions. He also brought the ponies that had belonged to Merry. After that, he broke the spells of the barrow. From the barrow's mighty hoard, he took a blue-jewelled brooch for Goldberry (probably belonging to the spouse of the [last prince of Cardolan](/wiki/Last_prince_of_Cardolan "Last prince of Cardolan") he seemingly met long ago),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> and gave a [dagger](/wiki/Daggers_of_Westernesse "Daggers of Westernesse") to each of the hobbits. He then advised them to make for *[The Prancing Pony](/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony)* in [Bree](/wiki/Bree).<sup id="cite_ref-FR|Barrow_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FR|Barrow-5">[5]</a></sup>

The peril of the hobbits was not over; an attack on their lives was carried out, and their ponies were set loose. The ponies apparently remembered the care they were given in the house of Tom Bombadil, and returned to stay beside Tom's own pony, [Fatty Lumpkin](/wiki/Fatty_Lumpkin). He returned them to [Barliman Butterbur](/wiki/Barliman_Butterbur), the proprietor of *The Prancing Pony*. Since he had paid eighteen pence as compensation for the loss, he was now the owner of five fine ponies.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

As he was merry and benevolent, some of the [Free Peoples](/wiki/Free_Peoples) considered him a potential ally against [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) during the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring). [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) and [Erestor](/wiki/Erestor) considered that he should be present at the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond). However, according to [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), Tom Bombadil was perhaps not fully aware of the struggle of Light and Darkness and could not prove useful to their causes.<sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup>

After the [fall of Sauron](/wiki/Fall_of_Sauron "Fall of Sauron") at the end of the War, the [Travelers](/wiki/Travelers) returned to the Shire, but Gandalf left them and went to talk to Tom.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:996</sup>

Eventually the defeat of Sauron and the victory of the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/West" title="West">West</a> allowed Tom to continue and "survive" in the [later Ages](/wiki/Later_Ages "Later Ages").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

### Legacy {#Legacy}

Tom Bombadil’s existence passed into Hobbit lore and was referenced in poems such as *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil)* and *[Bombadil Goes Boating](/wiki/Bombadil_Goes_Boating)*. The Hobbit, [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee), based and modeled his protagonist in *[The Stone Troll](/wiki/The_Stone_Troll)*, [Tom](</wiki/Tom_(The_Stone_Troll)> "Tom (The Stone Troll)"), after Bombadil, considering that Sam composed the poem soon after their meeting.<sup id="cite_ref-RF_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RF-14">[14]</a></sup> In the poem, Tom encounters an old [troll](/wiki/Stone-trolls "Stone-trolls"), gnawing for years on the shinbone of Tim, his [nuncle](/wiki/Unusual_words "Unusual words"), and Tom demands for the troll to let it down.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> It is likely that Tim was only an invention by Sam rather than actually being Tom Bombadil's nuncle.<sup id="cite_ref-RF_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RF-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

He also went by other names:

- The [Elves](/wiki/Elves) called him <i><b><a href="/wiki/Iaur" title="Iaur">Iar</a>wain <a href="/wiki/Penadar" title="Penadar">Ben-adar</a></b></i><sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup> in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin)<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>, which translates to "Oldest and Fatherless"<sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup>. *Iarwain* literally means "Old-young", presumably because he had always appeared to be much the same: "old, but very vigorous"<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>
- The "[Northern Men](/wiki/Northmen "Northmen")" gave him the name <i><b>Orald</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup> *Orald* is an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) word meaning "very ancient". Old English represents "the [language of the Rohirrim and their kin](/wiki/Rohanese "Rohanese")".<sup id="cite_ref-nomen_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nomen-19">[19]</a></sup>
- The Dwarves gave him the name <i><b>Forn</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-CoE_6-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoE-6">[6]</a></sup> *Forn* is [Scandinavian](/wiki/North_Germanic_languages "North Germanic languages") for "(belonging to) ancient (days)".<sup id="cite_ref-nomen_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nomen-19">[19]</a></sup> Since all the dwarf names are in Old Norse, which represents "a [northern language of Men](/wiki/Northern_Mannish "Northern Mannish")"<sup id="cite_ref-nomen_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nomen-19">[19]</a></sup> and the Dwarves from the region of Dale took their "outer" names in the [language of Dale](/wiki/Language_of_Dale "Language of Dale")<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>, it is possible that the name *Forn* came from the [language of Dale](/wiki/Language_of_Dale "Language of Dale"). In some imprints of *[The History of Middle-earth Index](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth_Index)*, this name was accidentally spelled with a "P" as the first letter: [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) (ed.), *[The History of Middle-earth Index](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth_Index)*, "Tom Bombadil (VII)", p. 435&lt;/ref&gt;

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Bombadil* is [Bucklandish](/wiki/Bucklandish) in form. It is probable that the [Hobbits from Buckland](/wiki/Bucklanders "Bucklanders") gave him this name.<sup id="cite_ref-Adv_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adv-7">[7]</a></sup>

[Paula Marmor](/wiki/Paula_Marmor) notes that *bobadil* is an archaic word meaning "braggart", as seen in the character "Captain Bobadill" in the English play <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Every_Man_in_His_Humour" title="wikipedia:Every Man in His Humour">Every Man in His Humour</a></i>. Because of its Bucklandish form, *[An Introduction to Elvish](/wiki/An_Introduction_to_Elvish)* lists the name *Bombadil* under the "[Celtic](/wiki/Celtic)-sounding names". However, it is said that the word derives from <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boabdil" title="wikipedia:Boabdil">Boabdil</a></b>, the Spaniard name of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_XII_of_Granada" title="wikipedia:Muhammad XII of Granada"><b>Abu Abdillah</b> Muhammad XII</a>, the last Moorish ruler of Granada.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

[Treebeard](/wiki/Treebeard) knew Tom Bombadil in an earlier manuscript version of the chapter *Treebeard*, but this was later struck out.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> According to Christopher Tolkien it is conceivable that the contrast between Tom Bombadil and the Ents confused the conflict between the Ents and the Entwives or that the struck out passage initiated the idea of a conflict between the Ents and the Entwives.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tom Bombadil was inspired primarily from a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peg_wooden_doll" title="wikipedia:Peg wooden doll">dutch doll</a> Tolkien's child(ren) toyed with.<sup id="cite_ref-intro_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-24">[24]</a></sup> The doll had a feathered hat. One time they found it in the lavatory, being stuffed there by little [John Tolkien](/wiki/John_Tolkien), who perhaps didn't like it much.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>

Tolkien began (probably in the 1920s) writing a story entitled *Tom Bombadil* set during the reign of "King Bonhedig" in the British prehistory, far before any recorded events or invasions. The protagonist Tombombadil is mentioned as one of the oldest inhabitants of Bonhedig's kingdom, which spanned many miles on either side of the Thames. Only the 3 opening paragraphs survive of the shortly-abandoned, story, and the fragment ends at the description of Tombombadil who "wore a tall hat with a blue feather; his jacket was blue, and his boots were yellow".<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

Around the 1930s or earlier Tolkien wrote a poem about some Tom Bombadil rowing down a River, a poem which Tolkien later identified as his "germ of Tom Bombadil".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> Later in [1934](/wiki/1934) he put him into [a poem](</wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_(poem)> "The Adventures of Tom Bombadil (poem)"), again described according to the appearance of the aforementioned doll (something that he did with other toys of his children, like [Rover](/wiki/Rover)).<sup id="cite_ref-intro_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-24">[24]</a></sup> At one time he described him as a "spirit of the (vanishing) [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford) and Berkshire countryside"<sup id="cite_ref-L19_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L19-28">[28]</a></sup>

When asked to make a sequel for *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, Tolkien briefly considered making that figure of his poem the central character.<sup id="cite_ref-L19_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L19-28">[28]</a></sup> Although it didn't happen, he eventually [appeared](/wiki/The_Old_Forest "The Old Forest") in the narrative as a supporting character. Tolkien wrote Bombadil as a direct contrast to the artistry and (sub)creative force of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves); whereas they seek to create, devise, alter and control, Bombadil only observes and contemplates the world outside him and takes joy in it. He is the fearless theoretical study of the world, and history.<sup id="cite_ref-coghill_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coghill-29">[29]</a></sup>

Justin Noetzel in his paper "Beorn and Tom Bombadil: Mythology, Narrative, and The Most (Non) Essential Characters in Middle-earth", suggests an association of Tom Bombadil with the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Otherworld" title="wikipedia:Celtic Otherworld">Celtic Otherworld</a> and tales of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann" title="wikipedia:Tuatha Dé Danann">Tuatha Dé Danann</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>

David Elton Gay compares Tom to the demigod <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4in%C3%A4m%C3%B6inen" title="wikipedia:Väinämöinen">Väinämöinen</a> from the *[Kalevala](/wiki/Kalevala)*: both are extremely old and powerful immortal figures who express their power in rhymes, and both have control over their small forested country.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>

## Outside the legendarium {#Outside_the_legendarium}

In addition to the aforementioned earlier works written independently to the [Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium), a figure that hints at Bombadil appears in the much later poem *[Once upon a Time](/wiki/Once_upon_a_Time)*. Tolkien wrote it around [1964](/wiki/1964) and reused the names of "Tom" and "Goldberry" (although the epithet "Bombadil" is not mentioned, the association can be made as he appears with Goldberry). Hammond &amp; Scull note that in this poem Tom appears less omnipotent; while he is known to talk to all creatures, who always obey him, the mysterious *[lintips](/wiki/Lintips "Lintips")* are the only ones who refuse to talk to him and hide away.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> No specific events are mentioned that can connect it to Tom Bombadil or the legendarium of Arda.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

Because he is left out of the three major adaptations ([Ralph Bakshi](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)> "The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"), [BBC's 1981 series](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)") and [Peter Jackson's](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring")), Tom Bombadil's main role (providing the [Barrow-blades](/wiki/Daggers_of_Westernesse "Daggers of Westernesse")) is omitted. He does have several appearances in other adaptations, though.

<b>1955: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1956_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1956 radio series)">BBC Radio&#39;s The Lord of the Rings</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Norman_Shelley" title="Norman Shelley">Norman Shelley</a> voiced Bombadil, and Tolkien thought his portrayal &#34;dreadful&#34;. <a href="/wiki/Goldberry" title="Goldberry">Goldberry</a> was portrayed as his daughter, rather than his wife.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1979: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1979_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1979 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1979 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In this series, Tom was voiced by <a href="/wiki/Bernard_Mayes" title="Bernard Mayes">Bernard Mayes</a>. Like Norman Shelley before him, he also voiced <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>1988: <i><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_War_in_Middle_Earth" title="J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s War in Middle Earth">J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s War in Middle Earth</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tom Bombadil can be found outside his house in the Old Forest.</dd></dl>

<b>1990: <i><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_The_Lord_of_the_Rings,_Vol._I_(1990_video_game)" title="J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I (1990 video game)">J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tom Bombadil rescues the party from the Willow Man, and provides information, supplies, shelter, and side-quests for the party.  He later rescues the party from the Barrow Wights, and very briefly joins as a temporary playable character while inside the barrow.  His role runs almost directly parallel to the original, with some related passages of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Fellowship of the Ring">The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i> quoted directly.  However, <a href="/wiki/Goldberry" title="Goldberry">Goldberry</a>&#39;s role is significantly changed to provide a quest for the party.</dd></dl>

<b>1992: <a href="/wiki/Der_Herr_der_Ringe_(1992_German_radio_series)" title="Der Herr der Ringe (1992 German radio series)"><i>Der Herr der Ringe</i> (1992 German radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tom Bombadil is played by Peter Ehrlich.</dd></dl>

<b>1992: <a href="/wiki/Tales_from_the_Perilous_Realm_(1992_radio_series)" title="Tales from the Perilous Realm (1992 radio series)"><i>Tales from the Perilous Realm</i> (1992 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>When he adapted the 1981 <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)">radio series</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brian_Sibley" title="Brian Sibley">Brian Sibley</a> deeply regretted cutting Bombadil from the radio series.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup> When he made <i><a href="/wiki/Tales_from_the_Perilous_Realm" title="Tales from the Perilous Realm">Tales from the Perilous Realm</a></i> into a radio series, he decided to change the section &#34;<a href="/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil" title="The Adventures of Tom Bombadil">The Adventures of Tom Bombadil</a>&#34;. Rather than several (unadaptable) Hobbitish poems, Sibley adapted the chapters from <i><a href="/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Fellowship of the Ring">The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>. Bombadil is voiced by <a href="/wiki/Ian_Hogg" title="Ian Hogg">Ian Hogg</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>1993: <i><a href="/wiki/Hobitit" title="Hobitit">Hobitit</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tom Bombadil is portrayed by Esko Hukkanen. It is the only screen adaptation that featured him before 2024.</dd></dl>

<b>2001-2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game">The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Although Tom Bombadil does not appear in <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> film series</a>, Decipher produced a card for the character. He was portrayed by Harry Weller-Chew.</dd></dl>

<b>2001–present: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Strategy_Battle_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game">The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Despite not appearing in the films the game is based on, Tom Bombadil and Goldberry were given several models by <a href="/wiki/Games_Workshop" title="Games Workshop">Games Workshop</a>, which has held rights for tabletop games since 1980.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/P%C3%A1n_prste%C5%88ov_(2001-2003_Slovak_radio_series)" title="Pán prsteňov (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)"><i>Pán prsteňov</i> (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The voice of Tom Bombadil is provided by Milan Lasica. He appears in the final third of the first episode, helping the four hobbits with Old Man Willow, guiding them to his house and taking them in as his guests for the night, along with his wife Goldberry. After they depart and get lost on the Barrow Downs, he once again aids in their rescue, and provides them with barrow-blades from the barrow of the defeated barrow-wight.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Daran_Norris" title="Daran Norris">Daran Norris</a> portrayed Bombadil with a Scottish accent. His role is much like that in the book, and as one of the few characters in this video game, he keeps most of his songs.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers">The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers_(extended_edition)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (extended edition)">extended edition</a>, some of Bombadil&#39;s poems are transferred to <a href="/wiki/Treebeard" title="Treebeard">Treebeard</a>, and so is his encounter with <a href="/wiki/Old_Man_Willow" title="Old Man Willow">Old Man Willow</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Bombadil is a summonable power. Once summoned, he can plow through enemy lines. His most powerful weapon is a &#34;Sonic Song&#34;. As soon as <a href="/wiki/Electronic_Arts" title="Electronic Arts">EA</a> secured the rights to the books, it was decided that Tom Bombadil should be in it; his appearance is kept close to his description in the book.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tom can be found either inside or outside his house in the Old Forest. He helps the player track down crebain scouts possessing important information, and later arrives to rescue the player from the Barrow-Downs when (s)he gets himself in more than (s)he is prepared for, much like the Hobbits in the Book. He later aids the player against agents of the Barrow-downs when the latter attempts to corrupt Old Man Willow with a <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Morgul-blade" title="Morgul-blade">Morgul-blade</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2012: <i><a href="/wiki/Lego_The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Video_Game" title="Lego The Lord of the Rings: The Video Game">Lego The Lord of the Rings: The Video Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Bombadil is a playable character.  He uses a trowel as a weapon and for digging in certain spots. Tom can be found in his house on the road to the north of <a href="/wiki/Bucklebury_Ferry" title="Bucklebury Ferry">Bucklebury Ferry</a> in the forested area between <a href="/wiki/Bree" title="Bree">Bree</a> and the Shire. It is unclear if this forest is meant to be the Old Forest or not; it is in the right location, but the game never specifically names it and its physical appearance does not fit the book&#39;s description.</dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="/wiki/Lego_The_Hobbit:_The_Video_Game" title="Lego The Hobbit: The Video Game">Lego The Hobbit: The Video Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Bombadil also appears as a playable character, and again wields a trowel. This time, Tom is found in a forest southwest of Bree, near a house atop a hill (likely his house in the Old Forest).</dd></dl>

<b>2024: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tom Bombadil will be portrayed by <a href="/wiki/Rory_Michael_Kinnear" title="Rory Michael Kinnear">Rory Michael Kinnear</a>, whose headwear and outfit was made by costume designer Luca Mosca to look similar to J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s description of Tom Bombadil. The production designer of the show, <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Kristian_Milstead&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Kristian Milstead (page does not exist)">Kristian Milstead</a>, incorporated a star map on the ceiling of Tom&#39;s cottage to imply that Bombadil &#34;has been watching the constellations for signs—and for the Stranger’s arrival&#34;, thereby connecting Tom to the larger story. The showrunners mention that they have given him a second home besides <a href="/wiki/Underhill_(house)" title="Underhill (house)">Underhill</a> which he uses as a &#34;summer cottage&#34; on the outskirts of the once &#34;green and beautiful&#34; <a href="/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn" title="Rhûn">Rhûn</a> &#34;which...now is...a dead wasteland&#34;. At the time of the show, Bombadil &#34;has gone out to...Rhûn...to see what&#39;s happened&#34; to the region in order to prevent the desolation from spreading westwards.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup></dd></dl>
