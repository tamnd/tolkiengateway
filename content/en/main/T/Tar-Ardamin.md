**Tar-Ardamin** (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[taˈrardamin]</span>) or **Ar-Abattârik** was the nineteenth ruler of Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ardamin_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ardamin-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Tar-Ardamin was the penultimate king of Númenor who took the sceptre with a royal name in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya). He was called Ar-Abattârik in [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic).<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ardamin_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ardamin-1">[1]</a></sup> His son [Ar-Adûnakhôr](/wiki/Ar-Ad%C3%BBnakh%C3%B4r) abandoned the old tradition of using an official [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name, and used Adûnaic in his official name.<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Ardamin* is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but as Paul Strack suggests it means "World Tower" as a compound of *[Arda](/wiki/Arda)* ("world") and *[mind(don)](/wiki/Min "Min")* ("tower").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Like all the rulers of Númenor who took their royal names in Quenya, *Ardamin* added the prefix *tar-* ("high") to his name when he received the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Sceptre of Númenor").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

*Abattârik* is an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "World Pillar" with the last element <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=T%C3%A2rik&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Târik (page does not exist)">târik</a> ("pillar") and that the first element may mean "World". [Andreas Möhn](/wiki/Andreas_M%C3%B6hn) suggests that this element is likely *aban* with the final *n* altered to *t* in the compound according to the rules of Adûnaic assimilation.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The meaning of the Adûnaic prefix *Ar-* is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "king" and is a prefixal form of *ârû* ("king") or derived from the same root.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

It should be said that there is some doubt over the character of Ar-Abattârik. He is listed in the detailed annals of *[The Line of Elros: Kings of Númenor](/wiki/The_Line_of_Elros:_Kings_of_N%C3%BAmenor)* as the son of [Tar-Calmacil](/wiki/Tar-Calmacil) and father of [Ar-Adûnakhôr](/wiki/Ar-Ad%C3%BBnakh%C3%B4r),<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-4">[4]</a></sup> but in Appendix A I (i) to *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, the relevant section of the King-lists<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> appears like this:

<p>...<a href="/wiki/Tar-Calmacil" title="Tar-Calmacil">Tar-Calmacil</a>. After Calmacil the Kings took the sceptre in names of the <a href="/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans" title="Númenóreans">Númenorean</a> (or <a href="/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic" title="Adûnaic">Adûnaic</a>) tongue: <a href="/wiki/Ar-Ad%C3%BBnakh%C3%B4r" title="Ar-Adûnakhôr">Ar-Adûnakhôr</a>...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Appendix_A" title="Appendix A">Appendix A</a> I (i)</cite></p>

It seems most likely, as Christopher Tolkien suggests, that this omission is probably a simple mistake, and that Tar-Ardamin or Ar-Abattârik should appear in the list at this point.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
