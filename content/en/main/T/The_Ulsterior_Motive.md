"**The Ulsterior Motive**" is an [unpublished essay](/wiki/Index:Unpublished_material "Index:Unpublished material") written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) in [1964](/wiki/1964). It originated as a response to [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis)'s book <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_to_Malcolm:_Chiefly_on_Prayer" title="wikipedia:Letters to Malcolm: Chiefly on Prayer">Letters to Malcolm</a></i>, which was published posthumously in that year,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and developed into a somewhat critical retrospective on Lewis, whose Protestantism put him at odds with Tolkien religiously.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The title is a reference to Lewis's <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Protestants" title="wikipedia:Ulster Protestants">Ulster Protestant</a> background, to which Tolkien attributed his reluctance to embrace Catholicism.

A few brief extracts from the currently restricted manuscript were published in A.N. Wilson's *C.S. Lewis: A Biography* and [Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter)'s *[The Inklings](</wiki/The_Inklings_(book)> "The Inklings (book)")*.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-4">[4]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>It was not for some time that I realized that there was more in the title <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pilgrim%27s_Regress" title="wikipedia:The Pilgrim&#39;s Regress">Pilgrim&#39;s Regress</a></i> than I had understood (or the author either, maybe). Lewis would regress. He would not re-enter <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by a new door, but by the old one: at least in the sense that in taking it up again he would also take up, or reawaken, the prejudices so sedulously planted in boyhood. He would become again a Northern Ireland protestant.
</p>

</blockquote>
