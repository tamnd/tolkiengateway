<i><b>The Chronology of The Lord of the Rings</b></i> is a 130-page scholarly article published in *[Tolkien Studies 19 Supplement](/wiki/Tolkien_Studies:_Volume_19_Supplement "Tolkien Studies: Volume 19 Supplement")* on [31 July](/wiki/31_July) [2022](/wiki/2022), edited and commentated by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cloud_Hicklin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="William Cloud Hicklin (page does not exist)">William Cloud Hicklin</a>.

The article presents [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s full set of manuscripts of the time-scheme, or "Chronology", that underlies the planning of *The Lord of the Rings*. There are multiple versions of the time-schemes, the final version contains 14 pages. It is this version that is fully transcribed, and provided with extensive notes in the article. Hicklin also provided an introduction and an in-depth commentary that traces the evolution of the Chronology.

Some excerpts from the Chronology have been quoted in *[The Lord of the Rings: A Reader's Companion](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_A_Reader%27s_Companion)* (2005). Photocopies of the Chronology's final-version manuscripts were afterwards published in *[The Art of the Manuscript](/wiki/The_Art_of_the_Manuscript)* (2022). But many of the draft-version manuscripts are presented exclusively in this work.

The Chronology provides new insights that do not appear in the published books. For example, concerning the fate of [Shagrat](/wiki/Shagrat), Orc Captain of the Tower of Cirith Ungol, it is stated that on Saturday March 17,

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>Shagrat brings the mithril coat and other spoils to Barad-dȗr; but is slain by Sauron</i>&#34;.</dd></dl>

Another hidden fact shows that, while the Fellowship is resting in Lothlórien on Tuesday, January 24, [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) lurks on the borders of that country; and there he is captured by [Uglúk](/wiki/Ugl%C3%BAk) and the Isengarders. The Chronology entry reads under the heading Gollum:

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>Gollum captured by Uglúk, but escapes after revealing that Hobbits of Shire were with Gandalf, and enough is said to make Uglúk certain that Ring was with the Company</i>&#34;.</dd></dl>

While under the heading of Enemies it reads:

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>Isengarders capture Gollum, and torment him for news. Uglúk sends news to Isengard of Hobbits; but not of the Ring</i>&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-W.F._1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-W.F.-1">[1]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<h2><span id="Editors.27_introduction"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Editors&#39;_introduction">Editors&#39; introduction</span></h2>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>This special issue, published between our regular annual volumes, is entirely devoted to a single scholarly work: William Cloud Hicklin’s textual edition and commentary of “The Chronology of The Lord of the Rings.” The sheer size, as well as the importance, of this work suggested this special treatment of it. Our grateful thanks go to Mr. Hicklin for giving us the opportunity to add to the mosaic of Middle-earth this essential and heretofore missing piece. It is a look behind the scenes at the painstaking planning that went into one of the most salient features of Tolkien’s great work: that back-and-forth switching among interconnecting narratives which Richard C. West called its “interlace” structure. The word describes a medieval narrative device meant to mirror “the flux of events . . . where everything is happening at once” (West 79). Such attention to detail gives reality to a world, especially a fantastical sub-created one that has to command belief instead of relying on it.
</p>
</blockquote>
