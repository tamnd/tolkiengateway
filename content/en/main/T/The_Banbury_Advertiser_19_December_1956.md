<b><i>The Banbury Advertiser</i> 19 December 1956</b> is an issue of *The Banbury Advertiser*, published on [19 December](/wiki/19_December) [1956](/wiki/1956). Included in the article "Deddington’s New Library Opened by Mrs. L. Hichens – Prof. Tolkien’s Whimsical Talk" (page 5) are quotes from [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s speech held at the opening of the new Deddington Library on [14 December](/wiki/14_December) [1956](/wiki/1956) (slightly different from the quotes appearing in the [*Oxford Mail* 15 December 1956](/wiki/Oxford_Mail_15_December_1956 "Oxford Mail 15 December 1956")).<sup id="cite_ref-Ded_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ded-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Excerpts from the speech {#Excerpts_from_the_speech}

"Nowadays books are besieged by a great many powerful embattled enemies, some of whom have been strongly entrenched, and to be here at the opening of a strong point from which troops can be sent out against those enemies is a great honour."

"The wealth of books to be found here is food for the mind, and everyone knows that for the stomach to go without food for a long time is bad, but for the mind to go without food is even worse."<sup id="cite_ref-Ded_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ded-1">[1]</a></sup>
