The **One Ring** was an ancient artifact created by the [Dark Lord](../D/Dark_Lord.md) [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) in the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) for the purpose of ruling over the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), mainly the [Elves](../E/Elves.md).

It was also known as the **Ruling Ring**, **Great Ring of Power** and **Isildur's Bane** (due to it causing the death of [Isildur](../I/Isildur.md)). In the [Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md) text of the [Ring Verse](../R/Ring_Verse.md), it is referred to as **Ash Nazg**.<sup id="cite_ref-council_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Origin and creation {#Origin_and_creation}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/bb/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="470" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/bb/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/bb/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Forging_of_the_One.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1022" data-file-height="1600"/></a><figcaption><i>The Forging of the One</i> by <a href="Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

During the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md), the Dark Lord [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) persuaded [Celebrimbor](../C/Celebrimbor.md) and his people, the [Elven](../E/Elves.md) smiths of [Eregion](../E/Eregion.md), to forge the [Rings of Power](../R/Rings_of_Power.md). Secretly, Sauron returned to [Orodruin](../O/Orodruin.md) and forged the One Ring in its fires.<sup id="cite_ref-RoP_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RoP-2">[2]</a></sup>

It was made as the Master Ring, the One Ring which would control all the others, and thereby dominate their bearers. Sauron allowed much of his will and power to go into it.<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Thus, he was at his most powerful when wearing the Ring; and although his power did not diminish if he was not in possession of it,<sup id="cite_ref-L131_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-5">[5]</a></sup> he would lose the ability to have a physical form once it was destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Although it appeared to be made of simple gold, the Ring was virtually impervious to damage, and could only be destroyed in the very fires where it had originally been forged - [Orodruin](../O/Orodruin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-council_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Dark Years {#Dark_Years}

When Celebrimbor and the other Ring-bearers realized Sauron's treachery, they took off their Rings, as while they were wearing them, Sauron knew where they were, and went to war with him. This war, the [War of the Elves and Sauron](../W/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron.md), destroyed Eregion and devastated much of Eriador. Sauron conquered and was able to claim all the Rings of Power (except the [Three](Three_Rings.md)) and distribute them.<sup id="cite_ref-RoP_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RoP-2">[2]</a></sup> During the era known as the [Dark Years](../D/Dark_Years.md), Sauron became master of almost all of Middle-earth beyond the coasts and was known as the [Dark Lord](../D/Dark_Lord.md) of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md). He raised [Barad-dûr](../B/Barad-dûr.md) near [Mount Doom](../M/Mount_Doom.md), constructed the [Black Gate](../B/Black_Gate.md) of Mordor to prevent invasion, and raised massive armies of [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md), [Trolls](Trolls.md), and Men, chiefly [Easterlings](../E/Easterlings.md) and [Southrons](../H/Haradrim.md).

The power of the Ring allowed Sauron to link it with some of his works, such as [Barad-dûr](../B/Barad-dûr.md).<sup id="cite_ref-council_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-1">[1]</a></sup> By the One Ring's influence he controlled the [Nine Rings](../N/Nine_Rings.md) which had been given to nine mortal Men, who were corrupted and turned into the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md), his chief servants. However, he was unable to control the [Seven Rings](../S/Seven_Rings.md) of the [Dwarf-lords](../D/Dwarves.md), because of their different, more hearty nature and natural resistance against domination by others—instead, the Rings increased the greed of the dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-RoP_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RoP-2">[2]</a></sup>

Sauron's rise offended the arrogant [Númenóreans](../N/Númenóreans.md), who attacked him with great force of arms. The forces of Sauron fled the onslaught, and Sauron realized that he could not overcome the Númenòreans through military might. Sauron allowed himself to be taken as a hostage to Númenor by King [Ar-Pharazôn](../A/Ar-Pharazôn.md). There, he quickly grew from captive to adviser. The King obviously knew nothing about the Ring and its significance<sup id="cite_ref-L211_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L211-7">[7]</a></sup> and Sauron corrupted many Númenóreans using the power of the Ring,<sup id="cite_ref-L211_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L211-7">[7]</a></sup> eventually convincing the king to rebel against the [Valar](../V/Valar.md). This resulted in the [Downfall of Númenor](../D/Downfall_of_Númenor.md). Sauron was diminished in the destruction and his spirit (presumably with the Ring<sup id="cite_ref-L211_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L211-7">[7]</a></sup>) fled back to Mordor, where he slowly rebuilt his strength.

### First defeat of Sauron {#First_defeat_of_Sauron}

When the [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md) Númenóreans founded the [Realms in Exile](../R/Realms_in_Exile.md), Sauron began an offence against [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), which was one of those realms; the Elves and the Númenóreans formed the [Last Alliance](../L/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men.md) against Sauron, who was vanquished by [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) and [Gil-galad](../G/Gil-galad.md). Prince [Isildur](../I/Isildur.md) then cut the Ring from Sauron's finger and took it for himself.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/A/Anke_Eißmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5e/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg/300px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5e/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg/450px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5e/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg/600px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_The_Ring_has_moved_on.jpg 2x" data-file-width="850" data-file-height="644"/></a><figcaption><i>The Ring has moved on</i> by <a href="../A/Anke_Eißmann.md" title="Anke Eißmann">Anke Eißmann</a></figcaption></figure>

Isildur was corrupted by his great desire for the Ring and took it for his own, instead of destroying it as should have been done. Though he bought it "with great pain", Isildur considered the Ring a most precious heirloom of his house, and documented its properties upon the [scroll he wrote](../S/Scroll_of_Isildur.md) in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md).<sup id="cite_ref-council_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-1">[1]</a></sup> He kept it secret even from his sons, except [his Heir](../H/Heir_of_Isildur.md) [Elendur](<../E/Elendur_(son_of_Isildur).md>) whom he trusted most.<sup id="cite_ref-Gladden_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gladden-8">[8]</a></sup> While wearing it around his neck as he travelled back to [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md), his party was attacked by a group of [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) during the [Disaster of the Gladden Fields](../D/Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields.md). Isildur jumped into the [Gladden River](../G/Gladden_River.md) to escape, but the Ring betrayed him, and in an attempt to return to its master, it slipped off his finger.<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup> Isildur was revealed to the Orcs when he climbed out of the river, and he was shot. Although this happened as an attempt to return to Sauron,<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup> the Orcs did not find the Ring, as it was lost in the [Gladden River](../G/Gladden_River.md).

The true fate of the One Ring was unknown for many years. To the North, the Ring would be known as "**Isildur's Bane**" for the role it played in causing his death.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

### Finding {#Finding}

The Ring remained hidden in the riverbed for over two millennia, despite [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md)'s many attempts to locate it, until it was discovered on a fishing trip by a [Stoor](../S/Stoors.md) named [Déagol](../D/Déagol.md). Once again, the Ring's evil powers acted upon Déagol's friend and relative, [Sméagol](../S/Sméagol.md), who murdered Déagol and took the Ring for himself. Over many ages, Sméagol was changed by the Ring's influence into the creature called [Gollum](../G/Gollum.md) (which is what he called himself, as it was similar to a sound he made). The Ring manipulated Gollum into settling in the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) near [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md), near a [colony of Orcs](../O/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains.md), where Sauron was beginning to resurface. But Gollum was too small and never left his deep pool so the Ring stayed with him for centuries. It was 500 years later, in the Third Age, when the "Necromancer" awoke once more in Mirkwood, sending his dark thoughts, and the Ring, wishing to be discovered by a new keeper and so find its way back to its Master, fell off Gollum's finger<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup> as he was returning from hunting a [Goblin](../O/Orcs.md#Orcs_and_Goblins).

The Ring was discovered a few hours later lying on the floor of a tunnel by a hobbit, [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md), who had been separated from his party of Dwarves and become lost in the caverns of the Misty Mountains, near Gollum's lair. After losing the [Riddle-game](../R/Riddle-game.md) to Bilbo, Gollum sneaked off to fetch his "[Precious](../P/Precious.md)" (as he always called the One Ring), so that he could kill Bilbo and eat him. But when Gollum arrived at his island, he found that the Ring was missing, and he let out a great wail. Deducing from Bilbo's last question--"What have I got in my pocket?"—that Bilbo had taken it, Gollum chased the hobbit through the caves, not knowing that Bilbo had discovered the Ring's powers of invisibility and was following him to the cave's exit. Bilbo escaped Gollum and the Orcs who inhabited the Misty Mountains by remaining invisible (although he lost a few of his nice brass buttons.) But it seems that the Ring once more tried to betray its new master and slipped from his finger, allowing the pursuing goblins to see him.<sup id="cite_ref-Riddles_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Riddles-10">[10]</a></sup>

When he told the story to the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) and Gandalf, however, he left the Ring out of the story. But Bilbo was aware that Gandalf suspected the presence of his magic Ring. Later on Bilbo was forced to tell the Dwarves about the Ring, to preserve their lives and continue their journey to Erebor.

Gollum, meanwhile, eventually left the Misty Mountains to track down and reclaim the Ring. He wandered for decades, only to be captured and interrogated by Sauron himself, to whom he revealed the existence of Bilbo and the Shire.

<h3><span id="Frodo.27s_quest_and_destruction"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Frodo&#39;s_quest_and_destruction">Frodo&#39;s quest and destruction</span></h3>

In [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3001](Third_Age_3001.md), following Gandalf's counsel, Bilbo gave the Ring to his nephew and adopted heir [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md). This first willing renunciation of the Ring in its history sparked the chain of events which eventually led to its unmaking. Gandalf was suspicious about the effect it had on Bilbo and went to [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) and found Isildur's account on the Ring, being lost since his death. He returned to [Hobbiton](../H/Hobbiton.md) and tested Frodo's Ring in fire, only to confirm his fears since the letters described by Isildur appeared upon it.

By this time Sauron had begun to regain his power, and the [Dark Tower](../B/Barad-dûr.md) in Mordor had been rebuilt. The [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) had begun. Gandalf urged Frodo to carry the Ring to [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) where the Wise would decide their actions. Despite Gandalf's warnings, Frodo did wear the Ring several times during his journey. On [Weathertop](../W/Weathertop.md) he was tempted by the Ring to wear it and evade the Ringwraiths, only to make him more visible to them and to be wounded by a [Morgul-knife](../M/Morgul-knife.md).

In order to prevent the recapture of the Ring, Frodo and eight other [companions](../F/Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md) set out from [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) for Mordor in an attempt to destroy the Ring in the fires of [Mount Doom](../M/Mount_Doom.md). During the quest, Frodo gradually became more and more susceptible to the Ring's power, and feared that it was going to corrupt him. When he and Sam discovered that Gollum was on their trail and "tamed" him into guiding them to Mordor, he began to feel a strange bond with the wretched, treacherous creature, seeing a possible future of himself that he felt he had to save in order to save himself. Gollum gave in to the Ring's temptation, however, and betrayed them to the spider [Shelob](../S/Shelob.md) who stung Frodo. Believing Frodo to be dead, Sam bore the Ring himself for a short time, and glimpsed its power, although he never gave in to it.

Sam rescued Frodo from a band of Orcs at the Tower of [Cirith Ungol](../C/Cirith_Ungol.md) and returned the Ring to him, but feared that the toll it was taking was too great; it nearly was. Although Frodo and Sam, followed by Gollum, eventually arrived at the [Cracks of Doom](../C/Cracks_of_Doom.md), Frodo decided to keep the Ring for himself and wore it rather than destroy it, evincing its corruptive nature. Seeing Frodo's footprints, Gollum pounced on the invisible Frodo and bit off the finger wearing the Ring. Having retrieved his long-lost "precious", Gollum rejoiced before tripping over the edge and falling into the lava pit with the Ring in hand, finally destroying it. The Ring's loss resulted in the permanent disembodiment of Sauron's spirit (*[ëala](../_/Ëalar.md)*) and crumbling of his structures, including [Barad-dûr](../B/Barad-dûr.md) and the [Black Gate](../B/Black_Gate.md).

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

### Appearance {#Appearance}

Physically the Ring resembled a geometrically perfect circle of pure gold, this perfection and purity being part of its allure. Unlike the lesser Rings, it bore no gem. It seems to have been able to expand and contract, in order to fit its wearer's finger or slip from it treacherously. Its identity could be determined by a simple (though little-known) test: when heated in fire, fine lines of fire, forming a script, would appear running along on the inside and outside of the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup> The letters were an [inscription](../R/Ring_Verse.md) in [Tengwar](Tengwar.md) of the [Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md) of Mordor, citing a section of poetry from part of its lore:

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/dc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="89" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/dc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png/450px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/dc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png/600px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png 2x" data-file-width="2035" data-file-height="605"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Ash nazg durbatulûk, ash nazg gimbatul,<br/>ash nazg thrakatulûk, agh burzum-ishi krimpatul.
</p>
</blockquote>

Which translates to:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>One Ring to rule them all, One Ring to find them,<br/>One Ring to bring them all and in the darkness bind them.
</p>
</blockquote>

### Effects {#Effects}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/R/Roger_Thomasson_-_The_One_Ring.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/6f/Roger_Thomasson_-_The_One_Ring.jpg/300px-Roger_Thomasson_-_The_One_Ring.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="379" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/6/6f/Roger_Thomasson_-_The_One_Ring.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="317" data-file-height="400"/></a><figcaption><i>The One Ring</i> by <a href="../R/Roger_Thomasson.md" title="Roger Thomasson">Roger Thomasson</a></figcaption></figure>

Like all Rings of Power, the One Ring enhanced the natural powers of its bearer, and it could also be used to decelerate decay and change in the world.<sup id="cite_ref-L131_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-5">[5]</a></sup> But unlike the other rings, the One Ring was forged by Sauron alone without any Elvish assistance<sup id="cite_ref-RoP_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RoP-2">[2]</a></sup> and was entirely evil. A person who bore the Ring could more easily dominate and command others; for instance, Sauron used this power to help convince the Númenóreans to attack [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md),<sup id="cite_ref-L211_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L211-7">[7]</a></sup> and Boromir desired to use this power to gain loyal subjects who would attack Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> The Ring also allowed its bearer to see and control the thoughts of anyone who wore any other Ring of Power.<sup id="cite_ref-RoP_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RoP-2">[2]</a></sup>

When [Men](../M/Men.md) wore the Ring, they would be partly "shifted" out of the physical realm into the [wraith-world](../W/Wraith-world.md). A side effect (but usually the first noticed) of the Ring was that it made the wearer invisible to most inhabitants of the physical realm (but highly visible to the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md)), it dimmed the wearer's sight, and it sharpened his hearing. When Men wore the ring, only their faint and shaky shadow could be seen, and only in the full light of the sun.<sup id="cite_ref-Riddles_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Riddles-10">[10]</a></sup> This "shadow world" was the world which Wraiths inhabited, but also where the [Calaquendi](../C/Calaquendi.md) (Elves of Light) lived at the same time as the normal world and held great power, as was evidenced by [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) viewing [Glorfindel](../G/Glorfindel.md) at the Ford of [Bruinen](../B/Bruinen.md) near [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> and later explained by [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> If a bearer managed to consciously subdue the Ring's will with his own, he could wield the powers that Sauron had before he lost the Ring; notably, he could control the will of others.

Part of the nature of the Ring was that it slowly and inevitably corrupted its wearer, regardless of any intentions to the contrary. Whether this was specifically designed into the Ring's magic or was simply an artefact of its evil origins is unknown. (Sauron might be expected to endow his One Ring with such a property, but he probably never intended anyone besides himself to wear it. It may be a side-effect of the portion of Sauron's will that lies within the Ring, influencing the wearer.) Part of its essential deceit was filling minds with imaginations of supreme power<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> For this reason, the Wise, including [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md), [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md) and [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md), refused to wield it in their own defence, but instead determined that it must be destroyed.

It appears that Hobbits, being more pure of heart than Men and far less powerful than Elves, were the ideal vessels to resist its seductive power; this explains why Frodo and Bilbo bore it for long periods of time with very little ill effect; while Sam wore it, his mind was filed with fantasies of him as a hero marching to the overthrow of Barad-dûr with a flaming sword and Gorgoroth turning into a garden with flower and trees that grew fruits, but he rejected them;<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> even Gollum had not turned into a Wraith after 500 years of bearing the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

Its power to lust was so great that it was impossible for anyone (even Sauron) to try to injure it<sup id="cite_ref-L131_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-5">[5]</a></sup> although unbreakable by itself.

The enigmatic [Tom Bombadil](Tom_Bombadil.md) was unaffected by the Ring, or rather, the Ring had no effect on him. This may be explained in many ways. (See the [article on Tom Bombadil](Tom_Bombadil%252FNature~74818.md), which includes some theories.)

While in possession of the Ring, Sam encountered two different groups of Orcs. Finding he could understand their speech, he speculated that this was made possible by the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> However, it is possible that both groups were using the [Common Tongue](../C/Common_Tongue.md).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

While writing the first drafts of *[A Long-expected Party](../A/A_Long-expected_Party.md)* (December [1937](../_/1937.md)), Tolkien already had decided to link the finding of the Ring with the mysterious figure of the "[Necromancer](../N/Necromancer.md)" of *The Hobbit*. In those drafts Bilbo would be unable to let go the Ring, so on a holiday leaves the Ring to his son, "[Bingo Baggins](../B/Bingo_Baggins.md)" as a parting gift, and then disappears. Bingo keeps the Ring hoping it will lead him to his father. Gandalf explains that they can "cheat" the Ring to guide them to Bilbo, if he also does the same, hence stages a "Farewell party".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

In order to counter the notion that *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* was inspired from <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II" title="wikipedia:World War II">World War II</a>, Tolkien himself provided a "what if" scenario in the Foreword to *[The Fellowship of the Ring](The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)* that shows what would happen should the Ring be used against [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md). Tolkien explained that if he had WWII in mind, then the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md) would enslave Sauron with the power of the Ring against him, and occupy [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md). [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md) (whose treachery would remain secret) would then use the Ring-lore found in Mordor to create a Great Ring of his own with which to challenge the self-styled Ruler of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien scholar [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) has at length discussed five different rings of invisibility which occur in works that predate Tolkien's:

- Plato's <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Gyges" title="wikipedia:Ring of Gyges">Ring of Gyges</a> (ca. 390 BC),
- the magic ring in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chr%C3%A9tien_de_Troyes" title="wikipedia:Chrétien de Troyes">Chrétien de Troyes</a>' <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvain,_the_Knight_of_the_Lion" title="wikipedia:Yvain, the Knight of the Lion">Yvain, the Knight of the Lion</a></i> (ca. 1177);
- <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelica_(character)" title="wikipedia:Angelica (character)">Angelica</a>'s ring, of French Renaissance literature;
- the Fairy's ring appearing in the tale "The Enchanted Ring" (by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_F%C3%A9nelon" title="wikipedia:François Fénelon">François Fénelon</a>) in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lang" title="wikipedia:Andrew Lang">Andrew Lang</a>'s <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lang%27s_Fairy_Books" title="wikipedia:Andrew Lang&#39;s Fairy Books">The Green Fairy Book</a></i> (a collection of fairy-tales referred to by Tolkien in his [Andrew Lang lecture](../O/On_Fairy-Stories.md));<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[note 1]</a></sup>
- the witch-maiden's ring in an Estonian folktale (ca. 1866) from the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevipoeg" title="wikipedia:Kalevipoeg">Kalevipoeg</a></i>, translated as "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dragon_of_the_North" title="wikipedia:The Dragon of the North">The Dragon of the North</a>" in <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lang%27s_Fairy_Books" title="wikipedia:Andrew Lang&#39;s Fairy Books">The Yellow Fairy Book</a></i> (1894).

Rateliff's proposes that the ring most likely to have provided an inspiration for Tolkien is a version of Chrétien's tale, namely Owein's ring in the [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md) <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Welsh_Romances#Owain.2C_or_the_Lady_of_the_Fountain" title="wikipedia:Three Welsh Romances">Owain, or the Lady of the Fountain</a></i>. He also regards the rings in Fénelon's and Plato's stories as possible influences, but concludes that: "*the primary influence on Frodo's ring is in fact* The Hobbit *itself: here, as so often, Tolkien is his own main source*".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup>

It has also been suggested that the One Ring may have been inspired by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Silvianus" title="wikipedia:Ring of Silvianus">Ring of Silvianus</a> and its inscribed curse. The hypothesis is based on Tolkien gaining knowledge about the Ring of Silvianus through the archaeologist <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortimer_Wheeler" title="wikipedia:Mortimer Wheeler">Mortimer Wheeler</a>, for whom Tolkien wrote the text "[The Name 'Nodens'](The_Name_'Nodens'.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup> However, Tolkien scholars [Wayne G. Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) have pointed out that there is no "*evidence, or good reason to believe, that Tolkien was inspired by the Roman ring*".<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

<b><a href="../_/1978.md" title="1978">1978</a>: <a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the scene in <a href="../B/Bag_End.md" title="Bag End">Bag End</a>, <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> asks <a href="../F/Frodo_Baggins.md" title="Frodo Baggins">Frodo</a> whether he can see any markings on the Ring and Frodo confirms it is a plain ring lacking any. Gandalf throws it in the fire and recites the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../R/Ring-verse.md" title="Ring-verse">Ring-verse</a> when it is taken out but the markings are never shown or explained.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/1977.md" title="1977">1977</a>: <i><a href="The_Hobbit_(1977_film).md" title="The Hobbit (1977 film)">The Hobbit</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In accordance with the novel, <a href="../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md" title="Bilbo Baggins">Bilbo</a> finds the One Ring in <a href="../G/Gollum.md" title="Gollum">Gollum</a>&#39;s cave. However, in a departure from the book, <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> reveals that he knows of Bilbo&#39;s ownership of the Ring and that it is indeed the One, thus setting the scene for an adaptation of <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></i>. For some reason, a loud &#34;vwoop!&#34; sound is heard whenever the Ring is put on.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/1980.md" title="1980">1980</a>: <i><a href="The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film).md" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)">The Return of the King</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The One Ring is unchanged from its appearance in the Rankin/Bass adaptation of <i><a href="The_Hobbit.md" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a></i>. In the <a href="../M/Mount_Doom_(chapter).md" title="Mount Doom (chapter)">Mount Doom scene</a>, oddly enough, the Ring itself can be seen even when <a href="../F/Frodo_Baggins.md" title="Frodo Baggins">Frodo</a> is invisible, allowing it to be easily located by Gollum with the result of him biting it off of Frodo&#39;s finger almost immediately.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2001.md" title="2001">2001</a>-<a href="../_/2003.md" title="2003">3</a>: <a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The One Ring is voiced by <a href="../A/Alan_Howard.md" title="Alan Howard">Alan Howard</a>. It is first mentioned in the Prologue narrated by <a href="../G/Galadriel.md" title="Galadriel">Galadriel</a> as the Ring that would rule all of the other <a href="../R/Rings_of_Power.md" title="Rings of Power">Rings of Power</a>, and is portrayed, in accordance with the book, as a simple gold band which gains <a class="mw-redirect" href="../R/Ring_Verse.md" title="Ring Verse">fiery letters</a> on the surface when exposed to fire (these letters being identical to those illustrated in the book). In the sequence of the <a href="../S/Siege_of_Barad-dûr.md" title="Siege of Barad-dûr">Siege of Barad-dûr</a>, Sauron channels the power of the Ring into his mace, sending many <a class="mw-redirect" href="../L/Last_Alliance.md" title="Last Alliance">Last Alliance</a> soldiers flying with every swing. It seems to have the ability to grow or shrink to fit the finger of whomever its master is, a trait touched upon in the novel, and is able to speak, albeit unintelligibly, into the mind of its bearer.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[27]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2011.md" title="2011">2011</a>: <i><a href="The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_War_in_the_North.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The One Ring is shown in the introduction of the game. In <a href="The_Prancing_Pony.md" title="The Prancing Pony">the Prancing Pony</a> <a href="../A/Aragorn.md" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> refers to Frodo and the Ring as a &#34;Hobbit with an important burden&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2012.md" title="2012">2012</a>-<a href="../_/2014.md" title="2014">14</a>: <a href="The_Hobbit_(film_series).md" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The One Ring is first seen by Bilbo when it falls out of Gollum&#39;s pocket while the former is strangling a small <a class="mw-redirect" href="../G/Goblin.md" title="Goblin">Goblin</a>. It is consistent in its appearance in the 2001-3 film series as a simple gold band. While wearing it, Bilbo gained the ability to understand the speech of the <a href="../S/Spiders.md" title="Spiders">spiders</a> of Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup></dd></dl>
