<i><b>The Shaping of Middle-earth</b></i>, published in [1986](/wiki/1986), is the fourth volume of [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien)'s 12-volume book series, *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)*, in which he analyzes the unpublished manuscripts of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

## Overview {#Overview}

In *The Shaping of Middle-earth* the gradual transition from the "primitive" [legendaria](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") of *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)* to what would become *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* is described, and it contains a text which could be seen as the first "Silmarillion" - the "Sketch of the Mythology".

Three other parts are the *Ambarkanta* or "Shape of the World", a collection of maps and diagrams of the world described by Tolkien; and the Annals of [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) and [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand), chronological works which started out as timelines but gradually turned into full narratives.

## Contents {#Contents}

- Preface

- I. "[Prose Fragments Following the Lost Tales](/wiki/Prose_Fragments_Following_the_Lost_Tales)" — brief, uncompleted texts which continue on from *The Book of Lost Tales*

- II. "[The Earliest 'Silmarillion'](/wiki/The_Earliest_%27Silmarillion%27)" — or, the "Sketch of the Mythology", this is the start of the *Silmarillion* proper

- III. "[The Quenta](/wiki/The_Quenta)" — *Quenta Noldorinwa*, a further developed version of that which appears in the "Sketch"
    - Appendix 1: Ælfwine's translation of the *Quenta* into Old English
    - Appendix 2: [The Horns of Ylmir](/wiki/The_Horns_of_Ylmir)

- IV. "[The First 'Silmarillion' Map](/wiki/The_First_%27Silmarillion%27_Map)" — a reproduction of the first map of Beleriand

- V. "[The Ambarkanta](/wiki/The_Ambarkanta)" — cosmological essays, maps, and diagrams

- VI. "[The Earliest *Annals of Valinor*](/wiki/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Valinor "The Earliest Annals of Valinor")"
    - Appendix: Ælfwine's translation of the *Annals of Valinor* into Old English

- VII. "[The Earliest *Annals of Beleriand*](/wiki/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Beleriand "The Earliest Annals of Beleriand")"
    - Second version of the earliest *Annals*
    - Appendix: Ælfwine's translation of the *Annals of Beleriand* into Old English

- Index

The *Annals of Valinor* and *Annals of Beleriand* show the earliest outline of the chronology of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) as envisaged in the early 1930s. The next volume in the series, *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](/wiki/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings)* (1987), outlined revisions to both texts made c. 1937 as "The Later Annals of Valinor" and "The Later Annals of Beleriand".

## Inscriptions {#Inscriptions}

There is an inscription in the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) characters in the first pages of every *History of Middle-earth* volume, written by Christopher Tolkien and describing the contents of the book. The inscription in Book IV reads:

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>Herein are the Quenta Noldorinwa, the History of the Gnomes, the Ambarkanta or Shape of the World by <a href="/wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)" title="Rúmil (elf of Tirion)">Rúmil</a>, the Annals of Valinor, and the Annals of Beleriand by <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Pengolod" title="Pengolod">Pengolod</a>, the Wise of Gondolin with maps of the world in the Elder Days and translations made by <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfwine" title="Ælfwine">Ælfwine the Mariner</a> of England into the tongue of his own land.</i>&#34;</dd></dl>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The fourth volume that contains the early myths and legends which led to the writing of Tolkien’s epic tale of war, The Silmarillion.<br/>
In this book, the shaping of the chronological and geographical structure of the legends of Middle-earth and Valinor is spread before us.<br/>
We are introduced to the hitherto unknown <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Ambarkanta" title="Ambarkanta">Ambarkanta</a> or &#39;Shape of the World&#39;, the only account ever given of the nature of the imagined <a href="/wiki/E%C3%A4" title="Eä">Universe</a>, accompanied by maps and diagrams of the world before and after the cataclysms of The War of the Gods and the <a href="/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor" title="Downfall of Númenor">Downfall of Númenor</a>. The first map of <a href="/wiki/Beleriand" title="Beleriand">Beleriand</a> is also reproduced and discussed.<br/>
In <a href="/wiki/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Valinor" title="The Earliest Annals of Valinor">The Annals of Valinor</a> and <a href="/wiki/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Beleriand" title="The Earliest Annals of Beleriand">The Annals of Beleriand</a> we are shown how the chronology of the <a href="/wiki/First_Age" title="First Age">First Age</a> was moulded; and the tale is told of <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfwine" title="Ælfwine">Ælfwine</a>, the Englishman who voyaged into the <a href="/wiki/Aman" title="Aman">True West</a> and came to <a href="/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa" title="Tol Eressëa">Tol Eressëa</a>, the Lonely Isle, where he learned the ancient history of <a href="/wiki/Elves" title="Elves">Elves</a> and <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a>.<br/>
Also included are the original &#39;The Silmarillion&#39; of 1926, and the Quenta Noldorinwa of 1930 - the only version of the myths and legends of the First Age that J.R.R. Tolkien completed to their end.
</p>
</blockquote>
