**Tighfield** was a village or town located somewhere in [the Shire](The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> It was the home of several families, specifically [Gamwich](../G/Gamwich_Family~66369.md) (later [Gammidge](../G/Gammidge_Family~66368.md)) and [Roper families](../R/Roper_family.md).

[Wiseman Gamwich](../W/Wiseman_Gamwich.md) came to Tighfield about two hundred years before the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), and Wiseman's son [Hob Gammidge](../H/Hob_Gammidge.md) is recorded as a roper, one of a long line of Tighfield rope-makers.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

Hob's son [Hobson](../H/Hobson.md), and in turn his son [Andwise](../A/Andwise_Roper.md), were said to have practised their craft in a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ropewalk" title="wikipedia:ropewalk">rope-walk</a> (that is, a rope-maker's yard) for many years.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

Hob's grandson was [Hamfast Gamgee](../G/Gaffer_Gamgee.md), Sam's father, who left Tighfield for [Hobbiton](../H/Hobbiton.md), to start a new life as a gardener.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Tighfield's name is associated with the tradition of rope-making there and contains an old word for "rope" (see English "tie", whose spelling was assimilated to verb "tie"). It was the site of a rope-maker's yard, also called "rope-walk" because the ropes were stretched out in long lines over trestles at intervals.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[4]</a></sup>

[Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) notes the modern place-name elements *tigh, teigh, tye, tey* which are unrelated; they mean "an enclosed piece of land" and do not occur as the first element in a compound.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[4]</a></sup>

Tolkien also noted that the term "rope-walk" in the text has confused several of his translators, to the point of imagining rope bridges.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[4]</a></sup>
