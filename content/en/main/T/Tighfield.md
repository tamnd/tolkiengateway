**Tighfield** was a village or town located somewhere in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> It was the home of several families, specifically [Gamwich](/wiki/Gamwich_Family "Gamwich Family") (later [Gammidge](/wiki/Gammidge_Family "Gammidge Family")) and [Roper families](/wiki/Roper_family "Roper family").

[Wiseman Gamwich](/wiki/Wiseman_Gamwich) came to Tighfield about two hundred years before the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), and Wiseman's son [Hob Gammidge](/wiki/Hob_Gammidge) is recorded as a roper, one of a long line of Tighfield rope-makers.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

Hob's son [Hobson](/wiki/Hobson), and in turn his son [Andwise](/wiki/Andwise_Roper "Andwise Roper"), were said to have practised their craft in a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ropewalk" title="wikipedia:ropewalk">rope-walk</a> (that is, a rope-maker's yard) for many years.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

Hob's grandson was [Hamfast Gamgee](/wiki/Gaffer_Gamgee "Gaffer Gamgee"), Sam's father, who left Tighfield for [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton), to start a new life as a gardener.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Tighfield's name is associated with the tradition of rope-making there and contains an old word for "rope" (see English "tie", whose spelling was assimilated to verb "tie"). It was the site of a rope-maker's yard, also called "rope-walk" because the ropes were stretched out in long lines over trestles at intervals.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[4]</a></sup>

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") notes the modern place-name elements *tigh, teigh, tye, tey* which are unrelated; they mean "an enclosed piece of land" and do not occur as the first element in a compound.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[4]</a></sup>

Tolkien also noted that the term "rope-walk" in the text has confused several of his translators, to the point of imagining rope bridges.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[4]</a></sup>
