**The Foundations of Stone** is the first scene of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers](The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Two_Towers.md)* and [*The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers* (extended edition)](<The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Two_Towers_(extended_edition).md>).

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

The film begins with an aerial view of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md). We then see a recap of [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md)'s confrontation with the [Balrog](../D/Durin's_Bane.md) in the scene "[The Bridge of Khazad-dûm](<The_Bridge_of_Khazad-dûm_(scene).md>)" from [*The Fellowship of the Ring*](The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md). Gandalf tells the Balrog it cannot pass, then breaks the [bridge](../B/Bridge_of_Khazad-dûm.md), sending it falling into the abyss. However, as it falls, it swings its whip upwards and pulls Gandalf over the edge.

Gandalf and the Balrog fall together, fighting, into a seemingly bottomless chasm, until finally they plunge into water. At that moment, the scene switches to [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) and [Sam](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) in the [Emyn Muil](../E/Emyn_Muil.md), where Frodo jerks awake, calling Gandalf's name. Reassuring Sam that nothing is wrong, just a dream, Frodo lies down again.

## Differences {#Differences}

The confrontation at the Bridge of Khazad-dûm follows the account in the chapter [The Bridge of Khazad-dûm](The_Bridge_of_Khazad-dûm.md) from *[The Fellowship of the Ring](The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)* fairly closely. Gandalf's fall into the abyss with the Balrog reflects the beginning of Gandalf's account in the chapter [The White Rider](The_White_Rider.md) from *[The Two Towers](The_Two_Towers.md)*: "*Long I fell, and he fell with me. His fire was about me. I was burned. Then we plunged into the deep water and all was dark*."<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The main deviation from the source material is presenting this fall separately from the [Battle of the Peak](../B/Battle_of_the_Peak.md), which will be shown later.

Frodo does not dream about Gandalf's fight with the Balrog in the book. However, in *The Fellowship of the Ring*, he has what appears to be a visionary dream about Gandalf's imprisonment in [Orthanc](../O/Orthanc.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
