**The Calendars** is the fourth chapter of *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*, in which [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) discussed the early texts of what would become [Appendix D](/wiki/Appendix_D) to *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. Christopher identified two versions of the text which he labeled D1 and D2, and stated that they were written in parallel with the two versions of the Appendix on Languages, F1 and F2. He believed that all four texts (F1, F2, D1, and D2) were written before the summer of 1950 and says that D1 was associated with an envelope postmarked August, 1949.

D1 was a brief, rough manuscript while D2 was a fair copy that was made not long after the composition of D1. However, the two texts differ significantly and so both were included in this chapter.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:119</sup>

The earlier text, D1, has an Eldarin calendar that is unrelated to the one in the published Appendix D. In D1 the Elves counted off in centuries with years 366 days in length, with subtractions made at regular intervals to bring the calendar into alignment with the astronomical year. The Númenórean system was much closer to the [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") calendar in this text than in Appendix D.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:124</sup>

In D2 the Eldarin system was altered but still does not match the details of Appendix D. The Númenórean system saw greater changes in the transition to D2, but again varies from Appendix D.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:131</sup> D2 did see the addition of the revised system under the [Stewards](/wiki/Stewards) that was not adopted by the Hobbits. D2 made no reference to the new calendar introduced after the fall of [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr) while D1 did have a brief note to that effect.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:132</sup>

The calendar used by [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age) is described next, again not in the final form published in Appendix D. Since the beginning of the year was substantially shifted in the new system, [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") created a table that clearly showed the correspondence of dates between the new system, the [Shire Reckoning](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning), and the Steward's Reckoning.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:134</sup>

Christopher then stated that the "elegantly balanced structure" of D2 was dropped and replaced by the text of Appendix D.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:135</sup> He found no further texts after D2 before the typescript of Appendix D, which he described as "rough and a good deal emended". He believed that his father was still in the process of developing the Calendars when the crush to submit the text became urgent, and had Tolkien been able to finish his ideas Appendix D would have been "markedly different".<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:136</sup>
