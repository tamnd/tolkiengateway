<i><b>The Lord of the Rings: Aragorn&#39;s Quest</b></i> is a third-person, action adventure video game in which the player fights through [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) as [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn). The game was developed for the Nintendo DS, PlayStation Portable, PlayStation 2 and PlayStation 3 by [TT Fusion](/wiki/TT_Fusion), and for the Wii by [Headstrong Games](/wiki/Headstrong_Games). It was released on [14](/wiki/14_September "14 September") [September](/wiki/September), [2010](/wiki/2010).

*Aragorn's Quest* is the first officially-licensed game to be released by [Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Interactive_Entertainment), who took over the license from [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts) to publish *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*-related games.

<br>

## Plot {#Plot}

Set in the early [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age), 15 years after the destruction of the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring) and downfall of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) waits for the return of [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) to the [Shire](/wiki/Shire) for a great party. As the [Mayor](/wiki/Mayor_of_Michel_Delving "Mayor of Michel Delving"), Sam is in charge of arranging the festivities for King Elessar's arrival; in the mean-time, he regales his children with tales of Aragorn's exploits. (Cf. [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and [Arwen](/wiki/Arwen) meeting [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") at [Brandywine Bridge](/wiki/Brandywine_Bridge) in [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [15](/wiki/Fourth_Age_15 "Fourth Age 15").)

Sam's son, [Frodo Gamgee](/wiki/Frodo_Gardner "Frodo Gardner"), runs around [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) completing tasks, whilst also re-living the Aragorn's adventures (as retold, not always perfectly accurately) by Sam.

## Gameplay {#Gameplay}

The player controls [Frodo Gamgee](/wiki/Frodo_Gardner "Frodo Gardner") in the hub world of the [Shire](/wiki/Shire), where he can talk to people and perform simple tasks. When sitting down with his father, the player can control Aragorn in a fairy tale-like setting. Wii players can also control [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) as a second character.

Aragorn will wield a sword, a shield, a bow and spears, and can mount a horse.

### Controls {#Controls}

The controls on the Wii have been compared with those of Nintendo's <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Twilight_Princess" title="wikipedia:The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess">The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Kotaku_Review_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kotaku_Review-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-1UP_Review_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1UP_Review-2">[2]</a></sup> with the analogue-stick being using to control the on-screen character, with swiping of the Wii Remote up, down or to the side makes Aragorn perform similar on-screen actions; the D-pad switches weapon (to bow-and-arrow and flaming torch, for example), and the Z-button locks on, and C looks around. The controls for the PlayStation 3 - which only works with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Move" title="wikipedia:PlayStation Move">PlayStation Move</a> - is much the same as those for the Wii.

The Nintendo DS version of the game uses rudimentary touch-screen controls (such as tracing a rune in order to open doors).<sup id="cite_ref-IGN_Review_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IGN_Review-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Multiplayer {#Multiplayer}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/7/73/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg/200px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/7/73/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg/300px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/7/73/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg/400px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Orcs.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="786"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> fighting <a href="/wiki/Orcs" title="Orcs">orcs</a></figcaption></figure>

At any point (on the Wii and PlayStation 3), a second player can drop into the game as [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), and work co-operatively in order to complete the tasks. Gandalf wields his staff and can utilise powerful magic to defeat enemies, such as hitting enemies with lightning and shooting firebolts, as well as the ability to heal, and use his sword; Gandalf's magic is recharged with orbs from dead enemies.

When in the [Shire](/wiki/Shire), a second player can also play as [Elanor](/wiki/Elanor_Gardner "Elanor Gardner") (dressed as Gandalf) to help Frodo Gamgee complete the mini-quests in Hobbiton; the second player has the same magical abilities as Gandalf in the Aragorn quests.

## History and development {#History_and_development}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/f/f5/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg/200px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/f/f5/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg/300px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/f/f5/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg/400px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Troll3.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="1180"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> fighting a <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Troll" title="Troll">troll</a></figcaption></figure>

In [May](/wiki/May) [2009](/wiki/2009), it was announced that [Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Interactive_Entertainment) had taken over from [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts) and entered into partnership with the [Saul Zaentz Company](/wiki/Middle-earth_Enterprises "Middle-earth Enterprises") and [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema) (itself a subsidiary of [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.)) to produce video games based on *The Lord of the Rings* [books](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Lord of the Rings") and [films](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").<sup id="cite_ref-Voodoo_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Voodoo-10">[10]</a></sup> The game's title was initially leaked at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Entertainment_Expo" title="wikipedia:Electronic Entertainment Expo">Electronic Entertainment Expo</a> (more commonly known as "E3"), before being confirmed a week later by Warner Bros., "*accessible gameplay set in a friendly, colorful rendition of Middle-Earth. Players assume the role of Strider and embark on his greatest quests from The Lord of the Rings trilogy.*"<sup id="cite_ref-Softpedia_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Softpedia-11">[11]</a></sup> The press release promised a more family-friendly experience in which the player relives [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn)'s adventures, as retold by [Mayor](/wiki/Mayor_of_Michel_Delving "Mayor of Michel Delving") [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee), available on the Wii, Nintendo DS, PlayStation 2 and PlayStation Portable in the Fall 2009.<sup id="cite_ref-Shack_News_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shack_News-12">[12]</a></sup> Kotaku were luke-warm to the announcement of "*a kids' game*".<sup id="cite_ref-Kotaku_kids_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kotaku_kids-13">[13]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The Lord of the Rings franchise is world renowned for its captivating adventures, and we are creating new experiences in the game space designed to appeal to a wide array of consumer tastes from core to casual... The Lord of the Rings: Aragorn&#39;s Quest is the first of many different types of games we will be developing with the film and literary rights. It is also the first game allowing kids and their families to interact in their favorite locations and stories from The Lord of the Rings with accessible gameplay.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Martin Tremblay, President of <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Warner_Bros._Interactive_Entertainment" title="Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment">Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment</a><sup id="cite_ref-IGN_Announce_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IGN_Announce-14">[14]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

In August 2009, Warner Bros. announced that *Aragorn's Quest* had been delayed until Spring 2010, but no reason was given for the delay.<sup id="cite_ref-Shacknews_Delay_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shacknews_Delay-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Kotaku_Delay_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kotaku_Delay-16">[16]</a></sup> Come Spring 2010, however, no release date had been announced; on [10](/wiki/10_March "10 March") [March](/wiki/March) 2010 Warner Bros. informed <a class="external text" href="http://www.kotaku.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Kotaku</a> that *Aragorn's Quest* would be ported to the PlayStation 3, supporting PlayStation Move. Kotaku speculated that this why the game was being delayed, again, until Fall 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-Kotaku_PS3_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kotaku_PS3-17">[17]</a></sup>

*The Lord of the Rings: Aragorn's Quest* has received generally mixed reviews, with reviewers praising the sound quality and accessibility, but ultimately believing that the easiness of the game and the visuals hold it back from achieving the sort of sword-fighting *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* game that many would like to see. There is a general feeling amongst reviewers that *Aragorn's Quest* was an opportunity missed to make a truly great *The Lord of the Rings* action adventure hack and slash game.

<a class="external text" href="http://www.1up.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">1UP</a> compared the controls of *Aragorn's Quest* to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo" title="wikipedia:Nintendo">Nintendo</a>'s <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Twilight_Princess" title="wikipedia:The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess">Twilight Princess</a>, praising the varied gameplay and the game's accessibility, but complaining that the game's side-quests were too repetitive, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii" title="wikipedia:Wii">Wii</a> controls were too simplistic and "*a bit iffy*", with the experience of the two-player co-op mode being ruined by lag.<sup id="cite_ref-1UP_Review_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1UP_Review-2">[2]</a></sup> Similarly, <a class="external text" href="http://www.gametrailers.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">GameTrailers</a> said "*The bottom line is that combat is not fun, and enemies are typically a cakewalk.*" and that the Wii controls were a nuisance, as was the lack of camera control; they also thought that the voice acting was "*awkward*" (although the soundtrack "*fits right in*") and that the game looks "*drab*".<sup id="cite_ref-GameTrailers_Review_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GameTrailers_Review-25">[25]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>the greatest strength of Aragorn&#39;s Quest comes not from its diverting but unspectacular combat, but the way it so accurately captures the atmosphere of the locations in the films and the resulting pleasure that comes from adventuring in these memorable Middle-earth locales.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a class="external text" href="http://uk.gamespot.com/wii/adventure/lordoftheringsaragornsquest/review.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">GameSpot Review</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg/200px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg/300px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/3/3b/Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg/400px-Aragorn%27s_Quest_-_Edoras.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="786"/></a><figcaption>An array of characters</figcaption></figure>

However, <a class="external text" href="http://www.gamespot.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">GameSpot</a>, in a more positive review, said, "*The environments impress with their variety, attention to detail, and the way they so faithfully reflect the visual style of the films.*" but that *Aragorn's Quest* lacks challenge, with the Wii controls being "*imprecise*"; they wish that more attention-to-detail had been paid to enemies.<sup id="cite_ref-GameSpot_Review_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GameSpot_Review-24">[24]</a></sup> <a class="external text" href="http://www.ign.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">IGN</a> thought the Wii version was far superior to the PlayStation 3 version of the game, but still suggested this was a game for just [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) aficionados as only they would be able to overlook the game's flaws; IGN also complained about the mixed visual style, but loved reliving moments from [the films](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").<sup id="cite_ref-IGN_Review_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IGN_Review-3">[3]</a></sup>

<a class="external text" href="http://reviews.cnet.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">CNET</a> describes the storytelling as "*charming*"; they also found the motion-controls on the Wii "*frustrating*" but felt it didn't matter because defeating the "*very easy*" enemies was still "*satisfying*". CNET also said the ending of the game was an "*anti-climax*", but praised the environments: "*The environments impress with their variety, attention to detail, and the way they so faithfully reflect the visual style of the films.*<sup id="cite_ref-CNET_Review_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CNET_Review-21">[21]</a></sup> Finally, <a class="external text" href="http://www.kotaku.com" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Kotaku</a> loved the game's film-based adventure, "*attractive*" visuals, variety of characters and film-based soundtrack, but absolutely hated the easiness of the game, claiming that one person could play the two-player co-op mode.<sup id="cite_ref-Kotaku_Review_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kotaku_Review-1">[1]</a></sup>

**In Short:** reviewers enjoyed the graphical style and musical score, faithfully recreating the feel and atmosphere of the films (although some thought the mixed style was confused, and some thought the attention-to-detail was inconsistent), but felt that the Wii controls were frustratingly inaccurate and that enemies were far too easy. Though aimed primarily at families and younger gamers, reviewers were keen to point out that anyone could enjoy it, but it would appeal primarily film-fans who would be able to over-look the game's faults.
