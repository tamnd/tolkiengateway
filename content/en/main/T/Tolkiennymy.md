**Tolkiennymy** is a term coined by [Mark T. Hooker](../M/Mark_T._Hooker.md). It refers to the lexicological study of place and person's names derived from first world languages.

## Overview {#Overview}

As a sub section of <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tolkien_linguistics&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tolkien linguistics (page does not exist)">Tolkien linguistics</a> in general, Hooker says that the word *Tolkiennymy* "describes a branch of Tolkienian Linguistics which studies the lexicology of place and personal names in Tolkien’s Legendarium derived from, and often intended to be perceived as names in first-world languages. It focuses on the etymology, history, and lore of real-world names used in Tolkien’s works, because as Tolkien noted, the Shire map is 'based on some acquaintance with English toponymical history' (L.250)."<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

One of the most important texts is Tolkien's own *[Nomenclature](../G/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.

One of the goals of *Tolkiennymy* is to provide readers with an acquaintance with the history of English toponyms used in Tolkien's works.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word is formed on an analogy of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy" title="wikipedia:Toponymy">toponymy</a></i>, the lexicological study of place names.
