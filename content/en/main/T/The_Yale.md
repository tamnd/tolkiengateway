**The Yale** was a region of lowlands that lay in the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing) of the [Shire](/wiki/Shire) between the [Bridgefields](/wiki/Bridgefields) and the [Marish](/wiki/Marish). The road from [Tuckborough](/wiki/Tuckborough) emerged from the [Woody End](/wiki/Woody_End) and descended into the Yale, on its way to join the [Causeway](/wiki/Causeway) at [Stock](/wiki/Stock).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The Yale was of old the home of the [Boffins](/wiki/Boffin_Family "Boffin Family").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) has suggested that Yale represents an [Old Hobbitish](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") form \**Geal*, from [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C3%A2l" title="wikipedia:Iâl">iâl</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> This fits right in with Tolkien's comment to the Dutch Translator, [Max Schuchart](/wiki/Max_Schuchart), that there were "'[Celtic](/wiki/Celtic)' elements in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and [East-farthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing "Eastfarthing") names."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

*Yale* is also an English surname derived from a [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) place name meaning "fertile upland".<sup id="cite_ref-Mathomium_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mathomium-6">[6]</a></sup> The best known *"Yale"* in Britain was the *commote* (traditional medieval Welsh district) of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_(commote)" title="wikipedia:Yale (commote)">Yale</a> in Wales, the home of the medieval <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Powys" title="wikipedia:Kingdom of Powys">kings of Powys</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mathomium_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mathomium-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The Yale appeared in the text, as well as in the *[A Part of the Shire](/wiki/A_Part_of_the_Shire)* map only after the second edition ([1966](/wiki/1966)); Tolkien himself had added the name on the map of his own copy of the (first edition) *Fellowship of the Ring*. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes that in printings of the map the name is near a square mark, suggesting that the Yale is a settlement, possibly a misunderstanding.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 1]</a></sup>
