**The Seafarer** is an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) poem recorded in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_Book" title="wikipedia:Exeter Book">Exeter Book</a>, one of the four surviving manuscripts of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English_literature" title="wikipedia:Old English literature">Old English poetry</a>.

Together with [E.V. Gordon](/wiki/E.V._Gordon), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) prepared an edition of the poem. The work was revised and completed by [Ida Gordon](/wiki/Ida_Gordon) and published in [1960](/wiki/1960) (see *[The Seafarer](</wiki/The_Seafarer_(I.L._Gordon)> "The Seafarer (I.L. Gordon)")*).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Tolkien's poem '<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Mona%C3%B0_Modes_Lust_mid_Mereflode&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Monað Modes Lust mid Mereflode (page does not exist)">Monað Modes Lust mid Mereflode</a>', appearing in the "[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)" and "[The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers)", is derived from *The Seafarer*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
