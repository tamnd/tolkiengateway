<i><b>The World of Tolkien: Mythological Sources of The Lord of the Rings</b></i> is a book writen by [David Day](/wiki/David_Day), first published in [2003](/wiki/2003).

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Go on a fascinating journey through the history of <a href="/wiki/Middle-earth" title="Middle-earth">Middle-earth</a>!
</p><p><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a> is commonly regarded as a work of fantasy. Yet <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">Tolkien</a> himself saw his work as a body of myth with an inherent veracity at its core, not an invention, but a recovered truth. In the Middle-earth of The Lord of the Rings, Tolkien created not an imaginary world, but an imaginary history of our own world.
</p><p>The World of Tolkien draws out the analogies within The Lord of the Rings that render this epic a timeless mythology for the modern age. Here is the most comprehensive guide to uncovering the &#34;real-world&#34; inspiration behind the gods and demigods, races of men, elves and dwarves, wizards and hobbits, creatures and monsters, cities, geography, battles, and major events in the history of Middle-earth. Essential reading for Tolkien enthusiasts of all generations.
</p><p>Stunningly illustrated with 65 color and 35 black and white illustrations.
</p>
</blockquote>
