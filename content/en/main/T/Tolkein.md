A lot of people misspell "[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")" as **Tolkein**. This was already the case in Tolkien's time; several passages in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien)* show Tolkien's frustration about the matter.

## History {#History}

The first recorded use of *Tolkein* stems from [1901](/wiki/1901). The *Blue Book* of [King Edward's School](/wiki/King_Edward%27s_School) in [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) for January of that year includes in the list of pupils: "\[*Name.*\] Tolkein … … \[*Initials.*\] J.R.R. \[*Class.*\] xi \[*Date of Birth.*\] 3.1.92 \[*Came.*\] 00/3 \[=third quarter of 1900\] \[then *Placed.* in class\] xi \[*Parent's Initials.*\] Mrs. \[*Address.*\] 214, Alcester Road \[*Business.*\] Independent".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [1922](/wiki/1922), [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford) bookseller <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Blackwell" title="wikipedia:Basil Blackwell">Basil Blackwell</a> in *[Fifty New Poems for Children: An Anthology Selected from Books Recently Published](/wiki/Fifty_New_Poems_for_Children "Fifty New Poems for Children")* included the poem *[Goblin Feet](/wiki/Goblin_Feet)*, which he ascribed to a J.R.R. Tolkein. The name appears in the table of contents, above the poem and in the index.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Later, the name was used, according to Tolkien, by college, bank, and lawyer's clerks,<sup id="cite_ref-L324_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L324-3">[3]</a></sup> and more or less all who wrote to him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Origin {#Origin}

The name *Tolkien* has often been associated with Jewry. This was in turn because the Jewry of the time had predominantly Yiddish and German names, and the Tolkien family hailed from Saxony, Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-L324_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L324-3">[3]</a></sup> Since these Yiddish and German names often ended in *-stein*, it may have been a hypercorrection.

## Other notable occurences {#Other_notable_occurences}

- The Index of *[Pictures under Discussion](/wiki/Pictures_under_Discussion)* has "Tolkein, J.R.R.".
- One of the songs of the [Brobdingnagian Bards](/wiki/Brobdingnagian_Bards) was titled "<a class="external text" href="http://www.thebards.net/music/lyrics/Tolkien_Lord_of_the_Rings.shtml" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tolkein (The Hobbit &amp; The Lord of the Rings)</a>", but it has been renamed since.
- The manual of the videogame <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonsphere" title="wikipedia:Dragonsphere">Dragonsphere</a></i> credits the works of "Tolkein" as inspiration of the game designers.
- Early prints of the stamp *The Hobbit*, issued by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mail" title="wikipedia:Royal Mail">Royal Mail</a> in [1998](/wiki/1998) for the set *Magical Worlds: Classical Fantasy Books for Children*, featured the name "Tolkein".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
- Early copies of a reissue of [Lin Carter](/wiki/Lin_Carter)'s *[Tolkien: A Look Behind The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/Tolkien:_A_Look_Behind_The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* in [2003](/wiki/2003) featured the word "Tolkein" on the cover. It has been corrected since.
- A plaque in [Pembroke College](/wiki/University_of_Oxford#Pembroke_College "University of Oxford") commemorates a donation from the [Saul Zaentz Trust](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz "Saul Zaentz") in honour of "J.R.R. Tolkein".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
