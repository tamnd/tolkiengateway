<i><b>The Four Loves</b></i> is a book by [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis) which explores the nature of love from a Christian perspective through thought-experiments and examples from literature. In a passage in the chapter on friendship ("Philia - Friendship"), Lewis invokes his friends [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) ("Ronald") and [Charles Williams](/wiki/Charles_Williams) ("Charles") as examples:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395753">.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider{margin-top:1rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem;color:var(--tg-color-subtle);display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:space-between}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider:not(:empty){gap:1rem;white-space:nowrap}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::before,.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::after{content:"";height:1px;width:100%;background-color:var(--tg-border-color-base)}</style><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>In each of my friends there is something that only some other friend can fully bring out. By myself I am not large enough to call the whole man into activity; I want other lights than my own to show all his facets. Now that Charles is dead, I shall never again see Ronald&#39;s reaction to a specifically Caroline joke. Far from having more of Ronald, having him &#39;to myself&#39; now that Charles is away, I have less of Ronald. ... We possess each friend not less but more as the number of those with whom we share him increases.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>C.S. Lewis</cite></p>
