<i><b>The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</b></i>, originally marketed as <i><b>The Lord of the Rings on Prime</b></i>, is an online television series based on the writings of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), namely *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* and its [Appendices](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Appendices "The Lord of the Rings Appendices"). It is created and produced by [Amazon Studios](/wiki/Amazon_Studios) working alongside the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate), the [Tolkien Trust](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Trust "The Tolkien Trust"), [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins) and [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema). Set in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth),<sup id="cite_ref-AMSA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AMSA-1">[1]</a></sup> it explores new storylines preceding J.R.R. Tolkien's *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring).*<sup id="cite_ref-AMNZ_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AMNZ-2">[2]</a></sup> The story, which in Tolkien's writings spanned thousands of years, is condensed into a single point in time to avoid time jumps in the interest of narrative cohesion.<sup id="cite_ref-VanityFair_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VanityFair-3">[3]</a></sup>

The first two episodes were released on [1 September](/wiki/1_September) of [2022](/wiki/2022),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> although they were originally scheduled to be released on [2 September](/wiki/2_September) of the same year, the anniversary of J.R.R. Tolkien's death.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Premise {#Premise}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Amazon Studios&#39; forthcoming series brings to screens for the very first time the heroic legends of the fabled Second Age of Middle-earth&#39;s history. This epic drama is set thousands of years before the events of J.R.R. Tolkien’s The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, and will take viewers back to an era in which great powers were forged, kingdoms rose to glory and fell to ruin, unlikely heroes were tested, hope hung by the finest of threads, and the greatest villain that ever flowed from Tolkien&#39;s pen threatened to cover all the world in darkness. Beginning in a time of relative peace, the series follows an ensemble cast of characters, both familiar and new, as they confront the long-feared re-emergence of evil to Middle-earth. From the darkest depths of the Misty Mountains, to the majestic forests of the elf-capital of Lindon, to the breathtaking island kingdom of Númenor, to the furthest reaches of the map, these kingdoms and characters will carve out legacies that live on long after they are gone.
</p>

## Source material and copyrights {#Source_material_and_copyrights}

The showrunners said that they wished to "come up with the novel Tolkien never wrote and do it as the mega-event series that could only happen now".<sup id="cite_ref-VanityFair_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VanityFair-3">[3]</a></sup> They explained that they only have the copyrights to adapt *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* along with its [Appendices](/wiki/Appendices), without the rights to *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)* or other books that contain the vast majority of Tolkien's writings on the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-VFQuestions_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VFQuestions-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Production {#Production}

### Development {#Development}

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Rings_of_Power_(TV_series)_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/b/b2/The_Rings_of_Power_%28TV_series%29_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg/300px-The_Rings_of_Power_%28TV_series%29_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="220" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/b/b2/The_Rings_of_Power_%28TV_series%29_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg/450px-The_Rings_of_Power_%28TV_series%29_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/b/b2/The_Rings_of_Power_%28TV_series%29_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg/600px-The_Rings_of_Power_%28TV_series%29_-_Middle-earth_map_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="8200" data-file-height="6000"/></a><figcaption>Fourth map of Middle-earth, released by Amazon on 6 March 2019</figcaption></figure>

Although the television rights were optioned when Tolkien sold the film rights in 1969, producer [Saul Zaentz](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz) deferred on the option fee deadline in 1983 which meant the option reverted back to Tolkien's Estate. Only after settling a lawsuit with the producers of [*The Hobbit*](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)> "The Hobbit (film series)") film trilogy in July 2017 had the Estate (still under Tolkien's son [Christopher](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien "Christopher Tolkien")) showed any willingness to shop the television rights. HBO had proposed to adapt the novels anew for television, which the Estate was not interested in, while Netflix pitched multiple connected series focusing on characters such as Aragorn and Gandalf which "completely freaked out the estate". Amazon did not pitch a specific story but promised to work closely with the estate to "protect Tolkien's legacy", which the estate felt they were unable to do with previous adaptations.

Amazon had wanted to produce the series themselves, but came to appreciate that it would be difficult to "start from scratch", thereby bringing New Line Cinema into the negotiations. Amazon's executive producer at the time, Sharon Tal Yaguado, pushed towards a partnership, including having Peter Jackson board as executive producer. Jackson's agent had already made an approach, but Peter had prior commitment and later said the arrangement "wasn't the way I wanted to work." They had already started looking for potential showrunners, each of whom would pitch their idea for a show: The Russo brothers pitched a drama about Aragorn, an idea previously explored by Netflix and Warners, while newcomers JD Payne and Patrick McKay proposed adapting the events of the Second Age.

On [3 November](/wiki/3_November) [2017](/wiki/2017), it was reported that Amazon Studios were in discussion with Warner Bros. Television and the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate) about developing a series based on *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">[63]</a></sup> Later, in November, Amazon Studios announced they have secured the rights to develop a multi-season television series set in Middle-earth with the potential for spin-offs.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">[64]</a></sup> The rights have been said to have cost close to $250 million.<sup id="cite_ref-DLAZ_65-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DLAZ-65">[65]</a></sup> Amazon Studios would cooperate in the production with the Tolkien Estate, [The Tolkien Trust](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Trust), [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins) and [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema).<sup id="cite_ref-DLAZ_65-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DLAZ-65">[65]</a></sup>

On [13 February](/wiki/13_February) [2019](/wiki/2019), Amazon's then newly created <a class="external text" href="http://twitter.com/LOTRonPrime" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Twitter account</a> associated with the show posted the quote "I wisely started with a map.", taken from Tolkien's [Letter 144](/wiki/Letter_144).<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">[66]</a></sup> On [15 February](/wiki/15_February), the Twitter account released a map of Middle-earth along with a line of the [Ring-verse](/wiki/Ring-verse), over the next few weeks different versions of the map were released with each successive one including more labels than the previous as well as more lines from the verse. On [7 March](/wiki/7_March), the final map was released which showed a wider view of Middle-earth, including the island of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) and the final post "Welcome to the Second Age".<sup id="cite_ref-AMSA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AMSA-1">[1]</a></sup> [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) and artist [John Howe](/wiki/John_Howe) had worked on the accuracy of the maps, however dedicated [fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") had detected several mistakes and anachronisms.<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">[67]</a></sup> The fact the series were going to be set in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) was predicted by [Tolkien Society](/wiki/Tolkien_Society) Chair [Shaun Gunner](/wiki/Shaun_Gunner) in February.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">[68]</a></sup>

After Yaguado's exit in May 2019, New Line Cinema became a "minor stakeholder" in the series, resulting in the show having to "keep distinct" from their productions. On [27 July](/wiki/27_July) 2019, a video was published that introduced the creative team; [J.D. Payne](/wiki/J.D._Payne) and [Patrick McKay](/wiki/Patrick_McKay) were announced as the showrunners, the writing team consisting of Gennifer Hutchison, Helen Shang, Jason Cahill, Glenise Mullins, Justin Dohle, Stephany Folsom and Bryan Cogman, Belén Atienza, Bruce Richmond, Gene Kelly and Lindsey Weber as executive producers, Ron Ames as co-producer, Kate Hawley as costume designer, Rick Heinrichs as production designer and Jason Smith as visual effects supervisor. The team will also include Tolkien scholar [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) and artist [John Howe](/wiki/John_Howe). [Bayona](/wiki/J._A._Bayona "J. A. Bayona") will direct the first two episodes.<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">[69]</a></sup> In an interview with the German Tolkien Society, scholar [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) stated that events from the First and Third Ages are "off-limits".<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">[70]</a></sup>

On [17 September](/wiki/17_September) 2019, Amazon Studios announced that the series first season would be filmed in New Zealand.<sup id="cite_ref-AMNZ_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AMNZ-2">[2]</a></sup>On [18 November](/wiki/18_November) 2019, Amazon Studios Head Jennifer Salke announced that the series has been renewed for a second season.<sup id="cite_ref-71" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-71">[71]</a></sup> Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, production of the series first season was suspended.<sup id="cite_ref-72" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-72">[72]</a></sup> On 9 May 2020 the New Zealand Film Commission has allowed the production of film and TV to continue in the country.<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">[73]</a></sup> In December 2020, [Bryan Cogman](/wiki/Bryan_Cogman) left the series for Entertainment One, after completing work on the scripts for the first season.<sup id="cite_ref-74" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-74">[74]</a></sup>

On [24 March](/wiki/24_March) [2021](/wiki/2021), Amazon Studios announced that [Wayne Che Yip](/wiki/Wayne_Che_Yip) had joined the creative team, as both a co-executive producer and director.<sup id="cite_ref-75" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-75">[75]</a></sup> In May 2021, it was announced that [Charlotte Brändström](/wiki/Charlotte_Br%C3%A4ndstr%C3%B6m) would direct another two episodes.<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">[76]</a></sup> In September 2021, it was reported that [Howard Shore](/wiki/Howard_Shore) and [Bear McCreary](/wiki/Bear_McCreary) were in talks to compose the soundtrack for the series.<sup id="cite_ref-77" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-77">[77]</a></sup> In July 2022, it was officially announced their hiring, with McCreary announced to be composing the score and Shore to be writing the main title theme.<sup id="cite_ref-78" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-78">[78]</a></sup>

On [3 October](/wiki/3_October) 2022, production for the second season of the series begun in the United Kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-79" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-79">[79]</a></sup>

### Casting {#Casting}

On 22 July 2019, it was reported that Amazon has made its first cast for the new series that Markella Kavenagh was in talks to play a character named "Tyra".<sup id="cite_ref-80" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-80">[80]</a></sup> Will Poulter was cast for an unknown role in September 2019<sup id="cite_ref-81" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-81">[81]</a></sup> however in December it was reported that he left the series due to scheduling conflicts.<sup id="cite_ref-82" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-82">[82]</a></sup> This was later confirmed by Poulter in an interview.<sup id="cite_ref-83" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-83">[83]</a></sup> He was replaced by Robert Aramayo who was reported to be playing a character named "Beldor".<sup id="cite_ref-84" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-84">[84]</a></sup>

### Pre production {#Pre_production}

Design for the show began in 2018 at Santa Monica "with production designer Rick Heinrichs and a small pool of local artists" and continued in <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Auckland&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Auckland (page does not exist)">Auckland</a> New Zealand starting June of that year. As mentioned, the producers did not want to "start from scratch" but couldn't infringe on New Line Cinema's copyright by reproducing designs and other elements used in their films. John Howe and other artists that had worked on Guillermo del Toro's *The Hobbit* like Wayne Barlowe and Eduardo Pena were already involved in the initial sessions: indeed, one of the show's producers was del Toro's producer Callum Greene.

Setting the production in New Zealand helped in drawing additional talent from *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* film productions. The overalp has been summarized as comprising "Roughly half of the visual effects department, as well as least two thirds of the art department, half of the property department, at least a third of the camera department, almost all the costume department, at least half of the hair and makeup department, most of the stunt department and most of the special effects department. There were also small but significant contributions made to the acting, storyboarding, casting, production, sound, assistant directors, locations and music departments."

In August 2019, it was decided that the designs drawn under Heinrichs "just didn't work" and he was replaced with Ramsey Avery. Pushed for time and with some of the budget already used up by Heinrichs, Avery had to produce easily-identifiable cultures across Middle-earth: Elves (from Lindon, Eregion and the Silvan garrison at the Southlands), Men (both from Tirharad and Numenor), Dwarves, Hobbits and Orcs. The Elves were given designs inspired by *art nouveau* but also be Pre-Raphaelite paintings: Lindon, in particular, was made into a golden wood embodying Ramsey's view of the setting as the "Golden age" of Elvendom in Middle-earth.

Much of this work paraphrased work done on the film productions: Khazad dum is done in the geometric style that Peter Jackson established for Moria and Erebor, although with a less chiseled look: the realm was further modelled on local caves in New Zealand so that it should feature subterannean waterfalls and vegetation, which was envisioned as providing sustenance for the Dwarves. For Numenor, a more eastern aesthetic was opted for, although elements like the sails on the Numenorean ships recall the film production: Ramsey based the latter off Aragorn's crown.

in particular, Weta Workshop had produced versions of Narsil and Aeglos as close to the film props as New Line Cinema would legally allow. For Durin's Bane, both Wayne Barlowe and Weta's Nick Keller proposed divergent designs, but the showrunners steered them towards a design that would "end up fairly close" to the film model. By contrast, although designs like the Elven Rings and the Snow Troll were made by Jasmine Watson and John Howe, respectively, they vary significantly from those seen in the film productions.

### Filming {#Filming}

#### Season 1 {#Season_1_2}

Although pre-production on the series reportedly began in filming began on [14 March](/wiki/14_March) [2020](/wiki/2020)<sup id="cite_ref-94" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-94">[94]</a></sup> in Auckland primarily at Kumeu Film Studios and Auckland Film Studios.<sup id="cite_ref-95" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-95">[95]</a></sup> Location filming took place around Auckland in February.<sup id="cite_ref-96" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-96">[96]</a></sup> Filming for the first two episodes was expected to continue through May,<sup id="cite_ref-97" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-97">[97]</a></sup> with a four or five-month production break then planned during which footage for the two episodes would be reviewed and writing on the second season would begin.<sup id="cite_ref-98" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-98">[98]</a></sup> Production was scheduled to resume in mid-October and continue until late June 2021.<sup id="cite_ref-99" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-99">[99]</a></sup> However, filming was placed on hold indefinitely in mid-March 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, with around 800 cast and crew members told to stay home.<sup id="cite_ref-100" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-100">[100]</a></sup> In early May, the majority of filming for the first two episodes was confirmed to have been completed before the COVID-19 shutdown. Filming on the series was allowed to resume then under new safety guidelines from the New Zealand government, but, instead of completing filming for the first two episodes at that time, the filming shutdown segued into the intended production break, with filming for the first two episodes set to be completed once filming on further episodes was ready to begin.<sup id="cite_ref-101" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-101">[101]</a></sup>

It was reported that the TV series was one of seven film and television productions that were granted exemptions to allow cast and crew members to enter New Zealand while its borders were closed to non-New Zealanders due to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19" title="wikipedia:COVID-19">COVID-19</a>. The exemptions were granted before [June 18](/wiki/June_18) by Economic Development Minister Phil Twyford, and applied to 93 members of the production as well as 20 family members. Around 10 percent of the series' crew were believed to be non-[New Zealanders](/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"), and many of them had remained in the country during its pandemic lockdown and did not require exemptions. Pre-production on further episodes began by July [2020](/wiki/2020),<sup id="cite_ref-102" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-102">[102]</a></sup> and filming on the series resumed on [September 28](/wiki/September_28).<sup id="cite_ref-103" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-103">[103]</a></sup> Bayona completed filming on his episodes by December 23,<sup id="cite_ref-104" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-104">[104]</a></sup> with production on further episodes set to begin in January 2021 following a two week break for Christmas.<sup id="cite_ref-105" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-105">[105]</a></sup> Yip confirmed that he had begun filming his episodes by March,<sup id="cite_ref-106" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-106">[106]</a></sup> and Brändström was in New Zealand for production on the series when she was announced as director in May.<sup id="cite_ref-107" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-107">[107]</a></sup>

#### Season 2 {#Season_2_2}

### Post Production {#Post_Production}

Amazon provided unlimited cloud-based storage for the production, allowing all technical data and footage to be accessible to anyone working on the series around the world. It was the first production to be completely cloud-based, which became crucial during the COVID-19 pandemic. The primary visual effects vendors were Industrial Light &amp; Magic (ILM), including the overall supervisor Jason Smith, and Wētā FX. The latter, who was the main vendor on the films, also provided the on set support and initial pipeline work. Other studios included Method Visual Effects (later subsumed into one of the show's other vendors, DNEG) and Rising Sun Pictures. WetaFX and Rising Sun dropped out of the series after season one.

Music was provided by Bear McCreary, with additional contributions by Howard Shore (who composed the theme for the main titles) and by David Long and the New Zealand ensemble Plan 9: together they composed most of the singing that takes place on the screen in the first season. Again given the copyright situation, McCreary was prohibited from quoting the themes Shore had composed for the films, but as with the production design decided to imitate some of Shore's timbral choices.

## Reception {#Reception}

The first season of the series received mixed reviews,<sup id="cite_ref-117" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-117">[117]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-118" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-118">[118]</a></sup> with generally positive critical and generally negative audience scores on review aggregation websites.<sup id="cite_ref-119" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-119">[119]</a></sup> Most viewers did not finish the show, particularly in the USA, with a domestic completion rate of 37%, while the rest of the world had a 45% completion rate.<sup id="cite_ref-120" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-120">[120]</a></sup> The series also proved controversial in many corners of Tolkien fandom with people questioning its fidelity to the source material and the depiction of certain characters.
