<i><b>The Darkening of Mirkwood</b></i> is a supplement for *[The One Ring](<The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game).md>)* role-playing game. A version for *[Adventures in Middle-earth](../A/Adventures_in_Middle-earth.md)*, titled <i><b>Mirkwood Campaign</b></i>, was also released.

## Official description {#Official_description}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The Necromancer may have been cast out of Dol Guldur, but a lingering darkness remains over Mirkwood, a shadow that will grow ever longer as the years draw on – unless a fellowship of heroes step forward and hold back the gloom.
</p><p><i>The Darkening of Mirkwood</i> is a complete campaign for <i>The One Ring</i>, set in Mirkwood over the course of three decades. It allows you to tell your own epic saga, following your heroes in their quest as the tale of years unfolds before them.
</p><p>This supplement includes enough adventure material to keep you playing for months or even years, as well as new rules that give your heroes a real stake in what happens to the world around them. Rules for Holdings allow them to carve out their own corner of Middle-earth, whilst new options for the Fellowship Phase and Undertakings to achieve allow them to chart their own path.
</p><p>Visit the Parliament of Spiders, do battle with the Nazgûl, meddle in the affairs of Wizards and enter the Halls of King Thranduil. Stand firm against the Shadow and maybe the Darkening of Mirkwood can be averted. Falter for even a moment and all that you know and love will be lost.
</p>
</blockquote>

## Reception {#Reception}

In a review for [Other Minds 17](../O/Other_Minds_17.md), Thomas Morwinsky found *The Darkening of Mirkwood* “a really impressive piece of work, \[…\] exemplary on how you can build an entire campaign on just one of Tolkien’s many frameworks”, while noting a handful of minor lore issues. The first is the “body hopping” of a villainous [Maia](../M/Maia.md) werewolf, which he felt was treated as something easily achievable, and thus contradicted what Tolkien wrote on the subject. The other is the appearance of a mysterious Hunter, considered by Morwinsky to be quite clearly intended to be interpreted as [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md), which would conflict with Tolkien’s portrayal of the Valar and Maiar refraining from direct intervention in Middle-earth after the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-otherminds_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otherminds-2">[2]</a></sup>
