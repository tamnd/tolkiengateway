**The Early History of the Legend** is the first chapter of the section, "Part One: The Fall of Númenor and the Lost Road", of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](/wiki/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings)*. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) does not provide a copy of any original writings, but explains how his father conceived of both *[The Fall of Númenor](</wiki/The_Fall_of_N%C3%BAmenor_(chapter)> "The Fall of Númenor (chapter)")* and *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)*.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) and [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis), unsatisfied with the stories they read, picked two themes and decided to write stories themselves:<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:7</sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>We agreed that he should try &#39;space travel&#39;, and I should try &#39;time-travel&#39;. His result is well known. My effort, after a few promising chapters, ran dry: it was too long a way round to what I really wanted to make, a new version of the <a href="/wiki/Atlantis-haunting" title="Atlantis-haunting">Atlantis</a> legend. The final scene survives as <i><a href="/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth" title="Akallabêth">The Downfall of Númenor</a></i>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Letter_294" title="Letter 294">Letter 294</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Lewis's result was *[Out of the Silent Planet](/wiki/Out_of_the_Silent_Planet)*, but Tolkien's abandoned effort was *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)*. From [a letter](/wiki/Letter_257 "Letter 257") written a few years before, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) suspects that his father's story was already conceived in connection with "The Silmarillion",<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:10</sup> as Tolkien seems to imply that there was an agreement with Lewis that his version of Atlantis "*was to be called Númenor*". [Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter) confirms this,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> but Christopher concludes that both the time-travel story and the Númenórean legend were probably written at the same time.<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:9</sup>

In any case, Tolkien explains that he began the story of a father and son travelling back through the different phases of the history, eventually reaching the time of the Downfall of Númenor. But soon he lost interest in the main time-travel plot, and became more interested in Númenor itself.<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:8</sup> Therefore, he abandoned the story, but the Downfall remained in his [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") and was later developed as the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age).

It is unknown the exact date of the initial conversation of Tolkien and Lewis, although we know that Lewis finished by the autumn of [1937](/wiki/1937) and Tolkien submitted *The Lost Road* (unfinished) to [Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin) on [15 November](/wiki/15_November) of the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
