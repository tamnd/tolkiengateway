**The Early History of the Legend** is the first chapter of the section, "Part One: The Fall of Númenor and the Lost Road", of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings.md)*. [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) does not provide a copy of any original writings, but explains how his father conceived of both *[The Fall of Númenor](<The_Fall_of_Númenor_(chapter).md>)* and *[The Lost Road](The_Lost_Road.md)*.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) and [C.S. Lewis](../C/C.S._Lewis.md), unsatisfied with the stories they read, picked two themes and decided to write stories themselves:<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:7</sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>We agreed that he should try &#39;space travel&#39;, and I should try &#39;time-travel&#39;. His result is well known. My effort, after a few promising chapters, ran dry: it was too long a way round to what I really wanted to make, a new version of the <a href="../A/Atlantis-haunting.md" title="Atlantis-haunting">Atlantis</a> legend. The final scene survives as <i><a href="../A/Akallabêth.md" title="Akallabêth">The Downfall of Númenor</a></i>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../L/Letter_294.md" title="Letter 294">Letter 294</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

Lewis's result was *[Out of the Silent Planet](../O/Out_of_the_Silent_Planet.md)*, but Tolkien's abandoned effort was *[The Lost Road](The_Lost_Road.md)*. From [a letter](../L/Letter_257.md) written a few years before, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) suspects that his father's story was already conceived in connection with "The Silmarillion",<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:10</sup> as Tolkien seems to imply that there was an agreement with Lewis that his version of Atlantis "*was to be called Númenor*". [Humphrey Carpenter](../H/Humphrey_Carpenter.md) confirms this,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> but Christopher concludes that both the time-travel story and the Númenórean legend were probably written at the same time.<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:9</sup>

In any case, Tolkien explains that he began the story of a father and son travelling back through the different phases of the history, eventually reaching the time of the Downfall of Númenor. But soon he lost interest in the main time-travel plot, and became more interested in Númenor itself.<sup id="cite_ref-Early_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Early-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:8</sup> Therefore, he abandoned the story, but the Downfall remained in his [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) and was later developed as the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md).

It is unknown the exact date of the initial conversation of Tolkien and Lewis, although we know that Lewis finished by the autumn of [1937](../_/1937.md) and Tolkien submitted *The Lost Road* (unfinished) to [Allen &amp; Unwin](../A/Allen_&_Unwin.md) on [15 November](../_/15_November.md) of the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
