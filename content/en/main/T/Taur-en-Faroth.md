**Taur-en-Faroth** was the range of wooded hills in [West Beleriand](/wiki/West_Beleriand) that rose up above the western side of the River [Narog](/wiki/Narog).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> They were part of the Realm of [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond).

The hills themselves were the western end of the [Andram](/wiki/Andram). It was among these hills that the stream of [Ringwil](/wiki/Ringwil) rose and cascaded down into the main river.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> In their steep eastern sides above the Narog, there was a network of caverns.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-3">[3]</a></sup>

In ancient times, these Caverns of Narog, known as *[Nulukkizdîn](/wiki/Nulukkizd%C3%AEn)*, had been home to [Petty-dwarves](/wiki/Petty-dwarves) who dwelt there in secret<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> before the arrival of [Finrod](/wiki/Finrod). What became of the Petty-Dwarves at this time is not recorded, but at least some escaped to dwell in [Amon Rûdh](/wiki/Amon_R%C3%BBdh).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name is translated in full as "Forest of the Hunters", from [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[taur](/wiki/Taur "Taur")* ("forest"), *[en](/wiki/En "En")* ("of") and <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Faroth&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Faroth (page does not exist)">faroth</a></i> (a word related to hunting).<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-6">[6]</a></sup> It was also referred to as "the hunters’ wold";<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> the word "<a class="external text" href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/wold#Middle_English" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">wold</a>", in Middle English, derives from a high land covered in forest.

These uplands were also called *High Faroth*<sup id="cite_ref-Return_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> or *Hills of the Hunters* in the *[Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)* an early [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) name *Taur-na-Faras* is given, translated as "Hill of the Hunters, Hunters' Wold".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

It was also named *Hills of the Hunters* or *Duil Rewinion* (these two indications appear on the [The First 'Silmarillion' Map](/wiki/The_First_%27Silmarillion%27_Map)<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>), or *Hunters' Wold*<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>.
