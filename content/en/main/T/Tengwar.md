The <i><b>Tengwar</b></i> (pron. \[<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">ˈteŋɡʷar</span>\], [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) for “letters”, singular *tengwa* \[<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">ˈteŋɡʷa</span>\], [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) *tîw*<sup id="cite_ref-App|E2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App|E2-1">[1]</a></sup>) was a writing system invented by [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) (and was therefore also called the **Fëanorian alphabet**<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> or **script**<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>). It was used for a variety of languages among the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md) and became perhaps the most prominent writing system of [Arda](../A/Arda.md), used by a variety of [Races](../../portal/C/Characters.md) through the [Ages](../A/Ages.md).

## History {#History}

[Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) invented the Tengwar in [Y.T.](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) [1250](../Y/Year_of_the_Trees_1250.md) of the [First Age of Arda](../F/First_Age.md), strongly influenced by the [Sarati](../S/Sarati.md) of [Rúmil the Loremaster](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Tirion).md>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ApD_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-5">[5]</a></sup> Fëanor both constructed the Tengwar as a general phonetic alphabet and devised special arrangements to fit the characteristics of all languages of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Over the course of the First Age, three different modes of Tengwar emerged: [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md), [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md), and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md). Sindarin is written in one of two modes, the mode of Beleriand, or the mode of Gondor (also known as <a class="external text" href="http://www.tecendil.com/tengwar-handbook/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Omatehtar</a> Mode).

### Classical Quenya mode {#Classical_Quenya_mode}

Unlike Rúmil, Fëanor considered vowels as independent sounds and not just “colours” of the consonants, so he devised the “full writing” ([Quanta Sarmë](../Q/Quanta_Sarmë.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-ApD_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-5">[5]</a></sup>

However, Fëanor also used a more 'conservative' system which seemed to have proved far more popular; he also held Rúmil's idea of syllabic analysis of the words by the Sarati and made use of [tehtar](Tehtar.md) instead of the full letters.<sup id="cite_ref-ApD_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-5">[5]</a></sup>

The classical Quenya mode of the Tengwar may have originated from Fëanor's own arrangement. There is no direct evidence for this hypothesis, but the use of the Quenya names for the individual letters hints to a primate of the Quenya mode. Another hint to a very old age of the classical Quenya mode is that it uses the [Calmatéma](../C/Calmatéma.md) as a k-series, like the mode of Beleriand which we can assume to have originated in the First Age.

### Mode of Beleriand {#Mode_of_Beleriand}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a class="mw-redirect" href="../M/Mode_of_Beleriand.md" title="Mode of Beleriand">Mode of Beleriand</a></b></div></div>

When the [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md) rebelled and came to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), they adapted their writing for the new languages they learned. Quanta Sarmë was used for languages where the tehtar were not useful<sup id="cite_ref-ApD_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-5">[5]</a></sup> which could have been the origin for the Mode of Beleriand.

It is also possible that the Tengwar influenced the evolution of the [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md) of [Daeron](../D/Daeron.md) in Beleriand, mainly in their shape and arrangement.

We know from the inscription on the [West-gate of Moria](../W/West-gate_of_Moria.md) that a Tengwar mode called the [Mode of Beleriand](../M/Mode_of_Beleriand.md) was used in [Eregion](../E/Eregion.md). This name allows for the assumption that the same mode was used already in the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).

At the end of the [Third Age](Third_Age.md), the Elves of [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) probably still used that mode, since a transcription of the Rivendell [aerlinn](../A/Aerlinn.md) *[A Elbereth Gilthoniel](../A/A_Elbereth_Gilthoniel.md)* features it.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Frodo, however, even though literate in Sindarin, was apparently unable to read this mode.

### General use {#General_use}

By the end of the Third Age, there was a *general use* that could be used for a variety of languages including Quenya, Sindarin and the Common Speech.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> This use could have evolved during the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) in [Eregion](../E/Eregion.md) or even in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md).

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/dc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="89" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/dc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png/450px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/dc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png/600px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_One_Ring_inscription.png 2x" data-file-width="2035" data-file-height="605"/></a><figcaption>The <a href="../R/Ring-inscription.md" title="Ring-inscription">Ring-inscription</a> on <a href="The_One_Ring.md" title="The One Ring">the One Ring</a>.</figcaption></figure>

The same mode appears on the [Ring-inscription](../R/Ring-inscription.md)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> that the [Númenorean](../N/Númenorean.md) [Isildur](../I/Isildur.md) observed it to be "fashioned in an elven-script of Eregion".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

It is not certain whether the whole *mode* itself was from Eregion or simply just the *script* was of an Elvish fashion. Some [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) words use this mode.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

Another mode that was based on the "general use" but used full letters for the representation of vowels was used in the Northern part of the [Westron](../W/Westron.md)-speaking regions, perhaps by influence of the Elves of [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md). This might explain why Frodo was unable to read either the Mode of Beleriand or the Ring Inscription.

## Spelling and pronunciation {#Spelling_and_pronunciation}

### Structure {#Structure}

The most notable characteristic of the Tengwar script is that the shapes of the letters correspond to the features of the sounds they represent.

The shape of the Tengwar uniformly consisted of two elements, the *[telco](Telco.md)* (stem) to which is attached a *[lúva](../L/Lúva.md)* (bow). It is noticeable that some of the letters of the [Sarati](../S/Sarati.md) resembled the telco/lúva shape seen on the Tengwar. Therefore it is possible that those particular letters influenced stylistically the Tengwar.

The telco could be normal, raised, shortened or heightened. The lúva would be single or doubled, and these could be open or closed.

All the above combinations can create 32 to 40 different shapes of letters. These shapes mirrored phonological significances: the basic form of a tengwa was used for the [patakar](../P/Patakar.md), the voiceless stops; telcor determined how the sound was articulated, and the lúvar where in the mouth it was made:

- Doubling the bow turns the voiceless consonant into a voiced one.
- Raising the stem above the line turns it into the corresponding fricative or a corresponding voiced version of it.
- Shortening it (so it is only the height of the bow) indicates the corresponding nasal or, mostly, the approximants.

According to their shape, the 32 different glyphs could be arranged and presented consistently on a table. The principal letters are divided into series (*témar*) that correspond to the main places of articulation and into six rows (*tyeller*) that correspond to the main manners of articulation. Both vary among modes.

By the end of the [Third Age](Third_Age.md), the Tengwar were somehow standardized. Their Quenya names became standard for all modes, and less used ones were not included (although still used), such as those of the extended stems and the [Tyelpetéma](Tyelpetéma.md). The table displayed 36 letters: the 24 standard Tengwar, plus 12 of the additional Tengwar.

## Other modes {#Other_modes}

Just as with any alphabetic writing system, every specific language written in Tengwar requires a specific orthography, depending on the phonology of that language. These Tengwar orthographies are usually called *modes*. All of them use as a basis the "<a class="mw-selflink-fragment" href="#Structure">theoretical values</a>" table above, corresponding the letters to the phonemes of each language's phonology, and even drop out the characters that would be useless.

All the modes can be divided into two large categories:

- Some represent vowels with diacritics called **[tehtar](Tehtar.md)** or <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=%C3%93matehtar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ómatehtar (page does not exist)">ómatehtar</a></i> ("vowel tehtar"), like the [Quenya mode](../Q/Quenya_mode.md).
- The "full writing" modes represent vowels by normal letters, such as the [Quanta Sarmë](../Q/Quanta_Sarmë.md) or the [Mode of Beleriand](../M/Mode_of_Beleriand.md).

Furthermore, some modes map the basic consonants to <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/t/</span>, <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/p/</span>, <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/k/</span>, and <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/kʷ/</span>, while others (generally [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md)) use them to represent <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/t/</span>, <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/p/</span>, <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/tʃ/</span>, and <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">/k/</span>.

## External history {#External_history}

The [Sarati](../S/Sarati.md), a script developed by Tolkien in the late 1910s that is described in <i><a href="../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_13.md" title="Parma Eldalamberon 13"><i>Parma Eldalamberon</i> 13</a></i>, anticipates many features of the Tengwar, especially the vowel representation by diacritics (which is found in many Tengwar varieties), different Tengwar shapes and a few correspondances between sound features and letter shape features (though inconsistent).

Even closer to the Tengwar is the [Valmaric script](../V/Valmaric_script.md), described in [*Parma Eldalamberon* 14](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_14.md), which Tolkien used from about 1922 to 1925. It features many Tengwar shapes and the inherent vowel <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[a]</span> found in some Tengwar varieties. The tables in the samples V12 and V13 show an arrangement that is very similar to the one of the primary Tengwar in the classical Quenya "mode".

The Tengwar were probably developed in the late 1920s or in the early 1930s. *The Lonely Mountain Jar Inscription*, the first published Tengwar sample, dates to 1937 (*[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)*, most editions). The full explanation of the Tengwar was published in Appendix E of *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* in 1955.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tengwar is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Featural_writing_system" title="wikipedia:Featural writing system">featural alphabet</a>, meaning there is correspondence between letter shape features and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/place_of_articulation" title="wikipedia:place of articulation">place</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/manner_of_articulation" title="wikipedia:manner of articulation">manner of articulation</a>. Perhaps the oldest and most famous featural system is the Korean <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul" title="wikipedia:Hangul">Hangul alphabet</a>.

[Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) (*[An Introduction to Elvish](../A/An_Introduction_to_Elvish.md)*, <a class="internal mw-magiclink-isbn" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Special:BookSources/0905220102">ISBN 0-905220-10-2</a>) compared the Tengwar with the *Universal Alphabet* of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lodwick" title="wikipedia:Francis Lodwick">Francis Lodwick</a> of 1686, both on grounds of the feature factor, but also the actual letter shapes, and the use of vowels as diacritics.

Linguist <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Kury%C5%82owicz" title="wikipedia:Jerzy Kuryłowicz">Jerzy Kuryłowicz</a> immediately recognised the resemblance of an ornamental variety of the [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md) script (perhaps referring to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_script" title="wikipedia:Insular script">Insular script</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

It is not known whether Tolkien was aware of these previous scripts. However, considering the sarati and the valmaric script that he already invented in his youth, it is conceivable that Tolkien developed the idea of featural notation by himself. However, in [The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md), Tolkien described it as not an alphabet, but a "haphazard series of letters," as they each had its own value.<sup id="cite_ref-App|E2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App|E2-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is likewise not known whether the shapes of several Tengwar were inspired by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_script" title="wikipedia:Insular script">insular minuscule script</a> (used to write <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Anglo-saxon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Anglo-saxon (page does not exist)">Anglo-saxon</a>) or the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/blackletter" title="wikipedia:blackletter">gothic textura</a> and cursive scripts (used to write Middle English), with which Tolkien was familiar through reading medieval manuscripts in these languages in his professional capacity as a linguist.

## Tengwar study {#Tengwar_study}

### Modes {#Modes}

As the peoples of Middle-earth used various modes of Tengwar, and as Tolkien wrote English, [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) and [Norse](../N/Norse.md) in Tengwar, [fans](../F/Fandom.md) have created Tengwar modes for real languages. There are modes to write French, Swedish, Latin, Italian or Spanish.

### Encoding schemes {#Encoding_schemes}

#### Non-Unicode {#Non-Unicode}

The contemporary de facto standard in the Tengwar user community maps the Tengwar characters onto the regular English character encoding following the example of the <a class="external text" href="http://www.gis.net/~dansmith/fonts/font_tengwar/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tengwar typefaces</a> by [Dan Smith](../D/Dan_Smith.md). A drawback of the font solution is that, if no corresponding Tengwar font is installed, an awful string of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojibake" title="wikipedia:Mojibake">nonsense characters</a> appears.

Since there is not enough place for all the signs, certain signs are included in a "Tengwar A" font which also maps its characters on ISO 8859-1, overlapping with the first font.

For each Tengwar diacritic there are four different codepoints that are used depending on the width of the character which bears it.

Other Tengwar typefaces with Dan Smith's encoding include <a class="external text" href="http://home.student.uu.se/j/jowi4905/fonts/annatar.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Johan Winge&#39;s Tengwar Annatar</a>, <a class="external text" href="https://at.boktypografen.se/parmaite.htm" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Måns Björkman&#39;s Tengwar Parmaite</a>, <a class="external text" href="http://www.geocities.com/enrombell/Archivos.htm" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Enrique Mombello&#39;s Tengwar Élfica</a> or <a class="external text" href="http://www.limes.com.pl/~miszka/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Michal Nowakowski&#39;s Tengwar Formal</a> (note that most of these differ in details).

#### Unicode {#Unicode}

A proposal has been made by Michael Everson to include the Tengwar in the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Unicode&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Unicode (page does not exist)">Unicode</a> standard. The codepoints are subject to change; the range U+016080 to U+0160FF in the Supplementary Multilingual Plane is tentatively allocated for Tengwar according to the current <a class="external text" href="http://www.unicode.org/roadmaps/smp/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Unicode roadmap</a>.

Tengwar are currently included in the unofficial ConScript Unicode Registry, which assigns codepoints in the Private Use Area. Tengwar are mapped to the range U+E000 to U+E07F. The following Unicode sample (which repeats the one above) is meaningful when viewed under a typeface supporting Tengwar glyphs in the area defined in the ConScript Tengwar proposal. Some typefaces that support this proposal are <a class="external text" href="http://www.evertype.com/emono/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Everson Mono</a>, <a class="external text" href="http://freetengwar.sourceforge.net/tengtelc.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tengwar Telcontar</a>, <a class="external text" href="http://www.kreativekorp.com/software/fonts/constructium.shtml" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Constructium</a>, <a class="external text" href="http://freetengwar.sourceforge.net/formal.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tengwar Formal Unicode</a>, and <a class="external text" href="http://freetengwar.sourceforge.net/freemonotengwar.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">FreeMonoTengwar</a> (<a class="external text" href="http://www.code2000.net/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">James Kass</a>'s Code2000 and Code2001 use an older, incompatible version of the proposal).

### Uses outside the legendarium {#Uses_outside_the_legendarium}

Random (meaningless) Tengwar appears stylistically in a prop of an ancient tome in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Within_Temptation" title="wikipedia:Within Temptation">Within Temptation</a> music video for "Stand My Ground".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Tengwar also appears in the computer game <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis:_The_Lost_Tales" title="wikipedia:Atlantis: The Lost Tales">Atlantis: The Lost Tales</a></i> and the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alone_in_the_Dark" title="wikipedia:Alone in the Dark">Alone in the Dark</a></i> comic book. Tengwar in the episode *The Monster and the Willow* of the 2004 animated series <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winx_Club" title="wikipedia:Winx Club">Winx Club</a></i>, as well as the 2007 movie adaptation <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winx_Club:_The_Secret_of_the_Lost_Kingdom" title="wikipedia:Winx Club: The Secret of the Lost Kingdom">The Secret of the Lost Kingdom</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

Several enthusiasts, such as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_S._Raymond" title="wikipedia:Eric S. Raymond">Eric S. Raymond</a>, promote the usage of Tengwar for the constructed language <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lojban" title="wikipedia:Lojban">Lojban</a> as it is culturally neutral and contains some main Lojban morphology rules, making Lojban easier to learn when it is written with Tengwar.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>
