<i><b>The Battle of Maldon: together with The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth</b></i> is a book of writings by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), edited by [Peter Grybauskas](/wiki/Peter_Grybauskas). It was published on [30 March](/wiki/30_March) [2023](/wiki/2023) first in UK, then on [6 June](/wiki/6_June) in US.

The book presents Tolkien's prose translation of "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Maldon" title="wikipedia:The Battle of Maldon">The Battle of Maldon</a>", a 10-century [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) poem fragment about a real world battle, together with the poem *[The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm's Son](/wiki/The_Homecoming_of_Beorhtnoth_Beorhthelm%27s_Son)*, an imaginary sequel to the battle and frame narrative to the poem. Also included is a previously [unpublished](/wiki/Index:Unpublished_material "Index:Unpublished material") lecture "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Tradition_of_Versification_in_Old_English&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Tradition of Versification in Old English (page does not exist)">The Tradition of Versification in Old English</a>", which deals with the nature of poetic tradition.

It includes a colour frontispiece reproducing a page from the original manuscript of *The Homecoming*. The deluxe edition also comes with a CD of J.R.R. Tolkien reading *The Homecoming* with his son Christopher reading "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Beorhtnoth%27s_Death&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Beorhtnoth&#39;s Death (page does not exist)">Beorhtnoth&#39;s Death</a>" and "[Ofermod](/wiki/Ofermod)". The recording was previously distributed on classic cassettes in [1992](/wiki/1992), which is now rare, but has been remastered on CD.

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>First ever standalone edition of one of J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s most important poetic dramas, that explores timely themes such as the nature of heroism and chivalry during war, and which features unpublished and never-before-seen texts and drafts.
</p><p>In 991 AD, vikings attacked an Anglo-Saxon defence-force led by their duke, Beorhtnoth, resulting in brutal fighting along the banks of the river Blackwater, near Maldon in Essex. The attack is widely considered one of the defining conflicts of tenth-century England, due to it being immortalised in the poem, The Battle of Maldon.
<br/>Written shortly after the battle, the poem now survives only as a 325-line fragment, but its value to today is incalculable, not just as an heroic tale but in vividly expressing the lost language of our ancestors and celebrating ideals of loyalty and friendship.
<br/>J.R.R. Tolkien considered The Battle of Maldon &#39;the last surviving fragment of ancient English heroic minstrelsy&#39;. It would inspire him to compose, during the 1930s, his own dramatic verse-dialogue, The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm&#39;s Son, which imagines the aftermath of the great battle when two of Beorhtnoth’s retainers come to retrieve their duke’s body.
</p><p>Leading Tolkien scholar, Peter Grybauskas, presents for the very first time J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s own prose translation of The Battle of Maldon together with the definitive treatment of The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth and its accompanying essays; also included and never before published is Tolkien&#39;s bravura lecture, &#39;The Tradition of Versification in Old English&#39;, a wide-ranging essay on the nature of poetic tradition. Illuminated with insightful notes and commentary, he has produced a definitive critical edition of these works, and argues compellingly that, Beowulf excepted, The Battle of Maldon may well have been &#39;the Old English poem that most influenced Tolkien&#39;s fiction&#39;, most dramatically within the pages of The Lord of the Rings.
</p><p><i>This slipcased edition includes a colour frontispiece reproducing a page of Tolkien’s original manuscript of The Homecoming, and is printed on acid-free paper with a ribbon marker. It is quarterbound with a unique illustration by Bill Sanderson gold-foiled on grey boards and is housed in a custom-built slipcase. It also includes a digitally remastered recording of The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth read by J.R.R. Tolkien &amp; Christopher Tolkien, which is available on CD for the first time.</i>
</p>
</blockquote>
