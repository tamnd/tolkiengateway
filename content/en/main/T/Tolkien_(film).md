<i><b>Tolkien</b></i> is a [2019](/wiki/2019) biographical drama film that depictes [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s early life in [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) and [King Edward's School](/wiki/King_Edward%27s_School), as an undergraduate at [Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") and serving in the [Great War](/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"). Directed by [Dome Karukoski](/wiki/Dome_Karukoski) and written by [David Gleeson](/wiki/David_Gleeson) and [Stephen Beresford](/wiki/Stephen_Beresford), the film stars [Nicholas Hoult](/wiki/Nicholas_Hoult) as Tolkien, [Lily Collins](/wiki/Lily_Collins) as [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Bratt), [Colm Meaney](/wiki/Colm_Meaney) as [Father Francis Morgan](/wiki/Francis_Morgan "Francis Morgan"), and [Derek Jacobi](/wiki/Derek_Jacobi) as Professor [Joseph Wright](/wiki/Joseph_Wright). It was released by Fox Searchlight Pictures in the United Kingdom on [3 May](/wiki/3_May) 2019 and in the United States on [10 May](/wiki/10_May) 2019. The film received mixed reviews from critics and grossed $7 million worldwide on a $20 million budget.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

Beginning with the childhood of Tolkien in [Sarehole Mill](/wiki/Sarehole_Mill), the film covers the early years of his life from his birth until the end of the [Great War](/wiki/Great_War).

## Reception {#Reception}

Some weeks before its release, the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate) released a statement wishing to make clear that they '*"did not approve of, authorise or participate in the making of this film"*, and that they *"do not endorse it or its content in any way"*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The film received mixed reviews upon release, having a rating of 50% critic score and 71% audience score on review aggregator <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes" title="wikipedia:Rotten Tomatoes">Rotten Tomatoes</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and an average rating of 6.8/10 on <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb" title="wikipedia:IMDb">IMDb</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Among Tolkien scholars, it is agreed that the film is hardly historically accurate.<sup id="cite_ref-tolkcast_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkcast-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-doug_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doug-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-edu_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-edu-7">[7]</a></sup> [Douglas A. Anderson](/wiki/Douglas_A._Anderson) said that he hopes "*that film passes into its deserved oblivion quickly*".<sup id="cite_ref-doug_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doug-6">[6]</a></sup> [Eduardo Segura](/wiki/Eduardo_Segura) found it "*epidermic and easy to forget*".<sup id="cite_ref-edu_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-edu-7">[7]</a></sup> [Marcel Bülles](/wiki/Marcel_B%C3%BClles) highlighted the fact, that even though the film doesn't represent the chronology and events of Tolkien's life accurately, "*the mood, the atmosphere, it's just right*".<sup id="cite_ref-tolkcast_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkcast-5">[5]</a></sup> Upon seeing the film, [John Garth](/wiki/John_Garth) humorously remarked "*Dang, I have to rewrite [my book](/wiki/Tolkien_and_the_Great_War "Tolkien and the Great War") now*".<sup id="cite_ref-tolkcast_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkcast-5">[5]</a></sup>

Another criticism held at the film was the apparent absence of Tolkien's [christianity](/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), despite it impacting his work.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
