<i><b>Tolkien</b></i> is a [2019](../_/2019.md) biographical drama film that depictes [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s early life in [Birmingham](../B/Birmingham.md) and [King Edward's School](../K/King_Edward's_School.md), as an undergraduate at [Oxford](../U/University_of_Oxford.md) and serving in the [Great War](../W/World_War_I.md). Directed by [Dome Karukoski](../D/Dome_Karukoski.md) and written by [David Gleeson](../D/David_Gleeson.md) and [Stephen Beresford](../S/Stephen_Beresford.md), the film stars [Nicholas Hoult](../N/Nicholas_Hoult.md) as Tolkien, [Lily Collins](../L/Lily_Collins.md) as [Edith Bratt](../E/Edith_Bratt.md), [Colm Meaney](../C/Colm_Meaney.md) as [Father Francis Morgan](../F/Francis_Morgan.md), and [Derek Jacobi](../D/Derek_Jacobi.md) as Professor [Joseph Wright](../J/Joseph_Wright.md). It was released by Fox Searchlight Pictures in the United Kingdom on [3 May](../_/3_May.md) 2019 and in the United States on [10 May](../_/10_May.md) 2019. The film received mixed reviews from critics and grossed $7 million worldwide on a $20 million budget.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

Beginning with the childhood of Tolkien in [Sarehole Mill](../S/Sarehole_Mill.md), the film covers the early years of his life from his birth until the end of the [Great War](../G/Great_War.md).

## Reception {#Reception}

Some weeks before its release, the [Tolkien Estate](Tolkien_Estate.md) released a statement wishing to make clear that they '*"did not approve of, authorise or participate in the making of this film"*, and that they *"do not endorse it or its content in any way"*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The film received mixed reviews upon release, having a rating of 50% critic score and 71% audience score on review aggregator <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes" title="wikipedia:Rotten Tomatoes">Rotten Tomatoes</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and an average rating of 6.8/10 on <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb" title="wikipedia:IMDb">IMDb</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Among Tolkien scholars, it is agreed that the film is hardly historically accurate.<sup id="cite_ref-tolkcast_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkcast-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-doug_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doug-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-edu_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-edu-7">[7]</a></sup> [Douglas A. Anderson](../D/Douglas_A._Anderson.md) said that he hopes "*that film passes into its deserved oblivion quickly*".<sup id="cite_ref-doug_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doug-6">[6]</a></sup> [Eduardo Segura](../E/Eduardo_Segura.md) found it "*epidermic and easy to forget*".<sup id="cite_ref-edu_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-edu-7">[7]</a></sup> [Marcel Bülles](../M/Marcel_Bülles.md) highlighted the fact, that even though the film doesn't represent the chronology and events of Tolkien's life accurately, "*the mood, the atmosphere, it's just right*".<sup id="cite_ref-tolkcast_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkcast-5">[5]</a></sup> Upon seeing the film, [John Garth](../J/John_Garth.md) humorously remarked "*Dang, I have to rewrite [my book](Tolkien_and_the_Great_War.md) now*".<sup id="cite_ref-tolkcast_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkcast-5">[5]</a></sup>

Another criticism held at the film was the apparent absence of Tolkien's [christianity](../C/Christianity.md), despite it impacting his work.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
