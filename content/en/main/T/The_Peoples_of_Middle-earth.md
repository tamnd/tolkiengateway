<i><b>The Peoples of Middle-earth</b></i>, published in [1996](/wiki/1996), is the twelfth and final volume of [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien)'s 12-volume book series, *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)*, in which he analyzes the unpublished manuscripts of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

This book covers the development of the [Prologue](/wiki/The_Prologue "The Prologue") and [Appendices](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Appendices "The Lord of the Rings Appendices") of *The Lord of the Rings*, information on [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) tongues and other [languages](/wiki/Languages "Languages") of Middle-earth as provided by [Pengoloð](/wiki/Pengolodh "Pengolodh"), and two stories Tolkien didn't finish: [The New Shadow](/wiki/The_New_Shadow) (a quickly-abandoned sequel to *TLotR*) and an account of [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) of which [Tal-Elmar](</wiki/Tal-Elmar_(character)> "Tal-Elmar (character)") is the protagonist.

## Inscriptions {#Inscriptions}

There is an inscription in the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) characters in the first pages of every *History of Middle-earth* volume, written by Christopher Tolkien and describing the contents of the book. The inscription in Book XII reads:

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>This is the last volume of the work of Christopher Tolkien in which he has collected a great part of all that his father John Ronald Reuel Tolkien wrote of Middle-earth and Valinor. In this book is traced the devising of the history of the later ages in the Northwest of Middle-earth after the <a href="/wiki/War_of_Wrath" title="War of Wrath">Great Battle</a> and the Fall of <a href="/wiki/Morgoth" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a>.</i>&#34;</dd></dl>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The concluding volume of The History of Middle-earth series, which examines the Appendices to The Lord of the Rings.<br/>
The Peoples of Middle-earth traces the evolution of the Appendices to The Lord of The Rings, which provide a comprehensive historical structure of the Second and Third Ages, including Calendars, Hobbit genealogies and the <a href="/wiki/Westron" title="Westron">Westron</a> language. The book concludes with two unique abandoned stories: <a href="/wiki/The_New_Shadow" title="The New Shadow">The New Shadow</a>, set in Gondor during the Fourth Age, and the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Tal-Elmar" title="Tal-Elmar">tale of Tal-Elmar</a>, in which the coming of the dreaded Númenórean ships is seen through the eyes of men of Middle-earth in the Dark Years.<br/>
With the publication of this book, the long history of J.R.R. Tolkien’s creation is completed and the enigmatic state of his work can be understood.
</p>
</blockquote>
