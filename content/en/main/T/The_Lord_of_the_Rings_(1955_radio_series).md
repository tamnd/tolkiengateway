In [1955](/wiki/1955), the [BBC Radio Third Programme](/wiki/BBC "BBC") aired a treatment of *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* in six episodes. A [year later](/wiki/1956 "1956"), six episodes on *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)* and *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)* followed. Though only the latter six episodes were called thus, the entire series has become known as the 1955 [radio series](/wiki/Category:Radio_adaptations "Category:Radio adaptations") of <i><b><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></b></i>. Each of these twelve episodes was repeated just once, almost immediately, between two and five days after its first broadcast. It is the first adaptation of *The Lord of the Rings* and the first [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) known adaptation in any form. No recordings of this series are known to have survived. However, the scripts<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> and some correspondence<sup id="cite_ref-L175_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L175-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-L176_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L176-3">[3]</a></sup> still exist, as well as some parts of the musical score.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Differences from the book {#Differences_from_the_book}

- In the first six episodes, dialogue was preserved as much as possible, but in the last six, some liberty had to be taken.<sup id="cite_ref-CaG_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CaG-5">[5]</a></sup>
- The second part of the series had to have a lot of cuts because executives at the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) were not as fond of *The Lord of the Rings* as the listeners were.<sup id="cite_ref-CaG_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CaG-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **Tom Bombadil:** Considered by Tolkien "dreadful", though it is left unexplained why.<sup id="cite_ref-L175_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L175-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **[Goldberry](/wiki/Goldberry):** She was portrayed as Bombadil's daughter.<sup id="cite_ref-L175_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L175-2">[2]</a></sup> Tiller later apologized to Tolkien, stating he had thought the age difference between the two too large to portray them as a couple.<sup id="cite_ref-CaG_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CaG-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **[Willowman](/wiki/Old_Man_Willow "Old Man Willow"):** He is shown in league with [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), rather than just an antagonist of the Hobbits.<sup id="cite_ref-L175_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L175-2">[2]</a></sup> The opening narrator reads 'He \[Sauron\] has other allies too, from one of which the hobbits have been rescued by the strange and ancient power of Tom Bombadil.'<sup id="cite_ref-Milestone_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Milestone-19">[19]</a></sup>
- **[Glóin](/wiki/Gl%C3%B3in):** He was given a German accent. Tolkien thought he was not too bad, though a bit exaggerated.<sup id="cite_ref-L176_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L176-3">[3]</a></sup>
- **The [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond)**: Severely trimmed, only the base actions and characters remained.<sup id="cite_ref-CaG_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CaG-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **The [Chamber of Mazarbul](/wiki/Chamber_of_Mazarbul)**: Omitted.<sup id="cite_ref-Milestone_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Milestone-19">[19]</a></sup>
- [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir) does not speak to [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-Milestone_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Milestone-19">[19]</a></sup>
