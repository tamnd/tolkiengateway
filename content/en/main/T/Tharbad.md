**Tharbad** was a ford<sup id="cite_ref-pat_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pat-1">[1]</a></sup> and fortified town<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-South_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-South-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-UI_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-4">[4]</a></sup> at the river [Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3) on the southern border of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.

## Geography {#Geography}

Tharbad was located on the southern border of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) a few miles downstream from the confluence of the river [Hoarwell](/wiki/Hoarwell) and the river [Glanduin](/wiki/Glanduin), which formed the river Gwathló.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-6">[6]</a></sup> The lower part of the river [Glanduin](/wiki/Glanduin) was a fenland called the [Swanfleet](/wiki/Swanfleet) and the natural state of the upper [Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3) near Tharbad was a slow, wide river that spread into a fenland.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup> Ships with a smaller draught could be sailed or rowed up the Gwathló as far as Tharbad.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

The land around Tharbad was later drained, and dykes were built to construct a great port<sup id="cite_ref-fifth_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fifth-9">[9]</a></sup> for seagoing ships,<sup id="cite_ref-Author_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Author-10">[10]</a></sup> a bridge,<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup> forts on great earthworks on both sides of the river to guard the bridge<sup id="cite_ref-Author_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Author-10">[10]</a></sup>, and long causeways on both sides of the Gwathló and Mitheithel on which the road ran to the bridge of Tharbad<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup>.

After its construction by the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), the [North-South Road](/wiki/North-South_Road) crossed the river Gwathló at Tharbad.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-6">[6]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

In the early [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), the river [Glanduin](/wiki/Glanduin) was the southern border of the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldor "Noldor") realm of [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion).<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup>

Before the year [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [800](/wiki/Second_Age_800 "Second Age 800"), [Aldarion](/wiki/Tar-Aldarion "Tar-Aldarion"), the son of King [Tar-Meneldur](/wiki/Tar-Meneldur) of Númenor, built the haven [Vinyalondë](/wiki/Vinyalond%C3%AB) – which means "New Haven" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) – at the mouth of the river Gwathló as a point where he could obtain timber and repair his ships in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). This was because his father had decreed a curb on the felling of trees in Númenor for the purpose of shipbuilding.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

After he took the sceptre in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [883](/wiki/Second_Age_883 "Second Age 883")<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>, the King of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), [Tar-Aldarion](/wiki/Tar-Aldarion) traveled up the River Gwathló as far as Tharbad and met [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) there.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> It is not known if Tharbad was just a ford or if somebody already settled at Tharbad at the time of their meeting.

The [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) constructed a small harbour at the river's estuary to exploit the local timber: *, 'the New Haven', which was later called [Lond Daer](/wiki/Lond_Daer)<sup id="cite_ref-GC_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GC-14">[14]</a></sup>.*

Before [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1695](/wiki/Second_Age_1695 "Second Age 1695"),<sup id="cite_ref-fourth_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fourth-15">[15]</a></sup> the Númenóreans had devastated the forests below Tharbad by felling trees to obtain timber to build ships. The local population who had lived in those forests had initially been in awe of the Númenóreans, but had become hostile to them and attacked and ambushed them when the felling of trees became devastating. In return, the Númenóreans treated their attackers as enemies.<sup id="cite_ref-third_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-third-16">[16]</a></sup> It is possible that the Númenóreans built the forts at the haven of Lond Daer and along the banks of the river Gwathló, because of those conflicts with the native population. The surviving native population fled from [Enedwaith](/wiki/Enedwaith) to the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) in the east, and from [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath) to the dark woods of the cape of [Eryn Vorn](/wiki/Eryn_Vorn).<sup id="cite_ref-third_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-third-16">[16]</a></sup>.

After the invasion of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), the exiled native population who had previously lived in the forests along the Gwathló welcomed Sauron and hoped that he would defeat the Númenóreans. Sauron knew that the haven of Lond Daer and its shipyards were important to the Númenóreans. As a consequence, he used the exiled natives as spies and guides for his raiding parties who started fires in the woods and burned the great wood-stores of the Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-fourth_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fourth-15">[15]</a></sup>

During the war in Eriador, Sauron did not have enough troops to spare for assaults on the forts of the Númenóreans at the haven of Vinyalondë and along the banks of the river Gwathló.<sup id="cite_ref-fourth_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fourth-15">[15]</a></sup> By [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1700](/wiki/Second_Age_1700 "Second Age 1700"), Sauron had destroyed the Elven realm of Eregion and conquered all of Eriador except [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), where he had left strong forces to prevent Elrond to attack him in the rear and had reached the river [Lhûn](/wiki/Lh%C3%BBn), which was defended by the Elves of the realm of [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) and by Númenórean forces that had been stationed in Lindon. Additional forces of Sauron approached from the south-east and were in Enedwaith at the crossing of Tharbad, which was only lightly held.<sup id="cite_ref-GC_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GC-14">[14]</a></sup> It is not known if there were Númenórean forts at the crossing of Tharbad or whether the crossing of Tharbad was lightly held by the Númenóreans or by forces of Sauron.

After a great fleet commanded by the Númenórean admiral [Ciryatur](/wiki/Ciryatur) landed with troops from Númenór in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1700](/wiki/Second_Age_1700 "Second Age 1700"), the forces of Sauron were defeated and driven back to the south-east to the river [Baranduin](/wiki/Baranduin). After another defeat at the [Sarn Ford](/wiki/Sarn_Ford), they were driven back further to the south-east to the river Gwathló, where the forces of Sauron were reinforced by his force at Tharbad. However, Ciryatur had sent a part of his fleet to the haven of Lond Daer and landed a strong Númenórean force there that attacked the forces of Sauron in his rear in the [Battle of the Gwathló](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Gwathl%C3%B3).<sup id="cite_ref-GC_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GC-14">[14]</a></sup> It is not known if the forces of Sauron that had approached from the south-east were still on the southern bank of the river Gwathló in Enedwaith at Tharbad, or whether they had already crossed to the northern side of the river. It is also not known if the forces of Sauron that retreated from Sarn Ford had already crossed the river to its southern side at Tharbad and where at the Gwathló the Battle of the Gwathló took place. In the Battle of the Gwathló, the forces of Sauron were utterly routed and Sauron only barely managed to escape with a small force to [Calenardhon](/wiki/Calenardhon).<sup id="cite_ref-GC_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GC-14">[14]</a></sup>

Tharbad is not mentioned in surviving records for more than 1600 years after the Battle of the Gwathló.

### Late Second Age and Third Age {#Late_Second_Age_and_Third_Age}

It is probable that Tharbad became part of the kingdom of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320"), when the kingdoms of Arnor and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) were founded. This is because in the earlier centuries of Arnor and Gondor<sup id="cite_ref-NM_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-19">[19]</a></sup>, the region of Enedwaith between the Gwathló and the [Isen](/wiki/Isen) belonged to neither of those two kingdoms<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup>, and the Gwathló was the eastern border of Arnor and the Isen was the western border of Gondor<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-20">[20]</a></sup>.

In the early days of the kingdoms of Arnor and Gondor, a seaport where seagoing ships could dock was constructed and a fort was built on great earthworks on both sides of the Gwathló at Tharbad to protect the bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-Author_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Author-10">[10]</a></sup> Both kingdoms together constructed the bridge at Tharbad, and the long causeways on both sides of the Gwathló on which the [North-South Road](/wiki/North-South_Road), the main route of transport between the two kingdoms except by sea, ran through the fens in Minhiriath and Enedwaith to the bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup> By the year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2](/wiki/Third_Age_2 "Third Age 2")<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>, the North-South-Road from Gondor to Arnor already existed, but [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur), who was in haste, chose to take the shorter route north from [Osgiliath](/wiki/Osgiliath) up the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin) to the [High Pass](/wiki/High_Pass) over the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) to return to the Elven refuge of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), where he had left his wife and his youngest [son](</wiki/Valandil_(King_of_Arnor)> "Valandil (King of Arnor)").<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>.

Until the decay of Arnor, both kingdoms shared an interest in Enedwaith, but were mainly concerned with the maintenance of the North-South Road, the bridge at the fortified town of Tharbad, and of the long causeways on which the road ran.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup> There were no permanent settlements of people of Númenórean origin in Enedwaith,<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup> except at Tharbad,<sup id="cite_ref-NM_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-19">[19]</a></sup> where a large garrison of soliders, mariners, engineers<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup>, and river-wardens were stationed.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-19">[19]</a></sup> In addition, drainage works were built and maintained, and the banks of the rivers [Mitheithel](/wiki/Mitheithel) and Gwathló were strengthened.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-19">[19]</a></sup>

When the Kingdom of Arnor was divided in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861"),<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath), the region between the river [Baranduin](/wiki/Baranduin) and the river Gwathló, became a part of [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan), one of the three successor states of Arnor.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> As a consequence, Tharbad probably became a part of Cardolan.

From about [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1150](/wiki/Third_Age_1150 "Third Age 1150"), the [Stoors](/wiki/Stoors), a tribe of hobbits, migrated to and settled in<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> the area between Tharbad and the borders of Dunland,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> but they left around [1630](/wiki/Third_Age_1630 "Third Age 1630")<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>.

After the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) spread north-west from Gondor in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1636](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636"),<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> most of the people of Cardolan died, especially in Minhiriath.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> After that, Minhiriath was almost completely deserted,<sup id="cite_ref-SecondParagraph_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SecondParagraph-30">[30]</a></sup>, but some survivors of the Plague continued to live in Tharbad. After the decay and receding of the kingdom of Arnor, which had originally included Minhiriath,<sup id="cite_ref-NM_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_18-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-19">[19]</a></sup> Enedwaith became a part of Gondor during the days of the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/King_of_Gondor "King of Gondor"). Like before, Gondor was mainly concerned with the maintenance and patrolling of the North-South-Road.<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-20">[20]</a></sup> As a consequence, it is possible that the town of Tharbad became a part of Gondor. However, after the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1636](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636"), the region fell quickly into decay and turned back into wild fenlands long before the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_7-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-7">[7]</a></sup>

The survivors of the Great Plague remained in Tharbad, and the North-South-Road continued to be an important trade route for hundreds of years. When the days of the kings in Arnor and Gondor ended between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1975](/wiki/Third_Age_1975 "Third Age 1975") and [2050](/wiki/Third_Age_2050 "Third Age 2050") after the fall of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain) in [1974](/wiki/Third_Age_1974 "Third Age 1974") and the vanishing of the last king of Gondor [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur) at [Minas Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2050](/wiki/Third_Age_2050 "Third Age 2050") and the decline of Gondor started, the nearby native [Dunlendings](/wiki/Dunlendings), as well as the people left in Tharbad no doubt, ceased in fact to be subjects of Gondor, the North-South-Road was no longer maintained in Enedwaith, the bridge in Tharbad became ruinous so that the Gwathló had to be crossed at a ford at the former location of the bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-20">[20]</a></sup>

After the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Fell_Winter" title="Fell Winter">Fell Winter</a> of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2911](/wiki/Third_Age_2911 "Third Age 2911")<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2912](/wiki/Third_Age_2912 "Third Age 2912"), Enedwaith and Minhiriath were devastated by great floods and Tharbad was ruined and deserted<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup>.

Over a century later in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"),<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> the North-South Road no longer existed except for the crumbling remains of the causeways on both sides of the river, and the river was crossed by [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir) on a ford that was formed by the ruins of the bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-GC_14-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GC-14">[14]</a></sup> The ford was so dangerous<sup id="cite_ref-GC_14-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GC-14">[14]</a></sup> that Boromir lost his horse when he crossed the river at the ford.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup>

Before 22 September [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Ringwraiths) cross the Greyflood on their way to the [Shire](/wiki/Shire).<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup>

On 27 September [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018") Gandalf crossed the Greyflood<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup> with his horse [Shadowfax](/wiki/Shadowfax) at Tharbad.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup> Others occasionally crossed the ruined bridge - at their peril - including the Ringwraiths, servants and spies of [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), as well as the [Rangers of the North](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North) who sought [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) in Rohan in [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019").

After the War of the Ring at the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), the North-kingdom of Arnor was reestablished by Aragorn II.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup> It is probable that the town of Tharbad and its bridge were rebuilt. [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) announced that the [Greenway](/wiki/Greenway) would be opened again, and that there would be people and fields where there was wilderness before and that there would be room enough for people between the Isen and the Greyflood.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[39]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Tharbad* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name. It means "road-crossing".<sup id="cite_ref-UI_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-4">[4]</a></sup> It is composed of the elements *thar* ("across", "over") and *pad* ("walk", "step").<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[40]</a></sup> Its original meaning is said to have been "the Stepping Stones". Tharbad was originally a ford over which one could walk.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[41]</a></sup> The name Tharbad was already used for the ford before the town was established.<sup id="cite_ref-pat_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pat-1">[1]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>First built by the Númenóreans as a ford and settlement, Tharbad has been rebuilt over the many centuries of its existence. Its position upon the river <a href="/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3" title="Gwathló">Gwathló</a> marks the boundary between the <a href="/wiki/Cardolan" title="Cardolan">Cardolan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Swanfleet" title="Swanfleet">Swanfleet</a> regions: thus making it a landmark in both regions. It is also the dividing point between the <a href="/wiki/Greenway" title="Greenway">Greenway</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Old_South_Road" title="Old South Road">Old South Road</a>. The ruins are currently occupied by <a href="/wiki/Orcs" title="Orcs">Orcs</a>. <b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game)" title="The One Ring (role-playing game)">The One Ring</a> — <a href="/wiki/Ruins_of_the_Lost_Realm" title="Ruins of the Lost Realm">Ruins of the Lost Realm</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Tharbad was led by the Captain of the Haven, and the city had considerable autonomy within <a href="/wiki/Cardolan" title="Cardolan">Cardolan</a>. The city was not completely abandoned after the floods of <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2912" title="Third Age 2912">2912</a>, though the quality of its captains dropped sharply. The worst of these was one called Master Heatherton, who had dropped the use of the title entirely. Heatherton imposed a steep tax to repair the bridge, only to abscond with the proceeds instead. His immediate successor also fled, stealing the chain of office. It was under these two rulers that many citizens fled to other parts.<sup id="cite_ref-historytor_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historytor-42">[42]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Sometime before <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2965" title="Third Age 2965">2965</a>, a brigand named Gurnow set himself up as the new Captain of the Haven and married Lady Stock, a local noblewoman. He appointed his band of thieves to major positions in the city, and forbade whatever citizens remained from leaving the city, unless they paid another tax.<sup id="cite_ref-historytor_42-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historytor-42">[42]</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_2970&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 2970 (page does not exist)">2970</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Ruins_of_the_Lost_Realm#Plot" title="Ruins of the Lost Realm">band of Black Númenórean spies</a> allied itself with a rival of Gurnow<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[note 1]</a></sup>, and helped him take over the city. They secured funding from Mordor, while the spies&#39; warlord Zoril assassinated loyalists such as Lady Stock. Their sorceress Usapthon began excavating the Ghost Pit of Tharbad&#39;s northern district in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2975" title="Third Age 2975">2975</a>, hoping to find relics of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">Second Age</a>, which released evil spirits. That same year, the thieves turned on the Black Númenóreans, fueled by rumours that the pit contained a great treasure.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[43]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[44]</a></sup></dd></dl>
