**Quenya mode** is a name of the writing method of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) using [Tengwar](Tengwar.md).

## Origin {#Origin}

[Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) invented the tengwar on the [Year of the Trees 1250](../Y/Year_of_the_Trees_1250.md), strongly influenced by the [Sarati](../S/Sarati.md) of [Rúmil the Loremaster](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Tirion).md>).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ApD_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-2">[2]</a></sup> Fëanor constructed the Tengwar both as a general phonetic alphabet, and devised special arrangements to fit the characteristics of all languages of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Unlike Rúmil, Fëanor considered vowels as indepedent sounds and not just “colours” of the consonants, so he devised the “full writing” ([Quanta Sarmë](../Q/Quanta_Sarmë.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-ApD_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-2">[2]</a></sup>

However Fëanor also used a more 'conservative' system which seem to have been proved far more popular; he held Rúmil's idea of syllabic analysis of the words by the Sarati, and made also use of [tehtar](Tehtar.md) (instead of the full letters).<sup id="cite_ref-ApD_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ApD-2">[2]</a></sup>

Although Fëanor was a speaker of Quenya it is possible that he did not invent the Tengwar with Quenya specifically in mind, since the strict Quenya phonology didn't fit absolutely consistently. It's more probable that the Tengwar was created as a [general phonological template](Tengwar.md#Structure),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> onto which he later "fit" the Quenya sounds as consistently as possible

## History and evolution {#History_and_evolution}

### First Age {#First_Age}

Despite Fëanor is said to prefer the Quanta Sarme, he also used a more 'conservative' system which (judging by the majority of the subsequent samples) seem to have been proved far more popular. Fëanor held the idea of syllabic analysis of the words by the Sarati, and made also use of signs (tehtar) for vowels (instead of the full letters), placed over the preceding tengwar/consonants, indicating their “colour”. A consonant followed by a vowel was concidered as a “full letter” (ñávëa or ñáva-tengwë). The sarat <em style="font-style: normal; font-family: &#39;Tirion Sarati&#39;; font-size: 2em;">‘</em> was imported from the previous system, and when a vowel had no preceding consonant, it was used as a carrier for convenience in writing. This system however was used by Fëanor mainly for tradition and brevity, favoring the Quanta Sarme instead.

Apart of the standard consistent characters, there were also additional tengwar which don't fit in the structure. These are usually modifications of the standard tengwar. Hyarmen is a modification of Charma, Úre is of Wilya, and Anna derived from an earlier sarat.

The only difference from the Tincotéma was the underposed (for tengwar with raised stems) and overposed (for tengwar with lowered stems) dots. Those tengwar, not included in the tables, must have been ommited from the standard letters.</span>

The usage of those tengwar (whose names, if they had, are of course unknown) was to represent spirant sounds. As we know, no Amanya language possessed spirant sounds, since the Primitive spirants <i>th, ph</i>, and <i>kh</i> had already became <i>th, f</i> and <i>ch</i> in Quenya. It is possible that they were used for recording Valarin or maybe archaic forms.</span>

This quote suggests that Quenya originally contrasted /ɹ/ and /r/, since there would be no need for independent letters if the two sounds were merely <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/allophone" title="wikipedia:allophone">allophones</a> of each other. In this case, however, the original distribution of the letters Óre and Rómen must have been different from the one we observe at the end of the Third Age, where both letters are in perfectly complementary distribution, no contrast being possible. Since the sound <i>ry</i> derives from original <i>sy &gt; zy</i> (still present in Vanyarin), the form of the letter should be Áze with the dots, changed after <i>z</i> merged with <i>r</i>. But then this tengwa should be placed among the Additional Tengwar, for it is not consisted of a telco and a lúva.</span>

This usage tried to explain the relation of words between Noldorin words starting with a vowel where Telerin had <i>g-</i> (cf. Quenya <i>alda</i> vs. Telerin <i>galla</i>), but did not explain all the cases of words without an initial consonant (<i>alca</i>, according to this, should be spelled *<span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">hDjaE</span> that time, although it had never been <i>**galka</i> before). ### Later evolution {#Later_evolution}

Some changes occurred in Noldorin phonology, already in Valinor, that differentiated it from the dialect of the Vanyar: *th* merged with *s*, initial *ch* with *h* and maybe later, *z* with *r*. Those changes were inherited to Exilic Quenya which slowly evolved further.

During the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md), Quenya was spoken and written in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[6]</a></sup>

<li id="cite_note-19"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-19">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">Some changes took place in Quenya and consisted Tyelpetéma obsoleted as a separate series. The palatalised sounds will be now shown with its remnant, the “following <i>y</i>” underdots.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-20"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-20">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">The Noldor simplified <i>ndy &gt; ny</i>. So Indyo must became *Inyo, making the letter useless and replaced by Nyellë</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-21"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-21">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">It is known that for orthographic reasons, Súlë was used for <i>s</i> in words that once were <i>th</i> (eg. <i>thanga</i> and later <i>sanga</i> would be written <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">3Ds#</span>, not **<span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">iEs#</span>). One can wonder why it didn’t get another useful value, like Árë did, since Súlë was confused with Silmë.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-22"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-22">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">Initial <i>ch</i> was softened and merged with <i>h</i>, and Charma became &#34;Harma&#34;. Harma might still be used initially, but it seems that it was eventually replaced by Hyarmen. Harma was renamed Acha and was used now only medially where the hard sound <i>ch</i> remained unchanged.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-23"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-23">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">As <i>z</i> merged with <i>r</i> and <i>zy</i> became <i>ry</i>. It seems that when Arya was obsoleted: Árë was renamed as Essë, and the <i>ry</i> sound began to be indicated by Rómen/Órë letters.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-24"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-24">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">The <i>3</i> sound had been disappeared and the Telco was now solely used to carry the tehtar. But Anna was now used with the “following <i>y</i>” nuntixi to represent the <i>y</i> consonant since it seems Yanta was now used only to represent <i>–i</i> in diphtongs.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-25"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-25">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">This letter was used even after <i>z</i> became <i>r</i> for orthographic reasons. Since there was already Rómen for intervocalic <i>r</i>, (<i>aurel</i> should still be written <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">.D,Rj</span>, but also as <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">.D7Rj</span>) Árë was renamed Essë and took the value of long <i>s</i>. Maybe the application of value <i>ss</i> was an influence from the earlier Mode of Beleriand.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-26"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-26">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">This sound was simplified to <i>h</i>, maybe because of Sindarin influence, and later displaced Halla (and maybe Harma) completely. Its old <i>hy</i> value was restored when borrowing the “following <i>y</i>” nuntixi.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-27"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-27">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">This letter was replaced by Hyarmen, but it was still used to indicate the soft initial <i>hl</i> and <i>hr</i> (<i>hrívë</i>, <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">½7~ByR</span>)</span>
