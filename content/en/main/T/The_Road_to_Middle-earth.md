<i><b>The Road to Middle-earth: How J.R.R. Tolkien Created a New Mythology</b></i> is a scholarly study of the [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) created by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md). The book is written by professor [Tom Shippey](Tom_Shippey.md), first published in [1982](../_/1982.md). It is one of the most highly regarded books in the Tolkien scholarship.

The book explores Tolkien's creativity and the sources of his inspiration, discusses the origins of *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)*, *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, *[The Silmarillion](The_Silmarillion.md)*, shows how they contribute to Tolkien's mythology and how they can be appreciated.

A second edition was published in [1992](../_/1992.md), to incorporate discussions on the *[History of Middle-earth](../H/History_of_Middle-earth.md)* series. And a third edition in [2003](../_/2003.md), to reflect on the new publications and new views on Tolkien studies.

Prior to the publication of the *[Collected Poems](../C/Collected_Poems.md)*, the book was notable for containing 4 rare poems originally from *[Songs for the Philologists](../S/Songs_for_the_Philologists.md)*, these are "[Bagme Bloma](../B/Bagme_Bloma.md)", "[Éadig Béo Þu!](../_/Éadig_Béo_Þu!.md)", "[Ides Ælfscýne](../I/Ides_Ælfscýne.md)", and "[Ofer Wídne Gársecg](../O/Ofer_Wídne_Gársecg.md)".

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>A detailed and fascinating journey to the roots of The Lord of the Rings, by award-winning Tolkien expert Professor Tom Shippey.
</p><p>The Road to Middle-Earth is a fascinating and accessible exploration of J.R.R.Tolkien&#39;s creativity and the sources of his inspiration. Tom Shippey shows in detail how Tolkien&#39;s professional background led him to write The Hobbit and how he created a work of timeless charm for millions of readers. He discusses the contribution of The Silmarillion and Unfinished Tales to Tolkien&#39;s great myth-cycle, showing how Tolkien&#39;s more &#39;complex&#39; works can be read enjoyably and seriously by readers of his earlier books, and goes on to examine the remarkable 12-volume History of Middle-earth by Tolkien&#39;s son and literary heir Christopher Tolkien, which traces the creative and technical processes through which Middle-earth evolved. The core of the book, however, concentrates on The Lord of the Rings as a linguistic and cultural map, as a twisted web of a story, and as a response to the inner meaning of myth and poetry.
</p><p>By following the routes of Tolkien&#39;s own obsessions – the poetry of languages and myth – The Road to Middle-earth shows how Beowulf, The Lord of the Rings, Grimm&#39;s Fairy Tales, the Elder Edda and many other works form part of a live and continuing tradition of literature. It takes issue with many basic premises of orthodox criticism and offers a new approach to Tolkien, to fantasy, and to the importance of language in literature.
</p>
</blockquote>
