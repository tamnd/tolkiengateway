## Sindarin {#Sindarin}

### Noun {#Noun}

<i><b>tîn</b></i> (also appearing as <i><b>tin</b></i> and <i><b>tĭn</b></i>) is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word meaning "spark, sparkle (esp. used of the twinkle of [stars](/wiki/Stars "Stars"))" or "spark, star".<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup>

#### Etymology {#Etymology}

- [Root](/wiki/Roots "Roots") TIN ("sparkle, spark")<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup>

#### Examples {#Examples}

- <i><a href="/wiki/Ithildin" title="Ithildin">Ithil<b>din</b></a></i>

#### See also {#See_also}

- [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) *[tinw](/wiki/Tinw "Tinw")*

### Adjective {#Adjective}

<i><b>tîn</b></i> (or *tínen*) is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word meaning "silent, quiet".<sup id="cite_ref-UI_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-2">[2]</a></sup>

#### Examples {#Examples_2}

- <i><a href="/wiki/Rath_D%C3%ADnen" title="Rath Dínen">Rath <b>Dínen</b></a></i><sup id="cite_ref-UI_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Neo-Sindarin {#Neo-Sindarin}

The unattested form <i><b>tîn</b></i> is commonly held to mean "his (of nonsubject)" in [Neo-Sindarin](/wiki/Neo-Elvish "Neo-Elvish"), related to *[dîn](/wiki/D%C3%AEn "Dîn")* and *[în](/wiki/%C3%8En "În")*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
