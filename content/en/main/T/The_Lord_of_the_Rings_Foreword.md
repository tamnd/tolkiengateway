The **Foreword** is the very first section appearing in <i><b><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></b></i>.

The Foreword notoriously introduces minor spoilers that concern much later events in the story, as if they are well-known facts. For example, the first edition Forword mentioned the descendants of Master [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee), thus reassuring that he would survive the adventure. Other references include that the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring) won't be used, and hints about the treachery of [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman).

## First edition {#First_edition}

In the Foreword to the first edition of *The Lord of the Rings*,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> Tolkien imposes himself as merely translator and editor of the text to come, thus sharing the [in-universe](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") character of the [1951](/wiki/1951) [prefatory note](/wiki/The_Hobbit_Preface "The Hobbit Preface") to the second edition of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*.

Tolkien disliked his own text, noting that it mixes real-life matters of his life (including referencing the [Inklings](/wiki/Inklings) and [his children](/wiki/Tolkien_family "Tolkien family")) with the [in-universe](/wiki/Secondary_world "Secondary world") "machinery" of the Tale.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

The full, original text of this Foreword can be found in *[The Lord of the Rings: A Reader's Companion](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_A_Reader%27s_Companion)*, under the "Foreword to the Second Edition" section.

## Second and later editions {#Second_and_later_editions}

In [1965](/wiki/1965) [Ballantine Books](/wiki/Ballantine_Books) needed a new text in order to renew the copyright in the United States, and to challenge the controversy raised by [Ace Books](/wiki/Ace_Books). For that edition Tolkien wrote a new Foreword.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-3">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxx</sup>

The Foreword written specifically for Ballantine ends with a paragraph where Tolkien thanks the American readers for their courtesy, and makes a reference to the copyright laws and the unauthorised edition (which he compares it to something that Saruman would do) and pleaded them to prefer only the edition authorised by him, dedicating the book to all those who will enjoy the story. This paragraph is absent to European editions.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-3">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxx</sup>

As for the previous paragraph (where Tolkien mentions that there have been alterations, corrections and additions in that edition, like notes and a small index) sometimes is omitted, or replaced by a new text describing alterations (like the absence of the [Appendices](/wiki/Appendices)).<sup id="cite_ref-rc_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-3">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxix</sup>

The original text wrote that his work "has been read by many people since it finally appeared in print ten years ago", a number that was updated in subsequent printings. The number was omitted in [1976](/wiki/1976) on a suggestion by Christopher Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-3">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxiiv</sup>

### Mistakes {#Mistakes}

Recollecting the writing of the book 25 years earlier, the references to dates and progress of writing, are inaccurate (possibly false memories), contradicting more detailed letters that Tolkien had written while composing the story.

For example Tolkien claims that he began writing *The Lord of the Rings* soon after completing *The Hobbit*; and when *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* was rejected, he "went back to the sequel". In reality it was right then, December [1937](/wiki/1937), when he *started* writing the book.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-3">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:pp. lxxi-lxxii</sup>

Another example is the hiatus in [1940](/wiki/1940), after writing about [Balin's Tomb](/wiki/Balin%27s_Tomb), until late [1941](/wiki/1941). [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) says that it contradicts contemporary [Letter 37](/wiki/Letter_37); the hiatus was probably in [1939](/wiki/1939) until [August](/wiki/August) [1940](/wiki/1940).<sup id="cite_ref-rc_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-3">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxiii</sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>
