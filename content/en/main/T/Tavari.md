**Tavari** were the [fays](../F/Fays.md) of the woods, according to the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

They arrived to the world with [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) and [Yavanna](../Y/Yavanna.md), along with many other types of fays.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Tavari* is in [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md), being a possible plural<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup> form of *tavar* ("dale-sprite").<sup id="cite_ref-Tav_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tav-4">[3]</a></sup> The [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) cognate of *tavar* was <i><b>tavor</b></i> ("a wood fay").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

The word *tavar* is derived from the root <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=TAVA&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="TAVA (page does not exist)">TAVA</a> ("beam"), with "beam" here referring to the [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *béam* ("tree, piece of wood, rafter, pillar").<sup id="cite_ref-Tav_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tav-4">[3]</a></sup>

The name suggests that the wood-fays 'used' the forest as their home, and that such forests were watched or guarded by the fays.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In a later version of the legendarium from *[The Etymologies](The_Etymologies.md)* (1930s), there appear the terms <i><b>tavaro(n)</b></i> (male)/<i><b>tavaril</b></i> (female), meaning "dryad; spirit of the woods".<sup id="cite_ref-Taw_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Taw-7">[6]</a></sup>

The hypothetical earlier forms of these names were <i><b>tawaro</b></i> or <i><b>tawarë</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Taw_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Taw-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

According to [C.S. Lewis](../C/C.S._Lewis.md), [Tolkien](Tolkien.md) once told him that the historical people in a sense did not falsely claim to see "[nymphs](../N/Nymphs.md) in the fountains and woods and dryads in the wood", since the production of food was closely connected to one's own land and territory: people came to internalize the nature surrounding them.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

It is possible that Tolkien at one point drew inspiration from the concept of the *Tavari* while creating the race of [Ents](../E/Ents.md), since in one letter, Tolkien explores the possibility that the Ents were "[souls](../S/Spirits.md)" that were sent to inhabit (animate) trees, or that became [trees](Trees.md) themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 2]</a></sup>
