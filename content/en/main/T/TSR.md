**TSR, Inc.** was a gaming company, founded in [1973](/wiki/1973). Their most famous product was [Dungeons and Dragons](/wiki/Dungeons_and_Dragons), though they also published [The Battle of the Five Armies](</wiki/Battle_of_the_Five_Armies_(board_game)> "Battle of the Five Armies (board game)") board game and [Dragon Magazine](/wiki/Dragon_Magazine). Both games came under legal scrutiny in [1977](/wiki/1977) for unlicensed use of rights held by [Tolkien Enterprises](/wiki/Tolkien_Enterprises).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

After [Iron Crown Enterprises](/wiki/Iron_Crown_Enterprises) got into trouble with its [Tolkien Quest](/wiki/Tolkien_Quest) series, Tolkien Enterprises approached TSR about the license. TSR was by that time making most money with its tie-in novels, leading <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine_Williams" title="wikipedia:Lorraine Williams">Lorraine Williams</a>, who then owned the company, to instead primarily aim for the ability to publish sequels. As [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) puts it: *“Lorraine wanted all the rights to create fiction, and computer games, and calendars, and spin-offs. Everything except publishing the books themselves.*” Seeking to obtain permission from the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate), Rateliff was sent to England around [1992](/wiki/1992) to negotiate with [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien). Nothing came out of it, however.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Deep in debt by the 1990s, TSR was acquired by [Wizards of the Coast](/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast) in [1997](/wiki/1997).
