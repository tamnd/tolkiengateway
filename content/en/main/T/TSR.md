**TSR, Inc.** was a gaming company, founded in [1973](../_/1973.md). Their most famous product was [Dungeons and Dragons](../D/Dungeons_and_Dragons.md), though they also published [The Battle of the Five Armies](<../B/Battle_of_the_Five_Armies_(board_game).md>) board game and [Dragon Magazine](../D/Dragon_Magazine.md). Both games came under legal scrutiny in [1977](../_/1977.md) for unlicensed use of rights held by [Tolkien Enterprises](Tolkien_Enterprises.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

After [Iron Crown Enterprises](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md) got into trouble with its [Tolkien Quest](Tolkien_Quest.md) series, Tolkien Enterprises approached TSR about the license. TSR was by that time making most money with its tie-in novels, leading <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine_Williams" title="wikipedia:Lorraine Williams">Lorraine Williams</a>, who then owned the company, to instead primarily aim for the ability to publish sequels. As [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) puts it: *“Lorraine wanted all the rights to create fiction, and computer games, and calendars, and spin-offs. Everything except publishing the books themselves.*” Seeking to obtain permission from the [Tolkien Estate](Tolkien_Estate.md), Rateliff was sent to England around [1992](../_/1992.md) to negotiate with [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md). Nothing came out of it, however.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Deep in debt by the 1990s, TSR was acquired by [Wizards of the Coast](../W/Wizards_of_the_Coast.md) in [1997](../_/1997.md).
