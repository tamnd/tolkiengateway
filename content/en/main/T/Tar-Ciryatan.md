**Tar-Ciryatan** ([S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1634](/wiki/Second_Age_1634 "Second Age 1634") – [2035](/wiki/Second_Age_2035 "Second Age 2035"), aged 401 years) was the twelfth [King of Númenor](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor), ruling for 160 years, from [1869](/wiki/Second_Age_1869 "Second Age 1869") to [2029](/wiki/Second_Age_2029 "Second Age 2029").<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ciryatan_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ciryatan-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Ciryatan was the son of [Minastir](/wiki/Tar-Minastir "Tar-Minastir"),<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-3">[3]</a></sup> who himself was the nephew of the [Ruling Queen](/wiki/Ruling_Queens_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Ruling Queens of Númenor") [Tar-Telperiën](/wiki/Tar-Telperi%C3%ABn).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Before taking [the Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Sceptre of Númenor"), Ciryatan scorned the yearnings of his father and eased his restlessness by voyaging east, north, and south.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ciryatan_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ciryatan-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [1693](/wiki/Second_Age_1693 "Second Age 1693") the [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron) began in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and the [Three Rings](/wiki/Three_Rings) that were forged by [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor) in the elven realm of [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion) were hidden.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> When [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad), the [Elven](/wiki/Elf "Elf") [High King of the Noldor](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor) in Middle-earth started to fear that Sauron would invade [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) with an army, he asked [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) for help and the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) started to station a Númenórean force and supplies for a war on the shores of his elven realm of [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon).<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup>

By [1695](/wiki/Second_Age_1695 "Second Age 1695")<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) moved with a great army through [Calenardhon](/wiki/Rohan "Rohan") and invaded [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) where he immediately turned north to attack Eregion. When Gil-galad received the news of the invasion in Lindon, he sent a force under the command of [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) to Eregion and asked [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) for help.<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup> Sauron could not spare enough forces to assault the Númenórean forts at their [Great Haven](/wiki/Lond_Daer "Lond Daer") on the estuary of the [Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3) or along both banks of the river, but his raiders burnt many of the wood-stores of the Númenóreans and started fires in the woods.<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1697](/wiki/Second_Age_1697 "Second Age 1697")<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> Sauron's army destroyed the realm of Eregion and tortured and killed Celebrimbor, but Elrond managed to retreat from Eregion with the surviving [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) and established the refuge of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell). Sauron then advanced to Lindon where Gil-galad and a Númenórean force that had already been stationed on the shores of Lindon in preparation for a possible war with Sauron held the River [Lhûn](/wiki/Lh%C3%BBn) in desperate defence of the [Grey Havens](/wiki/Grey_Havens). At the same time a strong detachment of Sauron's forces besieged [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell).<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup> Minastir, who loved the Eldar, but envied them,<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-3">[3]</a></sup> sent a great navy under the command of [Ciryatur](/wiki/Ciryatur) to Lindon to aid the Elves in [1700](/wiki/Second_Age_1700 "Second Age 1700").<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Telperien_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Telperien-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA1700_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA1700-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[note 1]</a></sup>

The delayed navy of Númenór arrived in Lindon<sup id="cite_ref-SA1700_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA1700-11">[11]</a></sup> just in time in [1700](/wiki/Second_Age_1700 "Second Age 1700") and Sauron's army was defeated and pushed back to the south-east. A part of the Númenórean ships was ordered by Ciryatur to land at their Great Haven at the mouth of the Gwathló. Subsequently, Sauron's army was defeated again at [Sarn Ford](/wiki/Sarn_Ford) and driven further to the south-east. It was eventually utterly routed in the [Battle of the Gwathló](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Gwathl%C3%B3) after the Númenórean force that had landed at the Great Haven appeared at its rear and Sauron narrowly escaped with a small remaining force that was further attacked in the east of Calenhardhon back to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). The strong detachment of Sauron's forces that had been besieging Rivendell was trapped between the forces of Elrond and Gil-galad and completely destroyed. Although Eriador was cleared of the enemy, it was mostly in ruins.<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [1731](/wiki/Second_Age_1731 "Second Age 1731") Tar-Telperiën surrendered the Sceptre and Minastir became King.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Telperien_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Telperien-10">[10]</a></sup> In [1800](/wiki/Second_Age_1800 "Second Age 1800") Ciryatan's son and heir, [Atanamir](/wiki/Tar-Atanamir "Tar-Atanamir") was born.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Atanamir_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Atanamir-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Númenóreans had made permanent havens in Middle-earth since [1200](/wiki/Second_Age_1200 "Second Age 1200"),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> having now tasted power in Middle-earth,<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup> began to establish dominions<sup id="cite_ref-SA1800_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA1800-16">[15]</a></sup> and permanent settlements on the western coasts of Middle-earth from about [1800](/wiki/Second_Age_1800 "Second Age 1800") onward<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup>. They became too powerful for Sauron to attempt to move west out of Mordor<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup> and he extended his power eastwards<sup id="cite_ref-SA1800_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA1800-16">[15]</a></sup>.

In [1869](/wiki/Second_Age_1869 "Second Age 1869") Tar-Minastir, at the age of 395, surrendered the Sceptre and Ciryatan became King.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-3">[3]</a></sup> It was said that Ciryatan constrained his father to surrender the sceptre to him before Tar-Minastir would have done so out of his free will. Tar-Ciryatan was a mighty King, but greedy of wealth; he built a great fleet of royal ships, and his servants brought back great store of metals and gems, and oppressed the men of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ciryatan_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ciryatan-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [2029](/wiki/Second_Age_2029 "Second Age 2029"), at the age of 395, he surrendered the Sceptre<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ciryatan_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ciryatan-2">[2]</a></sup> to his son, Tar-Atanamir,<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Atanamir_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Atanamir-4">[4]</a></sup> and died six years later in [2035](/wiki/Second_Age_2035 "Second Age 2035").<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ciryatan_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ciryatan-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Ciryatan* is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "Shipbuilder" or "Shipwright" as a compound of *[cirya](/wiki/Cirya "Cirya")* ("ship") and the suffixal form *-tan* ("builder", "wright") of *tamo* ("smith").<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> He is explicitly referred to as "the Shipbuilder"<sup id="cite_ref-akallabeth_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-akallabeth-18">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup> or with "(Shipwright)" after his name<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-1">[1]</a></sup>. Like all the rulers of Númenor who took their royal names in Quenya, Ciryatan added the prefix *tar-* ("high") to his name when he received the Sceptre.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup>

<i><b>Balkumagân</b></i> is an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-1">[1]</a></sup> Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "Shipbuilder" or "Shipwright" as a compound of the objective form of *balak* ("ship") and *magân* ("builder", "wright").<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> The meaning of the Adûnaic prefix *Ar-* is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "king" and is a prefixal form of *ârû* ("king") or derived from the same root.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### History of the Akallabêth {#History_of_the_Akallabêth}

The first mentions of Ciryatan (spelled as Kiryatan) are found in *[The History of the Akallabêth](/wiki/The_History_of_the_Akallab%C3%AAth)*.

Initially he was the eighth King of Númenor to succeed [Elros](/wiki/Elros), and was the son (rather than the father) of Atanamir. He was one of only four named kings, along with [Elros](/wiki/Elros), Atanamir, and [Pharazôn](/wiki/Pharaz%C3%B4n) (the thirteenth and final successor).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-1">[1]</a></sup>

Kiryatan's time is noted as such:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>In his time first began the division of the folk between the <a href="/wiki/King%27s_Men" title="King&#39;s Men">King’s folk</a> and the <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Nimruz%C3%AErim" title="Nimruzîrim">Nimruzîrim</a></i> (<i>Elendilli</i>) or Elf-friends. The King’s folk and Royal House cease to learn or use Elvish speech and are more usually known by their Númenórean names. This king was <i>Tar-Kiryatan</i> (Shipwright) or in Númenórean <i>Ar-Balkumagān</i>. Settlements of <i>dominion</i> in Middle-earth begin.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien</a> (ed.), <i><a href="/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth" title="The Peoples of Middle-earth">The Peoples of Middle-earth</a></i>, &#34;V. <a href="/wiki/The_History_of_the_Akallab%C3%AAth" title="The History of the Akallabêth">The History of the Akallabêth</a>&#34;, p. 186</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

It was under the reign of Atanamir that the Shadow first fell on Númenor, and the Númenóreans began to desire for everlasting life. Tar-Kiryatan, in a role which would later be taken by [Tar-Ancalimon](/wiki/Tar-Ancalimon), was Atanamir's son of like mind who's time led to division among the Númenóreans, building of great tombs for the dead, and, after his days, of neglecting the first fruits to [Eru](/wiki/Eru).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Other {#Other}

*[The Complete Guide to Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Complete_Guide_to_Middle-earth)* incorrectly states the year of his death as [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [2251](/wiki/Second_Age_2251 "Second Age 2251"). This is because the *[Tale of Years](/wiki/Appendix_B "Appendix B")* said that in that year "Tar-Atanamir takes the sceptre". However, Atanamir died in 2221. 2221 is itself an emendation of 2251, and the former (2221) appears in the later tables, while the latter (2251) in the earlier tables: therefore 2251 (properly 2221) should have read "Death of Tar-Atanamir. Tar-Ancalimon takes the sceptre".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup>

Yet in <i>The Line of Elros</i> Tar-Telperiën is said to have lived to <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Age_1731" title="Second Age 1731">1731</a> and not have given up the sceptre until that time, which would mean she was the ruler of Númenor when the great navy was sent (see <i>citation 2</i>). <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien</a> could not account for this discrepancy (see <i>citation 8</i>). It is possible to reconcile this discrepancy if Minastir sent the fleet in a capacity, such as <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">Regent</a> or <a href="/wiki/Captain_of_the_King%27s_Ships" title="Captain of the King&#39;s Ships">Captain of the Queen&#39;s Ships</a> before he ascended the throne and that later chroniclers simply referred to him as &#34;Tar-&#34; or &#34;the King&#34; without taking into account that he had not taken the sceptre and had not formally become the king when he sent the fleet to Lindon. In a <i>Note on the delay of Gil-galad and the Númenóreans</i> it is mentioned that the great host of &#34;Minastir&#34; landed in Middle-earth (see <i>citation 9</i>).</span>
