**Elwë**, better known as **Thingol**, was one of the two Kings of the [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri), the other being his younger brother [Olwë](/wiki/Olw%C3%AB).

As the [King of Doriath](/wiki/King_of_Doriath) and the acknowledged high-king of the [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar) in [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup> Thingol would become a central figure of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), instigating the [Quest for the Silmaril](/wiki/Quest_for_the_Silmaril), one of the most significant events of the First Age, but ultimately the cause of his own doom.

Thingol, alone of all the [Children of Ilúvatar](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar), married one of the [Ainur](/wiki/Ainur) - the [Maia](/wiki/Maia) [Melian](/wiki/Melian), and through their daughter [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) he became the ancestor to all the kings of the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) including [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn).

## History {#History}

### Ambassador of the Valar {#Ambassador_of_the_Valar}

Elwë was born at [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen) in the days before the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) and the [Moon](/wiki/Moon), when [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) was lit only by starlight.

After the [Chaining of Melkor](/wiki/Chaining_of_Melkor), the [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB) took three Elves, [Ingwë](/wiki/Ingw%C3%AB), Finwë, and Elwë, to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) to convince the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) to join the Valar in the [Blessed Realm](/wiki/Blessed_Realm). Upon their return, the Elven ambassadors convinced many to join them on the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey) to the West. During the thousand mile journey, the Elven host separated into three groups, the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar), the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor), and the Teleri.<sup id="cite_ref-capt_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-capt-11">[9]</a></sup>

The Teleri were the largest folk and they were divided in two hosts, led by Elwë and his brother Olwë. Elwë was more eager than his brother to see again Valinor, and wished to catch up with the Noldor because of his friendship with Finwë; but the Teleri were also the slowest and were the last to reach the western shores of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-capt_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-capt-11">[9]</a></sup>

After many years the Teleri crossed the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) and stayed for some time in [East Beleriand](/wiki/East_Beleriand).<sup id="cite_ref-capt_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-capt-11">[9]</a></sup> During this time Elwë returned from a meeting with his friend Finwë, and wandered into the [Forest of Nan Elmoth](/wiki/Nan_Elmoth "Nan Elmoth"), where he met and fell in love with [Melian](/wiki/Melian) the [Maia](/wiki/Maiar "Maiar"). Caught in an enchantment of their own making, Elwë and Melian could not be found by the searching Teleri. When the Valar returned to take the remaining Elves to Valinor, only some of them followed Olwë across the [Great Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer"). The rest had come to love the lands of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) and refused to leave without their missing lord.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup>

### King of Doriath {#King_of_Doriath}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/34/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg/100px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="100" height="100" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/34/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg/150px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/3/34/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg/200px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Elw%C3%AB_heraldic_device.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1300" data-file-height="1300"/></a><figcaption>Elwë&#39;s device with the Winged Moon by <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a></figcaption></figure>

In [Y.T.](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees "Years of the Trees") [1152](/wiki/Year_of_the_Trees_1152 "Year of the Trees 1152"), Elwë awoke from his trance and reappeared with his bride Melian.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[11]</a></sup> He was much changed since his people last they saw him: while he had been fair and noble before, he now stood like a Maia above them as the tallest of the Children of Ilúvatar. He gathered his people together and founded the Kingdom of [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath). His subjects would become the Sindar, or the "Grey Elves", and their language was [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin). From then on Elwë was known as King Elu Thingol, and he claimed lordship over all the lands in [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand). While Thingol was king of the Grey Elves, he saw the light of the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees) as an ambassador of the Valar and thus he was counted as a [High Elf](/wiki/High_Elves "High Elves"), and equal to any lord of the Eldar.<sup id="cite_ref-Princes_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Princes-6">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Thingol_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thingol-14">[12]</a></sup> He was even acknowledged as high-king of all the Teleri in Beleriand, even after Doriath became hidden.<sup id="cite_ref-PM_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-4">[2]</a></sup>

On Melian's advice, Thingol became an ally of the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) of [Belegost](/wiki/Belegost), who carved the caverns of [Menegroth](/wiki/Menegroth) for him. In payment he gave them, along with many other things, the great pearl [Nimphelos](/wiki/Nimphelos).<sup id="cite_ref-Thingol_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thingol-14">[12]</a></sup>

A thousand years before the first rising of the Sun and the Moon the [Green-elves](/wiki/Green-elves) entered Beleriand under [Denethor](/wiki/Denethor_of_the_Nandor "Denethor of the Nandor"). Thingol welcomed them and gave them the lands of [Ossiriand](/wiki/Ossiriand), which they named [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon). The Green Elves told of the spread of the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) and other foul creatures in the north and east of Middle-earth. The Grey Elves began arming themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-Thingol_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thingol-14">[12]</a></sup>

Centuries later [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) returned to Middle-earth. Now named Morgoth, he had destroyed the Two Trees, killed Finwë, the King of the Noldor, and stolen the fabled [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils). Seeking to claim dominion quickly, he launched a sudden assault on the lands of the Sindar. The [First Battle of Beleriand](/wiki/First_Battle_of_Beleriand) went well for Thingol, who prevented any Orcs from invading Doriath, but the Green Elves took horrific losses and the [Falathrim](/wiki/Falathrim) were besieged.<sup id="cite_ref-Thingol_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thingol-14">[12]</a></sup>

The Falathrim were finally saved by the unexpected arrival of the Noldor, who launched the [Dagor-nuin-Giliath](/wiki/Dagor-nuin-Giliath) ("Battle Under the Stars") in which Morgoth's forces were all but annihilated.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-15">[13]</a></sup>

After the First Battle of Beleriand, Doriath was encircled by the [Girdle of Melian](/wiki/Girdle_of_Melian), an impenetrable fence of enchantment that guarded the kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-Thingol_14-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thingol-14">[12]</a></sup> While his own kingdom was protected, Thingol was still loath to surrender any other lands in Beleriand to the Noldor as he was suspicious of the aggressive new lords in Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-15">[13]</a></sup>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

King Thingol's relations with the Noldor were strained, and grew worse decades later when he learned the truth of the [Kinslaying at Alqualondë](/wiki/Kinslaying_at_Alqualond%C3%AB). Thingol banned the use of [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) in his lands, which led to Sindarin being the common Elven tongue in Middle-earth. The King of Doriath refused to aid the Noldor in the war against Morgoth, and took little part in the ongoing struggle.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[14]</a></sup>

### Quest for the Silmaril {#Quest_for_the_Silmaril}

<p>If thou fearest neither spell, wall nor weapons, as thou saist, then go fetch me a Silmaril from the crown of Morgoth. Then we will give jewel for jewel, but thou shalt win the fairer: Lúthien of the First-born and of the Gods.

Thingol and Melian had one child, a daughter named [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien), said to be the fairest woman ever to live. Lúthien fell in love with a [Man](/wiki/Men "Men") named [Beren](/wiki/Beren). Thingol did not wish for the two to wed, as he valued his daughter very highly and disliked Men. As a bride-price he asked for a Silmaril from the crown of [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth), thinking there was no way that Beren could fulfill this demand.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-17">[15]</a></sup>

However, when Beren and Lúthien came back from [Angband](/wiki/Angband), and when Beren showed him his missing hand which still held the Silmaril in the belly of [Carcharoth](/wiki/Carcharoth), he softened his heart towards him. Soon, though, Carcharoth in his anguish and rage from the Silmaril that burned his insides, broke through the Girdle of Melian and entered Doriath. In that hour, Thingol, along with Beren, [Mablung](/wiki/Mablung), [Beleg](/wiki/Beleg), and [Huan](/wiki/Huan) the Hound of Valinor, began the [Hunting of the Wolf](/wiki/Hunting_of_the_Wolf). Carcharoth was finally slain by Huan, but both Huan and Beren died in the process. After that, Lúthien died also, and coming to the [Halls of Mandos](/wiki/Halls_of_Mandos) she begged [Mandos](/wiki/Mandos) that Beren be returned to life. However, it was beyond the power of Mandos to withhold the souls of Men from going beyond the [Circles of the World](/wiki/Circles_of_the_World).<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-17">[15]</a></sup>

Therefore, [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar), through [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB), offered Lúthien and Beren the opportunity to live again, but with Lúthien sharing the [Gift of Men](/wiki/Gift_of_Men). She agreed, and they both came back to life and lived out the remainder of their second lives at [Tol Galen](/wiki/Tol_Galen), far from the sight of any other mortals.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-17">[15]</a></sup>

### Fostering of Túrin {#Fostering_of_Túrin}

Thingol adopted as a son a young Man named [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin), son of [Húrin](/wiki/H%C3%BArin) and akin to Beren himself, who was sent to Doriath when he was seven years old.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[16]</a></sup> When he grew up, he was permitted to join Beleg Cúthalion on the marches of Doriath, fighting the Orcs of Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-Turin_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-19">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[18]</a></sup>

Túrin accidentally caused the death of [Saeros](/wiki/Saeros), one of Thingol's counselors who had provoked and attacked him. Before he could be either punished or forgiven he fled. Thingol however gave Beleg a leave to seek out his friend, who in the meantime had joined a [band of outlaws](/wiki/Gaurwaith "Gaurwaith").<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[19]</a></sup> Telling to Thingol that Túrin did not wish to return, Thingol allowed Beleg to go and join his friend and gave him the black sword [Anglachel](/wiki/Anglachel) that [Eöl](/wiki/E%C3%B6l) had forged and given to Thingol<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[20]</a></sup> in tribute.<sup id="cite_ref-Turin_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-19">[17]</a></sup>

### Nauglamír and death {#Nauglamír_and_death}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/46/Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg/300px-Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="414" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/46/Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg/450px-Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/46/Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg/600px-Peter_Xavier_Price_-_Thingol%27s_End.jpg 2x" data-file-width="797" data-file-height="1100"/></a><figcaption><i>Thingol&#39;s End</i> by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Xavier_Price" title="Peter Xavier Price">Peter Xavier Price</a></figcaption></figure>

Sometime after Túrin's tragic death, Húrin, now an old man released from Morgoth's captivity, was allowed to enter Menegroth. In anger, he threw the [Nauglamír](/wiki/Nauglam%C3%ADr), the treasure of [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond), before King Thingol, bitterly "thanking" him for aiding his son. Melian pierced through Húrin's madness and grief; shamed by his actions, he offered the Nauglamír to Thingol sincerely and left Menegroth a broken man.<sup id="cite_ref-Doriath_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doriath-23">[21]</a></sup>

At that time, a desire came into Thingol's heart to take the Nauglamír and place the Silmaril into it, thus melding together two of the greatest creations made by the Elves and the Dwarves. He hired skilled [Dwarven craftsmen](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Nogrod "Dwarves of Nogrod"), but they secretly desired to possess both treasures for themselves. After their work was completed, the Dwarves refused to give the Nauglamír to Thingol, claiming that he had no right to own the priceless necklace made by their ancestors. Realizing that they were coveting the Silmaril, Thingol became infuriated and insulted them. The Dwarves were angered by his harsh words and killed him. This led to the [sacking of Menegroth](/wiki/Battle_in_the_Thousand_Caves "Battle in the Thousand Caves") and the eventual [destruction of Doriath](/wiki/Second_Kinslaying "Second Kinslaying"), which scattered its people.<sup id="cite_ref-Doriath_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doriath-23">[21]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

His hair was grey as silver, his eyes were as stars, and he was the tallest of all the Elven-folk.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[22]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§27</sup> which means that he was taller than Elendil, probably the tallest man, who was 7'11" or 2.41 m tall.<sup id="cite_ref-linear_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-linear-25">[23]</a></sup>

**Thingol** was his [anessë](/wiki/Epess%C3%AB "Epessë") (given name) in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), which means "Greycloak"<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[24]</a></sup> or Greymantle.<sup id="cite_ref-Thingol_14-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thingol-14">[12]</a></sup> The first element of the name is *thin(d)* ("grey").<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[25]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[26]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that the second element *gol* is the [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") form of *coll* ("cloak", "mantle").<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[27]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

[Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) tradition names him <i><b>Elwë</b></i> and *[Singollo](/wiki/Singollo)*, for the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) pronounced his epessë as <i><b>Singollo</b></i>.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

His first name, *Elwë*, means "star-man" and contains *[el](/wiki/El "El")* and *[-we](/wiki/-we)*.

<i><b>Elwë Þindicollo</b></i> or **Sindicollo** was the ancient name of King Thingol; when he settled in [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath) with [Melian](/wiki/Melian), he took the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) form of his name, *Elu Thingol*.

In *[The Book of Lost Tales Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One)* his [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) name is *Linwë Tinto* or *Tinwë Linto* and his [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) name is *Tinwelint*.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[28]</a></sup> He was led away from [Solosimpi](/wiki/Solosimpi), his people, by the beautiful music of [Wendelin](/wiki/Melian "Melian"), and never returned to them. [Ellu](/wiki/Ellu) took his place as their leader.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[29]</a></sup> In a crossed out note, only Tinwelint's children, [Timpinen](/wiki/Tinfang_Warble "Tinfang Warble") and [Tinúviel](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien "Lúthien"), returned later to the Eldar.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[30]</a></sup>

In *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two)*, Tinwelint found Gwendeling by following the song of her nightingales. He stopped to listen for what he thought was a moment, although in reality it lasted many years. He came upon her in the forest, where she lay listening also to the nightingales. Thinking her very beautiful, he began to approach her, but she rose and ran away laughing. He then fell into a deep sleep while she remained, and kept watch over him a long time. His people, who had long ago departed to Valinor, were forgotten by Tinwelint when he at last awoke and found her still nearby. Eventually the two were wed.<sup id="cite_ref-lt2i_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2i-33">[31]</a></sup>

Tinwë Linto/Tinwelint reigned with Wendelin/Gwendeling ([Vëannë](/wiki/V%C3%ABann%C3%AB) and [Ausir](</wiki/Ausir_(boy)> "Ausir (boy)"), children talking to [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol), argued about their names) over the Lost Elves of [Artanor](/wiki/Doriath "Doriath"). Tolkien considered also the name *[Ellu](/wiki/Ellu)* for him.<sup id="cite_ref-lt2i_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2i-33">[31]</a></sup> His children were Tinúviel and [Dairon](/wiki/Daeron "Daeron").<sup id="cite_ref-lt2i_33-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2i-33">[31]</a></sup>
