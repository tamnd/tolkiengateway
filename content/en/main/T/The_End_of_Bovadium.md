<i><b>The End of Bovadium</b></i> (author's title)<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup> or <i><b>The Bovadium Fragments</b></i> is a short story by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) written between mid 1957<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> and early 1960.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> According to [Richard Ovenden](../R/Richard_Ovenden.md), the [Bodleian Library](../B/Bodleian_Library.md)'s librarian, *The Bovadium Fragments* "is about a scholar in the future looking at evidence of a society that is now lost" due to worshipping automobiles.<sup id="cite_ref-telegraph_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-telegraph-5">[5]</a></sup> [John Garth](../J/John_Garth.md) describes *The Bovadium Fragments* as "a satire on modernity, without adventures", taking place in "a post-apocalyptic future when civilisation as we know it has collapsed" because of cars.<sup id="cite_ref-road_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-road-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

### Foreword {#Foreword}

The story begins with a Foreword by [Doctor Sarevelk](../D/Doctor_Sarevelk.md) discussing "[a site](../B/Bovadium.md) of historical"<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> importance "found at *[Vasti](../V/Vâsti.md)*"<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> "on the borders of the [Southern Region](../S/Southern_Region.md)",<sup id="cite_ref-Foreword_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foreword-7">[7]</a></sup> and two" "equally dead and obscure"<sup id="cite_ref-road_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-road-6">[6]</a></sup> languages that are referred to as "*[A](../L/Latin.md)* and *[B](../M/Modern_English.md)*".<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> While Language B has been "almost entirely deciphered" at the time of the story using "the [Rotzopny](../R/Rotzopny.md)–[Dwarf](../D/Dwarf.md) treatment", language A is not. In the second part of the Foreword, [Dr. Gums](../D/Dr._Gums.md) summarizes the three fragments of the story proper and explains his method of translating them in a smug way.<sup id="cite_ref-Foreword_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foreword-7">[7]</a></sup>

### Fragment I {#Fragment_I}

The first of the three fragments discussed is "a chronicle or legend in language A"<sup id="cite_ref-Foreword_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foreword-7">[7]</a></sup> which Fragment II, according to Dr. Gums,<sup id="cite_ref-Foreword_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foreword-7">[7]</a></sup> is a translation of.<sup id="cite_ref-FI_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FI-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Fragment II {#Fragment_II}

The second of the three fragments discussed is a translation and expanded version of Fragment I,<sup id="cite_ref-FII_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FII-9">[9]</a></sup> describing the takeover of Bovadium by the Motores.<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Fragment III {#Fragment_III}

The third of the three fragments discussed is a text in Language B with inserts from language A that describes the aftermath of the end of Bovadium.<sup id="cite_ref-FIII_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FIII-10">[10]</a></sup>

### Postscript by the Editor {#Postscript_by_the_Editor}

The story concludes with a fictitious Postscript by Dr. Gums.<sup id="cite_ref-Postscript_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Postscript-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Other Texts of Fragment II {#Other_Texts_of_Fragment_II}

Following the story, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) describes two additional texts of Fragment II both entitled *[Domine defende nos contra hos Motores bos!](../D/Domine_defende_nos_contra_hos_Motores_bos!.md)*. The first is a poem "in thirty-one octosyllabic couplets"<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> while the second is a Latin text which Christopher Tolkien translated.<sup id="cite_ref-Other_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Other-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

Sometime between mid [1957](../_/1957.md)<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> and early [1960](../_/1960.md),<sup id="cite_ref-october_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-october-13">[13]</a></sup> Tolkien wrote a short<sup id="cite_ref-october_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-october-13">[13]</a></sup> "satire"<sup id="cite_ref-satire_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-satire-14">[14]</a></sup> entitled *The End of Bovadium*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup> or *The Bovadium Fragments*.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-15">[15]</a></sup> Tolkien initially wrote it "for his own amusement" as "a private academic jest" to gently satirize "pomposities of archaeologists" and the "hideousness of college crockery", being inspired by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Denis_Godley" title="wikipedia:Alfred Denis Godley">A.D. Godley</a>'s <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Motor_Bus" title="wikipedia:The Motor Bus">Motor Bus</a></i> comic poem and a "<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=The_Origin_of_Bovadium&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Origin of Bovadium (page does not exist)">recrudescence of the debate</a> about [Oxford](../O/Oxford.md) roads".<sup id="cite_ref-PN_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PN-16">[16]</a></sup>

On [25 October](../_/25_October.md) [1960](../_/1960.md), Tolkien sought (through his secretary <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Elisabeth_Lumsden&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Elisabeth Lumsden (page does not exist)">Elisabeth Lumsden</a>) to publish what is believed to be this writing (since it was described as "a sort of satirical fantasy") in the magazine <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_and_Tide_(magazine)" title="wikipedia:Time and Tide (magazine)">Time and Tide</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-october_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-october-13">[13]</a></sup> In a [letter](../L/Letter_231a.md) to <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Joan_Anne_Tolkien&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Joan Anne Tolkien (page does not exist)">Joan Anne Tolkien</a> in [November](../N/November.md) [1961](../_/1961.md), Tolkien mentioned the suffering of Oxford "from the ravages of the machine-worshippers".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> On [24 August](../_/24_August.md) [1966](../_/1966.md), Tolkien lent [Rayner Unwin](../R/Rayner_Unwin.md) the manuscript,<sup id="cite_ref-august_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-august-19">[18]</a></sup> who wrote to Tolkien already the next day, thinking that he "should publish it in the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Oxford_Magazine&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Oxford Magazine (page does not exist)">Oxford Magazine</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> Later, around the same time,<sup id="cite_ref-PN_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PN-16">[16]</a></sup> [Clyde S. Kilby](../C/Clyde_S._Kilby.md) was also given a copy, being asked if the piece was worthy of publication.<sup id="cite_ref-satire_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-satire-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-august_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-august-19">[18]</a></sup>

On or before [29 November](../_/29_November.md) [1968](../_/1968.md), *The End of Bovadium*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup> was included in a list of unpublished works that Tolkien had his secretary, [Joy Hill](../J/Joy_Hill.md), compile.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> In a letter to Rayner Unwin on [2 December](../_/2_December.md), Tolkien commented that, though *The End of Bovadium* was on the list of unpublished works, he had "no intention of publishing" it to keep it from preventing him from working on his "[proper work](The_Silmarillion.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup>

After Tolkien's death in [1973](../_/1973.md), Clyde S. Kilby would later recall the story in April [1976](../_/1976.md):

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Though the reading of <i>The Silmarillon</i> was proving about as much as I could handle during that summer of 1966, Tolkien from time to time handed me other shorter pieces and asked me about their publishability. One was called “The Bovadium Fragments,” a satire written long before and having as its main point the worship of the <i>Motores</i>, i.e., automobiles, and the traffic jams blocking the roads in and around Oxford. It was full of the inventiveness to be expected of Tolkien. Some of the characters are <a class="mw-redirect" href="../R/Rotzopny.md" title="Rotzopny">Rotzopny</a>, <a class="mw-redirect" href="../D/Dr._Gums.md" title="Dr. Gums">Dr. Gums</a>, and <a class="mw-redirect" href="../D/Doctor_Sarevelk.md" title="Doctor Sarevelk">Sarevelk</a>. I judged that it had two elements that would make it unpublishable. One was the more than liberal use of Latin, and the other the probability that a reader&#39;s eye would focus on its playfulness rather than its serious implications. Actually it was an early comment on the commercialization of our world.<sup id="cite_ref-satire_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-satire-14">[14]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

A few more details about the contents were provided in [Humphrey Carpenter](../H/Humphrey_Carpenter.md)'s summary dating from [1977](../_/1977.md), saying that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>‘The Bovadium Fragments’ (perhaps composed early in the nineteen-sixties) is a parable of the destruction of <a href="../O/Oxford.md" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> (<i>Bovadium</i>) by the <i>motores</i> manufactured by the <i><a href="../D/Daemon.md" title="Daemon">Daemon</a></i> of <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../V/Vaccipratum.md" title="Vaccipratum">Vaccipratum</a></i> (a reference to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Nuffield" title="wikipedia:Lord Nuffield">Lord Nuffield</a> and his motor-works at Cowley) which blocks the streets, asphyxiate the inhabitants, and finally explode.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-15">[15]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

Carpenter also notes that the theme of "motor transport" connects the story to *[Mr. Bliss](../M/Mr._Bliss.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-15">[15]</a></sup> In a footnote to [Letter 181](../L/Letter_181.md) in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)*, Carpenter speculated that Tolkien's statement that "the present design of destroying Oxford in order to accommodate motor-cars"<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> is "a reference to the proposal for a ‘relief’ road through Christ Church Meadow".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup> The writing was given to the [Bodleian Library](../B/Bodleian_Library.md) by [The Tolkien Trust](The_Tolkien_Trust.md) in [1985](../_/1985.md),<sup id="cite_ref-guide_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-24">[23]</a></sup> where it was kept in the Department of Western Manuscripts (Mss. Tolkien, Series A, folder A62, pages 38–91).<sup id="cite_ref-MB_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MB-25">[24]</a></sup> It is likely that the manuscript consists of 53 pages.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> The manuscript was reassigned in [2003](../_/2003.md) from "MS Tolkien A 62"<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup> to "MS. Tolkien B 62" alongside *[Sellic Spell](../S/Sellic_Spell.md)*, *[The New Shadow](The_New_Shadow.md)*, and other notes, thereby making it not available for research.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-24">[23]</a></sup> In [1992](../_/1992.md), *The Bovadium Fragments* was mentioned in Judith Priestman's *[J.R.R. Tolkien: Life and Legend](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien%253A_Life_and_Legend.md)* in the entry about Tolkien's copy of A.D. Godley's *Motor Bus* poem.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[27]</a></sup> Tolkien kept "the poem with his typescript of"<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-3">[3]</a></sup> *The End of Bovadium*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup> On [8 September](../_/8_September.md) of [2010](../_/2010.md), [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) stated on his blog that its "a pity" Tolkien decided "against publishing" *The Bovadium Fragments* "since an anti-car environmental message from Tolkien published in the mid-sixties might have done some good".<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup><br><br>Around [3 May](../_/3_May.md) of [2025](../_/2025.md), it was announced that *The End of Bovadium*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup> would be published as part of *[The Bovadium Fragments: together with The Origin of Bovadium](The_Bovadium_Fragments%253A_together_with_The_Origin_of_Bovadium.md)* on [9 October](../_/9_October.md).<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup> On [9 May](../_/9_May.md), [David Bratman](../D/David_Bratman.md) compared [Humphrey Carpenter](../H/Humphrey_Carpenter.md)'s description of the story to <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=The_Motor-cyclists&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Motor-cyclists (page does not exist)">The Motor-cyclists</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup> one of Tolkien's poems.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[32]</a></sup> On [24 May](../_/24_May.md), [John Garth](../J/John_Garth.md) explained in a blog post that *Vaccipratum* is a Latin name meaning "cow-meadow" and that *Bovadium* is a Latinized name Tolkien coined for [Oxford](../O/Oxford.md).<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup> On [26 May](../_/26_May.md), [Jason Fisher](../J/Jason_Fisher.md) claimed on his blog that the book isn’t likely to feel thin like some of the other "posthumous publications", but rather like "*[Farmer Giles of Ham](../F/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham.md)*, *[Smith of Wootton Major](../S/Smith_of_Wootton_Major.md)*, *[Roverandom](../R/Roverandom.md)*, and *Mr. Bliss*".<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup> On [27 May](../_/27_May.md), Ronald Kyrmse noted in a comment to Fisher's blog post that the names Rotzopny, Dr. Gums, and Sarevelk could be read backwards as impostor, smug, and cleverass respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[35]</a></sup> On [30 May](../_/30_May.md), the story was described by [Chris Smith](../C/Chris_Smith.md), [HarperCollins](../H/HarperCollins.md)'s Tolkien Publishing Director,<sup id="cite_ref-society_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-37">[36]</a></sup> as:<sup id="cite_ref-telegraph_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-telegraph-5">[5]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>a sharply satirical account of the perils of allowing car production and machine-worship to take over your town, where things ultimately all go to hell, in a very literal sense.<sup id="cite_ref-telegraph_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-telegraph-5">[5]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

On [27 June](../_/27_June.md), [Shaun Gunner](../S/Shaun_Gunner.md) noted in an article on the website of [The Tolkien Society](The_Tolkien_Society.md) that *The End of Bovadium*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup> is "Tolkien’s comment on industrialisation and environmental concerns" and is similar but different compared to *Mr. Bliss*.<sup id="cite_ref-society_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-37">[36]</a></sup> On [21 July](../_/21_July.md), Nancy Steinman stated in an article on [TheOneRing.net](TheOneRing.net.md) that *The End of Bovadium*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-2">[2]</a></sup> reflected "Tolkien’s disdain of industrialism", comparing the story to "[The Scouring of the Shire](The_Scouring_of_the_Shire.md)" chapter in *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[37]</a></sup> On [3 October](../_/3_October.md), Sam Leith wrote a harsh criticism of the 2025 book on the website of "The Telegraph".<sup id="cite_ref-criticism_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-criticism-1">[1]</a></sup> In another review, John Garth described "the vision of dead motorists reaching the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styx" title="wikipedia:Styx">River Styx</a> and encountering the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stygian" title="wikipedia:Stygian">stygian</a> ferryman <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charon" title="wikipedia:Charon">Charon</a>" with a "motor boat" was "particularly enjoyable".<sup id="cite_ref-road_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-road-6">[6]</a></sup>
