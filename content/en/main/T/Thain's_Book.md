The **Thain's Book** was a copy of the [Red Book of Westmarch](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch) made in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") and later augmented with additions made in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith).

## History {#History}

[King Elessar](/wiki/King_Elessar) requested a copy of the [Red Book](/wiki/Red_Book) of the [Periannath](/wiki/Periannath) from the Shire, and this book was the first copy ever made, and had most of its original contents, such as [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo)'s "[Translations from the Elvish](/wiki/Translations_from_the_Elvish)". [Thain](/wiki/Thain) [Peregrin Took](/wiki/Peregrin_Took) brought it to Gondor in [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [63](/wiki/Fourth_Age_63 "Fourth Age 63"). In [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith), this book received many annotations and corrections (particularly of Elvish names, words, and quotations).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup> A [Gondorian](/wiki/Gondorian) commentator noted on that book that [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) could not understand well the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) spoken by the [Galadhrim](/wiki/Galadhrim) because of their "accent".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

It was also enhanced with an abbreviated version of "[The Tale of Aragorn and Arwen](/wiki/The_Tale_of_Aragorn_and_Arwen)" (originally written by [Barahir](</wiki/Barahir_(grandson_of_Faramir)> "Barahir (grandson of Faramir)"), the grandson of [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir)) concentrating on the parts outside of the context of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is believed that the great-grandson of Peregrin requested in turn an exact copy of that book, and in [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [172](/wiki/Fourth_Age_172 "Fourth Age 172"), the [King's Writer](/wiki/King%27s_Writer), [Findegil](/wiki/Findegil), made an exact copy of the book in Minas Tirith, and that copy was brought and kept in the [Great Smials](/wiki/Great_Smials).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup>

Other subsequent copies of the Red Book omit Bilbo's "Translations", so the copy of the Great Smials was of particular interest, as they are preserved only there.<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup>
