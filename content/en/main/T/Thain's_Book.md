The **Thain's Book** was a copy of the [Red Book of Westmarch](../R/Red_Book_of_Westmarch.md) made in [the Shire](The_Shire.md) and later augmented with additions made in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md).

## History {#History}

[King Elessar](../K/King_Elessar.md) requested a copy of the [Red Book](../R/Red_Book.md) of the [Periannath](../P/Periannath.md) from the Shire, and this book was the first copy ever made, and had most of its original contents, such as [Bilbo](../B/Bilbo.md)'s "[Translations from the Elvish](Translations_from_the_Elvish.md)". [Thain](Thain.md) [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) brought it to Gondor in [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [63](../F/Fourth_Age_63.md). In [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md), this book received many annotations and corrections (particularly of Elvish names, words, and quotations).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup> A [Gondorian](../G/Gondorian.md) commentator noted on that book that [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) could not understand well the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) spoken by the [Galadhrim](../G/Galadhrim.md) because of their "accent".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

It was also enhanced with an abbreviated version of "[The Tale of Aragorn and Arwen](The_Tale_of_Aragorn_and_Arwen.md)" (originally written by [Barahir](<../B/Barahir_(grandson_of_Faramir).md>), the grandson of [Faramir](../F/Faramir.md)) concentrating on the parts outside of the context of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is believed that the great-grandson of Peregrin requested in turn an exact copy of that book, and in [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [172](../F/Fourth_Age_172.md), the [King's Writer](../K/King's_Writer.md), [Findegil](../F/Findegil.md), made an exact copy of the book in Minas Tirith, and that copy was brought and kept in the [Great Smials](../G/Great_Smials.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup>

Other subsequent copies of the Red Book omit Bilbo's "Translations", so the copy of the Great Smials was of particular interest, as they are preserved only there.<sup id="cite_ref-Records_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-1">[1]</a></sup>
