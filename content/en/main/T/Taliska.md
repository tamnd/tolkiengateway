**Taliska**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> was a [Mannish](/wiki/Mannish) language spoken by the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) of the [House of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor) and [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador).

## History {#History}

The ancestors of the [folk of Marach](/wiki/House_of_Hador "House of Hador") ("Greater Folk") and [of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor") ("Lesser Folk") were originally one group when they migrated to the West. In the northern [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn) they met some [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup> who probably influenced their language.<sup id="cite_ref-DM_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DM-3">[2]</a></sup> It was at the Northern shore of the [Sea of Rhûn](/wiki/Sea_of_Rh%C3%BBn) that the tribes separated. They didn't meet often, and their languages soon diverged.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

The House of Bëor, at least, had long had dealings with the [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor) east of the [Ered Luin](/wiki/Ered_Luin) and, having learned much of their speech, their language resembled the Elven-tongue in "many words and devices".<sup id="cite_ref-DM_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DM-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-men_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-men-5">[4]</a></sup> The Taliska spoken by these Men was thus considered a dialect of [East-Danian](/wiki/East-Danian).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

When the two tribes reached [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand), they already spoke different dialects. The language of the folk of Marach was more pure and they viewed the dialect of the folk of Bëor, with the adopted Elvish elements, of lower status.<sup id="cite_ref-DM_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DM-3">[2]</a></sup>

The language of the House of Bëor eventually almost went extinct, both because of the adoption of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and because the House was mostly annihilated after the [Dagor Bragollach](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup> On the other hand, the House of Hador did not wholly abandon their language, it survived the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) and was still spoken when the Edain migrated to [Elenna](/wiki/Elenna); thus it later became [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic).<sup id="cite_ref-men_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-men-5">[4]</a></sup>

## Related languages {#Related_languages}

The Greater Folk seemed to have been related to the ancestors of the [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen),<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> whose language eventually evolved in the various [languages of the Northmen](/wiki/Northern_Mannish "Northern Mannish") in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-app_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-app-9">[8]</a></sup>

The folk of Haleth spoke the unrelated [Haladin](/wiki/Haladin) tongue, which was unintelligible to the Houses of the Northern Tribes of Men.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

[Helge Fauskanger](/wiki/Helge_Fauskanger) and Ellen Schousboe noted several changes in the conception of Taliska:<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>

- The name "Taliska" later referred to the language of the Houses of Bëor and *Haleth* whereas it was the House of Hador that spoke another language, which would eventually be conceived as [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic). In an early text by Tolkien, some words he labels as Taliska refer to terms which in the later version would be used by the [Haladin](/wiki/Haladin).

- Much later, in the essay "[Of Dwarves and Men](/wiki/Of_Dwarves_and_Men)" (published in *[The Peoples of Middle-Earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-Earth)*), Tolkien wrote that the Bëorians and Halethians did not speak related languages: The Hadorians still spoke "ancient Adûnaic", and the Bëorians had a closely related language; it was the language of the Halethians that was unrelated to both of them (see [pre-Númenorean](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmenorean "Pre-Númenorean")).

The later conception establishes a wider Hadorian/Beorian language, which however is not given a name. The fate of the term *Taliska* is vague, and while it could refer to the Halethian isolate (cf. the older conception) it is used sometimes conventionally to refer to the Hadorian/Beorian family.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Taliska was an early language developed by Tolkien. Unlike his later languages which are <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_priori_(languages)" title="wikipedia:A priori (languages)">a priori</a></i>, it was based directly on the Germanic languages, and has a lot in common with the [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic) language, an early interest of Tolkien;<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> this shows that Tolkien tried to connect his mythos with the origins of Europe, something that was evident in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.

The connection with Germanic persisted in the framework of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. The Mannish languages that would derive from Adûnaic, are rendered as [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic), [Norse](/wiki/Norse), [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), modern English, etc. Non-Taliskan languages are usually rendered with [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) elements, if at all.<sup id="cite_ref-app_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-app-9">[8]</a></sup> (q.v. for more references).

A rather complete grammar and syntax of Taliska is known to exist,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup> but despite work by [Tolkienists](/wiki/Tolkienists), this has not yet been published.
