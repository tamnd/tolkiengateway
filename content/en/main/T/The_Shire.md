The **Shire** was a region in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) inhabited by [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits).

## Geography {#Geography}

The Shire is described as a small but beautiful and fruitful land, beloved by its inhabitants. The Hobbits had an extensive agricultural system in the Shire, but did not proceed with industrialisation. Various supplies could be found in the Shire, including cereals, fruit, wood and [pipe-weed](/wiki/Pipe-weed "Pipe-weed") (a favourite treat of Hobbits).

The Shire was quite densely populated in parts with many villages and a few towns, but it still was open enough to allow for wide forested areas and marshes. There were green, rolling hills and freshly tilled earth, along with gardens and flower boxes on window sills.

Thanks to the protective presence of the [Rangers of the North](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North) in the lands of the former Arnor, the Shire for centuries ignored the wide world outside, despite being traversed by the [East Road](/wiki/East_Road).

From the west to the east, the Shire originally measured 40 leagues (120 miles) from the [Far Downs](/wiki/Far_Downs) to the [Brandywine Bridge](/wiki/Brandywine_Bridge). From the north to the south, it measured 50 leagues (150 miles) from the [northern moors](/wiki/North_Moors "North Moors") to the [marshes in the south](/wiki/Marshes_in_the_south "Marshes in the south").<sup id="cite_ref-FRHobbits_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRHobbits-1">[1]</a></sup> The total area of the Shire must have extended roughly about 20,000 square miles.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup>

In [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1452](/wiki/Fourth_Age_31 "Fourth Age 31"), [King Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") gave the [Westmarch](/wiki/Westmarch) to the [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits) as a gift. This extended the western boundaries of the Shire to the [Tower Hills](/wiki/Tower_Hills).<sup id="cite_ref-AppB5_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB5-3">[3]</a></sup> [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland), to the east of the Brandywine, remained an independent region occupied by [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits).

The Shire was a fertile and well-tilled part of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor), with many royal farms, [cornlands](/wiki/Corn "Corn"), vineyards, and woods. It had long been deserted during the waning days of the Kingdom when it was known as the splinter-realm of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain);<sup id="cite_ref-FRHobbits_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRHobbits-1">[1]</a></sup> it had been the hunting grounds of the [King of Arnor](/wiki/King_of_Arnor).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup> The [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) from [Bree](/wiki/Bree) got official permission from King [Argeleb II](/wiki/Argeleb_II) at Norbury ([Fornost](/wiki/Fornost)) to settle the lands. This was finally done in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1601](/wiki/Third_Age_1601 "Third Age 1601") (Year 1 in [Shire Reckoning](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning)) by Hobbits from [Bree](/wiki/Bree), led by the brothers [Marcho](/wiki/Marcho) and [Blanco](/wiki/Blanco).<sup id="cite_ref-FRHobbits_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRHobbits-1">[1]</a></sup> They were joined by other hobbits who lived in [Dunland](/wiki/Dunland) and parts of depopulated [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur).

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d3/Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="167" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d3/Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d3/Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_One_Morning_Long_Ago.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1072" data-file-height="596"/></a><figcaption><i>One Morning Long Ago</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

By 30 years later, almost all of the Hobbits of Middle-earth could be found in the Shire. The [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits) considered themselves as subjects of the [King](/wiki/Kings_of_Arthedain "Kings of Arthedain"), at least nominally, considering the isolation of their country. Nevertheless the Hobbits sent some support troops to the great battles Arnor fought against [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar). After the fall of Arnor, the Shire remained a minor but independent political unit and the title of [Thain](/wiki/Thain) was established to fill the absence of a Kingship.<sup id="cite_ref-FRHobbits_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRHobbits-1">[1]</a></sup>

Its small size, relative lack of importance, and brave and resilient Hobbit population made it too modest an objective for conquest. More important was that the Shire was guarded and protected by the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) [Rangers of the North](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North) (and [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)), who watched the borders and kept out intruders. The only strangers to enter the Shire were the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) travelling on the [East Road](/wiki/East_Road) that ran through the Shire to and from their mines in the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains), and the occasional [Elves](/wiki/Elves) on their way to the [Emyn Beraid](/wiki/Emyn_Beraid) or the [Grey Havens](/wiki/Grey_Havens).

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2340](/wiki/Third_Age_2340 "Third Age 2340"), the [Oldbucks](/wiki/Oldbucks) of the [Marish](/wiki/Marish) crossed the [Brandywine](/wiki/Brandywine) because of overpopulation and founded [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland).

However, the Shire faced its adversities since it was inside the range of the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1636](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636"), and [Goblins](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains "Orcs of the Misty Mountains") from [Mount Gram](/wiki/Mount_Gram) invaded the Shire until they were driven out after the [Battle of Greenfields](/wiki/Battle_of_Greenfields) ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2747](/wiki/Third_Age_2747 "Third Age 2747")). The Shire also suffered because of the [Long Winter](/wiki/Long_Winter) ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2758](/wiki/Third_Age_2758 "Third Age 2758")) soon followed by the [Days of Dearth](/wiki/Days_of_Dearth) ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2760](/wiki/Third_Age_2760 "Third Age 2760")) and the [Fell Winter](</wiki/Fell_Winter_(Third_Age)> "Fell Winter (Third Age)") ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2911](/wiki/Third_Age_2911 "Third Age 2911")) when [White Wolves](/wiki/White_Wolves) entered the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-2">[2]</a></sup>

[Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) followed Gandalf's interest for the Hobbits and put spies in Bree and Southfarthing. In the years leading up to the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), his spies saw that the Shire was closely guarded by the Rangers. When Gandalf suspected that Bilbo Baggins possessed the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring) the guard was doubled<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-2">[2]</a></sup> (perhaps at his request to [Chieftain](/wiki/Chieftains_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain "Chieftains of the Dúnedain") [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn)).

### War of the Ring {#War_of_the_Ring}

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), the Shire was first visited by the Nine [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl") when the Rangers were off to the War. This left the Shire defenseless, leaving it open to be captured by Sharkey (Alias of Saruman) and some [Ruffians](/wiki/Ruffians), through the use of [Lotho Sackville-Baggins](/wiki/Lotho_Sackville-Baggins). It was liberated after [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"), [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee"), [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") returned from Gondor. Their victory at the [Battle of Bywater](/wiki/Battle_of_Bywater) and the death of Saruman put a final end to the War.

Yet the physical damage which Saruman caused by forced industrialisation was undone by the Hobbits' efforts and the soil from [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) (given to Sam by [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel)) and [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3021](/wiki/Third_Age_3021 "Third Age 3021") was considered to be the most productive and prosperous year in their history.

### Fourth Age {#Fourth_Age}

With [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn)'s return as the King of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), the Shire joined the [Reunited Kingdom](/wiki/Reunited_Kingdom)<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> and in [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [6](/wiki/Fourth_Age_6 "Fourth Age 6") became a Free Land, protected enclave inside the Kingdom; a royal order forbade the entrance of any one other than [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) into the Shire. In [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [31](/wiki/Fourth_Age_31 "Fourth Age 31"), King Aragorn extended the land westwards to [Emyn Beraid](/wiki/Emyn_Beraid); this land was named [Westmarch](/wiki/Westmarch) and many hobbits relocated there.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Administration {#Administration}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/8/80/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg/250px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="105" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/8/80/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg/375px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/8/80/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg/500px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_-_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_-_The_Shire.jpg 2x" data-file-width="956" data-file-height="402"/></a><figcaption>The Shire in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i></figcaption></figure>

At its founding, the Shire was a part of Arthedain and the Hobbits considered themselves to be subjects of the King. It was only after the fall of Arthedain that the Shire became an independent state. Once independent, the Hobbits of the Shire selected a **[Thain](/wiki/Thain)** to lead them. The office of Thain was hereditary, initially belonging to the [Oldbuck](/wiki/Oldbuck_Family "Oldbuck Family") family. When the Oldbucks left the Shire for [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland), the office passed to the [Took](/wiki/Took_Family "Took Family") family of [Tuckborough](/wiki/Tuckborough). While nominally the Thain ruled over the four [Farthings](/wiki/Farthings), in practice authority was so decentralised that the title was seen as more of a formality and ceremonial.

The Shire was originally divided into four [Farthings](/wiki/Farthings) ([Northfarthing](/wiki/Northfarthing), [Southfarthing](/wiki/Southfarthing), [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing), and [Westfarthing](/wiki/Westfarthing)). Within the Farthings there were smaller, unofficial divisions, such as towns or regions associated with particular families. The [Tooks](/wiki/Took_Family "Took Family"), for instance, mostly lived in or near [Tuckborough](/wiki/Tuckborough). In many cases, Hobbit family names were derived from their places of origin. For example, [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee)'s surname was derived from the town of [Gamwich](/wiki/Gamwich) whence the family originated.

The only elected official of the Shire was the **[Mayor of Michel Delving](/wiki/Mayor_of_Michel_Delving)** in the [White Downs](/wiki/White_Downs) (by extension seen as the mayor of the Shire). The Mayor's chief duties were serving as postmaster of the Shire's [Messenger Service](/wiki/Messenger_Service), charging the [Watch](/wiki/Watch), and presiding at fairs.

The sole law enforcement officials in the Shire were the [Shirriffs](/wiki/Shirriffs). Their primary responsibility was the protection of the Shire from trespassers. There were three in each Farthing and were distinguished from "civilians" by a feather worn in their caps. The [Bounders](/wiki/Bounders) were the land's border-guard, charged with turning back unwanted people or beasts; their number varied according to need.

After [Westmarch](/wiki/Westmarch) was added to the Shire, the Thain established the title of [Warden of Westmarch](/wiki/Warden_of_Westmarch). This was a hereditary office with unclear responsibilities. It was first given to the [Fairbairns](/wiki/Fairbairn_family "Fairbairn family"), descendants of Samwise Gamgee.

Buckland, which was not part of the Shire proper, was controlled by the **[Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland)** at [Bucklebury](/wiki/Bucklebury). The office of Master was established by the Oldbucks when they left the Shire. Their descendants continued to hold the office when they became the [Brandybucks](/wiki/Brandybuck_Family "Brandybuck Family").

## Etymology {#Etymology}

A "shire" ([Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *scír*) is an organised region with a county town, which in the case of the Hobbits' Shire was [Michel Delving](/wiki/Michel_Delving).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-8">[7]</a></sup> [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") notes that the usual Germanic word for "district", represented in Dutch by *gouw* and in German by *Gau*, was replaced in English by *scír* at an early date. *Ge*, the English cognate of these words, survives only in a handful of place-names, such as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey" title="wikipedia:Surrey"><i>Surrey</i></a> (from Old English *Suðer-ge*, "southern district").<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-8">[7]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I could draw you a map of every inch of [Sarehole] [...] I loved it with an intensity of love that was a kind of nostalgia reversed [...] It was a kind of lost paradise and it was wonderful.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a> interview to John Ezard<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

On Tolkien's maps, the Shire is located at about the same position as England is on modern European maps and has been cited as an example of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_England" title="wikipedia:Deep England">Deep England</a> ideology (though, of course, England is on an island while the Shire is a landlocked country). Throughout the narrative, Tolkien also implies numerous points of similarity between the two, such as weather, agriculture and dialect. One can also see England as Tolkien's source of inspiration for the Shire in its very name. Tolkien had spent his youth in [Sarehole](/wiki/Sarehole), Worcestershire.

According to [Paula Marmor](/wiki/Paula_Marmor), the hobbit brothers [Marcho](/wiki/Marcho) and [Blanco](/wiki/Blanco) bear names related to horses, parallel to [Hengest](/wiki/Hengest) and [Horsa](/wiki/Horsa), the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengest_and_Horsa" title="wikipedia:Hengest and Horsa">legendary leaders</a> who brought the Saxons to Britain. Hengest was the founder of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent" title="wikipedia:Kent">Kent</a> whose geography is similar to the Shire (North Downs above, hills to the west, water to the east and marshes to the southeast).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

The industrialisation of the Shire was based on Tolkien's witnessing of the extension of the Industrial Revolution to rural Warwickshire during his youth, and especially the deleterious consequences thereof. The rebellion of the hobbits and the restoration of the pre-industrial Shire may be interpreted as a prescription of voluntary simplicity as a remedy to the problems of modern society.
