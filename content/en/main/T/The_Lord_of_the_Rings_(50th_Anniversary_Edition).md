<b><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (50th Anniversary Edition)</b> was published in [21 October](../_/21_October.md), [2004](../_/2004.md) by [Houghton Mifflin](../H/Houghton_Mifflin.md).

## Features {#Features}

- Leather Bound
- Slipcase
- Gilded pages
- Two fold-out maps
- Ribbon bookmark
- Colored inserts of the [Book of Mazarbul](../B/Book_of_Mazarbul.md) by J.R.R. Tolkien
- Dimensions 15.7 x 10 x 2.7 inches
- Weight 4.6 pounds

## Background {#Background}

The text of *The Lord of the Rings* has been revised and corrected multiple times throughout its several publications and editions, but new errors, mainly typographical, always have been introduced, especially after each resetting of type;<sup id="cite_ref-xliv_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xliv-1">[1]</a></sup> notable cases were when the printer reset the metal types of *[The Fellowship of the Ring](The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)* soon after its publication, and in [1994](../_/1994.md) when [HarperCollins](../H/HarperCollins.md) digitized the text.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Both J.R.R. Tolkien and his son, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), had prepared lists of such errors to guide emendations for future editions. Steven M. Frisby had also made comparisons of different *LotR* copies, and researched Tolkien's original manuscripts in the libraries at the [Marquette University](../M/Marquette_University.md). [David Bratman](../D/David_Bratman.md) also authored *A Corrigenda to The Lord of the Rings* in [The Tolkien Collector](The_Tolkien_Collector.md) #6. Similar lists and comments have been made independently by scholars such as [Douglas Anderson](../D/Douglas_Anderson.md), [Charles Noad](../C/Charles_Noad.md) and [Arden Smith](../A/Arden_Smith.md).

[Wayne Hammond](../W/Wayne_Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) for many years also studied and compared the texts and their inconsistencies. Consulting the abovementioned works, along with their own examination of the Marquette manuscripts, the [Allen and Unwin](../A/Allen_and_Unwin.md) archive, unpublished correspondence between Tolkien and Allen &amp; Unwin, and of course [volumes 6-9](The_History_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) of *[The History of Middle-earth](The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)*, they had compiled "an exhaustive and up to date catalogue" of errors, corrections and their emendations throughout the publication's history.

For the 50th anniversary, the couple proposed a new effort to produce a text as close as possible to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/authorial_intent" title="wikipedia:authorial intent">authorial intent</a>. Tolkien's publishers and the [Tolkien Estate](Tolkien_Estate.md) agreed. HarperCollins provided searchable text files that helped detect inconsistencies in spelling and punctuation, despite Tolkien's stated preferences. They presented 229 such questionable points to Christopher Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Supervised by Christopher Tolkien, and based on the latest compared text, the couple made fixes to typographical errors and mistakes, and corrections to the text when it was certain that the latest text reflected Tolkien's preference. Whenever those were clear, spelling and capitalization were regularized. More substantial textual mistakes (see below) were conservatively considered by case.<sup id="cite_ref-xlii_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xlii-4">[4]</a></sup> Christopher Tolkien was positive in "getting rid" of such inconsistencies that distract observant readers, pointing out that were his father notified about them, he would immediately correct them.<sup id="cite_ref-xliv_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xliv-1">[1]</a></sup>

An example of textual mistake has been the phrase "from the lowlands to the Dwarf-kingdom" (Book II, Chapter 6) which has been changed to "from the lowlands **of** the Dwarf-kingdom" in a subsequent edition (whereas Moria had no lowlands); the couple restored it to its original correct form.<sup id="cite_ref-xlii_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xlii-4">[4]</a></sup>

Some mistakes that the editors failed to notice, or new ones introduced by bugs of the software, were further corrected for the [2005](../_/2005.md) reprint.<sup id="cite_ref-xliv_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xliv-1">[1]</a></sup>
