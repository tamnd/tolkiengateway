The **Ransom trilogy**,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> commonly known as the **Space Trilogy**<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup> is a series of novels by [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s friend and fellow [Inkling](/wiki/Inklings "Inklings").

The story of the first book, *Out of the Silent Planet*, shares a common genesis with Tolkien's unfinished *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)*. The two men had a conversation about the shortcomings of Science Fiction literature and Lewis remarked that "there is too little of what we really like in stories", suggesting they try their hand at the genre "as amateurs":<sup id="cite_ref-Letter294_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter294-2">[2]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>We... meant each to write an excursionary &#39;Thriller&#39;: a Space-journey and a Time-journey (mine) each discovering Myth.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a><sup id="cite_ref-Letter24_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter24-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Lewis' 'Space-journey' became *Out of the Silent Planet* and was published in [1938](/wiki/1938) by The Bodley Head. Lewis initially tried to interest [Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin) in the work, but despite the [positive review](/wiki/Letter_26 "Letter 26") Tolkien gave the publishers they passed.

The work belongs to the obscure subgenre of "Theological/Christian Science Fiction". The last part is much darker and occult in tone, similar to such works of fellow Inkling [Charles Williams](/wiki/Charles_Williams), who had some influence on Lewis.

An alternate version of the preface to *[The Screwtape Letters](/wiki/The_Screwtape_Letters)* (1943) includes references to Ransom, Malacandria, and Old Solar.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Tolkien noted that some of his onomatopoeia influenced names in this work, including [Numinor](/wiki/Numenor "Numenor") (*That Hideous Strength*), [Eldil](/wiki/Eldar "Eldar") (*Out of the Silent Planet*) and [Tor](/wiki/Tuor "Tuor") and [Tinidril](/wiki/Idril "Idril") (*Perelandra*).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Elwin Ransom {#Elwin_Ransom}

The protagonist of the trilogy is Dr. <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Ransom" title="wikipedia:Elwin Ransom">Elwin Ransom</a>, sharing the name with <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Elwin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Elwin (page does not exist)">Elwin</a>, one of the characters in Tolkien's contemporary *The Lost Road*. The modern form of [Alboin](/wiki/Alboin)/[Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), the name means "[Elf-friend](/wiki/Elf-friend)", a frequent motif in Tolkien's [Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium).

Dr. Ransom is apparently modelled in part on Tolkien (as well as Lewis himself and [Charles Williams](/wiki/Charles_Williams)): he was born in [1898](/wiki/1898) (like Lewis, Tolkien's junior by 6 years), fought in [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I) and [the Somme](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme "Battle of the Somme"), he is a philologist at an Oxbridge university (Tolkien was at [Oxford University](/wiki/Oxford_University)), and has a special interest in languages. Lewis himself <a class="external text" href="https://the-silent-planet.fandom.com/wiki/C.S._Lewis_(Character)" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">appears as a character</a> in the books, as a friend of Ransom's.

Tolkien played down this similarity on multiple occasions, admitting it only in that both were philologists.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter24_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter24-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Letter77_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter77-7">[7]</a></sup>
