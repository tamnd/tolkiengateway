<i><b>J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s The Lord of the Rings</b></i> is an animated film directed by [Ralph Bakshi](/wiki/Ralph_Bakshi), and released to theaters in 1978. It was an adaptation of the first half of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. Bakshi's most ambitious effort (and his most famous after his animated adaptation of the underground comic *Fritz the Cat*), the film was produced by [Saul Zaentz](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz)'s Fantasy Films, and distributed to theaters by United Artists.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

Long ago, in the early years of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), the great Elven-smiths forged Rings of Power — Nine for mortal [Men](/wiki/Men), Seven for the [Dwarf-lords](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves"), and three for the fair [Elf-rulers](/wiki/Elves "Elves"). But then, the Dark Lord [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) learned the craft of ring-making and made the Master Ring — [The One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring) to rule them all. With the One Ring, [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) is his and he cannot be overcome. As the last alliance of Men and Elves fell beneath his power, the Ring fell into the hands of Prince [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) of the mighty kings from across the sea. However, he did not destroy the ring, and, because of this, the spirit of Sauron lived on and began to take shape and grow again.

The Ring had a will of its own, and had a way of slipping from hand to hand, so that it might at last get back to its master. The Ring lay in the bottom of a lake for thousands of years. During those years, Sauron captured the nine Rings that were made for Men and turned their owners into the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl") - terrible shadows under his great shadow who roamed the world searching for the One Ring.

The Ring, meanwhile, was found by two friends. One of them, [Sméagol](/wiki/Gollum "Gollum"), was so enticed by the Ring's power that he killed his friend [Déagol](/wiki/D%C3%A9agol) to get it. Sméagol possessed the Ring for hundreds of years, during which it warped him into a twisted, gurgling wretch known only as Gollum, until his "Precious" was discovered (some might say stolen) by the hobbit [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins).

Several years later, in a land called [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), Bilbo is celebrating his 111th (or eleventy-first, as it is called) birthday, on the same day that his nephew [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") celebrates his 33rd birthday (his "coming of age"). During his speech, Bilbo slips the Ring on, and confusion arises as the party notices that their host has suddenly disappeared into thin air!

[Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) the wizard, however, knows the truth behind this act. In Bilbo's hobbit hole, Gandalf tells him to leave the Ring for Frodo, but Bilbo seems unwilling to give it up. He does, finally, agree to do so, and leaves the Shire.

Gandalf realises that the "magic" ring is in fact the One Ring forged by Sauron, the evil lord. He knows the Shire is in danger - soon Sauron will learn that the Ring is in the possession of a Baggins and send his "wraiths" after him. Heeding Gandalf's advice, Frodo leaves his home, taking the Ring with him. He hopes to reach [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), where he will be safe from Sauron, and where those wiser than him can decide what to do about the Ring. In his journey, he is accompanied by three hobbit friends, [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took"), [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck"), and [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee"). From the start, they are pursued by Black Riders (Sauron's Ringwraiths or Nazgûl). Narrowly escaping these and other dangers and meeting other interesting characters en route, they eventually come to [Bree](/wiki/Bree), where they meet [Strider](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") (who ultimately reveals himself to be Aragorn, heir to the Throne of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor)), another friend of Gandalf who leads them the rest of the way to Rivendell through further hardships. Frodo is stabbed upon the mountain of [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop) by the [chief of the Nazgûl](/wiki/Witch-king "Witch-king"), with a "[Morgul blade](/wiki/Morgul-knife "Morgul-knife")" — as part of the knife stays inside him, he gets sicker on the rest of the journey. Meanwhile, Gandalf rides to [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard) to consult with the head of his order of Wizards, Saruman. Saruman wants the Ring for himself and Gandalf refuses. For this, Saruman imprisons him on the top of [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc), his tall tower. Gandalf sends out a call for help and the great [eagle](/wiki/Eagles "Eagles") [Gwaihir](/wiki/Gwaihir) bears him away to [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell).

The Hobbits and Aragorn travel with the gravely injured Frodo toward Rivendell. On the road, they meet [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas), an [Elven](/wiki/Sindar "Sindar") friend of Aragorn's, who puts Frodo on [his horse](/wiki/Asfaloth "Asfaloth"). They travel together for a while, until the Ringwraiths close in on them. Alone, Frodo rides to the [Ford of Bruinen](/wiki/Ford_of_Bruinen). He crosses the stream and defies the Wraiths, refusing their temptation to come with them to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond), lord of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), uses his magic to summon a huge wave in the shape of horses right as the Wraiths cross the stream, washing them away.

At Rivendell, Frodo recovers from his wound. Gandalf explains why he could not meet with them at Bree and Frodo reunites with his uncle Bilbo whom he had not seen since he left Hobbiton years before. Bilbo seems much older and weaker, and, for a terrible moment, is once again held in sway of the Ring. Together, they attend the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond). Bilbo, Gandalf, and others argue about what should be done with the One Ring. Finally, Frodo stands up, and wilfully volunteers to go to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), where the Ring can be destroyed. Frodo sets forth from Rivendell with eight companions: two Men, [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir), son of the [Steward](/wiki/Stewards_of_Gondor "Stewards of Gondor") of the land of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor); an [Elven](/wiki/Elves "Elves") prince, Legolas; Frodo's old friend and powerful wizard, Gandalf; [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) the [Dwarf](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves"); and Frodo's original three hobbit companions. These Nine Walkers were chosen to represent all the free races of Middle-earth and as a balance to the Nine Riders.

Their attempt to cross the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) is foiled by heavy snow, so they are forced to take a path under the mountains via [Moria](/wiki/Moria), an ancient Dwarf kingdom, now full of [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) and other evil creatures, where Gandalf falls into the abyss after battling a [Balrog](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane "Durin's Bane").

The remaining eight members of the Fellowship then spend some time in the Elf-haven of [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien). They leave Lórien by the river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), and Frodo begins to realise the Ring is having a malevolent effect on some members of the party - especially Boromir, who tries to take the Ring from Frodo. In the process, Frodo puts it on to escape him. The Fellowship reunite before splitting up to look for Frodo. Aragorn orders Boromir to go with Merry and Pippin. Sam and Frodo leave together for Mordor, ending the Fellowship of the Ring. Boromir is killed by Orcs while trying to defend Merry and Pippin, whom the Orcs capture. Aragorn, Gimli and Legolas send Boromir over the [Rauros-falls](/wiki/Rauros "Rauros") in a funeral boat. Rather than following Sam and Frodo, the Three decide to track Merry and Pippin and rescue them from the Orcs.

Sam and Frodo discover that [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) is stalking them. They attack him and, while he is at their mercy, he swears to serve them. He tells them he knows a way across the [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes) and they set off.

Merry and Pippin are led towards [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard) by the Orcs, who are commanded by the [Uruk](/wiki/Uruk-hai "Uruk-hai") [Uglúk](/wiki/Ugl%C3%BAk). While they stop to rest, the Orcs are assailed by [Éored](/wiki/%C3%89ored) of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan), led by [King](/wiki/King_of_Rohan "King of Rohan") [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden)'s nephew, [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer). The Orcs hold their position. [Grishnákh](/wiki/Grishn%C3%A1kh), a [Mordor-orc](/wiki/Orcs_of_Mordor "Orcs of Mordor"), tries to kill Merry and Pippin, believing them to have [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). An Éored slays Grishnákh and Merry and Pippin flee into [Fangorn Forest](/wiki/Fangorn_Forest). There, they encounter [Treebeard](/wiki/Treebeard), a treelike giant known as an [Ent](/wiki/Ents "Ents"). Ents generally keep to themselves, but Treebeard is opposed to the menace posed to the trees by the wizard Saruman.

The Three Hunters track Merry and Pippin into Fangorn. There, they meet a white wizard who they at first believe to be [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), but who turns out to be their wizard friend Gandalf, whom they believed had perished in the mines of Moria. He tells them of his fall into the abyss, his battle to the death with the Balrog and his reawakening.

Gandalf tells them that Merry and Pippin are safe and the four ride to Rohan's capital, [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras). Meanwhile, [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), with his servant [Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma"), rally the [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai) to attack Rohan. Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli try to persuade King Théoden that his people are in danger. Théoden's advisor, Gríma, staunchly denies these claims and defends Saruman. Gandalf realises that Saruman's advisor, [Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma"), whom he saw at Orthanc, is Gríma, working against Théoden to keep had him subdued and weak for years. Wormtongue flees. Aragorn, Gimli and Legolas, having convinced Théoden, travel to the defensive fortification [Helm's Deep](/wiki/Helm%27s_Deep) with Théoden and the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) while Gandalf goes north in search of Éomer's men in the north of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) to bring as reinforcements.

In the Marshes, Sam, Frodo and Gollum evade a Ringwraith, riding a [Fell beast](/wiki/Fell_beast). After leaving the Marshes Frodo walks slowly, feeling the weight of the Ring. Gollum tries to take it from him and Frodo orders him to never again attempt this, lest he put the Ring on and command Gollum to kill himself. At night, Gollum ponders how to kill the Hobbits and take it for himself. He thinks that "[she](/wiki/Shelob "Shelob")" might help him.

At Helm's Deep, Théoden's forces resist an onslaught of Uruk-hai sent by Saruman. Meanwhile, Frodo and Sam accept that they may not return from Mount Doom. The Rohirrim are driven back into the [caves](/wiki/Glittering_Caves "Glittering Caves"). They sit in silence, mulling over their losses. Théoden approaches Aragorn and proposes that they die in a noble way, one worthy of a song, rather than "taken like an old badger in a trap". Aragorn agrees and, at dawn, Théoden sounds [Helm](/wiki/Helm)'s horn and the Rohirrim ride forth from the caves, slaying Uruks. Eventually, they are cornered. When all hope seems lost, Gandalf returns with Éomer's forces and they beat back the Uruks, ending the first part of the history of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).

## Differences from the book {#Differences_from_the_book}

The movie makes a few deviations from the book, but overall follows Tolkien's narrative quite closely. Many parts of the novel explaining the transition from one part of the plot to another were omitted which makes the middle part of the movie somewhat difficult to follow, if the viewer is unfamiliar with the story.

- In the film's prologue it is suggested that the Elves created the 19 [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power) for their Kings, and for those of the Dwarves and Men. Sauron then *learned* the craft of ring-making *after* that. In the original story it is Sauron who teaches the Elven smiths this ability, and only after their defiance he distributed them to the Dwarves and Men.
- Another prologue deviation suggests that the Last Alliance of Men and Elves was losing the war, which contradicts the original story.
- As in Tolkien's novel, Saruman the White adopts the title "Saruman of Many Colours"; however, his robes are neither white nor multi-coloured, but red.
- Saruman is sometimes called "Aruman". It is believed that this name was derived to keep viewers from confusing the character with Sauron, but it is not done consistently.
- [Gríma Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma") is seen giving Saruman his staff when [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) meets with him in [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc). In [Meduseld](/wiki/Meduseld), Gandalf knows for a fact that Gríma is an agent of Saruman, since he had already seen him in the Wizard's tower.
- The film greatly condenses Frodo's journey from [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) to [Bree](/wiki/Bree); for example, [Crickhollow](/wiki/Crickhollow), [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall), the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest), and the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs) do not appear. [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil) also does not appear in the film.
- The scene where the Nazgûl arrive in the hobbits' room at [The Prancing Pony](/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony) and begin slashing at their beds only to find that they are not there, and pillows have been placed to form the figures of their bodies is not in the book. A passage does appear that states that hobbit beds wind up slashed during the night, but the townsfolk of Bree are the perpetrators, not the Nazgûl. This scene also appears in Jackson's version.
- [Bill Ferny](/wiki/Bill_Ferny) is omitted, though the travellers leave Bree with [Bill the Pony](/wiki/Bill_the_Pony).
- The [first elf](/wiki/Glorfindel "Glorfindel") which the hobbits and Aragorn encounter on their journey is changed to [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas).
- [Arwen](/wiki/Arwen) does not appear, nor is she mentioned.
- Aragorn carries a broken sword (presumably [Narsil](/wiki/Narsil)) up to the Rivendell section of the story, where he presents it at the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond). However, the sword's reforging into [Andúril](/wiki/And%C3%BAril) is never shown (or mentioned) in the film, even though Aragorn carries an unbroken blade for the remainder of the film.
- [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) briefly tells the Hobbits the tale of [Beren](/wiki/Beren) and [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien), but does recite [the song](/wiki/Song_of_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien "Song of Beren and Lúthien"). Instead, Bilbo recites it with the Elves in Rivendell, in place of [the Eärendillinwë](/wiki/Song_of_E%C3%A4rendil "Song of Eärendil").
- Gimli appears to be about the same height as the rest of the non-hobbits in the Fellowship.
- Bill the Pony is killed by the [Watcher in the Water](/wiki/Watcher_in_the_Water).
- The [orc-chieftain](/wiki/Orc-chieftain "Orc-chieftain") is not accompanied by a group of Orcs, instead attacking alone.
- The [Gifts of Galadriel](/wiki/Gifts_of_Galadriel) are omitted.
- The term Uruk-hai is not used, though the rivalry between the Orcs of Mordor and Isengard remains.
- [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn) makes only a brief appearance and has no spoken dialogue.
- While [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer) is mentioned, he is never officially identified (thus, he also has no spoken dialogue). Also, as in the Jackson films, it is *he* and his forces with whom Gandalf arrives near the end of the Battle of Helm's Deep instead of [Erkenbrand](/wiki/Erkenbrand)'s.
- [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir) does not appear, nor is he mentioned.
- The [Fire of Orthanc](/wiki/Fire_of_Orthanc) is renamed the "Fire of Isengard" and they appear as magical projectiles.

## Production {#Production}

### Animation {#Animation}

Much of the film used live-action footage which was then rotoscoped to produce an animated look <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.ralphbakshi.com/biography/images/38.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[1]</a> <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.ralphbakshi.com/biography/images/39.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[2]</a>. This saved production costs and arguably gave the animated characters a more realistic look. For the live-action portion of the production, Bakshi and his cast and crew arrived in Spain where the rotoscope models acted out their parts in costume. The actions of [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) were performed by [Billy Barty](/wiki/Billy_Barty). [Sharon Baird](/wiki/Sharon_Baird) performed the part of [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and John A. Neris was Gandalf. <a class="external autonumber" href="http://books.google.com/books?id=fTI1yeZd-tkC&amp;pg=PA154&amp;lpg=PA154&amp;dq=%22aesop+aquarian%22+%22billy+barty%22+%22sharon+baird%22&amp;source=web&amp;ots=C5jvDP0VA9&amp;sig=_W2bXiWtu0mfcOJYZOxMBjapJR8&amp;hl=nl&amp;sa=X&amp;oi=book_result&amp;resnum=1&amp;ct=result" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[3]</a>

Of the rotoscoping, Bakshi said "I didn't start thinking about shooting the film totally in live action until I saw it really start to work so well. I learned lots of things about the process, like rippling. One scene, some figures were standing on a hill and a big gust of wind came up and the shadows moved back and forth on the clothes and it was unbelievable in animation. I don't think I could get the feeling of cold on the screen without showing snow or an icicle on some guy's nose. The characters have weight and they move correctly."

"Making two pictures (the live action reference and the actual animated feature) in two years is crazy. Most directors when they finish editing, they are finished; we were just starting. I got more than I expected. The crew is young. The crew loves it. If the crew loves it, it's usually a great sign. They aren't older animators trying to snow me for jobs next year."<a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.jimhillmedia.com/article_printer.php?id=768" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[4]</a>

Following the live-action shoot, each frame of the live footage was then broken down into individual frames, and then printed out, and placed behind animation cels. The details of each frame were copied and painted onto cels. Both the live-action and animated sequences were storyboarded. <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.ralphbakshi.com/gallery/displayimage.php?album=5&amp;pos=20" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[5]</a> <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.ralphbakshi.com/gallery/displayimage.php?album=5&amp;pos=10" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[6]</a> <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.ralphbakshi.com/gallery/displayimage.php?album=5&amp;pos=11" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[7]</a>

### Voice cast {#Voice_cast}

After finishing in Spain, Bakshi went to the [BBC](/wiki/BBC)'s recording studios in London. He cast several stars - William Squire, John Hurt - and used several of the BBC Repertory Company's stock actors, like Peter Woodthorpe, Anthony Daniels, Norman Bird and Michael Graham Cox. The actors recorded the lines with each other, but were required to pause for two seconds after each line, to smoothen the recording. This caused some stilted dialogue in the finished product <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.anthonydaniels.com/qa/index.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[8]</a>.

## Reception {#Reception}

The film was originally intended to be distributed as <i><b>The Lord of the Rings, Part One</b></i>, but United Artists dropped the "Part One" from the title, believing moviegoers would not pay full ticket prices to see half a movie. Says Ralph Bakshi: "United Artists at that time was terrified to say 'Part One.' I remember sitting in meetings screaming my head off saying, 'You can't do this!' Had it said 'Part One,' I think everyone would have respected it. But because it didn't say 'Part One,' everyone came in expecting to see the entire three books, and that's where the confusion comes in" <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.tolkiengesellschaft.de/v4/alleszutolkien/filme/bakshi/bakshisoutingheute.shtml" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[9]</a>. In interviews, Bakshi sometimes referred to the film as *The Lord of the Rings, Part One.*

Critics were generally mixed in their responses to the film. Roger Ebert called Bakshi's effort a "mixed blessing" and "an entirely respectable, occasionally impressive job ... \[which\] still falls far short of the charm and sweep of the original story." Vincent Canby of the *New York Times* called the film "both numbing and impressive". Film website Rotten Tomatoes, which compiles reviews from a wide range of critics, gives the film a score of 50.

Despite criticism, the film grossed $30,471,420 at the box office <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=lordoftherings78.htm" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[10]</a> (the budget was $8 million), but United Artists, who believed the film to be a flop, refused to fund a sequel which would have completed Tolkien's story on film.

## Follow-up {#Follow-up}

In light of the theatrical *The Lord of the Rings, Part Two* adaptation being scrapped, a means of "finishing" Tolkien's story and making it more complete for audiences then inadvertently fell on the [Rankin/Bass](/wiki/Rankin/Bass) animation studio (fresh on the heels of the success of its previous [TV adaptation of *The Hobbit*](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(1977_film)> "The Hobbit (1977 film)")) in producing an animated TV special based on the [final part](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King "The Return of the King") of *The Lord of the Rings*. Their adaptation of *[The Return of the King](</wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)> "The Return of the King (1980 film)")* finished the story and answered most of the questions raised by Bakshi's animated film. Despite this, several unresolved story developments between the ending of *The Lord of the Rings* and the beginning of *The Return of the King* were left unexplained, especially the Ents' march on Isengard (along with Merry eventually taking up with the Army of Rohan, and Gandalf and Pippin's subsequent journey to Gondor), the betrayal of Frodo by Gollum, and the attack of [Shelob](/wiki/Shelob) the monster spider-creature on Frodo and Sam. It should be noted, however, that production for this Rankin/Bass follow-up had begun even before their version of *The Hobbit* had originally aired (let alone before Bakshi's theatrical film had premiered)<a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.nytimes.com/1977/11/27/books/tolkien-hobbitani.html?_r=0" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[11]</a>, so Rankin/Bass can't be entirely blamed for this flaw in continuity.

[Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) (the rights holder to the post-1974 Rankin/Bass library and most of the Saul Zaentz theatrical backlog) has released *The Hobbit,* *The Lord of the Rings,* and *The Return of the King* on home video (VHS, DVD and Blu-ray). Both of these films are packaged separately and they are placed as a boxed-set "trilogy" of films.
