<i><b>J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s The Hobbit</b></i> was the first film adaptation of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, created by [Gene Deitch](/wiki/Gene_Deitch) in 1967. The film was produced by [William Lawrence Snyder](/wiki/William_Lawrence_Snyder) and took less than a month to create. The film was approximately 12 minutes long and was only created so Snyder could extend his license for *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* and sell it back to Tolkien and his publishers, which he did for $100,000.<sup id="cite_ref-Deitch_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deitch-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Plot {#Plot}

The story begins with [Dale](/wiki/Dale), the city of Golden Bells, just before [Men came to power](/wiki/Dominion_of_Men "Dominion of Men") in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). But then the [Monster Lizard](/wiki/Dragons "Dragons") [Slag the Terrible](/wiki/Smaug "Smaug"), the Agent of Evil on [Earth](/wiki/Arda "Arda"), came and ransacked the Golden Realm of Dale, taking all of their treasures for himself, including the precious [Arkenstone](/wiki/Arkenstone) of Old, the White Heart of the [Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"). Only General [Thorin Oakenshield](/wiki/Thorin "Thorin"), [Princess Mika](/wiki/Mika_Milovana "Mika Milovana") and a lone, unnamed watchman (who had been asleep when Slag attacked) survived. Desperate, they turned to the [Great Wizard](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards") [Gandalf the Grey](/wiki/Gandalf "Gandalf") in his lonely tower. He pointed them to a prophecy of the Dragon's death in the [Great Book](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch "Red Book of Westmarch"), for which they needed a [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits"). The whole party went to the [house](/wiki/Bag_End "Bag End") of [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) who, living a different sort of life than his [ancestor](/wiki/Bandobras_Took "Bandobras Took"), initially refused the task requested of him, but after some persuasion from Gandalf and the princess, joined the three on their quest.

Soon, they encountered two "[Groans](/wiki/Trolls "Trolls")", monstrous creatures that turned into trees when exposed to sunlight. Bilbo's companions were captured, but Bilbo tricked the two into a fight until the sun rose. They continued their journey, and General Oakenshield and the Princess started to appreciate their burglar. But suddenly, Bilbo disappeared. He had fallen through a crevasse in the mountain, and fell past the evil "[Grablins](/wiki/Orcs "Orcs")" into the lake where "[Goloom](/wiki/Gollum "Gollum")" dwelt. He found Goloom's [magic ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"), and dashed away through the tunnels. He hid behind a rock, and allowed Goloom to overtake him so he could find his way out.

After travelling through [Mirkwood Forest](/wiki/Mirkwood "Mirkwood"), Bilbo came to the [Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain). He stole the Arkenstone, crafted a bow from mining tools, and shot the Arkenstone through Slag's heart. Slag was killed, and Dale restored. Bilbo returned to [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) with Princess Mika as his wife.

## Production {#Production}

The film was produced under tight deadlines. As Gene Deitch lived in Czechoslovakia at the time, he employed his friend <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Adolf_Born&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Adolf Born (page does not exist)">Adolf Born</a>, Czech painter and illustrator, as the cinematographer. Born’s visual contributions gave the film its distinctive animated style. Though the film was conceived primarily as a contractual “ashcan copy” to preserve adaptation rights, Born’s participation links the project to the broader tradition of Czech animation and illustration. His later career established him as one of Central Europe’s most celebrated illustrators, but *The Hobbit* remains a notable early example of his work in international film.<sup id="cite_ref-Deitch_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deitch-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Status {#Status}

The film remained unknown to Tolkien fans until 2012, when Deitch posted on his blog about the film's history. The film was long considered lost until it was rediscovered by Snyder's son and released on YouTube in 2012.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Currently, it is available online.
