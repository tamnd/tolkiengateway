**Tyelpetéma** was one of the five [témar](/wiki/T%C3%A9mar "Témar") or sound-series of the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) writing system.

The Tyelpetéma appears to have been unique; while each of the other four series consisted of a set of six individual 'letters', the palatal sounds of the Tyelpetéma were formed by adding a diacritic mark of two dots beneath another character. This is the equivalent of adding a 'y' after the letter in English to form a single distinct consonant sound.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> As an example, the sound 'ty' here has the same sound as the initial consonant in the (British pronunciation of) the English word 'tune' - something close to, but distinct from, 'ch'.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The names of the tengwar of that series were: [Tyelpe](/wiki/Tyelpe), [Indyo](/wiki/Ando "Ando"), <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%BAle&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Thúle (page does not exist)">Istyar</a>, [Intya](/wiki/Anto "Anto"), [Nyelle](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen "Númen") and [Arya](/wiki/%C3%93re "Óre").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In languages like the [Westron](/wiki/Westron), which made much use of consonants such as our *ch*, *j*, *sh*, Series III was usually applied to these; in which case Series IV was applied to the normal *k*-series (*[calmatéma](/wiki/Calmat%C3%A9ma "Calmatéma")*). In Quenya, which possessed besides the *calmatéma* both a palatal series (*tyelpetéma*) and a labialize series (*[quessetema](/wiki/Quessetema "Quessetema")*), the palatals were represented by a Fëanorian diacritic denoting ‘following *y*’ (usually two underposed dots), while Series IV was a *kw*-series.

The representation of the sounds here is the same as that employed in transcription and described above, except that here ‘’ch’’ represents the ‘’ch’’ in English ‘’church’’; ‘’j’’ represents the sound of English ‘’j’’, and ‘’zh’’ the sound heard in ‘’azure’’ and ‘’occasion’’.<sup id="cite_ref-RotK_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RotK-4">[4]</a></sup> <span></span>
