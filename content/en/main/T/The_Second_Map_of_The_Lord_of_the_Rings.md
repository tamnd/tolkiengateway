<b>The Second Map of <i>The Lord of the Rings</i></b> is a map that was drawn by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) during the writing of *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-SecondMap_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SecondMap-1">[1]</a></sup>

It was his working map during the writing of book V of The Lord of the Rings. It has map-squares of 2 centimetres side. Its scale is 2 centimetres for 100 miles. The map squares have the same letters and numbers as on [The First Map of The Lord of the Rings](The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md). It was drawn in black ink. Red ink was used for a few alterations. In the final state corrections and additions were made in blue ink, in blue crayon and pencil.<sup id="cite_ref-SecondMap_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SecondMap-1">[1]</a></sup> J.R.R. Tolkien used contour lines for the mountains and hills on this map.<sup id="cite_ref-ContourLines_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ContourLines-2">[2]</a></sup> Lossarnach seems to have been a larger region than on the [General Map of Middle-earth](../G/General_Map_of_Middle-earth.md), but it is possible that the long name did not fit in the small space of the real size of the region of Lossarnach.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> It includes a dot with the name [Tarnost](Tarnost.md) in the map square Q 12,<sup id="cite_ref-Tarnost_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tarnost-4">[4]</a></sup> southeast of the crossings over the river [Ringló](../R/Ringló.md) and on the northeasternmost slopes of the mountains of [Belfalas](../B/Belfalas.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Tarnost is not included on any other map.<sup id="cite_ref-Tarnost_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tarnost-4">[4]</a></sup> In addition, the line of the journey of [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md), [Sam](../S/Sam.md) and [Gollum](../G/Gollum.md) from the [Emyn Muil](../E/Emyn_Muil.md) to the [Black Gate](../B/Black_Gate.md) is included on the map.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

The Second Map of *The Lord of the Rings* has not been published, but redrawings drawn by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) of its western and eastern portion with the vertical line of squares with the number 14 repeated on both portions are included in the book *[The War of the Ring](The_War_of_the_Ring.md)*, in chapter XIV "The Second Map".<sup id="cite_ref-ContourLines_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ContourLines-2">[2]</a></sup> The contents of the map are described in the same chapter.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
