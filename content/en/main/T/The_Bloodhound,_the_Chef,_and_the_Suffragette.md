<i><b>The Bloodhound, the Chef, and the Suffragette</b></i> is an unpublished comic play<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) for his Incledon relatives during his vacation at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnt_Green" title="wikipedia:Barnt Green">Barnt Green</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire" title="wikipedia:Worcestershire">Worcestershire</a><sup id="cite_ref-guide_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-2">[2]</a></sup> on Christmas in [1912](/wiki/1912). In the performance of the play as the seasonal theatrical<sup id="cite_ref-drama_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-drama-3">[3]</a></sup>, Tolkien played "Professor Joseph Quilter"<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-4">[4]</a></sup>, the lead role. The ties between the plot of the play and "Tolkien's own circumstances" regarding [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Tolkien "Edith Tolkien") are obvious.<sup id="cite_ref-chronology_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronology-5">[5]</a></sup>

<p>&#39;Professor Joseph Quilter, M.A., B.A., A.B.C., alias world-wide detective Sexton Q. Blake-Holmes, the Bloodhound&#39;, who is searching for a lost heiress named Gwendoline Goodchild. She meanwhile has fallen in love with a penniless student whom she meets while they are living in the same lodging-house, and she has to remain undiscovered by her father until her twenty-first birthday in two days&#39; time, after which she will be free to marry.
</p>
