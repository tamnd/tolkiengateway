**The Departure of Boromir** is the thirty-ninth scene of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)* and the forty-fifth of [*The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring* (extended edition)](<The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(extended_edition).md>). This scene was extended in the latter edition.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

After defeating [Lurtz](../L/Lurtz.md), a bloody [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) rushes to the wounded [Boromir](../B/Boromir.md) who is lying on the ground.

Boromir tells Aragorn that [Merry](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) and [Pippin](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) have been taken by the [Uruk-hai](../U/Uruk-hai.md) and asks about [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md). Aragorn glances at him while trying to heal his wounds and tells him he let Frodo go. Boromir admits to Aragorn that he tried to take the [Ring](The_One_Ring.md) from Frodo. and feels he has failed the [Fellowship](../F/Fellowship.md). However, Aragorn tells him he fought bravely.

Boromir is certain that [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) will take control of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), and that [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) will fall. But Aragorn swears to him *"I will not let the [White City](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) fall, nor our people fail"*. A dying Boromir fixates on Aragorn's acknowledgement of his ties to [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and the race of [Men](../M/Men.md), and reaches for his sword. Aragorn places it in his hands as [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md) and [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md) arrive in Boromir's final moments.

*"I would have followed you my brother. My captain. My king,"* Boromir says to Aragorn with a smile and a touch of reverence, before he passes away.

## Differences {#Differences}

While this scene occurs towards the end of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)*, in the books, the corresponding events occur in the first chapter of *[The Two Towers](The_Two_Towers.md)*.

In terms of differences in content, in this scene, Boromir is found pierced by three arrows by Lurtz – a character created for the movie – whereas in the books, Boromir is pierced by many arrows shot by [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md); he was found by Aragorn leaning against a tree with the bodies of many Orcs around him.<sup id="cite_ref-TT_Departure_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TT_Departure-1">[1]</a></sup> Aragorn's vulnerability is much more evident in the book; following the death of Boromir, Aragorn states *"Vain was [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md)'s trust in me"*, and *"All that I have done today has gone amiss"*.<sup id="cite_ref-TT_Departure_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TT_Departure-1">[1]</a></sup> In this scene, he is much more accepting of Boromir's death and (in the [next scene](<The_Road_Goes_Ever_On..._(scene).md>)) the wider events that have befallen the [Fellowship](../F/Fellowship.md).
