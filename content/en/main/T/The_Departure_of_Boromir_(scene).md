**The Departure of Boromir** is the thirty-ninth scene of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* and the forty-fifth of [*The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring* (extended edition)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(extended_edition)> "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (extended edition)"). This scene was extended in the latter edition.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

After defeating [Lurtz](/wiki/Lurtz), a bloody [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) rushes to the wounded [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir) who is lying on the ground.

Boromir tells Aragorn that [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") have been taken by the [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai) and asks about [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"). Aragorn glances at him while trying to heal his wounds and tells him he let Frodo go. Boromir admits to Aragorn that he tried to take the [Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") from Frodo. and feels he has failed the [Fellowship](/wiki/Fellowship). However, Aragorn tells him he fought bravely.

Boromir is certain that [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) will take control of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), and that [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) will fall. But Aragorn swears to him *"I will not let the [White City](/wiki/Minas_Tirith "Minas Tirith") fall, nor our people fail"*. A dying Boromir fixates on Aragorn's acknowledgement of his ties to [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) and the race of [Men](/wiki/Men), and reaches for his sword. Aragorn places it in his hands as [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) and [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) arrive in Boromir's final moments.

*"I would have followed you my brother. My captain. My king,"* Boromir says to Aragorn with a smile and a touch of reverence, before he passes away.

## Differences {#Differences}

While this scene occurs towards the end of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*, in the books, the corresponding events occur in the first chapter of *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)*.

In terms of differences in content, in this scene, Boromir is found pierced by three arrows by Lurtz – a character created for the movie – whereas in the books, Boromir is pierced by many arrows shot by [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs); he was found by Aragorn leaning against a tree with the bodies of many Orcs around him.<sup id="cite_ref-TT_Departure_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TT_Departure-1">[1]</a></sup> Aragorn's vulnerability is much more evident in the book; following the death of Boromir, Aragorn states *"Vain was [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)'s trust in me"*, and *"All that I have done today has gone amiss"*.<sup id="cite_ref-TT_Departure_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TT_Departure-1">[1]</a></sup> In this scene, he is much more accepting of Boromir's death and (in the [next scene](</wiki/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On..._(scene)> "The Road Goes Ever On... (scene)")) the wider events that have befallen the [Fellowship](/wiki/Fellowship).
