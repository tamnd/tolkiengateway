**Tar-Minastir** ([S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1474](/wiki/Second_Age_1474 "Second Age 1474") – [1873](/wiki/Second_Age_1873 "Second Age 1873"), aged 399 years) was the eleventh [King of Númenor](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor), ruling for 138 years, from [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1731](/wiki/Second_Age_1731 "Second Age 1731") to [1869](/wiki/Second_Age_1869 "Second Age 1869").<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Tar-Minastir was the grandson of King [Tar-Súrion](/wiki/Tar-S%C3%BArion). Before becoming King he used the name Minastir. He succeeded his aunt, Queen [Tar-Telperiën](/wiki/Tar-Telperi%C3%ABn).<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Telperien_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Telperien-2">[2]</a></sup> He acquired his name Minastir after building a high tower upon the hill of [Oromet](/wiki/Oromet) near [Andúnië](/wiki/And%C3%BAni%C3%AB) and the west shores of Númenor, to spend time gazing westwards.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-1">[1]</a></sup>

During his time, in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), the [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron) began in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1693](/wiki/Second_Age_1693 "Second Age 1693"),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) invaded [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1695](/wiki/Second_Age_1695 "Second Age 1695")<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and the realm of [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion) fell. Sauron then advanced to [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) and besieged [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell).<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-6">[6]</a></sup> Minastir, who loved the Eldar, but envied them,<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-1">[1]</a></sup> sent a navy under the command of [Ciryatur](/wiki/Ciryatur) to Lindon to aid the Elves in [1700](/wiki/Second_Age_1700 "Second Age 1700").<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Telperien_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Telperien-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA1700_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA1700-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup> Ciryatur's forces utterly routed Sauron's army in the [Battle of the Gwathló](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Gwathl%C3%B3) and reinforcements from Lindon and [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) helped defeat the enemy. Sauron retreated back to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor).<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-6">[6]</a></sup>

The Númenóreans who had tasted power in Middle-earth in the battles with Sauron, began establishing dominions on the coasts of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) starting around the year 1800.<sup id="cite_ref-SA_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-11">[10]</a></sup>

Tar-Minastir was one of the last "good" Kings of the realm, as his successors started becoming greedy of wealth and power.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

In 1869<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Minastir_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Minastir-1">[1]</a></sup> his son, [Tar-Ciryatan](/wiki/Tar-Ciryatan), constrained Tar-Minastir to surrender the sceptre before he would have done of his free will.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ciryatan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ciryatan-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Minastir* is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it might mean "Watch Tower" as a compound of a shortened form *minas* of *minassë* ("tower"), and a form of *tir* ("to watch").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup> Like all the rulers of Númenor who took their royal names in Quenya, Minastir added the prefix *tar-* ("high") to his name when he received the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Sceptre of Númenor").<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>

## Notes {#Notes}

<span><div class="mw-references-wrap"><ol class="references">
<li id="cite_note-10"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-10">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">In <i>The Tale of Years</i> it is stated that &#34;Tar-Minastir&#34;, his name with the prefix <i>Tar-</i> of the King of Númenor, sent a great navy from Númenor to Lindon in <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Age_1700" title="Second Age 1700">1700</a> (see <i>citation 7</i>). This event and date are restated in <i>The History of Galadriel and Celeborn</i>, mentioning &#34;Tar-Minastir the King&#34; (see <i>citation 6</i>). Yet in <i>The Line of Elros</i> Tar-Telperiën is said to have lived to <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Age_1731" title="Second Age 1731">1731</a> and not have given up the sceptre until that time, which would mean she was the ruler of Númenor when the great navy was sent (see <i>citation 2</i>). <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien</a> could not account for this discrepancy (see <i>citation 8</i>). It is possible to reconcile this discrepancy if Minastir sent the fleet in a capacity, such as <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">Regent</a> or <a href="/wiki/Captain_of_the_King%27s_Ships" title="Captain of the King&#39;s Ships">Captain of the Queen&#39;s Ships</a> before he ascended the throne and that later chroniclers simply referred to him as &#34;Tar-&#34; or &#34;the King&#34; without taking into account that he had not taken the sceptre and had not formally become the king when he sent the fleet to Lindon. In a <i>Note on the delay of Gil-galad and the Númenóreans</i> it is mentioned that the great host of &#34;Minastir&#34; landed in Middle-earth (see <i>citation 9</i>).</span>
</li>
</ol></div></span>
