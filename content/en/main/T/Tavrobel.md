**Tavrobel** was a village in [Tol Eressëa](Tol_Eressëa.md), according to the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*. It later became known as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Haywood" title="wikipedia:Great Haywood">Great Haywood</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Tol_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tol-1">[1]</a></sup>

The village was located in the west of the [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md)-speaking area of Tol Eressëa,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> near the confluence of the rivers [Afros](../A/Afros.md) and [Gruir](../G/Gruir.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Afr_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Afr-3">[3]</a></sup> where [Tram Nybol](Tram_Nybol.md), the grey bridge of Tavrobel, spanned the two rivers.<sup id="cite_ref-Nyb_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nyb-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

After the [March of Liberation](../M/March_of_Liberation.md) and the defeat of [Melko](../M/Melko.md), the exiled [Gnomes](../G/Gnomes.md) settled on the Lonely Isle and made many settlements, one of them being Tavrobel. One of its most famous inhabitants was [Gilfanon](../G/Gilfanon.md), the Lord of Tavrobel,<sup id="cite_ref-Afr_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Afr-3">[3]</a></sup> who lived in the [House of the Hundred Chimneys](../H/House_of_the_Hundred_Chimneys.md) nearby.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tower_of_Tavrobel&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tower of Tavrobel (page does not exist)">Tower of Tavrobel</a> was located there.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> The great [Pine of Tavrobel](../P/Pine_of_Tavrobel.md) was located there as well, which reached the [stars](../S/Stars.md). There, according to one outline, after Melko's escape from his second imprisonment, [Telimektar](Telimektar.md) son of [Tulkas](Tulkas.md) and [Ingil](../I/Ingil.md) son of [Inwë](../I/Inwë.md) chased Melko up the Pine, all the way to [Ilwë](../I/Ilwë.md). Afterwards, the Pine was cut down, and Melko was stranded outside of the world, perpetually guarded by Ingil and Telimektar, preventing his return until the [Great End](../G/Great_End.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

### Arrival of Eriol and the Faring Forth {#Arrival_of_Eriol_and_the_Faring_Forth}

Sometime during the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century" title="wikipedia:5th century">5th century AD</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> a [Mannish](../M/Men.md) mariner called [Eriol](../E/Eriol.md) arrived to Tol Eressëa, and was eventually hosted by Gilfanon at his home in Tavrobel, where he compiled the *[Golden Book](../G/Golden_Book.md)* from the tales he had heard from the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), and was then allowed to drink *[limpë](../L/Limpë.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Eri_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eri-9">[9]</a></sup>

During the [Faring Forth](../F/Faring_Forth.md), when the island of Tol Eressëa was dragged across the Ocean and anchored off the coast of the [Great Lands](../G/Great_Lands.md), there occurred a [great battle](../B/Battle_of_the_Heath_of_the_Sky-roof.md) not far from Tavrobel, on the [Heath of the Sky-roof](../H/Heath_of_the_Sky-roof.md). Following the battle, Men occupied Tol Eressëa, and the remaining Elves fled over Afros and Gruir, and Eriol<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Eri_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eri-9">[9]</a></sup> wrote the last words of the *Golden Book* in an empty Tavrobel.<sup id="cite_ref-Afr_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Afr-3">[3]</a></sup>

Later, Tavrobel was conquered by [Heorrenda](../H/Heorrenda.md) son of Eriol, and became known as *Gréata Hægwudu*, and eventually as *Great Haywood*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Tavrobel* is in [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md), meaning "Wood-home", consisting of the elements <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tavros_(Gnomish_word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tavros (Gnomish word) (page does not exist)">tavros</a></i> ("forest, wooded land") + *[pel](../P/Pel.md)* ("village, hamlet").<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

An earlier form of the name was *Taurobel*.<sup id="cite_ref-Nyb_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nyb-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Another (Gnomish) name for the place was <i><b>Tavrost</b></i>, from *tavros* + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Rost_(Gnomish_word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rost (Gnomish word) (page does not exist)">rost</a></i> ("slope, hill side, ascent").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup>

The [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) cognates of *Tavrobel*/*Tavrost* are <i><b>Taurossë</b></i> and <i><b>Tavaros(së)</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>

The place was also known as <i><b>Hægwudu se gréata</b></i> or <i><b>Gréata Hægwudu</b></i> in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Early legendarium {#Early_legendarium}

In some of the outlines for the continuation of *The Book of Lost Tales*, [Tolkien](Tolkien.md) decided to completely change the nature of the framework of the tales. In that reimagining of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), Tol Eressëa ceased to be identified with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a>/<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles" title="wikipedia:British Isles">British Isles</a> - instead, it became a completely different island, and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain" title="wikipedia:Great Britain">Great Britain</a> became the island of [Luthany](../L/Luthany.md).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup>

So, as opposed to the previous conception, where the Elves of the Great Lands sailed directly to Tol Eressëa following the defeat of Melko in the [March of Liberation](../M/March_of_Liberation.md), the Elves now sailed to Luthany instead.

There, they built many cities and settlements - and, after their *eventual* return to Tol Eressëa, named many places there *after* the ones in Luthany. For example, the village of Tavrobel (the New) in Tol Eressëa was named after **Tavrobel the Old** in Luthany.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>

In some of the contemporary texts, it is said that after [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md) wedded [Earissë](../E/Earissë.md) the daughter of [Lindo](../L/Lindo.md), he went to live in Tavrobel, where he had two sons - Heorrenda and [Hlúdwine](../H/Hlúdwine.md).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup>

According to one text, it is said that Ælfwine wrote the *Golden Book* and left it in the House of the Hundred Chimneys, "where it lieth still to read for such as may".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

According to the later legendarium, from the [1937](../_/1937.md) *[Quenta Silmarillion](<../Q/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road).md>)*, **Tavrobel** or **Tathrobel**<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[note 2]</a></sup> was the home of [Pengolod](../P/Pengolod.md) the Wise in Tol Eressëa, a [loremaster](../L/Lambengolmor.md) of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md), after his return to the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../W/West.md" title="West">West</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Gon_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gon-21">[19]</a></sup>

It was later visited by [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md), the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="wikipedia:Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> mariner, where Pengolod showed him the histories of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-Gon_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gon-21">[19]</a></sup>

The final mention of Tathrobel in the legendarium is in the preamble to *[the Later Quenta Silmarillion](The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion.md)* from the 1950s.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[20]</a></sup>

#### Later etymology {#Later_etymology}

The name *Tavrobel* as used above is in [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md). In *[The Etymologies](The_Etymologies.md)*, a text from the 1930s, it is explained as consisting of <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tavor&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tavor (page does not exist)">tavor</a></i> ("woodpecker, knocker") + *[gobel](../G/Gobel.md)* ("village").<sup id="cite_ref-Ety_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ety-23">[21]</a></sup>

The name was afterwards changed to *Tathrobel*.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[22]</a></sup> According to [John Garth](../J/John_Garth.md), *Tathrobel* means "willow-home" in Noldorin.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[23]</a></sup>

In Ælfwine's translation of the *[Annals of Valinor](The_Earliest_Annals_of_Valinor.md)* to Old English, Tavrobel is called <i><b>Taþrobel</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[24]</a></sup>

#### Tavrobel in Brethil {#Tavrobel_in_Brethil}

In *The Etymologies*, Tavrobel was also considered to be the name of a settlement of [Túrin](Túrin.md) and the [Woodmen](../H/Haladin.md) of [Brethil](../B/Brethil.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ety_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ety-23">[21]</a></sup> See: [Amon Obel](../A/Amon_Obel.md).

In the *[Grey Annals](../G/Grey_Annals.md)* from the 1950s, there likewise appears a location in Brethil called Tavrobel. It was later changed to [Bar Haleth](../B/Bar_Haleth.md), probably being a transient name for <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../E/Ephel_Brandir.md" title="Ephel Brandir">Ephel Brandir</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[25]</a></sup>

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) notes that real-life Great Haywood and the two Tavrobels are near the confluence of two rivers: The Great Haywood: <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Sow" title="wikipedia:River Sow">Sow</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent" title="wikipedia:River Trent">Trent</a>; Tavrobel of Tol Eressea: Afros and Gruir; Tavrobel of Brethil: [Taiglin](Taeglin.md) and [Sirion](../S/Sirion.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Elk_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elk-28">[26]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tavrobel was inspired by the village of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Haywood" title="wikipedia:Great Haywood">Great Haywood</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire" title="wikipedia:Staffordshire">Staffordshire</a>, where [Edith Bratt](../E/Edith_Bratt.md) lived for a while after her marriage to J.R.R. Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-Tol_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tol-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien was recovering in Great Haywood after returning from the [Great War](../G/Great_War.md), where he started writing [The Book of Lost Tales](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md).<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[27]</a></sup>

The two rivers of Tavrobel, Afros and Gruir, were probably inspired by the rivers <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Sow" title="wikipedia:River Sow">Sow</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent" title="wikipedia:River Trent">Trent</a>, which reach their confluence near Great Haywood.<sup id="cite_ref-Elk_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elk-28">[26]</a></sup> Also, Tram Nybol, the bridge of Tavrobel, was most likely based on the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_Bridge,_Staffordshire" title="wikipedia:Essex Bridge, Staffordshire">Essex Bridge</a>, which spans the river Trent near its confluence with Sow.<sup id="cite_ref-Nyb_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nyb-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Heath of the Sky-roof, located about a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_(unit)" title="wikipedia:League (unit)">league</a> from Tavrobel, might have been inspired by the Hopton Heath, a place where a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hopton_Heath" title="wikipedia:Battle of Hopton Heath">battle</a> of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_English_Civil_War" title="wikipedia:First English Civil War">English Civil War</a> was fought in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1643" title="wikipedia:1643">1643</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Elk_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elk-28">[26]</a></sup>

According to [John Garth](../J/John_Garth.md), however, the Heath of the Sky-roof could have been inspired by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase" title="wikipedia:Cannock Chase">Cannock Chase</a>, a small area of countryside not far from Great Haywood, instead. Likewise, the [Brook of Glass](../B/Brook_of_Glass.md), a stream near the Heath of the Sky-roof, mentioned in one outline, was most likely based on the Sher Brook that flows through the Cannock Chase.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[28]</a></sup>

Gilfanon's home, the House of the Hundred Chimneys, might have been based, according to G.L. Elkin, Acting Director of the Shugborough Estate, on the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shugborough_Hall" title="wikipedia:Shugborough Hall">Shugborough Hall</a>, which features prominent chimneys.<sup id="cite_ref-Elk_28-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elk-28">[26]</a></sup> Alternatively, it might have been inspired by a house called Gipsy Green near <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penkridge" title="wikipedia:Penkridge">Penkridge</a> in Staffordshire.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[29]</a></sup>
