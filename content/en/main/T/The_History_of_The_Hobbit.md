<i><b>The History of The Hobbit</b></i> is a two-volume study of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s novel *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)*, published in [2007](../_/2007.md).

It contains Tolkien's unpublished drafts of the novel, with commentary by [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md). It also details Tolkien's various revisions to *The Hobbit*, including abandoned revisions for the unpublished third edition of the work, intended for 1960, as well as previously unpublished original maps and illustrations drawn by Tolkien himself.

An abbreviated version was published by [HarperCollins](../H/HarperCollins.md) in [2015](../_/2015.md) as <i><b><a href="../A/A_Brief_History_of_The_Hobbit.md" title="A Brief History of The Hobbit">A Brief History of The Hobbit</a></b></i>. This version retains Tolkien's text and the textual notes provided by Rateliff but omits his essays on various aspects of the texts.

## First edition {#First_edition}

The first edition of *The History of The Hobbit* was released in two volumes:

1. <b>Part One: <i>Mr. Baggins</i></b>
2. <b>Part Two: <i>Return to Bag-End</i></b>

First in May and June of [2007](../_/2007.md) in the U.K. by HarperCollins, then in September in the U.S. by Houghton Mifflin, which also released it as a boxed set in October. The <a class="external text" href="http://www.houghtonmifflinbooks.com/catalog/titledetail.cfm?titleNumber=1022629" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Houghton Mifflin boxed set</a> also includes a new edition of *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)* with a short introduction by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), a reset text incorporating the most up-to-date corrections, and all of Tolkien's own drawings and color illustrations, including the rare "Mirkwood" piece.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In an interview conducted before the publication, Rateliff described the undertaking in the following way:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The big project, which I&#39;ve been working on for years and will finally be sending to the publisher near the end of this year, is a book called The History of The Hobbit. It&#39;s rather along the lines of Christopher Tolkien&#39;s editions that make up the History of Middle-Earth series (which I highly recommend if you haven&#39;t read them): an edition of the original manuscript of The Hobbit with extensive commentary on how Mr. Baggins&#39; story fits into Tolkien&#39;s legendarium. It&#39;s a big book filled with the entire text of Tolkien&#39;s first draft, along with short essays about everything from rings of invisibility or Tolkien&#39;s spiders to a detailed account of just when Tolkien wrote the book (which can actually be reconstructed from available evidence to within a month or two on either end). I hope people will like it when it finally sees the light of day.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

## Revised one-volume edition {#Revised_one-volume_edition}

On [24 March](../_/24_March.md) [2011](../_/2011.md), Rateliff announced that *The History of The Hobbit* will be reprinted, together with an Addendum, as a single-volume book.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> This "*Revised edition*" was released on [27 October](../_/27_October.md). The Addendum includes:<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

- "Timeline of Events"— a timeline of fifteen days following [Durin's Day](../D/Durin's_Day.md)
- "Notes on a Parley"— a detailed description of the [Front Gate](../F/Front_Gate.md)
- "Responses to Queries"— some proofreader's concerns addressed by J.R.R. Tolkien
- "Personae"— an interesting listing of [Thorin and Company](Thorin_and_Company.md)
- "Runic Charts"— details on using dwarven [runes](../R/Runes.md)
- "Feanorian Letters"— details on writing in [Tengwar](Tengwar.md), including punctuation and numbers
