**Thrór** ([T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2542](Third_Age_2542.md) – [2790](Third_Age_2790.md), lived 248 years) was King of [Durin's Folk](../D/Durin's_Folk~15256.md) for 201 years, from [2589](Third_Age_2589.md) to 2790. He was the eldest son of [Dáin I](../D/Dáin_I.md) and brother of [Grór](../G/Grór.md) and [Frór](../F/Frór.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After a great [Cold-drake](../C/Cold-drakes.md) killed both his father and brother [Frór](../F/Frór.md), the remaining brothers Thrór and [Grór](../G/Grór.md) led their people away from the [Grey Mountains](../G/Grey_Mountains.md). As Dáin's heir Thrór led many [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) back to [Lonely Mountain](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md) in [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2590](Third_Age_2590.md),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">[2]</a></sup> where he became [King under the Mountain](../K/King_under_the_Mountain.md), a title held earlier by his ancestor, [Thorin I](Thorin_I.md). Grór continued east with a great following of Durin's folk to the [Iron Hills](../I/Iron_Hills.md), where he founded his own [realm](../D/Dwarves_of_the_Iron_Hills.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

### King under the Mountain {#King_under_the_Mountain}

At Erebor, Thrór and his people re-established the [Kingdom under the Mountain](../K/Kingdom_under_the_Mountain.md) and became very prosperous. Thrór brought the [Arkenstone](../A/Arkenstone.md) back to [Erebor](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup> and the hall of feasting and council, the [Great Chamber of Thrór](../G/Great_Chamber_of_Thrór.md),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> was named after him.

Thrór and his people gained the friendship of the [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md) who lived along the river [Celduin](../R/River_Running.md). These people later founded the town of [Dale](../D/Dale.md) and had much trade of goods, beautiful trinkets, and weapons with the Dwarves. The [Dwarves of Erebor](../D/Dwarves_of_Erebor.md) also had much traffic of ores with their kinsman in the Iron Hills and the region had peace and prosperity for many years.

But in [2770](Third_Age_2770.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">[2]</a></sup> their prosperity ended. The Dragon [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md) heard of the wealth of Thrór and his people. He flew south from the [Ered Mithrin](../G/Grey_Mountains.md) and sacked the Lonely Mountain, killing many Dwarves. From the destruction many escaped, the last of them being Thrór and his son [Thráin](Thráin.md) from the hidden [Back Door](../B/Back_Door.md). With a small company of kin and faithful followers they made a great wandering southward, until they reached the hills of [Dunland](../D/Dunland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

On a [Mid-year's Day](../M/Mid-year's_Day.md) under a broad crescent moon, Thrór created his [map](Thrór's_Map.md) of the Lonely Mountain and the [Desolation of the Dragon](../D/Desolation_of_the_Dragon.md). In addition to the readily visible features on the map, Thrór added the details about the hidden [Back Door](../B/Back_Door.md) into the Mountain, written in [Moon-letters](../M/Moon-letters.md) for secrecy.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

### Dunland, and the journey to Moria {#Dunland,_and_the_journey_to_Moria}

In [Dunland](../D/Dunland.md), his people tried to make a living, but twenty years<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">[2]</a></sup> after the [Sack of Erebor](../S/Sack_of_Erebor.md), despondent and homeless, Thrór left his people and went north with a single companion, [Nár](../N/Nár.md). Before leaving, Thrór gave his son [Thráin II](Thráin.md) his [Ring of Power](../R/Ring_of_Thrór.md), along with the map and key to the Lonely Mountain. Thrór wished to see the fabled city of his ancestors, [Khazad-dûm](../M/Moria.md).

Eventually they made it over the [Redhorn Pass](../R/Redhorn_Pass.md) down into [Azanulbizar](../A/Azanulbizar.md), the Dimrill Dale. Coming to the [East-gate](../G/Great_Gates.md) of Moria Thrór found it open. Nár begged him to beware, but ignoring his companion Thrór walked through the gates as the heir who had returned. But he did not come back.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Death {#Death}

Nár hid nearby for many days until he saw Thrór's body flung down the steps; his severed head lay face downward on the ground. As he knelt there, Nar saw branded across his forehead in [Dwarvish runes](../A/Angerthas_Moria.md) the name [AZOG](../A/Azog.md). This [Orc](../O/Orcs.md) then threw a small purse of coins at him, calling him a beggar. Nár fled weeping down the [Silverlode](../C/Celebrant.md), while Orcs emerged and hacked Thrór's body into pieces and fed them to the crows.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

When Nár told Thráin what had happened, he declared war on the Orcs and called all the Seven Houses of the Dwarves together for vengeance. Nine years later in [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [2799](Third_Age_2799.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">[2]</a></sup> at the [Battle of Azanulbizar](../B/Battle_of_Azanulbizar.md), the climax of the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](../W/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs.md), the death of Thrór was avenged when Azog was beheaded by [Dáin Ironfoot](../D/Dáin_Ironfoot.md). Thráin took Azog's severed head, shoved the purse of coins into his mouth and then set it on a stake.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) has suggested that the name *Thrór* derives from *Þrór*, a dwarf from the *[Dvergatal](../V/Völuspá.md)*. According to Allan, *Þrór* means "boar", deriving from a root meaning "to expand" (linking the meaning to Thrór's expanding of the "*diggings and wealth of the Lonely Mountain*").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
