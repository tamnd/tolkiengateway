<i><b>The Adventures of Tom Bombadil and Other Verses from the Red Book</b></i> is a [1962](/wiki/1962) collection of poetry by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien). The book contains 16 poems, two of which feature [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil), the rest of the poems are an assortment of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/bestiary" title="wikipedia:bestiary">bestiary</a> verse and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/fairy_tale" title="wikipedia:fairy tale">fairy tale</a> rhyme. Three of the poems appear in *The Lord of the Rings* as well. The book was originally illustrated by [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes).

## Contents {#Contents}

The book's "[Preface](/wiki/Preface)" presents the poems as a translation from the *[Red Book of Westmarch](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch)*, and gives some background information that is not found elsewhere: e.g. the name of the tower at [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth) and the names of the [Seven Rivers](/wiki/Rivers_of_Gondor "Rivers of Gondor") of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). The poems carry some fictional backstory, linking them to [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbit) folklore; they are all supposedly works that Hobbits enjoyed and were preserved in the margins of the Red Book, with several of them being attributed to [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) and [Sam Gamgee](/wiki/Sam_Gamgee).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

- I. *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](</wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_(poem)> "The Adventures of Tom Bombadil (poem)")*
- II. *[Bombadil Goes Boating](/wiki/Bombadil_Goes_Boating)*
- III *[Errantry](/wiki/Errantry)*
- IV. *[Princess Mee](/wiki/Princess_Mee)*
- V. *[The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late](/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Stayed_Up_Too_Late)*
- VI. *[The Man in the Moon Came Down Too Soon](/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Came_Down_Too_Soon)*
- VII. *[The Stone Troll](/wiki/The_Stone_Troll)*
- VIII. *[Perry-the-Winkle](/wiki/Perry-the-Winkle)*
- IX. *[The Mewlips](/wiki/The_Mewlips)*
- X. *[Oliphaunt](</wiki/Oliphaunt_(poem)> "Oliphaunt (poem)")*
- XI. *[Fastitocalon](</wiki/Fastitocalon_(poem)> "Fastitocalon (poem)")*
- XII. *[Cat](</wiki/Cat_(poem)> "Cat (poem)")*
- XIII. *[Shadow-Bride](/wiki/Shadow-Bride)*
- XIV. *[The Hoard](/wiki/The_Hoard)*
- XV. *[The Sea-Bell](/wiki/The_Sea-Bell)*
- XVI. *[The Last Ship](/wiki/The_Last_Ship)*

The order of the poems form a thematical progress: two poems with the titular character, two "[faerie](/wiki/Faerie "Faerie")" poems, two with the [Man in the Moon](/wiki/Man_in_the_Moon), two with [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls); three "bestiary", and four "atmospheric/emotional". *The Mewlips* doesn't fit to a category, and placed in the middle as a divider.

- Poems proposed but eventually omitted:
    - *[The Trees of Kortirion](/wiki/The_Trees_of_Kortirion)* and *[You and Me](/wiki/The_Little_House_of_Lost_Play:_Mar_Vanwa_Tyali%C3%A9va "The Little House of Lost Play: Mar Vanwa Tyaliéva")*, later published in *[The Book of Lost Tales: Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales:_Part_One)*
    - *[The Dragon's Visit](/wiki/The_Dragon%27s_Visit)*, later published in the revised edition of *[The Annotated Hobbit](/wiki/The_Annotated_Hobbit)*

- Poems added in the 2014 extended edition:
    - *[Once upon a Time](/wiki/Once_upon_a_Time)*, a poem related to Tom Bombadil.
    - *[An Evening in Tavrobel](/wiki/An_Evening_in_Tavrobel)*, recounting an evening in [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel), a place in [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) in the early version of the legendarium.

## Background {#Background}

[Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil) was a figure in [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s mind that appeared in his writings at various times, including a [1937](/wiki/1937) poem; eventually he became a [canonical](/wiki/Canon "Canon") part of the [Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium) while Tolkien was writing *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.

Tolkien's aunt [Jane Neave](/wiki/Jane_Neave) enjoyed the figure of Tom in *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* and asked him if he could make a book out of him that would make an affordable [Christmas](/wiki/Christmas) present. As Tom was a vague, deliberately [unexplained figure](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil/Nature "Tom Bombadil/Nature"), Tolkien didn't feel that anything more could be told about him, but thought that his 1937 poem could be made into an illustrated booklet,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> with [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes) in his mind. [Rayner Unwin](/wiki/Rayner_Unwin) suggested to collect more poems with it so as to be a more publishable book. Tolkien then researched some older, half-forgotten poems (the value of which he doubted)<sup id="cite_ref-chron_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chron-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and started a laborious process to rediscover, rub up, improve and re-write them; something which, as he wrote to his aunt, he greatly enjoyed.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Tolkien thought (and Baynes agreed) that the poems didn't fit together as a collection.<sup id="cite_ref-chron_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chron-3">[3]</a></sup> Part of Tolkien's re-writing attempted to make them fit with each other and into [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbit)-lore; he decided to include a Foreword that would make this connection, and wrote [a second poem with Tom](/wiki/Bombadil_Goes_Boating "Bombadil Goes Boating") in order to fit him better into the world of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") and Hobbits.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Illustrations</dt></dl>

Despite Baynes suggested that his poems were rather "felt", Tolkien insisted that his images were definite, clear and precise.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> He instructed Baynes that the illustrations "shouldn't be comical". Then she collaborated with art editor Ronald Eames, and finished six illustrations by August [1962](/wiki/1962). Though there were some criticism from Tolkien to Baynes' work, in the end, Tolkien credited for a large part Baynes for the commercial success of the book.

## Extended edition {#Extended_edition}

An extended edition was published in [2014](/wiki/2014), edited by [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull). This edition includes: an introduction by the editors, earlier versions of 13 poems with textual notes, a later 'Bombodil' poem *[Once upon a Time](/wiki/Once_upon_a_Time)*, a previously unpublished text *The Bumpus*, the predecessor of *Perry-the-Winkle*, and the complete fragment of a prose story featuring Tom Bombadil.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Some revision history of the poems are as follows:

- Revised, title unchanged
    - *The Adventures of Tom Bombadil*
    - *Errantry* (eventually became the *[Eärendillinwë](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendillinw%C3%AB)*)
    - *Fastitocalon*

- Revised, title changed
    - *[The Princess Ní](/wiki/The_Princess_N%C3%AD)* → *Princess Mee*
    - *[The Cat and the Fiddle](/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Stayed_Up_Too_Late "The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late")* → *The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late*
    - *Why the Man in the Moon Came Down Too Soon* → *The Man in the Moon Came Down Too Soon*
    - *Pēdo &amp; Pōdex* → *[The Root of the Boot](/wiki/The_Root_of_the_Boot)* → *The Stone Troll*
    - *The Bumpus* → *Perry-the-Winkle*
    - *[Knocking at the Door](/wiki/Knocking_at_the_Door)* → *The Mewlips*
    - *Iumbo, or, Ye Kind of Ye Oliphaunt* → *Oliphaunt*
    - *The Shadow Man* → *Shadow-Bride*
    - *[Iúmonna Gold Galdre Bewunden](/wiki/I%C3%BAmonna_Gold_Galdre_Bewunden)* → *The Hoard*
    - *[Looney](/wiki/Looney)* → *The Sea-Bell*
    - *[Firiel](</wiki/Firiel_(poem)> "Firiel (poem)")* → *The Last Ship*

The [2024](/wiki/2024) edition includes an "About the Artist" section before the [Preface](/wiki/Preface).
