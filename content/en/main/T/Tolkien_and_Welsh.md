<i><b>Tolkien and Welsh (Tolkien a Chymraeg): Essays on J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s Use of Welsh in his Legendarium</b></i> is a collection of articles by [Mark T. Hooker](/wiki/Mark_T._Hooker) on [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s use of [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) language and culture.

*Tolkien and Welsh* has been invited to enter the 2013 Competition for the Literature Wales Book of the Year Award. Participation is by invitation only.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;Tolkien and Welsh&#39; provides an overview of J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s use of Welsh in his Legendarium, ranging from the obvious (&#39;<a href="/wiki/Roverandom" title="Roverandom">Gwynfa</a>&#39;—the Welsh word for Paradise), to the apparent (&#39;<a href="/wiki/Took_Family" title="Took Family">Took</a>&#39;—a Welsh surname), to the veiled (<i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Gerontius" title="Gerontius">Gerontius</a></i>—the Latinizaton of a royal Welsh name), to the hidden (&#39;<a href="/wiki/Goldberry" title="Goldberry">Goldberry</a>&#39;—the English <a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/calque" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">calque</a> of a Welsh <a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/theonym" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">theonym</a>).Though it is a book by a linguist, it was written for the non-linguist with the goal of making the topic accessible. The unavoidable jargon is explained in a glossary, and the narrative presents an overview of how Welsh influenced Tolkien&#39;s story line, as well as his synthetic languages <a href="/wiki/Quenya" title="Quenya">Quenya</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sindarin" title="Sindarin">Sindarin</a>.The study is based on specific examples of attested names, placed in the context of their linguistic and cultural background, while highlighting the peculiar features of Welsh, &#34;the senior language of the men of Britain&#34; (<a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics" title="The Monsters and the Critics">MC</a> p. 189), that Tolkien found so intriguing. It supplements, rather than competes with <a href="/wiki/Carl_Phelpstead" title="Carl Phelpstead">Carl Phelpstead</a>&#39;s excellent <i><a href="/wiki/Tolkien_and_Wales" title="Tolkien and Wales">Tolkien and Wales</a></i>, which sidestepped the topic of the Celtic linguistics behind Tolkien&#39;s work. Learn the story behind <i><a href="/wiki/Lithe" title="Lithe">Lithe</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Buckland" title="Buckland">Buckland</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Anduin" title="Anduin">Anduin</a></i>, and <i><a href="/wiki/Baranduin" title="Baranduin">Baranduin</a></i>.
</p>
</blockquote>
