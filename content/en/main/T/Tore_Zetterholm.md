**Tore Ulf Axel Zetterholm** ([October 4](/wiki/4_October "4 October"), [1915](/wiki/1915) - [November 9](/wiki/9_November "9 November"), [2001](/wiki/2001)) was a Swedish writer (author of 28 novels), literature historian, journalist, and translator.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [1947](/wiki/1947), Zetterholm published his translation of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* (the first translation of any of Tolkien's works to any language), calling it *[Hompen](/wiki/Hompen)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Bere_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bere-2">[2]</a></sup>

Perhaps by a strange chance, Zetterholm was one of the recipients of the prestigious annual prize of literature given by <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svenska_Dagbladet" title="wikipedia:Svenska Dagbladet">Svenska Dagbladet</a></i> in 1950, when [Britt G. Hallqvist](/wiki/Britt_G._Hallqvist), a later translator of *The Hobbit*, received the children's literature prize in the same year by the same newspaper.<sup id="cite_ref-Bere_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bere-2">[2]</a></sup>
