**Translations from the Elvish** was a work written by [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) between [T.A.](Third_Age.md) [3003](Third_Age_3003.md) and [3018](Third_Age_3018.md), during his stay in [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md), using both written sources found there, and interviewing the [Eldar he met there](../E/Elves_of_Rivendell.md).

This work "of great skill and learning" filled three volumes of red leather and concerned the [Elder Days](../E/Elder_Days.md).<sup id="cite_ref-records_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-records-1">[1]</a></sup> Possibly it included such works as the *[Quenta Silmarillion](../Q/Quenta_Silmarillion.md)*, *[Ainulindalë](<../A/Ainulindalë_(Rúmil's_work).md>)*, and the *[Valaquenta](../V/Valaquenta.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Bilbo gave the volumes to [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) as a parting gift. These were later annexed to [his diary](There_and_Back_Again.md), and the account of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), and (appended with a fifth volume later) formed the [Red Book of Westmarch](../R/Red_Book_of_Westmarch.md).

As these were used little by Frodo, and most copies of the Red Book involved mainly the first volume, the *Translations* were omitted from other versions. They were preserved only in the [Thain's Book](Thain's_Book.md), and its copy made by [Findegil](../F/Findegil.md) in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-records_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-records-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In the prologue to *[The Book of Lost Tales: Part One](The_Book_of_Lost_Tales%253A_Part_One.md)*, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) considers that Bilbo's Red Book, and this work, is supposed to be the in-universe origin of the [Legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) of the Elder Days, specifically *[The Silmarillion](The_Silmarillion.md)* that his father was writing. Christopher considered that this scenario obsoleted the earlier conception of [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md), a frame story "explaining" how Elvish lore passed to [Old English](../O/Old_English.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
