**Translations from the Elvish** was a work written by [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3003](/wiki/Third_Age_3003 "Third Age 3003") and [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), during his stay in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), using both written sources found there, and interviewing the [Eldar he met there](/wiki/Elves_of_Rivendell "Elves of Rivendell").

This work "of great skill and learning" filled three volumes of red leather and concerned the [Elder Days](/wiki/Elder_Days).<sup id="cite_ref-records_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-records-1">[1]</a></sup> Possibly it included such works as the *[Quenta Silmarillion](/wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion)*, *[Ainulindalë](</wiki/Ainulindal%C3%AB_(R%C3%BAmil%27s_work)> "Ainulindalë (Rúmil's work)")*, and the *[Valaquenta](/wiki/Valaquenta)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Bilbo gave the volumes to [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) as a parting gift. These were later annexed to [his diary](/wiki/There_and_Back_Again "There and Back Again"), and the account of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), and (appended with a fifth volume later) formed the [Red Book of Westmarch](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch).

As these were used little by Frodo, and most copies of the Red Book involved mainly the first volume, the *Translations* were omitted from other versions. They were preserved only in the [Thain's Book](/wiki/Thain%27s_Book), and its copy made by [Findegil](/wiki/Findegil) in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-records_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-records-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In the prologue to *[The Book of Lost Tales: Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales:_Part_One)*, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) considers that Bilbo's Red Book, and this work, is supposed to be the in-universe origin of the [Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium) of the Elder Days, specifically *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* that his father was writing. Christopher considered that this scenario obsoleted the earlier conception of [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), a frame story "explaining" how Elvish lore passed to [Old English](/wiki/Old_English).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
