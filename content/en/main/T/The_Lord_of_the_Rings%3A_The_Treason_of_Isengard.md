<i><b>The Lord of the Rings: The Treason of Isengard</b></i> was to be a third-person action-adventure video game under development by [Surreal Software](/wiki/Surreal_Software) for PlayStation 2 and Windows, and [The Whole Experience](/wiki/The_Whole_Experience) for Xbox. It was to be the sequel of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)> "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)")* and like its predecessor, it was to be based on the books. A separate edition was developed for GameBoy Advance by [Pocket Studios](/wiki/Pocket_Studios).<sup id="cite_ref-Gamespot-an_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gamespot-an-1">[1]</a></sup> It was scheduled to be released by [Vivendi Universal](/wiki/Vivendi_Universal) in late 2003 but was cancelled in September 2003 due to failing the standards Vivendi had set for their games.<sup id="cite_ref-Gamespot-interview_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gamespot-interview-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Plot {#Plot}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Lord_of_the_Rings-_The_Treason_of_Isengard_-_Treebeard.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/41/The_Lord_of_the_Rings-_The_Treason_of_Isengard_-_Treebeard.jpg/300px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings-_The_Treason_of_Isengard_-_Treebeard.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="312" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/4/41/The_Lord_of_the_Rings-_The_Treason_of_Isengard_-_Treebeard.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="430" data-file-height="447"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Treebeard" title="Treebeard">Treebeard</a> in <a href="/wiki/Isengard" title="Isengard">Isengard</a></figcaption></figure>

The storyline of Treason of Isengard is based on [books](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Lord of the Rings"), covering the second volume *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)*, and continues where [its' prequel](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)> "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)") ended. The storyline would have featured [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) and [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) pursuing the [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai) and entering [Fangorn](/wiki/Fangorn_Forest "Fangorn Forest"), the journey of [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"), [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") and [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) in their journey to the [Black Gate](/wiki/Black_Gate), in [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) and in [Shelob's Lair](/wiki/Shelob%27s_Lair).It would have also featured the [Battle of Helm's Deep](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Hornburg "Battle of the Hornburg") and the [Battle of Isengard](/wiki/Battle_of_Isengard).<sup id="cite_ref-Trailer_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trailer-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Gameplay {#Gameplay}

The player was able to take control of several characters. Whilst progressing the player would gain experience and could upgrade their characters. Other features included large battle sequences,<sup id="cite_ref-Gamespot-an_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gamespot-an-1">[1]</a></sup> playing as an [Ent](/wiki/Ents "Ents"), online gameplay and downloadable content.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

### Playable characters {#Playable_characters}

The game would include several playable characters. Due to the game's cancellation it is unknown which charactars would be playable. This list only includes confirmed characters:

## Cancellation {#Cancellation}

Treason of Isengard was cancelled officialy in September [2003](/wiki/2003). In an interview with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamespot" title="wikipedia:Gamespot">Gamespot</a>, Andrew Shiozaki, senior brand manager of Vivendi, explained that Vivendi had reviewed their complete line-up of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) video games, which besides Treason of Isengard included *[The Hobbit](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game)> "The Hobbit (2003 video game)")*, the [*War of the Ring* strategy game](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_War_of_the_Ring "The Lord of the Rings: War of the Ring") and the MMORPG *[Middle-earth Online](/wiki/Middle-earth_Online)* (which eventually was released as *[The Lord of the Rings Online](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online)* by [Turbine](/wiki/Turbine)). It was deemed that Treason of Isengard failed the standards that Vivendi had set for their Middle-earth video games and was therefore cancelled. Instead, they focussed on the remaining three video games.<sup id="cite_ref-Gamespot-interview_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gamespot-interview-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Soundtrack {#Soundtrack}

Brad Spear, who previously composed music for the *Fellowship of the Ring* video game, composed original music for Treason of Isengard, as well as tracks based on themes composed by [Chance Thomas](/wiki/Chance_Thomas) for *[Middle-earth Online](/wiki/Middle-earth_Online)* (which later became known as *The Lord of the Rings Online*). Several music demo's were available for download on the - now offline - site www.lotr.com. Some tracks were reused for *The Lord of the Rings: War of the Ring* and *[The Lord of the Rings Online](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online)*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
