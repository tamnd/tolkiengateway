<b><i>Tolkien and Wagner: The Ring and</i> Der Ring</b> investigates the relationship between the works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) and [Richard Wagner](/wiki/Richard_Wagner). It was published as No. 24 in the [Cormarë Series](/wiki/Cormar%C3%AB_Series).

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Tolkien famously rejected comparison of his Ring with Wagner&#39;s, though there is good evidence that Tolkien knew much more about <i><a href="/wiki/Der_Ring_des_Nibelungen" title="Der Ring des Nibelungen">Der Ring des Nibelungen</a></i> than he let on after the publication of <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>. Analysis of that work from a Wagnerian point of view enables consideration of it in a new way. By exploring the parallels between Wagner&#39;s Ring and Tolkien&#39;s, a fresh interpretation of Tolkien&#39;s work emerges, one that hinges on associating <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> with Wotan. Like Wagner&#39;s god, Gandalf has to find a way of solving the problems posed by the Ring and like Wotan he cannot succeed without other people. When the plots of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></i> (and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a></i>) are examined in this way it becomes apparent how much they owe to Wagner&#39;s music-drama, and the role of Gandalf is opened to new explanation.
</p>
</blockquote>
