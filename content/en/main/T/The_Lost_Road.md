**The Lost Road** is the third chapter of the first section, "Part One: The Fall of Númenor and the Lost Road", of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings.md)* by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).

## Plot {#Plot}

The theme of *The Lost Road* is one of "Preincarnation". There are a series of occurrences throughout the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) of father and son duos sharing names that are etymologically connected with [Amandil](../A/Amandil.md) ("Bliss-friend") and [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) ("Elf-friend"). These include [Eädwine](../E/Eädwine.md)-[Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md) of Anglo-Saxon legend, [Audoin](../A/Audoin.md)-[Alboin](../A/Alboin.md) of Lombardic, through to "the traditions of the North Sea concerning the coming of corn and culture heroes, ancestors of kingly lines, in boats".<sup id="cite_ref-Letter257_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter257-1">[1]</a></sup> In the story the present pair, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Edwin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Edwin (page does not exist)">Edwin</a> and <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Elwin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Elwin (page does not exist)">Elwin</a>, travel back through the different phases of the history of their names, eventually reaching the time of Amandil and Elendil and the *[Akallabêth](../A/Akallabêth.md)* or *Atalantie* ("Downfall" in [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) and [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) respectively) of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md).

Númenor at this stage in Tolkien's thought was not connected with the wider [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md). Rather it is a direct analogue of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis" title="wikipedia:Atlantis">Atlantis</a>, a "legend or myth or dim memory" that had always "[troubled](../A/Atlantis-haunting.md)" Tolkien and a theme to which he often returned.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter257_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter257-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In the chapter "[The Early History of the Legend](The_Early_History_of_the_Legend.md)", [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) explains the conception of the story as a challenge with [C.S. Lewis](../C/C.S._Lewis.md) and how this was developed simultaneously with the chapter "[The Fall of Númenor](<The_Fall_of_Númenor_(chapter).md>)". It is notable that this challenge, although abandoned by Tolkien, resulted in Lewis's successful [Space Trilogy](../S/Space_Trilogy.md)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>, whose <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Ransom" title="wikipedia:Elwin Ransom">protagonist</a> is also named Elwin.

However, only a fragment of *The Lost Road* was ever written: the two opening chapters, and two which take place in Númenor. According to Tolkien, he abandoned the story because "it was too long a way round to what I really wanted to make, a new version of the Atlantis legend".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> He did pass the completed chapters on to [Allen &amp; Unwin](../A/Allen_&_Unwin.md) in [1937](../_/1937.md) as a possible successor to *[The Hobbit](The_Hobbit.md)*. However, the publishers felt that even if it was finished, the story was unlikely to be a commercial success.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter24N3_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter24N3-4">[4]</a></sup>

This was not Tolkien's last attempt at integrating the Númenor story into a time travel frame work. He tried a second time a decade later with *[The Notion Club Papers](The_Notion_Club_Papers.md)*. It was only after both these works floundered that the legends of Númenor were finally integrated with the main mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter257_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter257-1">[1]</a></sup>
