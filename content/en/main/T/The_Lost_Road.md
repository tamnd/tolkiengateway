**The Lost Road** is the third chapter of the first section, "Part One: The Fall of Númenor and the Lost Road", of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](/wiki/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings)* by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

## Plot {#Plot}

The theme of *The Lost Road* is one of "Preincarnation". There are a series of occurrences throughout the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") of father and son duos sharing names that are etymologically connected with [Amandil](/wiki/Amandil) ("Bliss-friend") and [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) ("Elf-friend"). These include [Eädwine](/wiki/E%C3%A4dwine)-[Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) of Anglo-Saxon legend, [Audoin](/wiki/Audoin)-[Alboin](/wiki/Alboin) of Lombardic, through to "the traditions of the North Sea concerning the coming of corn and culture heroes, ancestors of kingly lines, in boats".<sup id="cite_ref-Letter257_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter257-1">[1]</a></sup> In the story the present pair, <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Edwin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Edwin (page does not exist)">Edwin</a> and <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Elwin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Elwin (page does not exist)">Elwin</a>, travel back through the different phases of the history of their names, eventually reaching the time of Amandil and Elendil and the *[Akallabêth](/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth)* or *Atalantie* ("Downfall" in [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") and [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) respectively) of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).

Númenor at this stage in Tolkien's thought was not connected with the wider [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"). Rather it is a direct analogue of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis" title="wikipedia:Atlantis">Atlantis</a>, a "legend or myth or dim memory" that had always "[troubled](/wiki/Atlantis-haunting "Atlantis-haunting")" Tolkien and a theme to which he often returned.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter257_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter257-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In the chapter "[The Early History of the Legend](/wiki/The_Early_History_of_the_Legend)", [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) explains the conception of the story as a challenge with [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis) and how this was developed simultaneously with the chapter "[The Fall of Númenor](</wiki/The_Fall_of_N%C3%BAmenor_(chapter)> "The Fall of Númenor (chapter)")". It is notable that this challenge, although abandoned by Tolkien, resulted in Lewis's successful [Space Trilogy](/wiki/Space_Trilogy)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>, whose <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Ransom" title="wikipedia:Elwin Ransom">protagonist</a> is also named Elwin.

However, only a fragment of *The Lost Road* was ever written: the two opening chapters, and two which take place in Númenor. According to Tolkien, he abandoned the story because "it was too long a way round to what I really wanted to make, a new version of the Atlantis legend".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> He did pass the completed chapters on to [Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin) in [1937](/wiki/1937) as a possible successor to *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*. However, the publishers felt that even if it was finished, the story was unlikely to be a commercial success.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter24N3_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter24N3-4">[4]</a></sup>

This was not Tolkien's last attempt at integrating the Númenor story into a time travel frame work. He tried a second time a decade later with *[The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers)*. It was only after both these works floundered that the legends of Númenor were finally integrated with the main mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter257_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter257-1">[1]</a></sup>
