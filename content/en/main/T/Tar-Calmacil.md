**Tar-Calmacil** was the eighteenth ruler of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Calmacil_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Calmacil-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

*Calmacil* was the son of [Tar-Alcarin](/wiki/Tar-Alcarin). He was a great captain of the soldiers of Númenor and conquered wide lands along the coasts of Middle-earth in his youth. As a consequence, Sauron withdrew from the coasts and built his power in the East far from the coasts.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Calmacil_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Calmacil-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the death of his father, he chose the name Tar-Calmacil when he took the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre), because of his conquests in his youth.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Calmacil_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Calmacil-1">[1]</a></sup>

During his reign the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men) called him Ar-Belzagar. This was the first time that the king's name was spoken in [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic).<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Calmacil_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Calmacil-1">[1]</a></sup>

He was succeeded by his son [Tar-Ardamin](/wiki/Tar-Ardamin).<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Ardamin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Ardamin-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Calmacil* is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but as Paul Strack suggests it means "Sword of Light" or "Shining Sword" as a compound of *[cala](/wiki/Cala "Cala")* ("light") and *[macil](/wiki/Macil "Macil")* ("sword").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> [Helge Fauskanger](/wiki/Helge_Fauskanger) suggests that the name means "light-sword".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Like all the rulers of Númenor who took their royal names in Quenya, *Calmacil* added the prefix *tar-* ("high") to his name when he received the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Sceptre of Númenor").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

*Belzagar* is an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "Sword of Light" if his Adûnaic and Quenya names have the same meaning. [Andreas Möhn](/wiki/Andreas_M%C3%B6hn) suggests that the first element *bel* might mean "light", but the element *bel* elsewhere means "to love". It is possible that the two meanings coexisted or that [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) changed the meaning from "love" to "light". The second element *zagar* most likely means "sword".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> The meaning of the Adûnaic prefix *Ar-* is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "king" and is a prefixal form of *ârû* ("king") or derived from the same root.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In Appendix A of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Ar-Adûnakhôr was listed as following Tar-Calmacil<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> while in the more detailed [The Line of Elros: Kings of Númenor](/wiki/The_Line_of_Elros:_Kings_of_N%C3%BAmenor) from the *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)* the next ruler was [Tar-Ardamin](/wiki/Tar-Ardamin). According to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), Tar-Ardamin was simply omitted and did belong in the list.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
