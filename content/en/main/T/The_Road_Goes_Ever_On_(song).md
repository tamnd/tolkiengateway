**The Road Goes Ever On** was a **walking-song** by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), fictionally written by [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins); verses of it are sung at various places in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. This poem was set to music by [Donald Swann](/wiki/Donald_Swann);<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> the sheet music and an audio recording are part of the song-cycle aptly named *[The Road Goes Ever On, A Song Cycle](</wiki/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book)> "The Road Goes Ever On (book)")*.

## *The Hobbit* {#The_Hobbit}

The original version of the song is recited by [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") in the last chapter of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, at the end of his journey back to [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"). Coming to the top of a rise he sees his home in the distance, and stops and says the following:

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Roads go ever ever on,<br/>
Over rock and under tree,<br/>
By caves where never sun has shone,<br/>
By streams that never find the sea;<br/>
Over snow by winter sown,<br/>
And through the merry flowers of June,<br/>
Over grass and over stone,<br/>
And under mountains in the moon.<br/>
<br/>
Roads go ever ever on<br/>
Under cloud and under star,<br/>
Yet feet that wandering have gone<br/>
Turn at last to home afar.<br/>
Eyes that fire and sword have seen<br/>
And horror in the halls of stone<br/>
Look at last on meadows green<br/>
And trees and hills they long have known.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

## *The Lord of the Rings* {#The_Lord_of_the_Rings}

There are three versions of this walking song in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><audio src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/4/44/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Road_Goes_Ever_On.mp3" width="300" class="mw-file-element" style="max-width: 100%; width: 300px;" controls=""><a href="https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Road_Goes_Ever_On.mp3">https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_The_Road_Goes_Ever_On.mp3</a></audio><figcaption><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a> reads The Road Goes Ever On</figcaption></figure>

The first is sung by [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") when he leaves [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") and is setting off to visit [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell):

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>The Road goes ever on and on,<br/>
Down from the door where it began.<br/>
Now far ahead the Road has gone,<br/>
And I must follow, if I can,<br/>
Pursuing it with eager feet,<br/>
Until it joins some larger way<br/>
Where many paths and errands meet.<br/>
And whither then? I cannot say.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

The second version is identical except for changing the word "eager" to "weary" in the fifth line. It is spoken aloud, slowly, by [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"), as he and his companions pause on their way to [Crickhollow](/wiki/Crickhollow), looking beyond to lands that some of them have never seen before.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The third version is spoken by Bilbo in Rivendell after the hobbits have returned from their journey. Bilbo is now an old, sleepy hobbit, who murmurs the verse and then falls asleep.

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>The Road goes ever on and on <br/>
Out from the door where it began.<br/>
Now far ahead the Road has gone,<br/>
Let others follow it who can!<br/>
Let them a journey new begin,<br/>
But I at last with weary feet<br/>
Will turn towards the lighted inn,<br/>
My evening-rest and sleep to meet.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1977: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1977_film)" title="The Hobbit (1977 film)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1977 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Sections of the poem are sung during the trip through <a href="/wiki/Mirkwood" title="Mirkwood">Mirkwood</a>. It appears on the soundtrack titled &#34;Roads&#34;.</dd></dl>

<b>1980: <a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)"><i>The Return of the King</i> (1980 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>A song inspired by the poem is sung at the end of the film called &#34;<a href="/wiki/Roads_Go_Ever,_Ever_On" title="Roads Go Ever, Ever On">Roads Go Ever, Ever On</a>&#34;.</dd></dl>

<b>1981: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1981 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Bilbo sings the song as he leaves <a href="/wiki/Bag_End" title="Bag End">Bag End</a>. It is sung by <a href="/wiki/John_Le_Mesurier" title="John Le Mesurier">John Le Mesurier</a> to a tune by <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Oliver" title="Stephen Oliver">Stephen Oliver</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>1985: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1985_television_film)" title="The Hobbit (1985 television film)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1985 television film)</a></b>

<dl><dd>Bilbo sings the second verse of the song at the end of the film.</dd></dl>

**1997: [An Evening in Rivendell](/wiki/An_Evening_in_Rivendell):**

<dl><dd>The Tolkien Ensemble adapted an original melody to the song, composed by Caspar Reiff.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Parts of the <a href="/wiki/Bag_End_(soundtrack)" title="Bag End (soundtrack)">song</a> are sung by <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> in his first appearance, and also by Bilbo as he leaves <a href="/wiki/Bag_End" title="Bag End">Bag End</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(musical)" title="The Lord of the Rings (musical)">The Lord of the Rings (musical)</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The poem is the basis of the song &#34;The Road Goes On&#34; sung by Sam, Frodo, Merry, and Pippin in the first act.</dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Lines of the poem partially make up the lyrics of <a href="/wiki/The_Last_Goodbye" title="The Last Goodbye">The Last Goodbye</a>, performed by <a href="/wiki/Billy_Boyd" title="Billy Boyd">Billy Boyd</a> for the credits of the film.</dd></dl>
