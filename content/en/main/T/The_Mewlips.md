<i><b>The Mewlips</b></i> was a nonsensical, but eerie [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") poem whose authorship is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Description {#Description}

The poem is the only one in the [collection](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil "The Adventures of Tom Bombadil") that is not referenced in the [Preface](/wiki/Preface), and therefore no background is given about it. Placed in the middle of the collection as the ninth poem, it seems to not fit in with the categories of the other poems: fairy-like, whimsical, bestiary, or emotional, but rather serves as a divider separating the two halves of the collection.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## First stanza {#First_stanza}

<div class="poem">
<p>The Shadows where the Mewlips dwell<br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">Are dark and wet as ink,</span><br/>
And slow and softly rings their bell,<br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">As in the slime you sink.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup></span>
</p>
</div>

## List of terms {#List_of_terms}

Below is a list of terms that are used within the poem.

- **Shadows** - The deep, dark, and cold wet cellars where the Mewlips lived. It had a door with a bell and there was only one lit sickly candle.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **[Mewlips](/wiki/Mewlips)** - A fictitious race of beings that dwelled in some cellars at the end of a long road, where they were said to count gold. They fed on the protagonist, keeping their bones for their collection.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup> They were possibly based on [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
- **You** - The term referring to the protagonist who dares to travel the long road to knock on the Mewlips's door, being devoured by the creatures as a result. Their bones are kept by the Mewlips in a sack.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **Gargoyles** - An unknown number of grinning statues above the door to the cellars of the Mewlips.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **[Gorcrows](/wiki/Gorcrows)** - A likely fictitious type of [crow](/wiki/Crows "Crows") that croaks in their sleep, presumably dwelling in or near the same place as the Mewlips.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup> *Gorcrow* ("gore crow") is an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrion_Crow" title="wikipedia:Carrion Crow">carrion crow</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **[Merlock Mountains](/wiki/Merlock_Mountains)** - A likely fictitious name for some [mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains "Misty Mountains") on the long, weary, and lonely road to the Mewlips' cellars.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup> [Fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") have speculated that the name *Merlock* was inspired by the name *Morlock* appearing in an earlier version, *Knocking at the Door*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **Spider-shadows** - A likely fictitious name for [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood) on the road to the Mewlips' cellars.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **[Tode](/wiki/Tode)** - A likely fictitious name for some [marshes](/wiki/Long_Marshes "Long Marshes") on the road to the Mewlips' cellars.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup> <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tode#Middle_English" title="wiktionary:tode">Tode</a></i> is the [Middle English](/wiki/Middle_English) spelling of "[toad](/wiki/Frog "Frog")"; it was also a word with evil connotations, as toads were considered to be demonic creatures according to Wiktionary (cf. abovementioned link). Furthermore, <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Tode#German" title="wiktionary:Tode">Tode</a></i> is also the [German](/wiki/Germanic "Germanic") word for "death".
- **Wood of hanging trees** - A fictitious name for a forest on the road to the Mewlips' cellars.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **Gallows-weed** - A likely fictitious type of hanging weed, found in the wood of hanging trees.<sup id="cite_ref-Mewlips_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mewlips-1">[1]</a></sup> It was only ever known by this name, and its physical description is never talked about nor described. Few people who ever entered these marshes, and thus even saw these gallows-weeds, ever returned, thus there was no description of them.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

"The Mewlips" was a rewrite of an earlier [poem](/wiki/Index:Poems_by_J.R.R._Tolkien "Index:Poems by J.R.R. Tolkien") that Tolkien wrote, titled **Knocking at the Door**, which was possibly written in [1927](/wiki/1927). Subtitled **Lines Induced by Sensation When Waiting for an Answer at the Door of an Exalted Academic Person**, the poem was published under the pseudonym *Oxymore* in <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Oxford_Magazine&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Oxford Magazine (page does not exist)">Oxford Magazine</a></i>, vol. 55, no. 13 ([18 February](/wiki/18_February) [1937](/wiki/1937)), page 403. Tolkien later rewrote the poem and included it in *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil)* in [1962](/wiki/1962).<sup id="cite_ref-guide_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-oxymore_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxymore-7">[7]</a></sup>

In the plot notes that [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) made while writing the *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, the [raftsmen](/wiki/Raft-elves "Raft-elves") told tales of rafts, men, and beasts disappearing in the [Long Marshes](/wiki/Long_Marshes);<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) suggests that the more ominous line in the notes may indicate that Tolkien had some unpleasantness in mind had [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) gotten into the marshes - perhaps meeting the Mewlips.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> If this is the case, the idea of these plot notes may possibly have been in Tolkien's mind when he eventually wrote the poem. If this is indeed the case, then the "you" in the poem may possibly be referring to one of the raftsmen.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i> took inspiration from the poem and came up with their own interpretations of Mewlips and Gorcrows on various supplements. Mewlips are described and given statistics as <a href="/wiki/Undead" title="Undead">Undead</a> beings living within swamps and marshes. The race was conceived as having been created by <a href="/wiki/Morgoth" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Elder_Days" title="Elder Days">Elder Days</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> Gorcrows are said to have 4 ft. wingspan and to be smaller cousins of the <i><a href="/wiki/Crebain" title="Crebain">crebain</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> being attracted to shiny things.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup> Northern Gorcrows lived in <a href="/wiki/Northern_Waste" title="Northern Waste">Forodwaith</a>, being described as larger versions of common Gorcrows. The <a href="/wiki/Lossoth" title="Lossoth">Lossoth</a> are said to have regarded northern Gorcrows as birds of ill-omen.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><i>Gore-crows</i> are a type of crebain native to the <a href="/wiki/Lone-lands" title="Lone-lands">Lone-lands</a>. They also appear in great number in <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Mordor</a>.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="/wiki/2008" title="2008">2008</a>: <i>The Mewlips</i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Richard Svensson released a stop-animation short film of &#34;The Mewlips&#34;, with accompanying music by <a href="/wiki/Colin_Rudd" title="Colin Rudd">Colin Rudd</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="/wiki/2014" title="2014">2014</a>: <i>03 - The Mewlips</i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Dutch black metal band Walpurgisnacht released their musical interpretation of &#34;The Mewlips&#34;, with the poem being the lyrics to their music.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup></dd></dl>
