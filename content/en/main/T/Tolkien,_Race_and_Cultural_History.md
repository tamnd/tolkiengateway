<i><b>Tolkien, Race and Cultural History: From Fairies to Hobbits</b></i> is a [2008](/wiki/2008) book by [Dimitra Fimi](/wiki/Dimitra_Fimi).

The book was the winner of the Mythopoeic Scholarship Award for Inklings Studies for 2010 ([Mythopoeic Society](/wiki/Mythopoeic_Society), USA), and was shortlisted for the Katharine Briggs Folklore Award for 2009 (Folklore Society, London).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;Tolkien, Race and Cultural History&#39; explores the evolution of Tolkien&#39;s mythology by examining how it changed as a result of Tolkien&#39;s life story and contemporary cultural and intellectual history. The book considers Tolkien&#39;s creative writing as an ever-developing &#39;legendarium&#39;: an interconnected web of stories, poems and essays, from his early poems in the 1910s to his latest writings in the early 1970s. Consequently, the book is not restricted to a discussion of Tolkien&#39;s best-known works only (&#39;The Hobbit&#39;, &#39;The Lord of the Rings&#39; and &#39;The Silmarillion&#39;) but examines the whole corpus of his legendarium, including the 12-volume History of Middle-earth series, which has received little attention from critics. This new approach and scope brings to light neglected aspects of Tolkien&#39;s imaginative vision and addresses key features of Tolkien&#39;s creativity: the centrality of the Elves and the role of linguistic invention in his legendarium, as well as race and material culture in Middle-earth.
</p>
</blockquote>
