The **Mode of Beleriand** was a mode of [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) which used full letters for vowels instead of [tehtar](/wiki/Tehtar "Tehtar").

## History {#History}

The history of this mode is not known but the name suggest it evolved in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), on [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand), when the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) came to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth); we are told that they were forbidden by King [Elu Thingol](/wiki/Thingol "Thingol") to use Quenya<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[1]</a></sup> and were forced to adopt [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin).

Normally, they adapted Tengwar to their new language, taking advantage of their inherent flexibility to other sounds, imitating maybe the way the [Falmari](/wiki/Falmari) of [Eldamar](/wiki/Eldamar) represented the [Telerin](/wiki/Telerin) sounds that didn't exist in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya). For some obscure reasons we do not know, they found the most usual tehta-mode inappropriate for Sindarin, and they took therefore also example of the original Fëanorian [Quanta Sarmë](/wiki/Quanta_Sarm%C3%AB), using distinct tengwar for vowels.

The Mode of Beleriand seems to have been held "traditionally" by the Noldor who were established in [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion) during the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), as seen on the [West-gate](/wiki/West-gate) of [Moria](/wiki/Moria) Inscription written by [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[2]</a></sup> After Eregion was destroyed in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1697](/wiki/Second_Age_1697 "Second Age 1697"), the Noldor migrated to [Imladris](/wiki/Rivendell "Rivendell"),<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[3]</a></sup> where the Mode of Beleriand was perhaps used even till the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[4]</a></sup>

## Structure {#Structure}

It seems that Sindarin speakers found use only for 3 of the 5 [témar](/wiki/T%C3%A9mar "Témar") since Sindarin lacked the palatalized (*ly, ny, ty*) sounds of Quenya as well as the labialized *qu*.

### Vowels {#Vowels}

Of the [Quessetéma](/wiki/Quesset%C3%A9ma), [Wilya](</wiki/Vilya_(word)> "Vilya (word)") was used for a final -*u* (or -*w*). There is also an *a*-tengwa called [Osse](/wiki/Osse), not corresponding with any of the older ones known from Quenya; it could have been imported from a Quenya semivowel unknown to us, which belonged to the Quanta-Sarme.

The carriers took vowel values too: The [telco](/wiki/Telco "Telco") was used for *i*, while the long carrier could have been used for the semiconsonantal initial sound *y-* (or *i-* as in *Ioreth*).<sup id="cite_ref-KL_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KL-14">[5]</a></sup>

Diphthongs were expressed by tehtar. Two <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Amatixi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Amatixi (page does not exist)">amatixi</a> (maybe a remnant of the earlier [Tyelpetéma](/wiki/Tyelpet%C3%A9ma)), used for -*i* in the diphthongs *ai*, *ei* and *ui*.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[6]</a></sup> *Au* (spelled -*aw* at the end of words) is not attested, but perhaps similarly the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=W-tehta&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="W-tehta (page does not exist)"><i>w</i>-tehta</a> was used (*[caun](/wiki/Caun "Caun")*, <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">a]é6</span><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395196"/>).

*ae*, although a diphthong, is attested written with the separate letters for *a* and *e*, and maybe the same applies to the diphthong *oe* too.

### Labialised consonants {#Labialised_consonants}

In Quenya mode, labialised consonants (*kw*, *ngw* etc) belonged to the Quessetéma. It's possible that the Mode of Beleriand used a <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=W-tehta&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="W-tehta (page does not exist)"><i>w</i>-tehta</a> <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">è</span><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395196"/> over consonants for this (<i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Edwen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Edwen (page does not exist)">edwen</a></i>, would perhaps be written here as <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">l2èl6</span><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395196"/>).<sup id="cite_ref-KL_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KL-14">[5]</a></sup>

### Nasalization {#Nasalization}

[Anto](/wiki/Anto), [Ampa](/wiki/Ampa) and other letters used for nasalized stops in Quenya took different values. A tilde is used before stops for nasalisation. (*mb*, <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">wP</span><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395196"/>)

### Length {#Length}

A well-known tehta that indicates long consonants, the under-tilde, was not used. Long *m* and *n* were written with [Malta](/wiki/Malta) and Númen (i.e., doubling of the [lúva](/wiki/L%C3%BAva "Lúva")), and other long consonants were written with two letters (*[mellon](/wiki/Mellon "Mellon")*, <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">yljjh6</span><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395196"/>).

A tehta was used to indicate long vowels, an acute accent called [Andaith](/wiki/Andaith) (*á*, <span class="tg-tengwar tg-tengwar--tengwar_eldamar">]R</span><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395196"/>)

<li id="cite_note-5"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-5">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">This letter (previously Ázë) became <i>ss</i> for <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Quenya_mode" title="Quenya mode">Quenya writing</a>. But it is not known which took it from which. There are two equal probabilities</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-6"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-6">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">Sindarin didn&#39;t possess palatalized sounds. <i>hy</i> had became <i>h</i>. Hyarmen was employed for this soft sound, not to be confused with <i>ch</i>.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-7"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-7">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">This mysterious letter, said to have the value <i>hw</i>, had never appeared in any of the Sindarin texts. It is unknown why was it employed instead of Hwesta and when. It was probably used much later by the <a href="/wiki/Mannish" title="Mannish">Mannish</a> modes, where Hwesta was used for the sound <i>ch</i> or <i>chw</i>.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-8"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-8">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">The lenited <i>g</i> sound was indicated by a sign called Gasdil, which is an elaboration of the letter <a href="/wiki/Halla" title="Halla">Halla</a>. It is used like the apostrophe, to indicate a missing <i>g</i>: <i><a href="/wiki/Galadh" title="Galadh">galadh</a></i>, &#34;tree&#34;, but <i>i ‘aladh</i>, &#34;the tree&#34;</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-9"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><a href="#cite_ref-9">↑</a></span> <span class="reference-text">This original letter is similar to Silme Nuquerna however it is a different shape. The sound it represents is the vowel <i>y</i> (not to be confused with the consonantal Quenya <i>y</i> in <i>yulma</i>)</span>
</li>
