**The Lay of Leithian** is the third chapter of *[The Lays of Beleriand](The_Lays_of_Beleriand.md)*. The full title is **The GEST of BEREN son of BARAHIR and Lúthien the FAY called TINUVIEL the NIGHTINGALE or the LAY OF LEITHIAN Release from Bondage**.

## Development {#Development}

Tolkien stated in his [diary](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien's_diaries.md) that he started the Lay during the time of summer examinations of [1925](../_/1925.md) at the [University of Leeds](../U/University_of_Leeds.md). Confirming this, the first recorded date of the writing of the Lay was at Line 557: [August 23](../A/August_23.md), [1925](../_/1925.md). The next date is two and a half years later, 27–8 March, [1928](../_/1928.md), at line 1161. Over the next nine days he wrote fully 1769 lines, up to 2929. These dates are for the copying out of the manuscript, not for their writing, so Tolkien may have had many passages earlier before he put them together. In September [1931](../_/1931.md), he abandoned the Lay.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> He sent it to [C.S. Lewis](../C/C.S._Lewis.md), who wrote back the following:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I sat up late last night and have read the <i>Geste</i> as far as to where Beren and his gnomish allies defeat the patrol of orcs above the sources of the <a href="../N/Narog.md" title="Narog">Narog</a> and disguise themselves in the <i>rëaf</i> [ <a href="../O/Old_English.md" title="Old English">OE</a>: &#39;garments, weapons, taken from the slain&#39;].  I can quite honestly say that it is ages since I have had an evening of such delight: and the personal interest of reading a friend&#39;s work had very little to do with it.  I should have enjoyed it just as well as if I&#39;d picked it up in a bookshop, by an unknown author.  The two things that come out clearly are the sense of reality in the background and the mythical value: the essence of a myth being that it should have no taint of allegory to the maker and yet should suggest incipient allegories to the reader
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>The Lay of Leithian introduction</cite></p>
</blockquote>

Later he wrote a detailed criticism, which pretends to treat the Lay as if it were a historical document. Tolkien was influenced by Lewis' comments, and made several minor changes based on them.

After the completion of *[The Lord of the Rings](The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, Tolkien began a new version of *The Lay*, which [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) includes in the same book as *[The Lay of Leithian Recommenced](The_Lay_of_Leithian_Recommenced.md)*.

### Canto {#Canto}

- **[Canto I](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_I.md)** - concerning [Elu Thingol](Thingol.md) and [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md)
- **[Canto II](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_II.md)** - concerning [Barahir the Bold](../B/Barahir.md), the treachery of [Gorlim](../G/Gorlim.md), his death, and [Beren Erchamion](../B/Beren.md)'s vow for revenge
- **[Canto III](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_III.md)** - concerning the meeting of Beren and [Lúthien Tinúviel](../L/Lúthien.md)
- **[Canto IV](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_IV.md)** - concerning Beren's capture and the bride-price of Lúthien as layed down by Thingol: a [Silmaril](../S/Silmarils.md)
- **[Canto V](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_V.md)** - concerning Lúthien in Doriath and her escape
- **[Canto VI](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_VI.md)** - concerning Beren in [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md), with [Finrod Felagund](../F/Finrod.md) and the scheming of [Celegorm](../C/Celegorm.md) and [Curufin](../C/Curufin.md)
- **[Canto VII](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_VII.md)** - concerning the battle of minds between Finrod and [Thû](Thû.md), and the fall of Finrod
- **[Canto VIII](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_VIII.md)** - concerning Celegorm and Curufin's capture of Lúthien, and her meeting with [Huan](../H/Huan.md)
- **[Canto IX](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_IX.md)** - concerning Huan's battle with Thû, the destruction of [Wizard's Isle](Tol-in-Gaurhoth.md), and the freeing of Beren
- **[Canto X](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_X.md)** - concerning the second parting of Beren and Lúthien and the battle with Celegorm and Curufin
- **[Canto XI](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_XI.md)** - concerning the reunion of Beren and Lúthien and the approach to [Thangorodrim](Thangorodrim.md)
- **[Canto XII](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_XII.md)** - concerning the battle of [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md) and [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md) and the enchanting of [Carcharoth](../C/Carcharoth.md)
- **[Canto XIII](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_XIII.md)** - concerning Lúthien's dance before Morgoth, his enchantment, and the stealing of the Silmaril
- **[Canto XIV](../L/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_XIV.md)** - concerning the flight of Beren and Lúthien and the rage of Carcharoth

## Recycling the Lay {#Recycling_the_Lay}

Tolkien recycled parts of the older version of the Lay, most notably in *[The Fall of Gil-galad](The_Fall_of_Gil-galad.md)* and the "[The world was young, the mountains green,](The_world_was_young,_the_mountains_green,.md)", both poems included in *[The Fellowship of the Ring](The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)*. Following are pieces found in both the *Lord of the Rings* and the Lay:

**Original Lay**

> *his silver lances long and keen;*<br> *the starlight in his shield was caught,*<br>

> *...There might and glory, wealth untold*<br> *Were wielded from his ivory throne*<br> *In many-pillared halls of stone.*<br> *There beryl, pearl, and opal pale*<br> *And metal wrought like fishes' mail*<br> *Buckler and corslet, axe and sword*<br> *And gleaming spears were laid in hoard*<br> *All these he had and loved them less*<br> *Than a maiden once in Elfinesse...*<br>

**Lord of the Rings**

> *his lance was keen.*<br> *His shining helm afar was seen;*<br> *the countless stars of heaven's field*<br> *were mirrored in his silver shield.*<br>

> *...There forged was blade, and bound was hilt;*<br> *The delver mined, the mason built.*<br> *There beryl, pearl, and opal pale,*<br> *And metal wrought like fishes' mail,*<br> *Buckler and corslet, axe and sword,*<br> *And shining spears were laid in hoard.*<br>
