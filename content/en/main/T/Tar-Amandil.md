**Tar-Amandil** was the third [King](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor "King of Númenor") of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor). He ruled de-facto for 148 years, from 442 to 590 of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age). Tar-Amandil was legally the third King, but actually its second ruler.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Amandil_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Amandil-1">[1]</a></sup> His father, [Vardamir Nólimon](/wiki/Vardamir "Vardamir"), declined to take the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre) upon the death of his own father, [Elros Tar-Minyatur](/wiki/Elros "Elros"), and was only a titular King that was deemed to have reigned one year.<sup id="cite_ref-Vardamir_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vardamir-2">[2]</a></sup>

Tar-Amandil had three children: two sons, [Elendil](/wiki/Tar-Elendil "Tar-Elendil") and [Eärendur](</wiki/E%C3%A4rendur_(son_of_Tar-Amandil)> "Eärendur (son of Tar-Amandil)"); and a daughter, [Mairen](/wiki/Mairen).<sup id="cite_ref-earlier_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-earlier-3">[3]</a></sup> Just as his father had yielded the Sceptre before his death, Tar-Amandil abdicated in 590<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Amandil_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Amandil-1">[1]</a></sup> and was succeeded by his eldest son Elendil, who would rule as [Tar-Elendil](/wiki/Tar-Elendil).<sup id="cite_ref-earlier_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-earlier-3">[3]</a></sup> This tradition of renouncing the Sceptre a few years before death was observed by most of the rulers of Númenor until [Tar-Atanamir](/wiki/Tar-Atanamir).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Amandil* is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya). The name is glossed to mean "Lover of Aman".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> According to Paul Strack, the name is a compound of *[Aman](/wiki/Aman)* ("Blessed Realm") and *[-(n)dil](/wiki/-ndil "-ndil")* ("friend, lover, devoted to").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Like all the rulers of Númenor who took their royal names in Quenya, Amandil added the prefix *tar-* ("high") to his name when he received the Sceptre.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

The [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) version of his name is <i><b>Aphanuzîr</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> which Paul Strack suggests probably means "Bliss-friend".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the twelfth note to a text written around [1959](/wiki/1959) and published in *[The Nature of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth)*, it is said that either the slow maturity of the [Half-elven](/wiki/Half-elven), or the "choice of kindred"<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-10">[10]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78</sup> extended to the third generation, suggesting that Tar-Amandil, and [Eldarion](/wiki/Eldarion), were among the Half-elven.<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-10">[10]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:82</sup> However, this was changed to the second generation in the actual text.<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-10">[10]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78</sup>
