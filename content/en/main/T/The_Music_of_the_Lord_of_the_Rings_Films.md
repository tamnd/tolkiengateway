<i><b>The Music of the Lord of the Rings Films</b></i> is a [2010](/wiki/2010) publication on [Howard Shore](/wiki/Howard_Shore)'s score for [*The Lord of the Rings* film series](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)") by music journalist [Doug Adams](/wiki/Doug_Adams).

It comes bundled with a CD for *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rarities Archive](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rarities_Archive)*, which contains previously unreleased and mock-up versions of tracks from the films.

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>(July 28, 2010—New York, NY) Carpentier and Alfred Music Publishing are pleased to announce the release of <i>The Music of the Lord of the Rings Films</i>, a comprehensive account of Howard Shore&#39;s score for the trilogy, by Doug Adams. The book will be available in the European Union on September 28 and in the U.S. and worldwide on October 5, 2010.
<br/><br/>
The culmination of almost a decade of writing and research, <i>The Music of the Lord of the Rings Films</i> is an unprecedented look at Howard Shore&#39;s Academy Award®-winning score, with extensive music examples, original manuscript scores, a rarities CD, and glimpses into the creative process from the composer, himself.
<br/><br/>
The 416-page full-color volume features a Foreword by Howard Shore, an Introduction by The Lord of the Rings screenwriter/producer Fran Walsh, original sketches by John Howe and Alan Lee, and numerous images from the films. Also included in the book, courtesy of Howe Records, is &#34;The Lord of the Rings: The Rarities Archive&#34; a CD presenting 21 tracks of previously unreleased music created for the films, and an audio interview with Howard Shore.
<br/><br/>
The score for The Lord of the Rings film trilogy has been hailed as some of the greatest film music ever written. Sweeping in scope, it is a musical interpretation of J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s Middle-earth—an operatic tapestry of cultures, histories, languages, and people.
<br/><br/>
&#34;Howard Shore&#39;s [LOTR] music has touched millions of people the world over,&#34; said Adams. &#34;It&#39;s been my great pleasure to assemble a piece that chronicles the creation of this music, and examines exactly how it tells this classic story.&#34;
<br/><br/>
&#34;Doug was a detective uncovering clues, tracing how one theme or character related to another,&#34; said Shore. &#34;He not only shows the themes and motifs for characters, cultures, objects, and their connection to Tolkien&#39;s work, but also the ideas that were sometimes buried deep inside the writing.&#34;
<br/><br/>
&#34;Researching Shore&#39;s music has been a true adventure,&#34; described Adams. &#34;His work is every bit as intricate and passionate as Tolkien&#39;s Middle-earth.&#34;
<br/><br/>
Doug Adams, a Chicago-based author and musicologist, was invited in 2001 by Howard Shore to observe and document his work on Peter Jackson&#39;s motion picture trilogy. Adams attended recording sessions, examined the original scores, and was given complete access to the composer&#39;s archives. As an acknowledged authority on Howard Shore&#39;s music, Adams has become a regular fixture at concerts and events across the globe. He runs the popular blog, www.musicoflotr.com, which documents his work on this project and brings fans together in ongoing discussions.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
