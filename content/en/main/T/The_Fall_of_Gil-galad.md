<i><b>The Fall of Gil-galad</b></i> was a lay that told of the loss of [Ereinion Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad "Gil-galad"), the last Great Elf-king of Middle-earth, in the [Siege of Barad-dûr](/wiki/Siege_of_Barad-d%C3%BBr) at the end of the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance).

## Poem excerpt {#Poem_excerpt}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Gil-galad was an elven-king.<br/>
Of him the harpers sadly sing;<br/>
the last whose realm was fair and free<br/>
between the Mountains and the Sea.
</p>
</div>

## History {#History}

According to [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), it was originally written in an ancient tongue. It seems to be a long poem, telling the story of the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), at least to the point where [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) aided in the overthrow of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), and was himself slain.

[Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) later translated it into the [Common Tongue](/wiki/Westron "Westron"), and taught it to [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) in his youth, who mistakenly thought that Bilbo was the composer.

Sam narrated the three introductory stanzas while approaching [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop), but he did not recall the following verses, which were "all about [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor)". Aragorn and Frodo knew more of the text.<sup id="cite_ref-FKnife_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FKnife-1">[1]</a></sup>
