The **inconsistencies** are various vague or contradictory statements that can be found throughout Tolkien's fiction.

Although [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) paid a great deal of attention to detail in his [Secondary world](/wiki/Secondary_world) to preserve a realistic consistency,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> in this vast creation, unavoidably, some more or less degrees of inconsistencies had slipped in. Most can be revealed after more than one reading of the books and through study. Regarding the inconsistencies, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) has noted that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...the credibility that my father was so anxious to maintain. Of course if he had noticed this inconsistency himself or had it pointed out to him he would have altered it without a second thought.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Christopher Tolkien&#39;s letter to <a href="/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond" title="Wayne G. Hammond">Wayne G. Hammond</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christina_Scull" title="Christina Scull">Christina Scull</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-comp_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comp-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xliv</sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

He also noted that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>However much my father desired to achieve consistency at every level of his work, from capital letters to the dates of dynasties, he was bound to fail. [...] His life was a perpetual battle against time (&amp; tiredness) [...] But he &#39;niggled&#39; on a grand and noble conception, &amp; indeed its coherence in fine detail is a part of its power.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Christopher Tolkien&#39;s letter to <a href="/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond" title="Wayne G. Hammond">Wayne G. Hammond</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christina_Scull" title="Christina Scull">Christina Scull</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-comp_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comp-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xliii</sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

J.R.R. Tolkien's comment, late in life, was quoted by Hammond and Scull:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Personally I have ceased to bother about these minor &#39;discrepancies&#39;, since if the genealogies and calendars etc. lack verisimilitude it is in their general excessive accuracy: as compared with real annals or genealogies! Anyway the slips are few, have mostly been removed, and the discovery of what remain seems an amusing pastime!
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Letter to <a href="/wiki/Joy_Hill" title="Joy Hill">Joy Hill</a>, October 30, 1967, quoted in the &#34;Note on the <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(50th_Anniversary_Edition)" title="The Lord of the Rings (50th Anniversary Edition)">50th Anniversary Edition</a>.&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Hammond and Scull added, "In fact Tolkien had not 'ceased to bother', and 'slips' were dealt with as opportunities arose."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Fans of Tolkien usually accept that in any work there are usually plot holes. In a larger, far more detailed and realistic book we expect fewer (if any) plot holes, when in reality there is a far greater chance because of its complexity.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

The [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") supposedly consists of translations and adaptations by a "translator Tolkien", the narrator of the Prologue and Appendices of *The Lord of the Rings,* from "ancient sources" such as the [Red Book](/wiki/Red_Book).<sup id="cite_ref-OldPrologue_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldPrologue-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Inconsistencies can be blamed on that "translator". For instance, he mentions in Appendix D that he might have made many errors on the calendar while translating the ancient sources, a comment Tolkien wrote as a fail-safe for any error he might have made. Likewise inconsistencies can be blamed on the sources. For instance, the "translator" mentions in Appendix F ("Of the Elves") that Frodo had erred in thinking the dialect of Sindarin spoken by the elves of Lórien was Silvan Elvish.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Such explanations attributing the inconsistencies to the "translator Tolkien" or the "original sources" are easy and unenlightening. Therefore many fans prefer to explain them with some internal explanation. Most of the explanations below are of this type. For example, at least some of the logical inconsistencies can be attributed to the characters' erroneous statements, since none of them has all knowledge about everything. Contradictions of this type are grouped under "Characters". Others are grouped under "Facts", which has subgroups.

For some readers, the consistency of detail is important, as Christopher Tolkien said, and for some finding the remaining inconsistencies is an amusing pastime, as J. R. R. Tolkien said (both quoted above). For others, though, such details are superficial and finding inconsistencies is over-analysis that distracts from values such as plot, character, atmosphere, and style.

## Characters {#Characters}

#### The Eldest {#The_Eldest}

Both [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil) and [Treebeard](/wiki/Treebeard) are referred to as the [eldest](/wiki/Eldest "Eldest") being in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). Tom says that about himself,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) mentions that the Elves knew Tom as "oldest and fatherless".<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup> However, [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) tells [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden) that Treebeard is "the oldest of all living things",<sup id="cite_ref-Isengard_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isengard-9">[9]</a></sup> and Celeborn addresses Treebeard as "Eldest".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Maybe Tom is not &#34;alive&#34; as Treebeard is.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> On this subject, Gandalf, <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a> have <i>existed</i> far longer than Treebeard, as they are <a href="/wiki/Maiar" title="Maiar">Maiar</a>, but they have not been <i>alive</i> (embodied physically) as long.</dd></dl>

#### Giving up a Ring of Power {#Giving_up_a_Ring_of_Power}

Gandalf says, "'*A [Ring of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power "Rings of Power") looks after itself, [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"). It may slip off treacherously, but its keeper never abandons it. At most he plays with the idea of handing it on to some one else's care—and that only at an early stage, when it first begins to grip. But as far as I know [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") alone in history has gone beyond playing, and really done it.'*"<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-13">[13]</a></sup> However, [Gandalf's own ring](/wiki/Narya "Narya") was given to him freely by [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan).<sup id="cite_ref-AppThird_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppThird-14">[14]</a></sup> Also, the dwarves of [Durin's Folk](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk) who held their Ring typically "surrendered" it when near death, and in particular [Thrór](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r) gave it to his son [Thráin II](/wiki/Thr%C3%A1in_II),<sup id="cite_ref-AppADurin_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppADurin-15">[15]</a></sup> as Gandalf says at the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond).<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Perhaps Gandalf meant only the Rings that <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a> had had a hand in making, which might be the only ones that &#34;grip.&#34; Gandalf&#39;s ring is one of the <a href="/wiki/Three_Rings" title="Three Rings">Three</a>, which Sauron never touched. Durin&#39;s Folk believed that they had received their Ring directly from the <a href="/wiki/Gwaith-i-M%C3%ADrdain" title="Gwaith-i-Mírdain">Elven-Smiths</a>, though Sauron did help to make it. Further, it is noted in the <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Appendices" title="The Lord of the Rings Appendices">Appendices</a> that the Rings could influence Dwarves to a much lesser extent than <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a>, specifically &#34;the only power over [Dwarves] that the Rings wielded was to inflame their hearts with a greed of gold and precious things.&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Gandalf may have been speaking implicitly only of Men, considering the context of the conversation and his audience.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup></dd></dl>

<h4><span id="Sam.27s_spying"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Sam&#39;s_spying">Sam&#39;s spying</span></h4>

[Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") tells Frodo that, as a result of [Sam's](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") eavesdropping, he and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") "know most of what Gandalf has told you about the [Ring](/wiki/One_Ring "One Ring")."<sup id="cite_ref-ACU_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACU-17">[17]</a></sup> Most of what Gandalf told Frodo was in one long conversation, at the end of which Gandalf caught Sam.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-13">[13]</a></sup> This contradicts Merry's statement that after Sam was caught, he "seemed to regard himself as on parole, and dried up."<sup id="cite_ref-ACU_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACU-17">[17]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Maybe Sam&#39;s information was what he learned before he was caught,<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup> though that&#39;s not what Merry says.</dd></dl>

#### A choice of dangers {#A_choice_of_dangers}

[Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) tells the hobbits, as they prepare to leave [Bree](/wiki/Bree), "*After [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop) our journey will become more difficult, and we shall have to choose between various dangers*."<sup id="cite_ref-Strider_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Strider-18">[18]</a></sup> The ridges they have to climb after Weathertop may be more difficult than the [Midgewater Marshes](/wiki/Midgewater_Marshes), but they encounter no dangers on the route Aragorn chooses, and he does not mention any choices of dangers or even warn the hobbits of any dangers, except the chance that the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) will find them as they cross the [Last Bridge](/wiki/Last_Bridge).<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-19">[19]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Not all risked dangers actually materialize, and Aragorn does cite two other possible courses that have their own possible hazards. One is going north through the <a href="/wiki/Ettenmoors" title="Ettenmoors">Ettendales</a> instead of crossing the <a href="/wiki/Ford_of_Bruinen" title="Ford of Bruinen">Ford of Bruinen</a>, but in addition to the danger of <a href="/wiki/Trolls" title="Trolls">trolls</a>, that route would take too long and the Company could run out of food. The other is finding the Ford without following the Road, but Aragorn regards that as impossible.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> In his estimation, the Road was clearly the path most likely to get the Company to Rivendell safely, regardless of whether the alternatives were truly impossible or merely less practical than the best alternative.</dd></dl>

<h4><span id="Aragorn.27s_knowledge"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Aragorn&#39;s_knowledge">Aragorn&#39;s knowledge</span></h4>

Aragorn tells the hobbits in Bree, "I know all the lands between [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") and the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains), for I have wandered over them for many years."<sup id="cite_ref-Strider_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Strider-18">[18]</a></sup> But later, speaking of the Ettendales, he says, "That is troll-country, and little known to me," and "I do not know the way".<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-19">[19]</a></sup> The Ettendales or Ettenmoors are on a line between the Shire and the northern part of the Misty Mountains.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>If we take Aragorn&#39;s line &#34;wandered over them&#34; literally, we can accept that Aragorn has also wandered over the Ettendales. Of course that does not necessarily means that Aragorn should know <i>everything</i> about those lands, or even know <i>all</i> ways (e.g., to Rivendell) through them. He says that he knows those lands &#34;little.&#34;</dd></dl>

#### Building Barad-dûr {#Building_Barad-dûr}

[Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) says at his Council that the foundations of [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr) were made with the [One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"),<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> which is consistent with its destruction when the Ring is destroyed. However, according to the Tale of Years, Sauron began building Barad-dûr in about [S.A. 1000](/wiki/Second_Age_1000 "Second Age 1000") and forged the Ring in about [S.A. 1600](/wiki/Second_Age_1600 "Second Age 1600").<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Maybe Sauron ordered his Orcs to begin gathering materials and preparing the land for construction in S.A. 1000, and only commenced building once he forged the Ring in S.A. 1600.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup> Six hundred years is a long time, but the Barad-dûr was a monumental structure that would have required an unprecedented amount of stone, iron, and other materials to erect. Or maybe the foundations were built before the making of the Ring, but lacking a last piece, or some power of will that the Ring provided.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup></dd></dl>

#### Heirlooms of Arnor {#Heirlooms_of_Arnor}

At the Council of Elrond, Aragorn says of [Narsil](/wiki/Narsil), the Sword of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil), "It has been treasured by his heirs when **all other heirlooms were lost**" (emphasis added).<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup> However, Appendix A lists other heirlooms that were kept with it: "...*there \[at [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell)\] also were kept the heirlooms of their house: the [Ring of Barahir](/wiki/Ring_of_Barahir), the shards of Narsil, the [star of Elendil](/wiki/Star_of_Elendil "Star of Elendil"), and the [sceptre of Annúminas](/wiki/Sceptre_of_Ann%C3%BAminas "Sceptre of Annúminas")*."<sup id="cite_ref-AppEriador_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppEriador-23">[23]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>The Ring of Barahir was briefly lost to the <a href="/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain" title="Dúnedain">Dúnedain</a> when Arvedui bartered it to the Lossoth for supplies. No loss of the other heirlooms appears.</dd></dl>

#### Possession of the [Nine Rings](/wiki/Nine_Rings) {#Possession_of_the_Nine_Rings}

In *[The Council of Elrond](/wiki/The_Council_of_Elrond)* [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) says that the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) kept their Rings by saying "The Nine the Nazgûl keep".<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup> However in most other references, it is mentioned that Sauron had taken them.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> Furthermore, Frodo does not see any Rings on them on [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop), and it is believed that if they did wear the Rings, they would have been fully [invisible](/wiki/Unseen "Unseen") (including their cloaks).<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> The line in the Council of Elrond represents Tolkien's earlier intention that the Nazgûl should still be wearing their Rings, but he later changed his mind and simply missed revising that sentence.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>The phrase can be also interpreted as &#34;The Nine <i>keep</i> the Nazgûl <i>in Sauron’s thrall</i>,&#34; though this would be an awkward construction. The doings of Sauron and the Nazgûl were mostly unknown to the Council, so it is likely that Gandalf did not actually know the physical disposition of the Rings and was merely alluding to the indelible association between the Nine Rings and the Nine Nazgûl. Moreover, as far as the Council was concerned, the situation was equivalent whether the Nine Rings were on Sauron&#39;s fingers or on those of his slaves, so the lack of precision was immaterial.</dd></dl>

#### Feeling the Mithril-coat {#Feeling_the_Mithril-coat}

The [Mithril-coat](/wiki/Mithril-coat) that Bilbo gives Frodo is "almost as supple as linen". However, when Bilbo slaps Frodo on the back after giving him the coat, he says, "*Ow!... You are too hard now to slap*!"<sup id="cite_ref-Ring_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ring-27">[27]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Perhaps Bilbo is joking. Or perhaps the mail tenses in response to an impact, similar to non-Newtonian fluids solidifying under stress.  It&#39;s fairly common in Middle-earth for works of master craftsmanship to have some &#34;magical&#34; attributes, and given the cost of mithril, it&#39;s a safe bet that only the best smiths in Erebor would have undertaken such a project. Of all of the magical abilities one might want for a coat of nigh-unbreakable chain mail, the ability to turn solid when struck while being as soft as cloth at all other times would be high on the list.</dd></dl>

<h4><span id="Galadriel.27s_mind-reading"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Galadriel&#39;s_mind-reading">Galadriel&#39;s mind-reading</span></h4>

[Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) tells Frodo and Sam that she knows Sauron's thoughts that concern the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-Mirror_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mirror-28">[28]</a></sup> It seems strange that they do not ask her whether she has any information they might find useful and she does not offer them any. Also, she does not seem to have known that [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) had switched to Sauron's side nineteen years earlier, though it must have been in Sauron's thoughts and the defection of a member of the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) seems to concern the Elves. At least, she did not warn Gandalf in the messages he got from [Lóthlorien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien "Lothlórien") after reading the [Scroll of Isildur](/wiki/Scroll_of_Isildur), the year before he trustingly entered [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc).<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup> (Also, when Aragorn was serving in Gondor under the name [Thorongil](/wiki/Thorongil), he "often warned [Ecthelion](/wiki/Ecthelion_II "Ecthelion II") not to put trust in Saruman".<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> It is strange that he knew not to trust Saruman but Gandalf did not.)

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Conceivably Galadriel gained the ability to read Sauron&#39;s mind sometime after the messages went to Gandalf.</dd></dl>

<h4><span id="Galadriel.27s_role"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Galadriel&#39;s_role">Galadriel&#39;s role</span></h4>

Galadriel tells [The Fellowship](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring "Fellowship of the Ring"), "I will not give you counsel, saying do this, or do that. For not in doing or contriving, or in choosing between this course and another, can I avail; but only in knowing what was and is, and in part also what shall be."<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup> Later she tells Frodo, "I do not counsel you one way or the other. I am not a counsellor."<sup id="cite_ref-Mirror_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mirror-28">[28]</a></sup> However, the rhyme she sends Aragorn advises a specific course: the [Grey Company](/wiki/Grey_Company) should come out of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), and Aragorn should take the [Paths of the Dead](/wiki/Paths_of_the_Dead).<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> Likewise [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) and [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) conclude that Galadriel sent the message to the Grey Company telling them to join Aragorn in [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan); this seems to be "contriving" and "choosing between one course and another". Incidentally, it is odd that the Grey Company got this message without knowing who it was from.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>One could imagine that someone else (<a href="/wiki/Celeborn" title="Celeborn">Celeborn</a>?) made the decisions and Galadriel only sent the messages; Legolas and Gimli may have erred in thinking she was the source. Alternatively, it is conceivable that Galadriel&#39;s words to the Fellowship were calculated to have a desired impact, even though at face value they seemed to be of no use.</dd></dl>

#### The origin of orcs {#The_origin_of_orcs}

Treebeard tells Merry and Pippin that [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) made trolls and orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> However, Frodo says to Sam, "*The [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) that bred them \[Orcs\] can only mock, it cannot make real new things of its own. I don't think it gave life to the Orcs, it only ruined and twisted them*."<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Tolkien went through several attempts to explain <a href="/wiki/Orcs/Origin" title="Orcs/Origin">the origin of orcs</a> and never stated a definitive answer. However, when he addressed this point in &#34;<a href="/wiki/Letter_153" title="Letter 153">Letter 153</a>&#34;, he described Treebeard as &#34;not one of the Wise&#34;, and he quoted and endorsed Frodo&#39;s line above.</dd></dl>

#### Tales of hobbits {#Tales_of_hobbits}

Pippin tells [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden), "I have wandered in many lands, since I left my home, and never till now have I found people that knew any story concerning hobbits."<sup id="cite_ref-Isengard_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isengard-9">[9]</a></sup> But hobbits live in [Bree](/wiki/Bree), Tom Bombadil knows many stories about hobbits, one would think the [Rangers](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North "Rangers of the North") (who guard [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") and Bree) and the Elves of Rivendell (where Bilbo has been living) would know some, and the Elves of Lórien have at least heard of hobbits.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Pippin has just woken up from a nap, after a lunch that included wine, and is talking to a king for the first time in his life; he may not be thinking clearly.  Alternatively, Pippin is the most glib and smooth or courtly of the hobbits in the Fellowship, and he may have exaggerated to flatter the king, even without realizing he was doing it.</dd></dl>

#### The peril of deep arts {#The_peril_of_deep_arts}

In connection with the *[palantír](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri "Palantíri")* of Orthanc, Gandalf observes to Pippin, "*Perilous to us all are the devices of an art deeper than we possess ourselves.*"<sup id="cite_ref-Palantir_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palantir-36">[36]</a></sup> However, never in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* or *The Lord of the Rings* does he warn [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin) or the hobbits against using elvish swords, which glow in the presence of orcs, or [daggers from the barrow](/wiki/Barrow-blades "Barrow-blades"), which are especially effective against Ringwraiths. He returns the [Phial of Galadriel](/wiki/Phial_of_Galadriel) to Frodo and [Galadriel's box of earth](/wiki/Gifts_of_Galadriel "Gifts of Galadriel") to Sam without any warnings. Also, there is no apparent danger in characters' using other products of elven arts ([elven cloaks](/wiki/Elven_cloak "Elven cloak"), a *[hithlain](/wiki/Hithlain "Hithlain")* rope, *[lembas](/wiki/Lembas "Lembas")*).

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Maybe the <i>palantíri</i> are &#34;devices&#34; in a sense in which the other things named are not.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Furthermore, it&#39;s possible that Gandalf was only attempting to discourage Pippin&#39;s curiosity, worrying that it could lead to another dangerous situation like that with the <i>palantír</i>.</dd></dl>

#### The eyes in Orthanc {#The_eyes_in_Orthanc}

When Aragorn tells Gimli and Legolas that he's confronted Sauron in the *palantír*, he says that Sauron had not previously known Aragorn was alive. In explanation, he adds, "*The eyes in Orthanc did not see through the armour of Théoden*".<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup> But [Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma") knew that Aragorn claimed to be Isildur's heir, as Gandalf points out to Pippin.<sup id="cite_ref-Palantir_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palantir-36">[36]</a></sup> Thus whether Wormtongue recognized Aragorn from Orthanc does not matter to Sauron's knowledge of Aragorn's existence. (What Aragorn should have deduced was that either Wormtongue never told Saruman about him, or Saruman did not reveal the knowledge, whether to Sauron through the *palantír* or to the Nazgûl who came to demand Saruman's supposed captive hobbit. The latter is what Gandalf tells Pippin he fears.)

<h4><span id="Mouth_of_Sauron_and_.22Sauron_the_Great.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Mouth_of_Sauron_and_&#34;Sauron_the_Great&#34;"><a href="/wiki/Mouth_of_Sauron" title="Mouth of Sauron">Mouth of Sauron</a> and &#34;Sauron the Great&#34;</span></h4>

[Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) mentions that the name "[Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)" (meaning "Abominable") is the name used by his enemies, and Sauron does not permit it to be pronounced.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup> Therefore it would be problematic, if not logically impossible, for the Ringwraiths to think "Sauron would deal with them later" as they rode away from [Crickhollow](/wiki/Crickhollow),<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[39]</a></sup> for the messenger to [Dáin](/wiki/D%C3%A1in_Ironfoot "Dáin Ironfoot") to refer to his master as "the Lord Sauron the Great," as Aragorn had heard at the Council of Elrond,<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup> and for a servant of Sauron to say, "*I am the [Mouth of Sauron](/wiki/Mouth_of_Sauron).*"<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[40]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>It could be that Aragorn was mistaken, perhaps thinking of the time before Sauron had declared himself. Another possibility is that the &#34;Mouth&#34; used a different name or title, perhaps in the Black Speech, and Frodo or the translator Tolkien &#34;translated&#34; it as Sauron to clarify it for readers. As the Ringwraiths&#39; thoughts would not have been known to anyone else, the passage presenting those thoughts must have been invented by Frodo or the translator Tolkien, who would be responsible for the choice of &#34;Sauron&#34; there. A similar possibility is that despite Aragorn&#39;s blanket statement, Sauron sometimes allowed his servants to use the name in communicating with others who used it. As many of his enemies only knew his &#34;true&#34; name as Sauron, it would also allow him to keep his true names and aliases hidden from them, as well as allow them to immediately recognize whom his servants were referring to.</dd></dl>

#### Hewing Orcs {#Hewing_Orcs}

At [Helm's Deep](/wiki/Helm%27s_Deep), [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) tells Éomer that he had "hewn naught but wood since I left Moria," forgetting that he [fought Orcs](/wiki/Breaking_of_the_Fellowship "Breaking of the Fellowship") not long before.

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>This discrepancy was noted by <a href="/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond" title="Wayne G. Hammond">Wayne G. Hammond</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christina_Scull" title="Christina Scull">Christina Scull</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_A_Reader%27s_Companion" title="The Lord of the Rings: A Reader&#39;s Companion">The Lord of the Rings: A Reader&#39;s Companion</a></i>, where they explained that they left it unchanged in the <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(50th_Anniversary_Edition)" title="The Lord of the Rings (50th Anniversary Edition)">50th anniversary edition</a> because correcting it was impossible, as it would require rewriting the dialogue.<sup id="cite_ref-comp_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comp-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xliv</sup> <a href="/wiki/Amon_Hen" title="Amon Hen">Amon Hen</a> was a week before the Battle of the Hornburg; even for a stout Dwarven warrior, lamenting not killing an Orc for that short period would make him look too bloodthirsty. However, it is possible to interpret Gimli as being scornful towards his latest opponents, deliberately meaning that they were no better than &#34;wood.&#34;</dd></dl>

## Facts {#Facts}

#### [Doors of Durin](/wiki/Doors_of_Durin) {#Doors_of_Durin}

*[Moria](/wiki/Moria)* translates as "Black Pit" or "Black Chasm" in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), and the name was said to have been given by the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) "without love,"<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-41">[41]</a></sup> possibly indicating that it was a derogatory description. Furthermore, *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* states that [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Khazad-d%C3%BBm) was "afterwards in the days of its darkness called Moria,"<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[42]</a></sup> suggesting the name was not widely used until after [Durin's Bane](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane) took over the city and it was overrun by [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs). It is therefore a paradox why that name appears on the [Doors of Durin](/wiki/Doors_of_Durin) (*Ennyn Durin Aran Moria*), made in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), and with the consent of the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves).

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>There are many possible explanations to this apparent inconsistency.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[43]</a></sup> The name may have been given by the Elves in reference to Moria&#39;s inherent darkness from being underground (in contrast with their love for &#34;green earth and the lights of heaven&#34;), and therefore was in use before Moria&#39;s fall to the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Balrog" title="Balrog">Balrog</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-41">[41]</a></sup> There is also no clear evidence that the Dwarves found this name to be offensive, and they may have had no objection to its use on the doors. Some &#34;external&#34; explanations suggest that since the translated names <a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin_(disambiguation)" title="Durin (disambiguation)">Durin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Narvi" title="Narvi">Narvi</a> are seen in the inscription, <i>Moria</i> may also be a &#34;translated&#34; name.</dd></dl>

#### Eagles {#Eagles}

One of the best-known alleged plot holes is why the [Eagles](/wiki/Eagles) came to carry [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") back from [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) but did not help them to fly the [One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") there, or at least help them at other points in their journey such as the crossing of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains). It is particularly hard to understand why this idea was not proposed in the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond).

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>This question is discussed in detail in <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Eagles#Flying_the_Ring_to_Mount_Doom" title="Eagles">the article on the Eagles</a>, which gives several explanations.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In general the explanations for not flying the Ring to Mount Doom are better than those for the Fellowship&#39;s not at least trying to have the Eagles fly them across the Misty Mountains.</dd></dl>

#### Distances {#Distances}

The distances of the Dwarves' travel to [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* seem to have different proportions than those in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. Tolkien tried to reconcile the *Hobbit* description with the scale of the *LotR* map but could not find an appropriate solution.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[44]</a></sup>

While Frodo and his companions needed 28 days from [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) to [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) (10.7 miles/day),<sup id="cite_ref-atlas_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atlas-45">[45]</a></sup> [Karen Wynn Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad) calculated that Bilbo and [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) needed 38 days (17.5 miles/day).<sup id="cite_ref-atlas_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atlas-45">[45]</a></sup> [Andreas Moehn](/wiki/Andreas_M%C3%B6hn "Andreas Möhn") goes further and supposes that Thorin and Co. wanted two weeks from the [Trollshaws](/wiki/Trollshaws) till Rivendell (a distance which [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel) covered in two days), resulting in c. 48 days total.<sup id="cite_ref-lalaith_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lalaith-46">[46]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>The distances and days are not described in the narrative and can be measured only by references such as the moon phases and other fan calculations; therefore there can be a margin of miscalculation.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In general, perhaps the Dwarves are by nature slower travelers than Men and/or Hobbits. In <i><a href="/wiki/The_Departure_of_Boromir" title="The Departure of Boromir">The Departure of Boromir</a></i> it is seen that <a href="/wiki/Gimli" title="Gimli">Gimli</a> had a problem keeping pace with <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> and <a href="/wiki/Legolas" title="Legolas">Legolas</a>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The errand of bringing the Ring to Rivendell was much more pressing than the Dwarves&#39;. The dragon was not going anywhere. And Frodo and his companions were hunted down by the <a href="/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl" title="Nazgûl">Nazgûl</a>.</dd></dl>

#### Beater and Biter {#Beater_and_Biter}

The swords [Glamdring](/wiki/Glamdring) and its "mate" [Orcrist](/wiki/Orcrist) are said to have belonged to King [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age). They never appeared much in battle (Turgon fought only in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad)) and they were witnessed only by the Orcs of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand). However, in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) the swords are found in a [Troll](/wiki/Troll) hoard in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), and the [Orcs of the Misty Mountains](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains) recognize them by their names. The Orcs do not seem to react similarly in the sight of Glamdring in *The Lord of the Rings*, nor do they seem to recognize [Narsil](/wiki/Narsil)/[Andúril](/wiki/And%C3%BAril), which is much more "recent".

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>There can be several theories and explanations of how the swords and even their reputations reach <a href="/wiki/Eriador" title="Eriador">Eriador</a>. However, the narrative of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Silmarillion" title="The Silmarillion">The Silmarillion</a></i> does not justify their significance to the extent of being remembered and recognized by the Goblins of the Third Age, even by tradition.</dd></dl>

<h4><span id="The_elf-king.27s_favorite_gems"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="The_elf-king&#39;s_favorite_gems">The elf-king&#39;s favorite gems</span></h4>

The narration of *The Hobbit* says the [elf-king](/wiki/Thranduil "Thranduil")'s favorite gems are "white."<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[47]</a></sup> However, after the [Battle of Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies), the narration says, "*To the Elven-king he \[[Bard](/wiki/Bard)\] sent the emeralds of [Girion](/wiki/Girion), such gems as he most loved*..."<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[48]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>The sentence is somewhat ambiguous: &#34;he&#34; could refer to Bard or Girion instead of the elf-king. However, the elf-king&#39;s preference in gems, not the others&#39;, would be relevant to Bard&#39;s choice of what to give him.</dd></dl>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">

#### Westron and English {#Westron_and_English}

In a few places, Tolkien might be thought to have forgotten that the English, including [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), in *The Lord of the Rings* is supposed to be translated from [Westron](/wiki/Westron) and related languages. Some of these are easily explained, and Tolkien explained the similarity between the Sindarin *[Baranduin](/wiki/Baranduin)* and the English "Brandywine".<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[50]</a></sup> The most difficult is the comment, "This was *[Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc)*, the citadel of [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), the name of which had (by design or chance) a twofold meaning; for in the Elvish speech *orthanc* signifies Mount Fang, but in the language of the Mark of old *the Cunning Mind*."<sup id="cite_ref-Isengard_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isengard-9">[9]</a></sup> However, *orthanc* means "cunning" not in [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) but in Old English, which Tolkien used to translate Rohanese.

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>By a further coincidence, the unattested name for <i>Orthanc</i> in Rohanese could also be &#34;Orthanc&#34; and mean &#34;cunning mind&#34;.</dd></dl>

#### Chronology {#Chronology}

##### When Bilbo departed {#When_Bilbo_departed}

In the chapter *[Roast Mutton](/wiki/Roast_Mutton)*, [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) depart from the *[Green Dragon](/wiki/Green_Dragon)* "one fine morning just before May." In the later written and published "[The Quest of Erebor](/wiki/The_Quest_of_Erebor)", part of "[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)", the author established that the day of departure was [27 April](/wiki/27_April).<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">[51]</a></sup> However, the chapter *[Flies and Spiders](/wiki/Flies_and_Spiders)* refers to what has happened "since they started their journey that May morning long ago."

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>The fifth month of the <a href="/wiki/Shire_Calendar" title="Shire Calendar">Shire Calendar</a>, Thrimidge, falls between 22 April to 21 May. By the human calendar, the journey would have started just before May; but by the hobbit calendar, the journey started during Thrimidge. The second reference to May could have been a &#34;translation error,&#34; where &#34;Thrimidge&#34; was translated as &#34;May&#34; regardless of the actual date.</dd></dl>

##### [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) during the [Watchful Peace](/wiki/Watchful_Peace) {#White_Council_during_the_Watchful_Peace}

According to [Appendix A](/wiki/Appendix_A), "The Stewards", during the [Watchful Peace](/wiki/Watchful_Peace) "*Sauron withdrew before the power of the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) and the Ringwraiths remained hidden in Morgul Vale*". However the Watchful Peace ended in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2460](/wiki/Third_Age_2460 "Third Age 2460"), three years before the White Council was formed. Thus according to [Robert Foster](/wiki/Robert_Foster), the reference to the Council is "incorrect".<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[52]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Foster suggests that the reference to the &#34;White Council&#34; is rather to &#34;the <a href="/wiki/Wise" title="Wise">Wise</a>&#34; in general.</dd></dl>

##### Moon phases {#Moon_phases}

Tolkien was particularly careful about the phases of the [Moon](/wiki/Moon) in the *The Lord of the Rings*. Yet some errors did elude him. See for example [January 13](/wiki/13_January "13 January"), [January 16](/wiki/16_January "16 January"), [February 22](/wiki/22_February "22 February"), [September 22](/wiki/22_September "22 September").

In general, it is possible that Tolkien consulted a modern almanac to model the moon phases, and also possible that he confused the meanings of "New Moon": the astronomical (the moment when the moon is darkest) and the colloquial (appearance of the new crescent moon).<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">[53]</a></sup>

Another error appears in *The Hobbit*: [Bard I](/wiki/Bard "Bard") killed [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) "at the rising of the moon"<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[54]</a></sup> when "the moon rose above the eastern shore and silvered his \[Smaug's\] great wings... the waxing moon rose higher and higher". Also the [thrush](/wiki/Thrush "Thrush") tells Bard, "*Wait! Wait!... The Moon is rising.*" <sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">[55]</a></sup> However, according to astronomy a waxing moon rises only in the morning, after the sun. We can be certain the moon was waxing because this occurs the day after [Durin's Day](/wiki/Durin%27s_Day), which is the first day in the last month of autumn that the new moon is visible together with the sun.<sup id="cite_ref-lalaith_46-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lalaith-46">[46]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>One might imagine that Bard needed to wait for the moon to fall below a cloudbank and that the tradition is corrupt.<sup id="cite_ref-lalaith_46-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lalaith-46">[46]</a></sup></dd></dl>

##### [Shadow over Hollin](/wiki/Shadow_over_Hollin) {#Shadow_over_Hollin}

While the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) traverses [Hollin](/wiki/Hollin), they see and feel a flying shadow that causes the stars to fade.<sup id="cite_ref-Ring_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ring-27">[27]</a></sup> Since no other such phenomena occur, when the [Fell Beasts](/wiki/Fell_Beast "Fell Beast") are introduced, the reader makes such a connection. However [Grishnakh](/wiki/Grishnakh) later tells [Uglúk](/wiki/Ugl%C3%BAk) that Sauron was not yet permitting the Nazgûl to traverse to the west side of the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin),<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[56]</a></sup> and still later Gandalf says, "*The Nazgûl have crossed the River!*" as if it were something new.<sup id="cite_ref-Palantir_36-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palantir-36">[36]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Possibly a Nazgûl got lost or disobeyed orders and prematurely crossed the <a href="/wiki/Anduin" title="Anduin">Anduin</a>.  Or possibly the fellowship noticed something unexplained and unrelated to the Fell Beasts. Perhaps it was some feeling of foreboding as they would eventually have to go to Moria. It could also be a sort of metaphor of Sauron observing them, as often throughout the books Sauron&#39;s gaze is compared to a heavy shadow bearing down on what it sees. Another possibility is that it was a flock of Saruman&#39;s <i><a href="/wiki/Crebain" title="Crebain">crebain</a></i> (crows) flying overhead.  Finally, it may be an intentionally vague event intended to create a feeling of unknown menace.</dd></dl>

##### Nights in Lórien {#Nights_in_Lórien}

The surviving members of the Company spend their first night in [Lórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien "Lothlórien") in a "flet" in a tree. On their second night, "they rested and slept without fear on the ground".<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">[57]</a></sup> On their third night, they sleep on the ground again, in a pavilion in [Caras Galadon](/wiki/Caras_Galadon). "For a little while the travellers talked of their night before in the tree-tops, and of their day's journey..." And Aragorn says, "*But tonight I shall sleep without fear for the first time since I left Rivendell.*"<sup id="cite_ref-Mirror_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mirror-28">[28]</a></sup> The night in the tree-tops was not the night before, and Aragorn did sleep without fear on the previous night, so the second night appears to be forgotten.

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>In Lórien at least some members of the Fellowship lose track of the flow of time. Shortly after Aragorn&#39;s remark, the narration says, &#34;They remained some days in Lothlórien, so far as they could tell or remember.&#34; Also, after leaving Lórien, Sam feels sure they had not spent a whole month there, despite the evidence of the phase of the moon, and Frodo thinks while in Lórien they were in the past and mentions that he does not remember seeing the moon while there. However, Legolas assures him that only their perception of time was changed, and Aragorn points out that the time had indeed been a month.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">[58]</a></sup> Aragorn&#39;s forgetting his night without fear could be an effect of this changed sense of time. The phrase in the narration &#34;the night before in the tree-tops&#34; is harder to explain within the story, as the narration does include the intervening night, but the inconspicuous contradiction might be deliberate foreshadowing of what the Company will experience.</dd></dl>

<h5><span id=".C3.89omer_and_.C3.89owyn_after_Aragorn.27s_coronation"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Éomer_and_Éowyn_after_Aragorn&#39;s_coronation">Éomer and Éowyn after Aragorn&#39;s coronation</span></h5>

In the chapter *[The Steward and the King](/wiki/The_Steward_and_the_King)*, it is stated: "So the glad days passed; and on the eighth day of May the Riders of Rohan made ready, and rode off by the North-way, and with them went the sons of Elrond. All the road was lined with people to do them honour and praise them, from the Gate of the City to the walls of the [Pelennor](/wiki/Pelennor_Fields "Pelennor Fields")."<sup id="cite_ref-SaK_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SaK-59">[59]</a></sup> However, in *[Appendix B](/wiki/Appendix_B)* of some editions of the novel<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">[60]</a></sup> there is the entry: "May 8 (of [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019")) Éomer and Éowyn depart **from** Rohan with the sons of Elrond".<sup id="cite_ref-AppB4_61-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB4-61">[61]</a></sup> (Emphasis added.)

<h5><span id="The_sapling.27s_discovery"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="The_sapling&#39;s_discovery">The sapling&#39;s discovery</span></h5>

In the text of *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)* it is stated: "And Aragorn planted the new tree in the court by the fountain, and swiftly and gladly it began to grow; and when the month of June entered in it was laden with blossom".<sup id="cite_ref-SaK_59-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SaK-59">[59]</a></sup> The wording of this sentence suggests that Aragorn planted the sapling *before* June began. However, in *Appendix B* there is the entry: "June 25 (of [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019")) King Elessar finds the sapling of the White Tree",<sup id="cite_ref-AppB4_61-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB4-61">[61]</a></sup> in which case it could not have blossomed until late in the month.

<h5><span id="Walda.27s_death"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Walda&#39;s_death">Walda&#39;s death</span></h5>

King [Walda](/wiki/Walda)'s death date is recorded in [Appendix A](/wiki/Appendix_A) as [Third Age 2851](/wiki/Third_Age_2851) but in [Appendix B](/wiki/Appendix_B) as [Third Age 2861](/wiki/Third_Age_2861).

## Later corrected {#Later_corrected}

Several errors were simply remnants of Tolkien's earlier writings, which later escaped his attention when revising the book. Some of them have been corrected in the later editions of *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit*.

#### Bridle and headstall {#Bridle_and_headstall}

The first edition referred to the "bridle and bit" of [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel)'s horse, [Asfaloth](/wiki/Asfaloth).<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-19">[19]</a></sup> [Rhona Beare](/wiki/Rhona_Beare) wrote to Tolkien asking how that was possible when elves do not use bridles. Tolkien replied in [Letter 211](/wiki/Letter_211) that he had written "bridle and bit" before thinking about how elves ride, and he changed it to "headstall" in the second edition. However, a later mention of Asfaloth's bridle remained in the chapter.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">[62]</a></sup>

<h4><span id="Durin.27s_Day"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Durin&#39;s_Day">Durin&#39;s Day</span></h4>

The original text of *The Hobbit* described [Durin's Day](/wiki/Durin%27s_Day) as occurring on "the first day of the last moon of autumn",<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">[63]</a></sup> the "first moon of autumn",<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">[64]</a></sup> and "the last week of autumn".<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">[65]</a></sup> In the [1995](/wiki/1995) edition the mention in Chapter 4 was revised to place the day at the end of autumn, in line with the other two mentions.<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">[66]</a></sup>

<h4><span id="Bandobras.27_parentage"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Bandobras&#39;_parentage">Bandobras&#39; parentage</span></h4>

The "[Prologue](/wiki/Prologue_Concerning_Hobbits,_and_other_matters "Prologue Concerning Hobbits, and other matters")" mentions that [Bandobras Took](/wiki/Bandobras_Took) was the son (not grandson) of [Isengrim Took II](/wiki/Isengrim_Took_II). This has been corrected in the 50th Anniversary edition.

<h4><span id="Sam.27s_birth"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Sam&#39;s_birth">Sam&#39;s birth</span></h4>

In the second edition of *The Lord of the Rings*, [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee)'s year of birth was added to *[The Tale of Years](/wiki/Appendix_B "Appendix B")* as [Third Age 2963](/wiki/Third_Age_2963). This contradicts both a later entry in *The Tale of Years* and the [Appendix C](/wiki/Appendix_C) given as [Third Age 2980](/wiki/Third_Age_2980).<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">[67]</a></sup>

<h4><span id="Gandalf.27s_letter"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Gandalf&#39;s_letter">Gandalf&#39;s letter</span></h4>

The letter Gandalf leaves for Frodo at the *[Prancing Pony](/wiki/Prancing_Pony)* is dated "[Midyear's Day](/wiki/Midyear%27s_Day), Shire Year, 1418."<sup id="cite_ref-Strider_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Strider-18">[18]</a></sup> However, in editions published during Tolkien's lifetime, Appendix B says that on June 29, "Gandalf meets [Radagast](/wiki/Radagast)." Then Gandalf says he left [Bree](/wiki/Bree) at dawn of the following day,<sup id="cite_ref-CofE_8-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofE-8">[8]</a></sup> which would be June 30, two days before Midyear's Day (as 1 [Lithe](/wiki/Lithe) comes between).

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Perhaps Gandalf, who was in a hurry and had been traveling for days, made the error. However, the entry in Appendix B for June 29, 3018, has been deleted from the 50th Anniversary Edition.</dd></dl>

#### Crossing Rohan inconspicuously {#Crossing_Rohan_inconspicuously}

As they ride away from [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard), Gandalf tells Merry that the [Lidless Eye](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron "Eye of Sauron") will be looking toward Rohan, so "*He \[Théoden\] will ride from there \[Helm's Deep\] to Dunharrow by paths among the hills. From now on no more than two or three together are to go openly over the land, by day or night, when it can be avoided."*<sup id="cite_ref-Palantir_36-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palantir-36">[36]</a></sup> However, later that night, after Pippin looks into the *palantír* and Gandalf says they must move from the spot, Théoden says he will go in a group of twelve, and Gandalf agrees.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">[68]</a></sup> Then when the trip to Helm's Deep starts, the number has increased to twenty-six, and Aragorn goes with Théoden.<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">[69]</a></sup> The trip from Helm's Deep to Dunharrow has a group of five hundred.<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">[70]</a></sup> They do ride through the hills, as Gandalf had said. "Most of the time" they're in a group bigger than three.<sup id="cite_ref-71" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-71">[71]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>The Fiftieth Anniversary Edition contains a clarifying addition to contextualize the order: &#34;He will ride from there <b>with many men</b> to Dunharrow by paths among the hills.&#34; (Emphasis added.) This may suggest that &#34;by paths among the hills&#34; is in opposition to &#34;openly over the land&#34;. So long as the large groups traveled by the hills, they were not conspicuous to the Lidless Eye.</dd></dl>

#### Knowledge of the *Palantíri* {#Knowledge_of_the_Palantíri}

After Gandalf learns that the crystal ball he has recovered is the *[palantír](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADr "Palantír")* of [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc), he tells [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) did not know any of the *palantíri* (presumably those of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor)) survived disaster in Gondor (presumably the [Kin-strife](/wiki/Kin-strife)).<sup id="cite_ref-72" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-72">[72]</a></sup> However, after [Denethor](/wiki/Denethor) reveals his *palantír*, Gandalf says in earlier editions, "*Though the Stewards deemed that it was a secret kept only by themselves, long have I known that here in the White Tower, as at Orthanc, one of the Seven Stones was preserved.*"<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">[73]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>Gandalf could have learned about the two <i>palantíri</i> by himself, after the last time the White Council met (66 years earlier), or could have been concealing his knowledge so as to keep secret his source for this information.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>However, Tolkien revised the sentence to read, &#34;Though the Stewards deemed that it was a secret kept only by themselves, <b>long ago I guessed</b> that here in the White Tower, one at least of the Seven Seeing Stones was preserved.&#34; (Emphasis added.) The revision appeared first in the second impression (1967) of the second <a href="/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin" title="Allen &amp; Unwin">Allen &amp; Unwin</a> edition,<sup id="cite_ref-74" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-74">[74]</a></sup> but is not in some subsequent printings.</dd></dl>

#### Addition of the Westmarch (and Buckland) to the Shire {#Addition_of_the_Westmarch_(and_Buckland)_to_the_Shire}

In early editions, the "Prologue" to *The Lord of the Rings* contained the sentence "Outside the [Farthings](/wiki/Farthing "Farthing") were the East and West Marches: the [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and the [Westmarch](/wiki/Westmarch) added to the Shire in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1462](/wiki/Fourth_Age_41 "Fourth Age 41")."<sup id="cite_ref-OldPrologue_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OldPrologue-4">[4]</a></sup> That had two inconsistencies with other parts of the text. First, the "Tale of Years" dates the event to [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1452](/wiki/Fourth_Age_31 "Fourth Age 31").<sup id="cite_ref-AppLater_75-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppLater-75">[75]</a></sup> Second, various points indicate that Buckland was part of the Shire. The clearest may be Merry's comment to the other hobbits, when they have gone through the tunnel under the [High Hay](/wiki/High_Hay) from Buckland into the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest), that they have left the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">[76]</a></sup>

<dl><dt>Explanation</dt>
<dd>In the 50th Anniversary Edition, the sentence was changed to &#34;Outside the Farthings were the East and West Marches: the Buckland; and the Westmarch added to the Shire in S.R. 1452.&#34; In addition to the correction of the date, the semicolon after &#34;Buckland&#34; indicates that Buckland was not added to the Shire after the War of the Ring, making the sentence consistent with the idea that it was already part of the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-NewPrologue_77-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NewPrologue-77">[77]</a></sup></dd></dl>

#### Mirror of Galadriel {#Mirror_of_Galadriel}

In editions prior to the 50th Anniversary Edition, the [Tale of Years](/wiki/Appendix_B "Appendix B") mentions that Frodo and Sam looked into the [Mirror of Galadriel](/wiki/Mirror_of_Galadriel) on [14 February](/wiki/14_February). However it is clear from the narrative that this occurred *one day* before departure on [16 February](/wiki/16_February), not *two*. [Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond "Wayne G. Hammond") and [Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull "Christina Scull") decided to fix the Tale of Years so that the Mirror of Galadriel sequence happened on 15 February.

## From the publishers {#From_the_publishers}

#### Nameless Pass as an alternative name for Cirith Ungol in the index {#Nameless_Pass_as_an_alternative_name_for_Cirith_Ungol_in_the_index}

The index entry for Cirith Ungol has Nameless Pass as an (alternative) name in brackets, and the index entry for Nameless Pass has "see Cirith Ungol" after it in the following e-book editions:

- The Lord of the Rings (i.e. The Fellowship of the Ring, The Two Towers and The Return of the King together in one e-book) published by HarperCollinsPublishers 2005 EPub Edition © MARCH 2009 <a class="internal mw-magiclink-isbn" href="/wiki/Special:BookSources/9780007322596">ISBN 978-0-007-32259-6</a>
- The Return of the King, J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Part 3 published by HarperCollins Publishers 2008 EPub Edition © MARCH 2009 <a class="internal mw-magiclink-isbn" href="/wiki/Special:BookSources/9780007322558">ISBN 978-0-007-32255-8</a>

The description of the landscape below seen by Frodo from the winding stairs in the chapter *The Stairs of Cirith Ungol* and the description of the landscape below seen by Sam from the pass of Cirith Ungol contradict that the Nameless Pass was an alternative name for the Pass of Cirith Ungol. In the chapter *The Stairs of Cirith Ungol* Frodo from above on the winding stair sees the wraith-road running from the dead city in a great revine at the head of the Morgul Valley to the "Nameless Pass" that is also referred to as the "main pass."

In the chapter *The Tower of Cirith Ungol* Sam sees a broad road running from the Tower of Cirith Ungol down to join the road that came over the Morgul Pass. "The dead city" seems to be an alternative name for Minas Morgul. "Wraith-road" seems to be an alternative name for the Morgul-road.

Frodo can see the road that runs from Minas Morgul in the great ravine at the head of the Morgul Vale to the Nameless Pass from his observation point high up on the winding stair that leads to the tunnel and then on to the pass of Cirith Ungol. Since the road leads from the Tower of Cirith Ungol and winds down to join the road that came from the Morgul Pass, then Cirith Ungol must be higher up on the left side of the Nameless Pass, and the Nameless Pass cannot be the same as the Pass of Cirith Ungol. Since no other pass is mentioned in the landscape, the "Nameless Pass" must be the Morgul Pass (also referred to as the "main pass"), and Cirith Ungol must be the pass that is also referred to as the "high pass."

#### HarperCollins 2005 EPub Edition of March 2009 {#HarperCollins_2005_EPub_Edition_of_March_2009}

The entry that King Argeleb I was slain in battle in appendix B The Third Age is erroneously dated with the year 1977 instead of the year 1356 that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966. As a consequence the following entries up to and including the entry that many Periannath migrate from Bree erroneously have the date from the entry that immediately precedes each entry that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966.

The entry that the Corsairs ravage Pelargir and slay King Minardil is erroneously dated with the year 1601 instead of the year 1634 that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966. The year 1601 is the year for the entry that is two entries above this entry.

The entry that the Great Plague devastates Gondor is erroneously dated with the year 1634 instead of the year 1636 that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966. The following entries up to and including the entry that Frumgar leads the Éothéod into the North also erroneously have the date from the entry that immeditately precedes each entry that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966.

The entry that the Nazgûl issue from Mordor and besiege Minas Ithil is erroneously dated with the year 2002 instead of the year 2000 that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966.

The entry for the fall of Minas Ithil is erroneously dated with the year 2043 instead of the year 2002 that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966.

The entry that King Eärnur becomes King of Gondor and is challenged by the Witch-king is erroneously dated with the year 2000 instead of the year 2043 that was in the edition of the ROTK by George Allen &amp; Unwin from 1966.
