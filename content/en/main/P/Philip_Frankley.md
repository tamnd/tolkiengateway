**Philip Frankley** (born in [1932](../_/1932.md)) was a member of the [Notion Club](../N/Notion_Club.md).

## History {#History}

Frankley was a poet educated at Queen's, at one time well-known as a leader of the Queer Metre movement, but now known as just a poet who had published several volumes of collected verses. He is said to have suffered from *"horror borealis"* - intolerance of all things Northern or Germanic, but nonetheless was a close friend of [Lowdham](../A/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham.md) whose chief interest lay in the [Anglo-Saxon](../A/Anglo-Saxon.md) language and history.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Sometimes Lowdham refered to him with made-up titles based on his name (<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Greek (page does not exist)">Greek</a> *phil-ippos*, "horse-loving"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>), like "Lover of Horses and Horseplay" or "Horsefriend of Macedon".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

According to [Guildford](../N/Nicholas_Guildford.md) he regards any knowledge of his own language at any period before the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bosworth_Field" title="wikipedia:Battle of Bosworth Field">Battle of Bosworth</a> as a misdemeanour.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

During one of the meetings of the Notion Club, he recited the poem *[Imram](../I/Imram.md) (The Death of [Saint Brendan](../S/Saint_Brendan.md))* before the other members. He said that it came to him, more or less complete, in his dream.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [1989](../_/1989.md), Frankley published a volume of a collection of poems entitled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Experiments_in_Pterodactylics&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Experiments in Pterodactylics (page does not exist)">Experiments in Pterodactylics</a></i>. Of which, the opening poem was a short poem that he read at the [Notion Club](../N/Notion_Club.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
