<i><b>Picturing Tolkien: Essays on Peter Jackson&#39;s</b></i> **The Lord of the Rings** <i><b>Film Trilogy</b></i> is a [2011](/wiki/2011) collection of scholarly essays about [*The Lord of the Rings* (film series)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>This group of new critical essays offers multidisciplinary analysis of director Peter Jackson&#39;s spectacularly successful adaptations of J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s <i>Lord of the Rings</i> trilogy: <i>The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (2001), <i>The Two Towers</i> (2002) and <i>The Return of the King</i> (2003). Part One of the collection, &#34;Techniques of Structure and Story,&#34; compares and contrasts the organizational principles of the books and films. Part Two, &#34;Techniques of Character and Culture,&#34; focuses on the methods used to transform the characters and settings of Tolkien&#39;s narrative into the personalities and places visualized on screen. Each of the sixteen essays includes extensive notes and a separate bibliography.Janice M. Bogstad is a professor of women&#39;s studies and English and is head of technical services at the McIntyre Library, University of Wisconsin-Eau Claire. Her written work has appeared in more than 60 reference books. University of Wisconsin-Madison Emeritus Philip E. Kaveny is an independent scholar, author, playwright, poet and lecturer. In 1976 he cofounded Madison&#39;s Feminist Oriented Conference.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
