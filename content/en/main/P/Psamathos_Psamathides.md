**Psamathos Psamathides** was a wizard in *[Roverandom](/wiki/Roverandom)*. Psamathos is the oldest of the *Psamathists* - sand-sorcerers - is exceedingly old and wise, and enjoys spending his days sleeping in the sand.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:11–12 ff</sup>

In the story, Psamathos helps [Rover](/wiki/Rover) by turning him back into a real dog (albeit of smaller size).<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:12</sup> As Rover doesn't wish to go home, Psamathos has his postman [Mew](/wiki/Mew) (a seagull) take Rover to the moon. When Rover (now "Roverandom") returned from the [moon](/wiki/Moon "Moon"), Psamathos tried to return Roverandom to his normal size but his magic could not undo [Artaxerxes](/wiki/Artaxerxes)'s spell.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:17</sup> Psamathos summons the giant whale [Uin](/wiki/Uin) to take Roverandom to the bottom of the Deep Blue Sea to persuade Artaxerxes to return him to the normal size.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:55–8</sup> At the end of the story, Roverandom is reunited with Psamathos and they become good friends.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:9</sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Psamathos is an ancient Greek poetic word for "sand", which includes the word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CF%88%CE%AC%CE%BC%CE%BC%CE%BF%CF%82" title="wiktionary:ψάμμος">psammos</a></i> "sand".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-%CE%AF%CE%B4%CE%B7%CF%82" title="wiktionary:-ίδης">-ides</a></i> is an ancient Greek name ending indicating parentage.

The meaning of *Psamathos Psamathides* name is "Sandy, the son of Sandy" whilst *Psamathist* is "expert on sand".<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:95</sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond "Wayne G. Hammond") and [Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull "Christina Scull") note that the Psamathists were originally named <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psammead" title="wikipedia:Psammead">Psammead</a></i>, the sand fairies in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Nesbit" title="wikipedia:E. Nesbit">E. Nesbit</a> story <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Children_and_It" title="wikipedia:Five Children and It">Five Children and It</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:94</sup> Nesbit's Psammeads were also named after <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CF%88%CE%AC%CE%BC%CE%BC%CE%BF%CF%82" title="wiktionary:ψάμμος">psammos</a></i>. <span></span>
