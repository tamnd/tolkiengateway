**Pembroke College** is one of the [University of Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford)'s 39 constituent colleges.

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) became [Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon](/wiki/Rawlinson_and_Bosworth_Professor_of_Anglo-Saxon) in [1925](/wiki/1925). Despite being a fellow at the college he chose not to live at the college. During this period he wrote *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* and *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)* as well as [Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics](/wiki/Beowulf:_The_Monsters_and_the_Critics). He kept the position for twenty years before moving to Merton College in [1945](/wiki/1945) and taking up the position of Professor of English Language and Literature.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien seemed to have disliked Pembroke. In a <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Letter" title="Letter">letter</a> ([Letter 96](/wiki/Letter_96)) to his son, he described Pembroke as "miserable" and said he desired be in a "real college".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Since 2013 Pembroke Colleges has delivered an annual lecture called The J.R.R. Tolkien Lecture on Fantasy.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In [2024](/wiki/2024) a memorial to J.R.R. Tolkien was unveiled, created by [Tim Tolkien](/wiki/Tim_Tolkien), the great-nephew of J.R.R. Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
