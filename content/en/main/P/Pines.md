**Pines** were common evergreen trees, found throughout the lands of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), but especially in the highlands of [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion), a land which took its name from the pine trees that grew there.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name for a "pine-tree" is <i><b>thôn</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the *[Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies")*, [Ilkorin](/wiki/Ilkorin) <i><b>thōn</b></i> and [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) <i><b>thaun</b></i> (pl. *thuin*), deriving from root [THON](/wiki/THON), are said to mean "pine-tree".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In Tolkien's very early conception of the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) languages (dating from ca. [1917](/wiki/1917)), the [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) name for a "pinetree" is <i><b>aigos</b></i> and the [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) name is <i><b><a href="/wiki/Aikasse#Qenya" title="Aikasse">aikasse</a></b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1994: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a>—<a href="/wiki/Treasures_of_Middle-earth_(2nd_edition)" title="Treasures of Middle-earth (2nd edition)">Treasures of Middle-earth</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>A type of pine tree, namely <i><b>Blue Pine</b></i>  (<a href="/wiki/Sindarin" title="Sindarin">S</a>. <i>Luinthon</i>, <a href="/wiki/Quenya" title="Quenya">Q</a>. <i>Luinisono</i>), also called <i>Mother Pine</i>, is described as a common evergreen tree in <a href="/wiki/Rhudaur" title="Rhudaur">Rhudaur</a>. As the name suggests, its wood had a blue-silver hue. The wood&#39;s high amounts of tannic acid made it act as a natural preservative, while its large cones carried edible seeds which were consumed by <a href="/wiki/Hillmen" title="Hillmen">Hillmen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>
