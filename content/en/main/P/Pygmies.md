"**Pygmies**" was a term used in relation to the height and intelligence of peoples and creatures.<sup id="cite_ref-nature_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nature-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After the [Awakening of Men](/wiki/Awakening_of_Men) during the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), the group of [Men](/wiki/Men) known as the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) did not rise that much above the state of pygmies.<sup id="cite_ref-nature_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nature-1">[1]</a></sup>

Sometime during or after the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age), [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) "relapsed into the state of 'pygmies'" after some [Men](/wiki/Men) drove them from their lands, making them afraid of being seen since they were hunted like animals, with future generations scarcely reaching a height of three feet tall<sup id="cite_ref-prologue_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prologue-2">[2]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-nature_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nature-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, an unspecified group referred to as "pygmies" are mentioned in one outline of *[the Tale of Eärendel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_E%C3%A4rendel "The Tale of Eärendel")* in which [Eärendel](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel) was supposed to have an encounter with them somewhere in the south of the world along with [Tree-men](/wiki/Tree-men) and *[Sarqindi](/wiki/Sarqindi)*.<sup id="cite_ref-south_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-south-3">[3]</a></sup>

In an early linguistic text, pygmies were grouped with the [dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") and the [giants](/wiki/Giants "Giants") under the category of "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Earthlings&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Earthlings (page does not exist)">Earthlings</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In the same text, pygmies are also called <i><b>Pilkir</b></i> in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya), possibly being derived from the root <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=PIKI&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="PIKI (page does not exist)">PIKI</a> ("small").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In an early draft of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, after the [Eagles](/wiki/Eagles) saved [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) from the [Wolves](/wiki/Wolves), Tolkien wrote an outline of potential ideas on some loose paper, mentioning pygmies in the second line: "[Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood) &amp; &lt;pygmies&gt;".<sup id="cite_ref-first_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-6">[6]</a></sup>

In a reply to a letter, Tolkien denied that his Hobbits were consciously inspired by pygmies. Despite this, however, Tolkien was skeptical of whether any influence was subconscious or conscious.<sup id="cite_ref-second_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-7">[7]</a></sup>

In a reply to a different letter, Tolkien mentioned "pygmies of the African forest".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
