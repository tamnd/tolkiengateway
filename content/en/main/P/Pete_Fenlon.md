**Peter C. Fenlon, Jr.** (born [1955](/wiki/1955)) is an American table-top game designer/author and illustrator. He was the former president and one of the founders of [Iron Crown Enterprises](/wiki/Iron_Crown_Enterprises), and contributed extensively to the [Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing) game.

Around [2000](/wiki/2000), after ICE went bankrupt, Pete Fenlon and long-time colleague [Coleman Charlton](/wiki/Coleman_Charlton) joined the Mayfair Game company, which ICE had previously bailed out.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> When Mayfair's rights to Catan were transferred to [Asmodee](/wiki/Asmodee), Fenlon became the head of the newly created Catan Studio subsidiary.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## The Fenlon Style {#The_Fenlon_Style}

Most of the regional maps were designed by Pete Fenlon at the scale 1’’ = 20 miles. Infographic by <a href="/wiki/User:Airyn" title="User:Airyn">Airyn</a> (2025).</figcaption></figure>

As an illustrator, Fenlon is especially known for his maps of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). These were a large-scale map of the continent of Middle-earth (first released in [1982](/wiki/1982) as *[An Artist's Interpretation of Middle Earth](/wiki/An_Artist%27s_Interpretation_of_Middle_Earth)*) and several smaller-scale maps of different regions of Middle-earth (most of whom were printed in *[Northwestern Middle-earth Map Set](/wiki/Northwestern_Middle-earth_Map_Set)*). The smaller-scale maps were often included as separate color fold-outs with the [MERP](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing "Middle-earth Role Playing") 1st Ed. modules. With MERP 2nd Ed., no new maps were released.

Fenlon's maps have continued to have an explicit influence on later Middle-earth gaming cartographers (e.g., [Thomas Morwinsky](/wiki/Thomas_Morwinsky) and <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Sampsa_Rydman&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sampsa Rydman (page does not exist)">Sampsa Rydman</a>), and his continental map has been discussed and revised in the magazines *[Other Hands](/wiki/Other_Hands)*<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and *[Other Minds](/wiki/Other_Minds)*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The characteristical maps of Fenlon have even created an expression known as 'Fenlon Style maps'. In January 2008, the *Cartographer's Annual* released a Pete Fenlon style pack for use with ProFantasy Software cartography tools.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
