<i><b>Pearl</b></i> is an edition of the [Middle English](/wiki/Middle_English) manuscript <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_(poem)" title="wikipedia:Pearl (poem)">Pearl</a></i>, edited by [E.V. Gordon](/wiki/E.V._Gordon). It was first published in [1953](/wiki/1953).

Originally *Pearl* was planned to be edited by [E.V. Gordon](/wiki/E.V._Gordon) and [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), like they did with their previous work *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight](</wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight_(edition)> "Sir Gawain and the Green Knight (edition)")*. But Tolkien couldn't give enough time to it, so Gordon continued the work alone. At his death in [1938](/wiki/1938) the work was almost complete, and his wife [I.L. Gordon](/wiki/I.L._Gordon) helped finish editing it and published it in [1953](/wiki/1953).

Nevertheless, Tolkien contributed notes and corrections to the book, and provided a part of the introduction ("Form and Purpose", pp. xi–xix). "Form and Purpose" was reprinted as part III of the Introduction to *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Pearl, and Sir Orfeo](/wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight,_Pearl,_and_Sir_Orfeo)* (pp. 18–23).

## From the inside flap {#From_the_inside_flap}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>This is a new edition of the fourteenth century alliterative poem Pearl, which, though generally regarded as one of the greatest of medieval poems, has suffered neglect of late years, since earlier editions have long been out of print. The text is more conservative than in earlier editions. The introduction includes an analysis of the form and purpose of the poem, a discussion of the doctrinal theme and the symbolism, and a survey of the dialect. There are explanatory notes, a glossary with etymologies, linguistic appendixes, and a selected bibliography. Emphasis throughout is on the poem itself: more general controversial topics, and speculations, are avoided.
</p>
</blockquote>
