**Parmatéma** was one of the four series of the [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) writing system. As with all of the series it consisted of six characters in each of six *[tyeller](../T/Tyellë.md)* or 'grades'. This particular series took its name from the first of its signs, *[parma](Parma.md)* (literally meaning 'book', but here simply representing the character 'p'). Half of the symbols in this series had one bow or *[lúva](../L/Lúva.md)* while the other half had two bows. All bows in this series were closed (having a bar across the 'opening' of the bow), curving downwards and to the right from a stem, or *[telco](../T/Telco.md)*, which was either lowered, raised, or shortened.

In principle, the characters in this series could represent a variety of different sounds, though in practice their use had become essentially standardised by the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md). The first four signs in the series, *[parma](Parma.md)*, *[umbar](<../U/Umbar_(word).md>)*, *[formen](../F/Formen.md)* and *[ampa](../A/Ampa.md)*, each represented a variant of the basic 'p' sound: 'p', 'b', 'f' and 'v', respectively. The fifth sign, *[malta](../M/Malta.md)*, represented 'm', and finally *[vala](../V/Vala.md)* indicated 'w'. The names of the characters had been assigned in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) and had also become standardised, which is why the names *ampa* and *vala* did not match the sound of the consonants that they represented.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
