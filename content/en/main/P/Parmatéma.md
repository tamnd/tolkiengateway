**Parmatéma** was one of the four series of the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) writing system. As with all of the series it consisted of six characters in each of six *[tyeller](/wiki/Tyell%C3%AB "Tyellë")* or 'grades'. This particular series took its name from the first of its signs, *[parma](/wiki/Parma "Parma")* (literally meaning 'book', but here simply representing the character 'p'). Half of the symbols in this series had one bow or *[lúva](/wiki/L%C3%BAva "Lúva")* while the other half had two bows. All bows in this series were closed (having a bar across the 'opening' of the bow), curving downwards and to the right from a stem, or *[telco](/wiki/Telco "Telco")*, which was either lowered, raised, or shortened.

In principle, the characters in this series could represent a variety of different sounds, though in practice their use had become essentially standardised by the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). The first four signs in the series, *[parma](/wiki/Parma "Parma")*, *[umbar](</wiki/Umbar_(word)> "Umbar (word)")*, *[formen](/wiki/Formen "Formen")* and *[ampa](/wiki/Ampa "Ampa")*, each represented a variant of the basic 'p' sound: 'p', 'b', 'f' and 'v', respectively. The fifth sign, *[malta](/wiki/Malta "Malta")*, represented 'm', and finally *[vala](/wiki/Vala "Vala")* indicated 'w'. The names of the characters had been assigned in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and had also become standardised, which is why the names *ampa* and *vala* did not match the sound of the consonants that they represented.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
