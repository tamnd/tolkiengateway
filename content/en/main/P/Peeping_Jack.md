**Peeping Jack** was a [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") inhabitant of the town of [Delving](/wiki/Delving) who appeared in *[Perry-the-Winkle](/wiki/Perry-the-Winkle)*, a nursery rhyme<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> by [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee)<sup id="cite_ref-preface_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-preface-3">[3]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the poem, Peeping Jack espied [Perry-the-Winkle](</wiki/Perry-the-Winkle_(hobbit)> "Perry-the-Winkle (hobbit)") riding to <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Faraway&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Faraway (page does not exist)">Faraway</a> on the [Lonely Troll](/wiki/Lonely_Troll)'s back. Later, upon Perry's return, Peeping Jack revealed his spying to the rest of the People.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

Peeping Jack was one of the [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits) who travelled to Faraway in order to demand food from the Lonely Troll. Peeping Jack was among four Hobbits who the Troll never wanted to see again.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name "Peeping Jack" alludes to the character's role as a spy and is a reference to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peeping_Tom" title="wikipedia:Peeping Tom">Peeping Tom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-IE_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IE-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In the first version of the poem in early [1928](/wiki/1928),<sup id="cite_ref-commentary_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-commentary-5">[5]</a></sup> which was entitled *The Bumpus*, Peeping Jack didn’t exist and William Winkle himself told the People where he had been.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> Peeping Jack was also absent in the second version on [29 March](/wiki/29_March),<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> in which Tolkien revised the poem as part<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> of his *[Tales and Songs of Bimble Bay](/wiki/Tales_and_Songs_of_Bimble_Bay)* series. He was absent in the third version as well.<sup id="cite_ref-commentary_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-commentary-5">[5]</a></sup>

In early [1961](/wiki/1961),<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> when Tolkien revised the poem for inclusion within *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil)*, Peeping Jack first appeared with no difference in details to the published version<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup>
