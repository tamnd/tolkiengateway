**Pards** were <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felidae" title="wikipedia:Felidae">feline creatures</a> only mentioned in [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") poetry. Apparently the pards lived in wooded lands of the East (perhaps referring to the lands beyond [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn), the [Uttermost East](/wiki/Uttermost_East)). The beast, "fleet upon feet", leaped on its prey from above.<sup id="cite_ref-ATB12_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ATB12-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

At the [Bodleian Library](/wiki/Bodleian_Library) in [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford) are kept several manuscripts of medieval bestiaries containing descriptions of the legendary *pard*. The animal is described as very swift, killing its prey with a single leap. It was believed that the mating of a pard and a [lion](/wiki/Lions "Lions") produced the leopard.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> In [1962](/wiki/1962) [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") published the poem "[Cat](</wiki/Cat_(poem)> "Cat (poem)")",<sup id="cite_ref-ATB12_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ATB12-1">[1]</a></sup> which, carrying many similarities with this medieval notion, shows that he was likely familiar with these manuscripts.
