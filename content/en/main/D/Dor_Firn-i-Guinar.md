**Dor Firn-i-Guinar**, the **Land of the Dead that Live**, was the name given by the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md) to the region surrounding the isle of [Tol Galen](../T/Tol_Galen.md) in southern [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md), where [Beren](../B/Beren.md) and [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md) lived after their return from the dead.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It is told that the country was exceptionally beautiful, even reminding of the blessed land of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In an early version of the legendarium, *Dor Firn-i-Guinar* (called at this time *Land of the Cuilwarthin*) was located south-west to Nargothrond, on the Hills of the Hunter (later [Taur-en-Faroth](../T/Taur-en-Faroth.md))<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>. This location offered an "enchanted west" to Beleriand<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>. Then, the name was moved in the empty land between the Sirion and the Gelion. Still then, the name was struck out and a note in pencil was added : "Lies to the east of this and beyond the Great Lands of the East and of wild men"<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>. And indeed, on the Eastward extension of [The First 'Silmarillion' Map](../T/The_First_'Silmarillion'_Map.md), in Ossiriand, appears "Gweirth-i-Cuina", written over "Cuilwarthin"<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Dor Firn-i-Guinar* is the name appearing in the published *[Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> apparently consisting of the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) elements *[dor](Dor.md)* "land" + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Firn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Firn (page does not exist)">firn</a></i> "dead" + *[in](../I/In.md)* "who, that" + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Cuinar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Cuinar (page does not exist)">cuinar</a></i> "live".<sup id="cite_ref-Tolkiendil_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolkiendil-8">[8]</a></sup>

In his manuscripts, [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) experimented with many variations on how to translate *Land of the Dead that Live*: *I·Cuilwarthon*, *I·Guilwarthon*, *Cuilwarthien*, *Gwerth-i-cuina*, and *Gwerth-i-guinar*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> In a [1972](../_/1972.md) letter, Tolkien used the name *Dor Gyrth i chuinar*.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
