The **Dúnedain** ([S](../S/Sindarin.md): "West-men"), singular **Dúnadan**, were the [Men of Númenor](../M/Men_of_Númenor.md) and their descendants who peopled the [Westlands](../W/Westlands.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) in the [Second](../S/Second_Age.md) and [Third Ages](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup>

Although they were from the beginning far fewer in number than the [lesser men](../M/Middle_Men.md), the Dúnedain were lords of long life, great power, and wisdom; far superior to the [Men of Middle-earth](../M/Middle_Men.md) among whom they dwelt and whom they ruled.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early History {#Early_History}

The Dúnedain descend from the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), having been granted the island of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) in the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md). Many [Men of Númenor](../M/Men_of_Númenor.md) settled in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), such as in [Belfalas](../B/Belfalas.md), [Umbar](../U/Umbar.md) and [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md). The ancestors of the [Princes of Dol Amroth](../P/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth.md), were the most prominent of these.<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-4">[3]</a></sup>

After the [Downfall of Númenor](Downfall_of_Númenor.md), [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) and his sons led the [Exiles](../E/Exiles_of_Númenor.md) to Middle-earth. Many who were in whole or part of [Númenorean](../N/Númenorean.md) blood welcomed them and proceeded to found the [Realms in Exile](../R/Realms_in_Exile.md), [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) and [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

Originally ruled by their [High King](<../H/High_King_(Dúnedain).md>), they were divided as the [Dúnedain of Arnor](Dúnedain_of_Arnor.md) and the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md), following the death of [Isildur](../I/Isildur.md), son of [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md), in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2](../T/Third_Age_2.md).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppB_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-6">[5]</a></sup>

### Division {#Division}

#### In Arnor {#In_Arnor}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/c/c6/CdxIconArticleDisambiguation.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content">See also: <a href="Dúnedain_of_Arnor.md" title="Dúnedain of Arnor">Dúnedain of Arnor</a></div></div>

[Valandil](<../V/Valandil_(King_of_Arnor).md>), [Isildur](../I/Isildur.md)'s youngest son, took up his rule in [Annúminas](../A/Annúminas.md), but his people were diminished, and there were too few [Númenoreans](../N/Númenoreans.md) and native [Men of Eriador](../M/Men_of_Eriador.md) to people the land or maintain the places [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) built; many had died in the [War of the Last Alliance](../W/War_of_the_Last_Alliance.md) and the [Disaster of the Gladden Fields](Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields.md).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

After the reign of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../E/Eärendur.md" title="Eärendur">Eärendur</a>, the seventh king that followed <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../V/Valandil.md" title="Valandil">Valandil</a>, the [Dúnedain of the North](Dúnedain_of_the_North.md) became divided into petty realms and lordships, and the witch-realm of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) destroyed them one by one.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

The remnants of the [Northern Dúnedain](../N/Northern_Dúnedain.md) were also heavily affected by the [Great Plague](../G/Great_Plague.md); the joint garrison (of the North and South Kingdoms) at [Tharbad](../T/Tharbad.md) ceased to exist,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> and the last of the Dúnedain of [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md) died on the [Barrow-downs](../B/Barrow-downs.md).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

After the [Angmar War](../A/Angmar_War.md), the [Dúnedain of the North](Dúnedain_of_the_North.md) were reduced to [Rangers](../R/Ranger_of_the_North.md) wandering secretly in the wild, and their heritage was forgotten, save in [Imladris](../I/Imladris.md), where the [Heirs of Isildur](../H/Heir_of_Isildur.md) were harboured and their line, from father to son, remained unbroken.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

#### In Gondor {#In_Gondor}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/c/c6/CdxIconArticleDisambiguation.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content">See also: <a class="mw-redirect" href="Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md" title="Dúnedain of Gondor">Dúnedain of Gondor</a></div></div>

In the south, the realm of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) endured and the splendour and might of the [Dúnedain of the South](Dúnedain_of_the_South.md) grew, until it resembled the wealth and majesty of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) during the reign of [Hyarmendacil I](../H/Hyarmendacil_I.md).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppB_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-6">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

However, in the later [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) waned for their blood became much mingled with that of other men, especially the [Northmen of Rhovanion](../N/Northmen_of_Rhovanion.md). King [Eldacar](<../E/Eldacar_(King_of_Gondor).md>), who himself had [Northmannish](../N/Northmen.md) blood, showed favour to the [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md) who supported him. This led to the [Kin-strife](../K/Kin-strife~3650.md), when many of the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) were slain. After his return from exile, many noble houses, including the royal [House of Anárion](../H/House_of_Anárion.md), became more mingled with the blood of "lesser" Men.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

After the reign of King [Eärnur](../E/Eärnur.md), royal descendants among the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) had become few and no claimant for the throne could be found of pure [Númenórean](../N/Númenórean.md) blood, or whose claim all would accept, and people were afraid of a new [Kin-strife](../K/Kin-strife~3650.md) that would devastate the kingdom. Thus, by default, [Mardil](../M/Mardil.md) began the line of [Ruling Stewards of Gondor](../R/Ruling_Stewards_of_Gondor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

After the [Stewards](../S/Stewards.md) picked up the southern rule, the remnant of the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) still defended the passage of the [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) against the terrors of [Minas Morgul](../M/Minas_Morgul.md) and against all the enemies of the West.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

By the time of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) lived in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) and the adjacent townlands, as well as the tributary fiefs and royal lands of [Anórien](../A/Anórien.md), [Ithilien](../I/Ithilien.md), and [Belfalas](../B/Belfalas.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Language_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Language-10">[9]</a></sup>

### Reunification {#Reunification}

Upon the conclusion of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), the [Kingdoms of the Dúnedain](../K/Kingdoms_of_the_Dúnedain.md) were [reunited](../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md) under [Aragorn II Elessar](../A/Aragorn_II_Elessar.md), [Isildur's Heir](../I/Isildur's_Heir.md), and their glory was renewed.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

### Appearance {#Appearance}

They were tall, pale-skinned, with dark hair, shining grey eyes, and proud faces.<sup id="cite_ref-Herbs_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herbs-11">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Minas_Tirith_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Minas_Tirith-12">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Sign_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sign-13">[12]</a></sup> Although the stature of those with [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) descent decreased from what it once was (more than 2 [ranga](../R/Ranga.md)), the Dúnedain were still about 2 [ranga](../R/Ranga.md) or 6'4" on average.<sup id="cite_ref-height_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-height-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Waning of the Dúnedain {#Waning_of_the_Dúnedain}

The [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) marked the beginning of the waning of the Dúnedain, in which their gifts of wisdom, nobility, and long life were slowly withdrawn due to the [Downfall of Númenor](Downfall_of_Númenor.md) and their mingling with [lesser men](../M/Middle_Men.md).<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

In the beginning of their history, the Dúnedain were blessed with a lifespan thrice the life of [lesser men](../M/Middle_Men.md), yet this ever-diminished over the course of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

In [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md), the strife and dissensions between the kingdoms of [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md), [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md), and [Rhudaur](../R/Rhudaur.md) hastened the waning of the Dúnedain.<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup> "More swift was the waning in the [North-kingdom](../A/Arnor.md), for [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md) became colder and there, the Dúnedain became ever less".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup> The Dúnedain of Arnor, unlike their [kin to the South](../G/Gondorians.md), were of unmingled [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) blood.<sup id="cite_ref-blood_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blood-15">[14]</a></sup> Although, their lifespans ever continued to shorten, the [Dúnedain of Arnor](Dúnedain_of_Arnor.md), especially their [Chieftains](../C/Chieftain_of_the_Dúnedain.md), maintained significant longevity living to twice the age of [lesser men](../M/Middle_Men.md).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> The [Heirs of Isildur](../H/Heir_of_Isildur.md) even lived up to 160 years or more.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-17">[16]</a></sup>

After [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md)'s numbers were replenished by lesser [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md) after the [Kin-Strife](../K/Kin-Strife.md), the mingling did not at first hasten the waning of the Dúnedain, as had been feared, but it still proceeded little by little as it had before.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup> However, after the fall of the [Kings](../K/Kings_of_Gondor.md), the waning was much swifter in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) than in [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup> In fact, [Hador](<../H/Hador_(Steward_of_Gondor).md>) the seventh [Ruling Steward](../R/Ruling_Steward.md) of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) was the last [Gondorian](../G/Gondorian.md) to live 150 years and after his time the life-span of those with [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) blood waned more rapidly.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-18">[17]</a></sup> By the time of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), few among the [Gondorians](../G/Gondorians.md) passed 100 years with vigour, except in the more pure and noble houses.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>

Upon the reunification of the [Kingdoms of the Dúnedain](../K/Kingdoms_of_the_Dúnedain.md), the might and dignity of the Dúnedain was lifted up and their glory was renewed.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup> Greatest among them was their [High King](<../H/High_King_(Dúnedain).md>) [Aragorn II Elessar](../A/Aragorn_II_Elessar.md) who lived up to 210 years (the longest since King [Arvegil](../A/Arvegil.md)),<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup> and he received in some measure their former gifts. He wedded [Arwen Undomiel](../A/Arwen_Undomiel.md), daughter of [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md), brother of [Elros](../E/Elros.md) first [King of Númenór](../K/King_of_Númenor.md), and so restored the majesty and high lineage of the royal [House of Telcontar](../H/House_of_Telcontar.md), but their life-span was not restored and continued to wane until it became as that of other men.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Language {#Language}

After the [Downfall](Downfall_of_Númenor.md), [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) was the regular spoken tongue of the [Exiles of Númenor](../E/Exiles_of_Númenor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> When they arrived on the coasts of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), few among the colonists on the coasts remembered [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) since the neglected [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) was used as a *lingua franca* among the [Men](../M/Men.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), which developed into the [Westron](../W/Westron.md) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Atani_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-21">[20]</a></sup> They used the [Common Speech](../C/Common_Speech.md) in their dealings with other folk and in their governmental affairs. They enlarged the language and enriched it with many words from the Elven-tongues. In the days of the Númenorean kings, [Westron](../W/Westron.md) became used more and more by the Dúnedain themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup>

Among the Dúnedain, "the kings and high lords, and indeed all those of [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) blood in any degree," for long used [Sindarin](../N/Númenórean_Sindarin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> Many of the [Men of Gondor](../M/Men_of_Gondor.md) could also speak the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) tongues, a notable distinction and characteristic among the Dúnedain there.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup> [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) had long ceased to be a "first language" in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), but was learned in early youth (by the Dúnedain) from loremasters, and used by them as a mark of rank and high-blood. For example, the [Stewards of Gondor](../S/Stewards_of_Gondor.md) belonged to a [noble family of Dúnedain](../H/House_of_Húrin.md) of the ancient [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md) who used (beside the [Common Speech](../C/Common_Speech.md)) the [Númenórean Sindarin](../N/Númenórean_Sindarin.md) tongue after the fashion of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md). It had changed very little since the [Downfall of Númenor](Downfall_of_Númenor.md) and though the [Men of Gondor](../M/Men_of_Gondor.md) altered some of the sounds, they could still understand the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) and be understood by them.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-18">[17]</a></sup> [Westron](../W/Westron.md) became used more and more by the [Dúnedain of Gondor](Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) themselves, so that at the time of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) was known to only a small part of the peoples of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) (and spoken daily by fewer); they dwelt mostly in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) and the adjacent townlands, and in the land of the tributary princes of [Dol Amroth](Dol_Amroth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup>

[Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) was used by the Dúnedain, a tradition which has continued from the loremasters of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md), to be used for places of fame and reverence in addition to the names of royalty and men of great renown.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-quenya_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quenya-23">[22]</a></sup> All the royal names of the [Kings of Gondor](../K/Kings_of_Gondor.md) as well as all the [Stewards](../S/Stewards.md) until [Mardil Voronwë](../M/Mardil_Voronwë.md) were [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) names.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup> All the royal names of the [Kings of Arnor](../K/Kings_of_Arnor.md) were [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) names. The [Kings of Arthedain](../K/Kings_of_Arthedain.md) and later the [Chieftains of the Dúnedain](../C/Chieftain_of_the_Dúnedain.md), however, took [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) names.<sup id="cite_ref-North_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-North-25">[24]</a></sup> At the end of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), there were more Men (being of Dúnedain descent) that knew [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) or spoke [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) than there were [Elves](../E/Elves.md) (those of [Lindon](../L/Lindon.md), [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md), and [Lórien](../L/Lórien.md)) who did either.<sup id="cite_ref-quenya_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quenya-23">[22]</a></sup>

They are also called the *Men of the West* and the *Men of Westernesse* (direct translations of the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) term).

The [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) name was **Núnatan** (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈnuːnatan]</span>), pl. **Núnatani** (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[nuːˈnatani]</span>).

The [Westron](../W/Westron.md) name for *Dúnadan* was simply **[Adûn](../A/Adûn.md)**, "westerner", but this name was seldom used.

*[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* is explicit in noting that "Númenoreans" and "Dúnedain" are synonymous terms. Nevertheless, it seems more usual to call those born in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) "**Númenoreans**" and their descendants living in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) post-[Akallabêth](../A/Akallabêth.md) "**Dúnedain**".
