The **Dúnedain** ([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"): "West-men"), singular **Dúnadan**, were the [Men of Númenor](/wiki/Men_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and their descendants who peopled the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) in the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and [Third Ages](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup>

Although they were from the beginning far fewer in number than the [lesser men](/wiki/Middle_Men "Middle Men"), the Dúnedain were lords of long life, great power, and wisdom; far superior to the [Men of Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle_Men "Middle Men") among whom they dwelt and whom they ruled.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early History {#Early_History}

The Dúnedain descend from the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), having been granted the island of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age). Many [Men of Númenor](/wiki/Men_of_N%C3%BAmenor) settled in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), such as in [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas), [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar) and [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir). The ancestors of the [Princes of Dol Amroth](/wiki/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth), were the most prominent of these.<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-4">[3]</a></sup>

After the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor), [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and his sons led the [Exiles](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Exiles of Númenor") to Middle-earth. Many who were in whole or part of [Númenorean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenorean) blood welcomed them and proceeded to found the [Realms in Exile](/wiki/Realms_in_Exile), [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

Originally ruled by their [High King](</wiki/High_King_(D%C3%BAnedain)> "High King (Dúnedain)"), they were divided as the [Dúnedain of Arnor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Arnor) and the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor), following the death of [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur), son of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil), in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2](/wiki/Third_Age_2 "Third Age 2").<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppB_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-6">[5]</a></sup>

### Division {#Division}

#### In Arnor {#In_Arnor}

[Valandil](</wiki/Valandil_(King_of_Arnor)> "Valandil (King of Arnor)"), [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur)'s youngest son, took up his rule in [Annúminas](/wiki/Ann%C3%BAminas), but his people were diminished, and there were too few [Númenoreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenoreans) and native [Men of Eriador](/wiki/Men_of_Eriador) to people the land or maintain the places [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) built; many had died in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance) and the [Disaster of the Gladden Fields](/wiki/Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

After the reign of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/E%C3%A4rendur" title="Eärendur">Eärendur</a>, the seventh king that followed <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Valandil" title="Valandil">Valandil</a>, the [Dúnedain of the North](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_the_North) became divided into petty realms and lordships, and the witch-realm of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) destroyed them one by one.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

The remnants of the [Northern Dúnedain](/wiki/Northern_D%C3%BAnedain) were also heavily affected by the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague); the joint garrison (of the North and South Kingdoms) at [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) ceased to exist,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> and the last of the Dúnedain of [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) died on the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

After the [Angmar War](/wiki/Angmar_War), the [Dúnedain of the North](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_the_North) were reduced to [Rangers](/wiki/Ranger_of_the_North "Ranger of the North") wandering secretly in the wild, and their heritage was forgotten, save in [Imladris](/wiki/Imladris), where the [Heirs of Isildur](/wiki/Heir_of_Isildur "Heir of Isildur") were harboured and their line, from father to son, remained unbroken.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

#### In Gondor {#In_Gondor}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/c/c6/CdxIconArticleDisambiguation.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content">See also: <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor" title="Dúnedain of Gondor">Dúnedain of Gondor</a></div></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r398211"/>

In the south, the realm of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) endured and the splendour and might of the [Dúnedain of the South](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_the_South) grew, until it resembled the wealth and majesty of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) during the reign of [Hyarmendacil I](/wiki/Hyarmendacil_I).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppB_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-6">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

However, in the later [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) waned for their blood became much mingled with that of other men, especially the [Northmen of Rhovanion](/wiki/Northmen_of_Rhovanion). King [Eldacar](</wiki/Eldacar_(King_of_Gondor)> "Eldacar (King of Gondor)"), who himself had [Northmannish](/wiki/Northmen "Northmen") blood, showed favour to the [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen) who supported him. This led to the [Kin-strife](/wiki/Kin-strife), when many of the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) were slain. After his return from exile, many noble houses, including the royal [House of Anárion](/wiki/House_of_An%C3%A1rion), became more mingled with the blood of "lesser" Men.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

After the reign of King [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur), royal descendants among the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) had become few and no claimant for the throne could be found of pure [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) blood, or whose claim all would accept, and people were afraid of a new [Kin-strife](/wiki/Kin-strife) that would devastate the kingdom. Thus, by default, [Mardil](/wiki/Mardil) began the line of [Ruling Stewards of Gondor](/wiki/Ruling_Stewards_of_Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

After the [Stewards](/wiki/Stewards) picked up the southern rule, the remnant of the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) still defended the passage of the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) against the terrors of [Minas Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul) and against all the enemies of the West.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

By the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) lived in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) and the adjacent townlands, as well as the tributary fiefs and royal lands of [Anórien](/wiki/An%C3%B3rien), [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien), and [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Language_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Language-10">[9]</a></sup>

### Reunification {#Reunification}

Upon the conclusion of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), the [Kingdoms of the Dúnedain](/wiki/Kingdoms_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain) were [reunited](/wiki/Reunited_Kingdom "Reunited Kingdom") under [Aragorn II Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn_II_Elessar), [Isildur's Heir](/wiki/Isildur%27s_Heir), and their glory was renewed.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

### Appearance {#Appearance}

They were tall, pale-skinned, with dark hair, shining grey eyes, and proud faces.<sup id="cite_ref-Herbs_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herbs-11">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Minas_Tirith_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Minas_Tirith-12">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Sign_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sign-13">[12]</a></sup> Although the stature of those with [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") descent decreased from what it once was (more than 2 [ranga](/wiki/Ranga "Ranga")), the Dúnedain were still about 2 [ranga](/wiki/Ranga "Ranga") or 6'4" on average.<sup id="cite_ref-height_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-height-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Waning of the Dúnedain {#Waning_of_the_Dúnedain}

The [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) marked the beginning of the waning of the Dúnedain, in which their gifts of wisdom, nobility, and long life were slowly withdrawn due to the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and their mingling with [lesser men](/wiki/Middle_Men "Middle Men").<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup>

In the beginning of their history, the Dúnedain were blessed with a lifespan thrice the life of [lesser men](/wiki/Middle_Men "Middle Men"), yet this ever-diminished over the course of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup>

In [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor), the strife and dissensions between the kingdoms of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan), and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur) hastened the waning of the Dúnedain.<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup> "More swift was the waning in the [North-kingdom](/wiki/Arnor "Arnor"), for [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) became colder and there, the Dúnedain became ever less".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup> The Dúnedain of Arnor, unlike their [kin to the South](/wiki/Gondorians "Gondorians"), were of unmingled [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") blood.<sup id="cite_ref-blood_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blood-15">[14]</a></sup> Although, their lifespans ever continued to shorten, the [Dúnedain of Arnor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Arnor), especially their [Chieftains](/wiki/Chieftain_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain "Chieftain of the Dúnedain"), maintained significant longevity living to twice the age of [lesser men](/wiki/Middle_Men "Middle Men").<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> The [Heirs of Isildur](/wiki/Heir_of_Isildur "Heir of Isildur") even lived up to 160 years or more.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-17">[16]</a></sup>

After [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor)'s numbers were replenished by lesser [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen) after the [Kin-Strife](/wiki/Kin-Strife), the mingling did not at first hasten the waning of the Dúnedain, as had been feared, but it still proceeded little by little as it had before.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[8]</a></sup> However, after the fall of the [Kings](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor "Kings of Gondor"), the waning was much swifter in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) than in [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup> In fact, [Hador](</wiki/Hador_(Steward_of_Gondor)> "Hador (Steward of Gondor)") the seventh [Ruling Steward](/wiki/Ruling_Steward) of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) was the last [Gondorian](/wiki/Gondorian) to live 150 years and after his time the life-span of those with [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") blood waned more rapidly.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-18">[17]</a></sup> By the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), few among the [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) passed 100 years with vigour, except in the more pure and noble houses.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>

Upon the reunification of the [Kingdoms of the Dúnedain](/wiki/Kingdoms_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain), the might and dignity of the Dúnedain was lifted up and their glory was renewed.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-5">[4]</a></sup> Greatest among them was their [High King](</wiki/High_King_(D%C3%BAnedain)> "High King (Dúnedain)") [Aragorn II Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn_II_Elessar) who lived up to 210 years (the longest since King [Arvegil](/wiki/Arvegil)),<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-7">[6]</a></sup> and he received in some measure their former gifts. He wedded [Arwen Undomiel](/wiki/Arwen_Undomiel), daughter of [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond), brother of [Elros](/wiki/Elros) first [King of Númenór](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor "King of Númenor"), and so restored the majesty and high lineage of the royal [House of Telcontar](/wiki/House_of_Telcontar), but their life-span was not restored and continued to wane until it became as that of other men.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Language {#Language}

After the [Downfall](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor"), [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) was the regular spoken tongue of the [Exiles of Númenor](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> When they arrived on the coasts of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), few among the colonists on the coasts remembered [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) since the neglected [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) was used as a *lingua franca* among the [Men](/wiki/Men) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), which developed into the [Westron](/wiki/Westron) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Atani_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-21">[20]</a></sup> They used the [Common Speech](/wiki/Common_Speech) in their dealings with other folk and in their governmental affairs. They enlarged the language and enriched it with many words from the Elven-tongues. In the days of the Númenorean kings, [Westron](/wiki/Westron) became used more and more by the Dúnedain themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup>

Among the Dúnedain, "the kings and high lords, and indeed all those of [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") blood in any degree," for long used [Sindarin](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean_Sindarin "Númenórean Sindarin").<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> Many of the [Men of Gondor](/wiki/Men_of_Gondor) could also speak the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) tongues, a notable distinction and characteristic among the Dúnedain there.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup> [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) had long ceased to be a "first language" in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), but was learned in early youth (by the Dúnedain) from loremasters, and used by them as a mark of rank and high-blood. For example, the [Stewards of Gondor](/wiki/Stewards_of_Gondor) belonged to a [noble family of Dúnedain](/wiki/House_of_H%C3%BArin "House of Húrin") of the ancient [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful) who used (beside the [Common Speech](/wiki/Common_Speech)) the [Númenórean Sindarin](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean_Sindarin) tongue after the fashion of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). It had changed very little since the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and though the [Men of Gondor](/wiki/Men_of_Gondor) altered some of the sounds, they could still understand the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and be understood by them.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-18">[17]</a></sup> [Westron](/wiki/Westron) became used more and more by the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) themselves, so that at the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) was known to only a small part of the peoples of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) (and spoken daily by fewer); they dwelt mostly in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) and the adjacent townlands, and in the land of the tributary princes of [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup>

[Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) was used by the Dúnedain, a tradition which has continued from the loremasters of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), to be used for places of fame and reverence in addition to the names of royalty and men of great renown.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-quenya_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quenya-23">[22]</a></sup> All the royal names of the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor) as well as all the [Stewards](/wiki/Stewards) until [Mardil Voronwë](/wiki/Mardil_Voronw%C3%AB) were [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) names.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup> All the royal names of the [Kings of Arnor](/wiki/Kings_of_Arnor) were [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) names. The [Kings of Arthedain](/wiki/Kings_of_Arthedain) and later the [Chieftains of the Dúnedain](/wiki/Chieftain_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain "Chieftain of the Dúnedain"), however, took [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) names.<sup id="cite_ref-North_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-North-25">[24]</a></sup> At the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), there were more Men (being of Dúnedain descent) that knew [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) or spoke [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) than there were [Elves](/wiki/Elves) (those of [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon), [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), and [Lórien](/wiki/L%C3%B3rien)) who did either.<sup id="cite_ref-quenya_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quenya-23">[22]</a></sup>

They are also called the *Men of the West* and the *Men of Westernesse* (direct translations of the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) term).

The [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name was **Núnatan** (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈnuːnatan]</span>), pl. **Núnatani** (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[nuːˈnatani]</span>).

The [Westron](/wiki/Westron) name for *Dúnadan* was simply **[Adûn](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBn)**, "westerner", but this name was seldom used.

*[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* is explicit in noting that "Númenoreans" and "Dúnedain" are synonymous terms. Nevertheless, it seems more usual to call those born in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) "**Númenoreans**" and their descendants living in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) post-[Akallabêth](/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth) "**Dúnedain**".
