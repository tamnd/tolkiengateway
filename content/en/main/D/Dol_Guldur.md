**Dol Guldur** ([S.](../S/Sindarin.md) "*Hill of Sorcery*"),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> was a stronghold<sup id="cite_ref-Stronghold_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stronghold-3">[3]</a></sup> of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Sauron_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sauron-4">[4]</a></sup> at the highest point in a highland in the southwest of [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Note12_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note12-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Dol Guldur was originally called **[Amon Lanc](../A/Amon_Lanc.md)** ("Naked Hill"), because trees did not grow on its summit.<sup id="cite_ref-Note12_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note12-5">[5]</a></sup> The [Silvan Elves](../S/Silvan_Elves.md) of King [Oropher](../O/Oropher.md) had ancient dwellings near Amon Lanc, but left them and migrated northwards when they received disturbing rumours about the rising power of Sauron long before the [War of the Last Alliance](../W/War_of_the_Last_Alliance.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Somewhere after [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1000](../T/Third_Age_1000.md), an evil presence took over Amon Lanc. It was in [1050](../T/Third_Age_1050.md) that a [shadow](../S/Shadow.md) fell upon Greenwood and it began to be called [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> The hostile entity was known as "the [Necromancer](../N/Necromancer.md)" by the peoples. The Silvan Elves of King [Thranduil](../T/Thranduil.md), the son of Oropher who had died in the War of the Last Alliance retreated from the spreading shadow further north to the north-east of Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/John_Howe_-_Pass_the_Doors_of_Dol_Guldur.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/d0/John_Howe_-_Pass_the_Doors_of_Dol_Guldur.jpg/300px-John_Howe_-_Pass_the_Doors_of_Dol_Guldur.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="252" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/d0/John_Howe_-_Pass_the_Doors_of_Dol_Guldur.jpg/450px-John_Howe_-_Pass_the_Doors_of_Dol_Guldur.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/d/d0/John_Howe_-_Pass_the_Doors_of_Dol_Guldur.jpg 2x" data-file-width="534" data-file-height="448"/></a><figcaption><a href="../J/John_Howe.md" title="John Howe">John Howe</a> - <i>Pass the Doors of Dol Guldur</i></figcaption></figure>

Around [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1100](../T/Third_Age_1100.md) the Wise consisting of the [Istari](../W/Wizards.md) and the chief [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md) found out that an evil power had made a stronghold at Dol Guldur and thought that it was one of the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Stronghold_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stronghold-3">[3]</a></sup>

The [Council of the Wise](../W/White_Council.md) long feared the Necromancer might be none other than [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) himself who secretly regained his powers after his defeat in the [War of the Last Alliance](../W/War_of_the_Last_Alliance.md). In [2063](../T/Third_Age_2063.md) [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) went to Dol Guldur, and the "Necromancer", not yet powerful, fled to the [East](../E/East.md) so as not to be identified.

After [four centuries](../W/Watchful_Peace.md) in [2460](../T/Third_Age_2460.md), just as [the One Ring](../T/The_One_Ring.md) was found by [Sméagol](../S/Sméagol.md) the [Stoor](../S/Stoors.md), the "Necromancer" returned there.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2510](../T/Third_Age_2510.md)<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> a dark [shadow](../S/Shadow.md) and cloud flowed from Dol Guldur whereas a white mist that came from [Lothlórien](../L/Lothlórien.md) to the west contended with the darkness coming from Mirkwood when [Eorl](../E/Eorl.md) the Young was leading his riders to the [Battle of the Field of Celebrant](../B/Battle_of_the_Field_of_Celebrant.md). In reaction Eorl steered his force westward to avoid the phenomenon of the shadow, and they entered the white mist.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

In [2845](../T/Third_Age_2845.md) [Thráin II](../T/Thráin.md), [King of Durin's Folk](../K/Kings_of_Durin's_Folk.md)-in-exile and holder of the [last](../R/Ring_of_Thrór.md) of the [Seven Rings](../S/Seven_Rings.md) of the [Dwarves](Dwarves.md), was on his way to the [Lonely Mountain](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md). He was captured near the eaves of Mirkwood, imprisoned and tortured in Dol Guldur's dungeons, and his Ring taken from him. In [2850](../T/Third_Age_2850.md) Gandalf again entered Dol Guldur, found the dying Thráin, and was entrusted with the [map](../T/Thrór's_Map.md) and key to give to [Thorin](../T/Thorin.md), although Thráin could not tell him his own or his son's name before he died. Gandalf confirmed that the Necromancer, the master of Dol Guldur at that time, was Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-Sauron_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sauron-4">[4]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/A/Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/7a/Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg/300px-Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="289" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/7/7a/Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="385"/></a><figcaption>Angus McBride - <i>Dol Guldur</i></figcaption></figure>

In [2851](../T/Third_Age_2851.md) Gandalf returned to the [White Council](../W/White_Council.md) and urged an attack on Dol Guldur, but was overruled by [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md), who secretly had begun searching for the One Ring in the area by then.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

Gandalf was concerned that Sauron planned to attack Lórien and Rivendell from Dol Guldur and that he might use the dragon [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md) at [Erebor](../E/Erebor.md) against the [Dwarves](Dwarves.md) of the [Iron Hills](../I/Iron_Hills.md) who could resist an attack by the forces of Sauron on the northern passes in the Misty Mountains. Gandalf wanted to find a way to deal with Smaug, but thought that a direct attack against Dol Guldur was needed even more to disturb Sauron's plans and wanted to convince the White Council.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> In [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md) Saruman finally agreed to an attack, which occurred at the same time as the [Quest of Erebor](../Q/Quest_of_Erebor.md) and Sauron fled from Dol Guldur to the East.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

In [2951](../T/Third_Age_2951.md)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Sauron came to [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md), his plans now ready, and sent [Khamûl](../K/Khamûl.md), the second chief,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> and two other [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md) back to Dol Guldur.

In the following decades Dol Guldur must have rebuilt some of its power until the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), during which its forces (most likely [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md)) made an [attack](../B/Battle_under_the_trees.md) upon the [Woodland Realm](../W/Woodland_Realm.md) on [March 15](../M/March_15.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md)<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> and three assaults on [Lórien](../L/Lothlórien.md) on March 11, 15 and 22 of [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> causing grievous damage to the outlying woodlands of Lórien.<sup id="cite_ref-Final_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Final-20">[20]</a></sup> However each time they were driven back by [Thranduil](../T/Thranduil.md), [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md) and [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) who held the power of [Nenya](../N/Nenya.md); which only Sauron himself could have overcome.<sup id="cite_ref-Final_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Final-20">[20]</a></sup>

On [March 28](../M/March_28.md) of [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md)<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> Dol Guldur was finally destroyed and cleansed by the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) of Lórien, led by [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md) and [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md), after Sauron's fall.<sup id="cite_ref-Final_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Final-20">[20]</a></sup>

*Dol Guldur* is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name, which means "Hill of Sorcery".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> It is a combination of the elements *[dol](Dol.md)* ("head", "hill"<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup>) and *[guldur](../G/Guldur.md)* ("black arts", "sorcery").<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* the name *Dol Guldur* does not appear, but a "dark tower"<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> or "dark hold" of the Necromancer in the south of Mirkwood<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> are mentioned.

On [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s [First Map of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) the original name of Dol Guldur was Dol Dúgol and was later changed to *Dol Dúghul*.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> It was originally located much farther east than its later location (in square M-15 of Map II).<sup id="cite_ref-Map_II_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map_II-29">[29]</a></sup> [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) explained that Map II had faint traces of green which suggested that Mirkwood originally extended farther to the east too.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup> The name Dol Dúgol was stricken out and the hill was moved to its later location on this map, but its name became Dol Dúghul.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_II_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map_II-29">[29]</a></sup> In a manuscript for a chapter titled The Mines of Moria (i)<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> and in a manuscript for the chapter Lothlórien<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> the name of Sauron's dwelling the south of Mirkwood is *Dol-Dúgol*. In the latter manuscript the name was later changed to *Dol Dûghul*.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> In a manuscript of the chapter The Black Gate is Closed the name of the stronghold of Sauron was *Dol Dúghol*. It was changed to *Dol Guldur* at a very late stage.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup>

### Films {#Films}

<b>2012: <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_An_Unexpected_Journey.md" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Dol Guldur is depicted as a ruined and abandoned fortress of unknown origin. It features statues of the Nazgûl. <a href="../R/Radagast.md" title="Radagast">Radagast</a>, not <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a>, enters Dol Guldur. He is attacked by the <a href="../W/Witch-king.md" title="Witch-king">Witch-king</a>, obtains a <a href="../M/Morgul-knife.md" title="Morgul-knife">Morgul blade</a>, and finds that Dol Guldur is occupied by a <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">Necromancer</a>. He then travels to Gandalf to tell him the news and gives him the sword as proof. (According to the concept artists <a href="../A/Alan_Lee.md" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a> and <a href="../J/John_Howe.md" title="John Howe">John Howe</a>, the film&#39;s version of Dol Guldur was designed to look like an old structure built by the <a href="../N/Númenóreans.md" title="Númenóreans">Númenóreans</a> during the <a href="../S/Second_Age.md" title="Second Age">Second Age</a> that had subsequently fallen into disrepair.)<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2013: <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_The_Desolation_of_Smaug.md" title="The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug">The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Gandalf enters Dol Guldur to investigate its ruins. There he meets <a href="../A/Azog.md" title="Azog">Azog</a> and his army. He tries to escape, but is trapped by <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">the Necromancer</a>, who reveals to Gandalf his true identity. Gandalf is later seen trapped in one of the cages.</dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies.md" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="../G/Galadriel.md" title="Galadriel">Galadriel</a> enters Dol Guldur in order to rescue Gandalf. Accompanied by <a href="../S/Saruman.md" title="Saruman">Saruman</a> and <a href="../E/Elrond.md" title="Elrond">Elrond</a> they encounter Sauron and <a href="../N/Nazgûl.md" title="Nazgûl">the Nine</a>. The <a href="../A/Attack_on_Dol_Guldur.md" title="Attack on Dol Guldur">Attack</a> begins and Sauron flees to <a href="../M/Mordor.md" title="Mordor">the East</a>.</dd></dl>

### Games {#Games}

<b>2004: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_War_of_the_Ring.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: War of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: War of the Ring</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Dol Guldur makes appearance during both &#34;Good&#34; and &#34;Evil&#34; campaigns as a site of a major battleground.</dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Dol Guldur is commanded by the <a href="../M/Mouth_of_Sauron.md" title="Mouth of Sauron">Mouth of Sauron</a>, not <a href="../K/Khamûl.md" title="Khamûl">Khamûl</a>. Dol Guldur (or Mordor itself) sent a huge army of Orcs, Haradrim, and Trolls to assault <a href="../L/Lonely_Mountain.md" title="Lonely Mountain">Erebor</a>. The attack fails, and the Mouth of Sauron is killed along with all the attacking force. Dol Guldur is then itself attacked by a combined army of Elves and Dwarves led by <a href="../E/Elrond.md" title="Elrond">Elrond</a>, <a href="../A/Arwen.md" title="Arwen">Arwen</a>, <a href="../G/Glorfindel.md" title="Glorfindel">Glorfindel</a>, <a href="../G/Glóin.md" title="Glóin">Glóin</a>, and <a href="Dáin_Ironfoot.md" title="Dáin Ironfoot">Dáin Ironfoot</a>. After a long and hard struggle, the Elves and Dwarves finally overrun Dol Guldur and lay waste to the evil that dwelled there, ending Sauron&#39;s war campaign in the North once and for all.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In the Evil Campaign, the Goblins from Dol Guldur eliminate the Elves and the Ents that guard the Forest Road in Mirkwood.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Dol Guldur is a central point in the game&#39;s second expansion, <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online%253A_Siege_of_Mirkwood.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online: Siege of Mirkwood">The Lord of the Rings Online: Siege of Mirkwood</a></i>. In its storyline, Celeborn and Galadriel organize a military campaign against Dol Guldur while the Fellowship still resides in Lothlórien (not to be confused with the later battle during the War of the Ring). The announced goal is to sow chaos into Orc forces by destroying as many of their troops, weapons and supplies as possible, to delay an inevitable assault upon Lothlórien. The true purpose, however, is to distract the Eye of Sauron from the company departing down the shores of Anduin.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>An assault by the &#34;Golden Host&#34; of the Galadhrim is successful despite minor losses and after establishing multiple camps throughout southern Mirkwood arrives at the walls of the fortress itself. However, without the White Lady they do not have the means of bringing down the walls, so the Elves prepare to swiftly fall back beyond Anduin before the main forces of the Enemy arrive. The players can also explore multiple locations within the fortress, among them the &#34;Necromancer&#39;s Gate&#34;, the baileys of Dol Guldur, the &#34;Sword-hall of Dol Guldur&#34;, the Dungeons of Dol Guldur, the chief tower &#34;Barad Guldur&#34;, and &#34;Sammath Gul&#34; - the chambers once occupied by the Dark Lord himself.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Much later, after the <a href="../W/War_of_the_Ring.md" title="War of the Ring">War of the Ring</a>, the Galadhrim and <a href="../E/Elves_of_Mirkwood.md" title="Elves of Mirkwood">Elves of Mirkwood</a> lead a great assault on Dol Guldur, where Galadriel casts down the walls of the stronghold, and renames the hill <i>Dol Dhannen</i>, &#34;The Hill of Ruin&#34;. In the process of assisting the Lady in this players discover another location, &#34;Gostador&#34;, the secret dungeon in which Sauron let his physical form recover from his defeat at the end of the Second Age, and where he held audiences with agents carrying out his plans in other parts of Middle-earth.</dd></dl>
