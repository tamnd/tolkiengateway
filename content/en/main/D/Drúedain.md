The **Drúedain** (sg. **Drúadan**) or **Drúath** (sg. **Drû**) was a race of wild [Men](../M/Men.md). The [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) called them **Woses** or **Wild Men of the Woods**.

They were a good-hearted, though primitive people who suffered persecution, both by the malice of [evil people](../M/Men_of_Darkness.md); and by ignorance, as their "unlovely" appearance (according to the [Elves](../E/Elves.md)) led many to believe they were savage beasts. Although some of their numbers joined the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) and some briefly remained on [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md), they largely held themselves apart from the troubles and calamities of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), and possessed their own mysterious ways and magic.

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

The Drúedain were part of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) who left [Hildórien](../H/Hildórien.md) for the [West](../W/Westlands.md). They had been harried and persecuted by other [Men](../M/Men.md) as long as they could remember. This treatment turned them to be secretive and suspicious of other kinds of Men and made them migrate to the west to find a land where they could live hidden and in peace.<sup id="cite_ref-TD_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TD-2">[2]</a></sup>

Historians in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) believed that the Drúedain came from lands south of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md), and turned north into [Ithilien](../I/Ithilien.md) before they reached the coasts of [Haradwaith](../H/Harad.md) and were the first Men to cross the [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) (probably near [Cair Andros](../C/Cair_Andros.md)). They then settled in the vales on both sides of the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md) and in the wooded lands at the northern feet of the White Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-TD_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TD-2">[2]</a></sup> The Drúedain carved statues of themselves of stone in [Dunharrow](Dunharrow.md), which were later called [Púkel-men](../P/Púkel-men.md) by the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) when the Rohirrim settled in Rohan in the Third Age.<sup id="cite_ref-Note14_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note14-3">[3]</a></sup>

At the end of the First Age most of the Drúedain remained in the White Mountains despite attacks by Men from the East who had relapsed to the service of the Dark. A small branch of the Drúedain, which consisted of a few hundred people,<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup> migrated to the west and accompanied the [Haladin](../H/Haladin.md) to [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) and settled in the forest of [Brethil](../B/Brethil.md).<sup id="cite_ref-TD_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TD-2">[2]</a></sup> Few of the Drúedain ever left the Forest of Brethil,<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup> but a few of them lived in the household of [Húrin](../H/Húrin.md) of the folk of [Hador](../H/Hador.md) who was related to the lord of the Haladin and had lived with the Haladin in his youth.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> When the Haladin fell into ruin and [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md) had destroyed all the realms and strongholds of [Elves](../E/Elves.md) and Men, the Drúedain of Brethil dwindled to a few families, mostly women and children, of which some found refuge at the [Mouths of Sirion](../M/Mouths_of_Sirion.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

When the Dúnedain of Beleriand set forth for the island of [Elenna](../E/Elenna.md), where they would establish the kingdom of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md), the Drúedain refugees who had dwelt at the Mouths of Sirion were permitted to join them, where their numbers increased again.<sup id="cite_ref-Note7_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note7-6">[6]</a></sup>

Meanwhile in the east, the Drúedain of the White Mountains were driven off their homes by wicked, tall [Men](../M/Men_of_Darkness.md) from the East. Remnants of the Drúedain survived in [Drúadan Forest](Drúadan_Forest.md) in [Anórien](../A/Anórien.md) at the eastern end of the White Mountains and in [Drúwaith Iaur](Drúwaith_Iaur.md) in the mountains of the [Cape of Andrast](../A/Andrast.md) at the western end of the White Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-TD_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TD-2">[2]</a></sup>

After the felling of trees in [Enedwaith](../E/Enedwaith.md) by the [Númenóreans](../N/Númenóreans.md) as a source of timber for the building of ships at ship-yards at [Lond Daer](../L/Lond_Daer.md) and elsewhere in Middle-earth and in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) became devastating, the native [Pre-Númenóreans](../P/Pre-Númenóreans.md) who lived in the forests ambushed the Númenóreans and had to flee east to the foothills of the southern [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) to the region, which was later called [Dunland](Dunland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Gwathlo34_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwathlo34-7">[7]</a></sup> They did not cross the river [Isen](../I/Isen.md) to [Drúwaith Iaur](Drúwaith_Iaur.md) in the promontory between the Isen and the [Lefnui](../L/Lefnui.md), because they were afraid of the Drúedain who lived there and whom they regarded as a "fell people".<sup id="cite_ref-TD_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TD-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Gwathlo34_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwathlo34-7">[7]</a></sup>

Meanwhile, the Drúedain living on Númenor started leaving the island during the time of [Tar-Aldarion](../T/Tar-Aldarion.md), [foreseeing](../F/Foresight.md) the evil that would come. By the [Downfall of Númenor](Downfall_of_Númenor.md), all Drúedain had left and returned to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Note7_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note7-6">[6]</a></sup>

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/John_Howe_-_Ghan-Buri-Ghan.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/4/44/John_Howe_-_Ghan-Buri-Ghan.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="197" height="400" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="197" data-file-height="400"/></a><figcaption><i>Ghan-Buri-Ghan</i> by <a href="../J/John_Howe.md" title="John Howe">John Howe</a></figcaption></figure>

After the [Great Plague](../G/Great_Plague.md) of the year 1636<sup id="cite_ref-Gwathlo_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwathlo-8">[8]</a></sup> and in the days of the Kings of Gondor a few tribes of Drúedain lived as fishers and fowlers in the marshlands of the mouths of the river [Gwathló](../G/Gwathló.md) and the river [Isen](../I/Isen.md)<sup id="cite_ref-I_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-I-9">[9]</a></sup> and on the coasts between the mouths of those rivers.<sup id="cite_ref-Gwathlo_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwathlo-8">[8]</a></sup>

At the end of the Third Age a few Drúedain still lived in the [Drúadan Forest](Drúadan_Forest.md) in Anórien north of the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md),<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-10">[10]</a></sup> and in [Drúwaith Iaur](Drúwaith_Iaur.md) in the mountains of the cape of [Andrast](../A/Andrast.md) in the west of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) between the rivers [Isen](../I/Isen.md) and [Lefnui](../L/Lefnui.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

During the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), the chieftain of the Drúedain of Drúadan Forest was [Ghân-buri-Ghân](../G/Ghân-buri-Ghân.md). On March 14, [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> Ghân-buri-Ghân and the Drúedain helped the army of the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) that was led by king [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md) of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md) to evade being trapped by an army of more than six thousand [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) and Men that was waiting for them along the [North-South Road](../N/North-South_Road.md). The Drúedain lead the Rohirrim along an alternate road through the [Stonewain Valley](../S/Stonewain_Valley.md) in Drúadan Forest, and scouted for and hunted possible enemy spies therein, so that the Rohirrim could reach the [Pelennor Fields](../P/Pelennor_Fields.md) without being ambushed. When king Théoden offered the Drúedain a rich reward and the eternal friendship of the Rohan for their offered help, Ghân-buri-Ghân asked king Théoden to leave the Drúedain alone in the woods and to never again hunt them like beasts.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

After the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), towards the end of July [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> [King Elessar](../A/Aragorn.md) granted the Drúadan Forest to be theirs forever, forbidding anyone to enter without their permission.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The appearance of the Drúedain was regarded as "unlovely" by Elves and Men. They were short with an average height of four feet,<sup id="cite_ref-atani_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-1">[1]</a></sup> very broad with heavy buttocks and thick short legs.<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup> In build, stature, and endurance, they resembled the [Dwarves](Dwarves.md).<sup id="cite_ref-atani_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-1">[1]</a></sup>

They had wide faces with deep-set eyes with heavy brows and flat noses. Their wide mouths were the most expressive of their usually impassive features. They had small, sunken eyes that were so black the pupils could not be distinguished, though their eyes glowed red when they were angry or suspicious. Their hair was sparse and lank, never growing below the eyebrows with the exception of some men who grew black tufts on their chins.<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Drúedain spoke in deep, guttural voices, though their laughter was hearty and pleasantly contagious to other Men and even Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Culture {#Culture}

The Drúedain were primitive, but marvellously skilled trackers with a better sense of smell than other Men, knew all about plants, were skilled in the carving of wood or stone and had mysterious powers of clairvoyance<sup id="cite_ref-Note7_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note7-6">[6]</a></sup> and [magic](../M/Magic.md) related to the animation of statues made from stone in their likeness.<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Drúedain were merry in temperament and character like [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md), but could be sardonic and ruthless on the grimmer side of their nature, but were less grim than Dwarves. They were frugal and ate sparingly and drank only water, even during peaceful or plentiful times.<sup id="cite_ref-atani_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-1">[1]</a></sup>

A hardy people, in the late First Age they used caves in the mountains as store-houses, which they used also as sleeping-places during severe weather. They maintained this custom in Beleriand (except the most hardy). These places were guarded and didn't allow even their [Haladin](../H/Haladin.md) friends to enter. Otherwise they were content to live in tents or makeshift shelters built round large trees.<sup id="cite_ref-atani_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-1">[1]</a></sup>

For weapons, the Drúedain used poisoned darts<sup id="cite_ref-TD_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TD-2">[2]</a></sup> and poisoned arrows.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

The Drúedain had a lore of plants not possessed by most of the Edain, nor even the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), including knowledge about the edible [fungi](../M/Mushrooms.md). To the surprise of the other peoples, the Drúedain cultured fungi and enjoyed them as food. They passed this knowledge to the [Haladin](../H/Haladin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

## Language {#Language}

The Drúedain of the Drúadan Forest spoke their own language, [Drúadan](Drúadan_language.md),<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-10">[10]</a></sup> which was completely alien to [Westron](../W/Westron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-OfMen_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OfMen-18">[18]</a></sup> The only known words in Drúadan are the name *[Ghân](../G/Ghân.md)*, the element *[buri](../B/Buri.md)* ("son of")<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> and the name *[gorgûn](../G/Gorgûn.md)* ("orcs", "orc-folk").<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

The Drúedain of the Forest of Brethil who lived there with the [Folk of Haleth](../H/Haladin.md) spoke the language of the Folk of Haleth after their own fashion, but retained a number of words of their own.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> It is possible that they retained their own language and that they only used the language of the Folk of Haleth when they talked with them, because even the Folk of Haleth called them *drûg*, which was their name in the "own language" of the Drúedain.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

*Drúedain* is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name. It is the plural form of Drúadan<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-23">[23]</a></sup> and means "wild men" or "woses".<sup id="cite_ref-druadan_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-druadan-24">[24]</a></sup> The element *[Drû](Drû.md)* is an adaptation into Sindarin of *[Drughu](Drughu.md)*, which is what the Drúedain call themselves in their own language. As the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) came to know the Drû better, and to recognise their bitter enmity to the [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md), they added the title *[Edain](../E/Edain.md)* to their name.<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-23">[23]</a></sup>

The word *Wose* represents [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s translation of the actual word *[róg](../R/Róg.md)* in the language of the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Note14_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note14-3">[3]</a></sup> It means "wild man of the woods". It is a modernization of the Anglo-Saxon word *wása*.<sup id="cite_ref-Note14_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note14-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) element *wasa* originally meant a forlorn or abandoned person. It occurs in *wudewasa* meaning "wild, neglected". It is seen in the name *Wuduwasas*, which is the direct inspiration for the Woses and means "savage men" (of the woods).<sup id="cite_ref-Woses_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woses-25">[25]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

- **[Drú](Drú.md)/Drúin:** the simple [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name for the Drughu, singular and plural.<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-23">[23]</a></sup>
- **Drúath:** another Sindarin plural form of Drú.<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_23-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-23">[23]</a></sup>
- **Drû-folk:** rarely used collective term.<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_23-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-23">[23]</a></sup>
- **[Púkel-men](../P/Púkel-men.md):** the name used by the Rohirrim in Rohan for the statues on the road to Dunharrow<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> and for the Drúedain.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>
- **[Rú](../R/Rú.md)/[Rúatani](../R/Rúatani.md):** Quenya terms for the Drughu, derived from their Sindarin counterparts. Singular/plural respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_23-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-23">[23]</a></sup>
- **[Róg](../R/Róg.md)/[Rógin](../R/Rógin.md):** the name in [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md), singular/plural respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-Note14_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note14-3">[3]</a></sup>
- **[Oghor-hai](../O/Oghor-hai.md):** the name used by [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Druedain_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Druedain-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In Western folklore, the "wuduwasa" or "wood man" is a hairy, troll-like being supposed to inhabit woods and forests; the figure was used on coats-of-arms and illuminations during the Middle Ages up to the Renaissance. Both the description of Woses, as well as the word "Wose" itself, derives from this folkloric figure. According to [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) his idea was to show the actual existence of wild folk, remnants of former peoples driven out by invaders, living a debased and savage life in forests and mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-Woses_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woses-25">[25]</a></sup>

In the *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight](<../S/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight_(poem).md>)* poem, the word <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Wodwo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Wodwo (page does not exist)">wodwos</a></i> appears, which Tolkien translated as "wood-[trolls](../T/Trolls.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup>
