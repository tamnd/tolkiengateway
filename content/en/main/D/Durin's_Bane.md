**Durin's Bane** was a [Balrog](/wiki/Balrog), most well known for the destruction of the [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") city of [Moria](/wiki/Moria).

Durin's Bane was notable for surviving the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath), and after sleeping deep within the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) for thousands of years, it was awoken by the [Dwarves of Moria](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Moria) in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). It was responsible for the death of [King](/wiki/King_of_Durin%27s_Folk "King of Durin's Folk") [Durin VI](/wiki/Durin_VI) and the ruin of [Moria](/wiki/Moria). It was also this Balrog that was encountered by the [Fellowship](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring "Fellowship of the Ring") during the [Quest of the Ring](/wiki/Quest_of_the_Ring).

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

Durin's Bane was first created as a [Maia](/wiki/Maia). However, like other [evil spirits](/wiki/Evil_spirits "Evil spirits"), the Balrog followed [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) to [Arda](/wiki/Arda) soon after its creation, contending with [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) and other [Valar](/wiki/Valar).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

It is not known in which of the [Battles of Beleriand](/wiki/Battles_of_Beleriand) Durin's Bane fought in until the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), nor whether it served under [Gothmog](</wiki/Gothmog_(Lord_of_Balrogs)> "Gothmog (Lord of Balrogs)"), the Lord of Balrogs. When the [Host of the Valar](/wiki/Host_of_the_Valar) defeated the hosts of Morgoth in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath), Durin's Bane managed to flee the overthrow of [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim) and escaped into the east and hid in the roots of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) beneath [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Moria "Moria").<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

### Awakening and the Third Age {#Awakening_and_the_Third_Age}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Dwarves_Delve_Too_Deep.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/05/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Dwarves_Delve_Too_Deep.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Dwarves_Delve_Too_Deep.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="253" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/0/05/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Dwarves_Delve_Too_Deep.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="398" data-file-height="336"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Eddy in Fate&#39;s Tide&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

For more than five millennia, Durin's Bane hibernated in his deep hiding place at the roots of the mountains in Khazad-dûm. It remained undisturbed throughout the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) and most of the [Third](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age"), before the *[mithril](/wiki/Mithril "Mithril")*-miners of [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") King [Durin VI](/wiki/Durin_VI) awoke him in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1980](/wiki/Third_Age_1980 "Third Age 1980").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Durin was slain by the Balrog, at which point it became known as "Durin's Bane".<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[3]</a></sup>

The Dwarves attempted to fight the Durin's Bane, but his power was far too great. Despite their efforts to hold Khazad-dûm against it, [King Náin](/wiki/N%C3%A1in_I "Náin I") and many of the Dwarves were killed in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1981](/wiki/Third_Age_1981 "Third Age 1981")<sup id="cite_ref-Year1981_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Year1981-6">[6]</a></sup>, and the survivors were forced to flee.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[3]</a></sup> [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) were able to creep into Moria<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and, as a result, many of the [Silvan Elves](/wiki/Silvan_Elves) of [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) fled to the south.<sup id="cite_ref-Year1981_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Year1981-6">[6]</a></sup>

For five hundred years, Moria was left to the Balrog.<sup id="cite_ref-January_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-January-8">[8]</a></sup> Over the course of many years, it became acquainted with the ancient tunnels delved by mysterious [nameless things](/wiki/Nameless_things "Nameless things") underneath Moria and the Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-White_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-9">[9]</a></sup>

Around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2480](/wiki/Third_Age_2480 "Third Age 2480"), [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) began to make secret strongholds in the Misty Mountains to block all of the passes, and [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) began to send some of his creatures to Moria.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> The Balrog allowed the Orcs and trolls to remain in Moria while it dwelt there, but they feared him.<sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 1]</a></sup>

The [Battle of Azanulbizar](/wiki/Battle_of_Azanulbizar) was the climactic battle in the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](/wiki/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs). It took place before the [Great Gate](/wiki/Great_Gate) of Moria in [T.A. 2799](/wiki/Third_Age_2799 "Third Age 2799") and was a victory for the Dwarves. However, the victors did not conquer Moria because [Dáin Ironfoot](/wiki/D%C3%A1in_Ironfoot), having slain the Orc [Azog](/wiki/Azog), felt the terror of the Balrog at the gate.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup> Despite a failed attempt to recolonize Moria by [Balin](/wiki/Balin) in [T.A. 2989](/wiki/Third_Age_2989 "Third Age 2989"),<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup> Durin's Bane remained a menace in the ancient kingdom of the Dwarves, and its nature remained hidden to the outside world.

### Passing of the Fellowship {#Passing_of_the_Fellowship}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/64/Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg/300px-Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="168" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/64/Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg/450px-Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/6/64/Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg/600px-Manuel_Casta%C3%B1%C3%B3n_-_Flame_of_Ud%C3%BBn.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2500" data-file-height="1400"/></a><figcaption>&#39;<i>Flame of Udûn</i>&#39; by Manuel Castañón</figcaption></figure>

In January of [T.A. 3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"), the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) travelled through Moria on the way to [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom), reaching the [Bridge of Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Bridge_of_Khazad-d%C3%BBm) on [15 January](/wiki/15_January) of that year.<sup id="cite_ref-January_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-January-8">[8]</a></sup> It was there they encountered the terrible creature, and the Elf [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) instantly recognized it as a Balrog, while [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) dropped his axe in awe, recognizing Durin's Bane. At once, the [Wizard](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards") [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) warned the members of the Fellowship that this creature was beyond them, commanding them to leave while he faced it.<sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-1">[1]</a></sup>

Standing firmly in the narrow bridge, Gandalf cried at the Balrog, which remained silent. The creature's fire dimmed and darkness spread around it while Gandalf's light stood. The Balrog struck out at the Wizard, but [Glamdring](/wiki/Glamdring), Gandalf's sword, prevailed and the creature's sword was broken. The Balrog then jumped ahead and Gandalf broke the Bridge in front of him, but as the Balrog fell, it wrapped his whip around Gandalf's knees. This caused Gandalf to stagger and fall, and he was dragged into the abyss as he cried "*Fly, you fools!*" to the Fellowship.<sup id="cite_ref-Bridge_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bridge-1">[1]</a></sup>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/4c/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg/250px-The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="157" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/4c/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg/375px-The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/4c/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg/500px-The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Battle_on_Durin%27s_Tower.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1440" data-file-height="905"/></a><figcaption>&#34;The Battle on <a href="/wiki/Durin%27s_Tower" title="Durin&#39;s Tower">Durin&#39;s Tower</a>&#34; by <a href="/wiki/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt" title="The Brothers Hildebrandt">Greg Hildebrandt</a></figcaption></figure>

Neither he nor the Balrog were killed by the fall, and Gandalf pursued the creature for eight days until they climbed to the peak of [Zirakzigil](/wiki/Celebdil "Celebdil"), where [they fought](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Peak "Battle of the Peak") for three days and two nights. In the end, the Balrog was cast down and his body broke the mountain-side as it fell. Gandalf himself died following this ordeal, but was later sent back to Middle-earth with even greater powers as Gandalf the White.<sup id="cite_ref-White_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

During the composition of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, in the first "Sketch of the Moria chapter", [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) conceived that the Fellowship was pursued by Orcs and a [Black Rider](/wiki/Black_Rider), but soon wrote above it was a Balrog.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>

In the first full narrative of the chapter, the description of the Balrog is more human-like, which Tolkien noted should be changed to be less clear: "*A figure strode to the fissure, no more than man-high yet terror seemed to go before it. They could see the furnace-fire of its yellow eyes from afar; its arms were very long; it had a red \[?tongue\]. Through the air it sprang over the fiery fissure. The flames leaped up to greet it and wreathed about it. Its streaming hair seemed to catch fire...*". Tolkien also rejected a sentence of Gandalf's cry to the Balrog: "*Go back into the fiery depths. It is forbidden for any Balrog to come beneath the sky since [Fionwë](/wiki/Fionw%C3%AB) son of [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) overthrew Thangorodrim.*"<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

Although it had no repercussion on the final version of the story, Tolkien considered in a hasty note that the battle on the Bridge was actually between Gandalf and [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), apparently resulting on Gandalf coming out clad in white.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup>

In the drafts of the "[Lothlórien](</wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien_(chapter)> "Lothlórien (chapter)")" chapter, [Celeborn](/wiki/Celeborn) and [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) think that no Balrog could lie in the Misty Mountains, so it should have been sent by Sauron from [Orodruin](/wiki/Orodruin).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>

<b>1978: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Balrog has wings and appears capable of limited flight. The head resembles a lion but the rest of the body was rendered in matte black, a technique commonly used for shadowy surreal effect in rotoscope animation.</dd></dl>

**1981:** <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i><b>The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)</b></i></a>

The identity of Durin's Bane as a Balrog is apparently common knowledge among the Wise as Gandalf explains its history to the Fellowship in Moria before their encounter with it. He initially fails to connect this with the dark power he encounters, and Celeborn still has no knowledge of it. Balrogs are bafflingly stated to be the deadliest of the servants of Sauron though Gandalf also refers to it as a Spawn of [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth")

<b>2001: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Durin&#39;s Bane, as designed by by <a href="/wiki/Ben_Wootten" title="Ben Wootten">Ben Wootten</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Howe" title="John Howe">John Howe</a>, has wings. Jackson&#39;s Demon of Might was indistinct, a real blend of shadow and fire. Only his horned head, cloven feet, and clawed hands could clearly be seen. In the film continuity, the Orcs of Moria (here specified as &#34;goblins&#34;) have crafted their armor and weapons to match the fiery appearance of the Balrog. <sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> Gandalf is also aware of the creature&#39;s identity prior to entering Moria, having previously learned of it from <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Balrog is the boss of the level &#34;Abyss Fight&#34;. It cannot be harmed except when it is stunned with Gandalf&#39;s lightning.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Third_Age" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Third Age">The Lord of the Rings: The Third Age</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The players must assist Gandalf in his fight with the Balrog. This fight is notable among players of the game for its difficulty and the Balrog&#39;s very high amount of health points.</dd></dl>

<b>2004: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Many of the Evil factions can summon a Balrog, specifically Durin&#39;s Bane, for a short period of time. It is one of the mightiest units in the game, only able to be summoned with experience points. It is similar in design to the Balrog in the movie.</dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Like in the previous installment, the Balrog can be summoned by evil factions.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The real name of Durin&#39;s Bane is given as &#34;<b>Askâdurs</b>&#34; and it is revealed to have fought against Dwarves as part of <a href="/wiki/Morgoth" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a>&#39;s armies during the <a href="/wiki/First_Age" title="First Age">First Age</a>. It is encountered in various flashback instances (where the player controls historical characters), which showcase his awakening by Dwarves in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1980" title="Third Age 1980">TA 1980</a>, the fall of Khazad-dûm <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1981" title="Third Age 1981">the year after</a> and the Dwarves of <a href="/wiki/Balin" title="Balin">Balin</a>&#39;s company fleeing from the ancient evil, among others. After Gandalf defeats him, the lifeless body of Durin&#39;s Bane can be found on the slopes of Zirakzigil. Despite the players knowing that the Balrog is dead, another Servant of Sauron tests their will and fears by portraying an illusion of him. In the illusion, the fight between Gandalf and the Balrog on the <a href="/wiki/Endless_Stair" title="Endless Stair">Endless Stair</a> is recreated, until it ends the opposite way of the actual event: the Balrog defeats the Wizard, throwing his lifeless body from Zirakzigil. Players have to defeat the Balrog in order to combat the illusion.</dd></dl>

<b>2009: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Conquest" title="The Lord of the Rings: Conquest">The Lord of the Rings: Conquest</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Durin&#39;s Bane is resurrected after his defeat by Gandalf, and must be fought again in Moria. In the Evil Campaign, Durin&#39;s Bane is playable in two battles. It kills Gimli, who makes his last stand in Moria, and <a href="/wiki/Treebeard" title="Treebeard">Treebeard</a>, who takes part in the final battle for <a href="/wiki/The_Shire" title="The Shire">the Shire</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2013: <i><a href="/wiki/Lego_The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Video_Game" title="Lego The Lord of the Rings: The Video Game">Lego The Lord of the Rings: The Video Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Durin&#39;s Bane is only seen as a light that chases the player(s) through Moria. When they reach the Bridge of Khazad-dûm, it appears in a cutscene where Gandalf destroys the bridge and the Balrog pulls him down. The player then controls Gandalf and has to grab Glamdring and land on Durin&#39;s Bane and stab him while avoiding the Balrog&#39;s fiery breath. When this is over, the game starts another cutscene that ends the level. Later, Gandalf appears on a snowy mountain with a tower on top. When he reaches the top of the tower, the Balrog climbs on. The Balrog repeatedly sweeps his fiery whip and strikes the ground with his sword. Lightning then begins to strike at one place and the Balrog breathes fire and is weakened. Gandalf then uses his sword to direct the lightning at Durin&#39;s Bane and his health goes down. The lightning will strike at three different places and when the Balrog loses all of his health by being hit three times, it will fall off the mountain and die.</dd></dl>

<b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>, Season One:</b>

<dl><dd><b>Episode 7: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Eye" title="The Eye">The Eye</a></i>:</b>
<dl><dd>Durin&#39;s Bane makes an appearance lurking beneath a large <a href="/wiki/Mithril" title="Mithril">Mithril</a> vein at the very depths of <a href="/wiki/Moria" title="Moria">Khazad-dûm</a>. When an orange leaf lands directly in front of him and catches on fire, disturbing his slumber, the Balrog roars. The design, by <a href="/wiki/W%C4%93t%C4%81_FX" title="Wētā FX">Weta FX</a>&#39;s Nick Keller, is similar but not identical to the Balrog in <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">the 2001 film</a>. Both Weta and other artists had proposed more divergent designs, but the showrunners steered them as close to the film iteration as copyright would allow.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup></dd></dl></dd></dl>

<b>2024: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>, Season Two:</b>

<dl><dd><dl><dd>Durin&#39;s Bane appears after <a href="/wiki/Durin_III" title="Durin III">Durin III</a> digs into a cavern full of Mithril at the urging of a Dwarven <a href="/wiki/Rings_of_Power" title="Rings of Power">Ring of Power</a>. In spite of his son <a href="/wiki/Durin_IV" title="Durin IV">Durin IV</a>&#39;s attempts at saving him, he is killed after removing his ring and jumping toward the Balrog with his axe before the entire cavern collapses around him.</dd></dl></dd></dl>
