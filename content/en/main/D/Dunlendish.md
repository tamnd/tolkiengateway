**Dunlendish**,<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-1">[1]</a></sup> also known as the **Dunland tongue**,<sup id="cite_ref-Deep_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deep-2">[2]</a></sup> was a [Mannish](/wiki/Mannish) [language](/wiki/Languages "Languages") that was spoken by the [Dunlendings](/wiki/Dunlendings) of [Dunland](/wiki/Dunland).<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The [Pre-Númenórean](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) language that was spoken by ancestors of the Dunlendings in the forests south of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) in [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath) and [Enedwaith](/wiki/Enedwaith) during the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> was related to the language of the [Folk of Haleth](/wiki/Haladin "Haladin") of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The language of the Folk of Haleth was not related to the language of the [Folk of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador "House of Hador") and the [Folk of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor") from which [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), the language of the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) was descended.<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-6">[6]</a></sup> Dunlendish was not related to or only remotely akin to [Westron](/wiki/Westron),<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup> because Westron was descended from Adûnaic mingled with many words of the languages of other Men.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

It appears that a language related to Dunlendish was adopted by the southern group of [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) of [Stoor-kind](/wiki/Stoors "Stoors") while they settled between [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) and the borders of Dunland<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1150](/wiki/Third_Age_1150 "Third Age 1150") until about [1630](/wiki/Third_Age_1630 "Third Age 1630"),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> before they migrated north to [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), but by the later [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) their descendants in the Shire and in [Bree](/wiki/Bree) had long abandoned it for the [Common Speech](/wiki/Westron "Westron").<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-1">[1]</a></sup>

The [Men of Bree](/wiki/Men_of_Bree) once spoke a language that was related to Dunlendish, because they were descended from the same [people](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Pre-Númenóreans") as the Dunlendings, but the Men of Bree adopted [Westron](/wiki/Westron) after they became subjects of the kingdom of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor).<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup> The odd names of [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders) and the name of Bree and the names of locations near Bree were traces of the older language of the southern Stoors and the Men of Bree.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-10">[10]</a></sup>

On 3 March [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> during the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) [Gamling](/wiki/Gamling) said that Dunlendish was once spoken in many western valleys in [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) and was able to understand Dunlendish.<sup id="cite_ref-Deep_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deep-2">[2]</a></sup> It is not recorded if Dunlendish was still spoken by some inhabitants of Rohan as a native language at that time and how Gamling learned Dunlendish.

Apparently Dunlendish was known also beyond the other side of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains); the [Great Gates](/wiki/Great_Gates) of [Moria](/wiki/Moria) included Dunlendish inscriptions in [Cirth](/wiki/Cirth).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

To this language belongs the name *[Forgoil](/wiki/Forgoil)*, which was said to mean 'Strawheads' and was given to the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim),<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup> and was used as an insult during the battle of Helm's Deep.<sup id="cite_ref-Deep_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deep-2">[2]</a></sup> The only analysis that can be done with some degree of credibility, is that the final part -il could be the plural marker of the word; then, it would be reminiscent to the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) partitive plural -li, and perhaps related.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) used [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) names for many first names of male Hobbits of [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and for place names in the region of Bree.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Portrayals in Adaptations {#Portrayals_in_Adaptations}

<b>1997: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a>—<a href="/wiki/Arnor:_The_People" title="Arnor: The People">Arnor: The People</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>Dunlendish was called Dunael, and it was represented by Gaelic. Before this was properly established, some Dunael names in earlier publications were rendered in <a href="/wiki/Welsh" title="Welsh">Welsh</a>, the language which represents <a href="/wiki/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing#Blarm" title="Languages in Middle-earth Role Playing">Blarm</a>. This discrepancy was explained as cultural interaction between Dunlendings and <a href="/wiki/Hillmen" title="Hillmen">Hillmen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup></dd></dl>
