**Dunlendish**,<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-1">[1]</a></sup> also known as the **Dunland tongue**,<sup id="cite_ref-Deep_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deep-2">[2]</a></sup> was a [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md) [language](../L/Languages.md) that was spoken by the [Dunlendings](Dunlendings.md) of [Dunland](Dunland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The [Pre-Númenórean](../P/Pre-Númenórean.md) language that was spoken by ancestors of the Dunlendings in the forests south of the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) in [Minhiriath](../M/Minhiriath.md) and [Enedwaith](../E/Enedwaith.md) during the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> was related to the language of the [Folk of Haleth](../H/Haladin.md) of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The language of the Folk of Haleth was not related to the language of the [Folk of Hador](../H/House_of_Hador.md) and the [Folk of Bëor](../H/House_of_Bëor.md) from which [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md), the language of the [Númenóreans](../N/Númenóreans.md) was descended.<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-6">[6]</a></sup> Dunlendish was not related to or only remotely akin to [Westron](../W/Westron.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup> because Westron was descended from Adûnaic mingled with many words of the languages of other Men.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

It appears that a language related to Dunlendish was adopted by the southern group of [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) of [Stoor-kind](../S/Stoors.md) while they settled between [Tharbad](../T/Tharbad.md) and the borders of Dunland<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> between [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1150](../T/Third_Age_1150.md) until about [1630](../T/Third_Age_1630.md),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> before they migrated north to [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md), but by the later [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) their descendants in the Shire and in [Bree](../B/Bree.md) had long abandoned it for the [Common Speech](../W/Westron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-1">[1]</a></sup>

The [Men of Bree](../M/Men_of_Bree.md) once spoke a language that was related to Dunlendish, because they were descended from the same [people](../P/Pre-Númenóreans.md) as the Dunlendings, but the Men of Bree adopted [Westron](../W/Westron.md) after they became subjects of the kingdom of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup> The odd names of [Bucklanders](../B/Bucklanders.md) and the name of Bree and the names of locations near Bree were traces of the older language of the southern Stoors and the Men of Bree.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-10">[10]</a></sup>

On 3 March [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> during the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) [Gamling](../G/Gamling.md) said that Dunlendish was once spoken in many western valleys in [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md) and was able to understand Dunlendish.<sup id="cite_ref-Deep_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deep-2">[2]</a></sup> It is not recorded if Dunlendish was still spoken by some inhabitants of Rohan as a native language at that time and how Gamling learned Dunlendish.

Apparently Dunlendish was known also beyond the other side of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md); the [Great Gates](../G/Great_Gates.md) of [Moria](../M/Moria.md) included Dunlendish inscriptions in [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

To this language belongs the name *[Forgoil](../F/Forgoil.md)*, which was said to mean 'Strawheads' and was given to the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Men_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-3">[3]</a></sup> and was used as an insult during the battle of Helm's Deep.<sup id="cite_ref-Deep_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deep-2">[2]</a></sup> The only analysis that can be done with some degree of credibility, is that the final part -il could be the plural marker of the word; then, it would be reminiscent to the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) partitive plural -li, and perhaps related.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) used [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md) names for many first names of male Hobbits of [Buckland](../B/Buckland.md) and for place names in the region of Bree.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Portrayals in Adaptations {#Portrayals_in_Adaptations}

<b>1997: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a>—<a href="../A/Arnor%253A_The_People.md" title="Arnor: The People">Arnor: The People</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>Dunlendish was called Dunael, and it was represented by Gaelic. Before this was properly established, some Dunael names in earlier publications were rendered in <a href="../W/Welsh.md" title="Welsh">Welsh</a>, the language which represents <a href="../L/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md#Blarm" title="Languages in Middle-earth Role Playing">Blarm</a>. This discrepancy was explained as cultural interaction between Dunlendings and <a href="../H/Hillmen.md" title="Hillmen">Hillmen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup></dd></dl>
