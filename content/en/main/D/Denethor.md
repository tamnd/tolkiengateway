**Denethor II** was the twenty-sixth and last [Ruling Steward](/wiki/Steward_of_Gondor "Steward of Gondor") of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). He succeeded his father [Ecthelion II](/wiki/Ecthelion_II) at his death in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2984](/wiki/Third_Age_2984 "Third Age 2984"). Denethor was described as appearing noble and powerful, even to rival the lordliness of Gandalf. He was proud, tall, wise, farsighted, and valiant; "more Kingly" than any of his predecessors for a long time. He proved a masterful lord and a great ruler, seeing to all things large and small under his command.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup> During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), however, his good judgement became tainted by "pride and despair," resulting from recent military losses at [Osgiliath](/wiki/Sauron%27s_attack_on_Osgiliath "Sauron's attack on Osgiliath"), the use of a <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Palantir" title="Palantir">palantir</a>, the death of his son [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir), and the threat to his Stewardship represented by [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn)'s impending arrival in Gondor. <sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> During the [siege of Minas Tirith](/wiki/Siege_of_Gondor "Siege of Gondor"), Denethor set himself ablaze after wrongly believing [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir), his only other living child (whom he also attempted to burn), was killed in the battle. <sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Denethor was born the third child and first son of Ecthelion II, Ruling Steward of Gondor, in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2930](/wiki/Third_Age_2930 "Third Age 2930").<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-2">[2]</a></sup>

A northerner known as [Thorongil](/wiki/Thorongil) came to the service of his father. He was the same age as Denethor and appeared like the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor) of old. He was a war genius and won the trust and affection of Ecthelion and the people, and because of his great renown many Gondorians saw him and Denethor as rivals. The stranger talked in favour of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) whom Denethor didn't trust at all; it is possible that Denethor figured out that Thorongil was really [Aragorn II](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn"). If Denethor knew that the stranger was the Heir of Isildur, he could have thought that he would conspire with Gandalf to supplant him. Thus, Denethor was not sorry when Thorongil vanished to the east.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/8/8a/Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg/200px-Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="247" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/8/8a/Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg/300px-Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/8/8a/Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg/400px-Catherine_Chmiel_-_Denethor_and_Finduilas.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1484"/></a><figcaption><i>Denethor and Finduilas</i> by <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Catherine_Chmiel" title="Catherine Chmiel">Catherine Chmiel</a></figcaption></figure>

In [2976](/wiki/Third_Age_2976 "Third Age 2976") he married [Finduilas](</wiki/Finduilas_(wife_of_Denethor)> "Finduilas (wife of Denethor)"), daughter of [Prince](/wiki/Prince_of_Dol_Amroth "Prince of Dol Amroth") [Adrahil](/wiki/Adrahil) of [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth), whom he loved deeply. She gave birth to two sons: [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir) and [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir). He doted upon his eldest son, Boromir, while Faramir, his second son, with his actions and friendship with [Mithrandir](/wiki/Gandalf "Gandalf"), often displeased his father.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup> In the meantime, Faramir studied the lore of Gondor, including the archives that only he and his father could access. Part of his studies were devoted to the lore of the *[palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri "Palantíri")*.<sup id="cite_ref-pal_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pal-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Early stewardship {#Early_stewardship}

Eight years after Denethor's marriage, Ecthelion died and he became Ruling Steward. Denethor was not viewed very highly in the eyes of his people in comparison to [Thorongil](/wiki/Thorongil), but Denethor proved a masterful ruler. He listened to counsel, said little, and followed his mind, holding everything in his own hand.

Only four years after the death of Ecthelion, Finduilas died as well. Denethor was grief-stricken, and became more grim and silent. Moreover he anticipated [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)'s assault during his time and desperately needed knowledge. Being proud and overconfident in his willpower, Denethor looked into the long-forbidden [Anor-stone](/wiki/Anor-stone) to seek knowledge.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup>

The *palantír* showed him much of what he wanted to see, and his knowledge grew exceedingly, so that he was wondered at by the people. But eventually Sauron, looking into the captured [Ithil-stone](/wiki/Ithil-stone), discovered that Denethor was using the *palantír*.<sup id="cite_ref-pal_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pal-8">[8]</a></sup> Sauron attempted to wrench the Anor stone to his will, but failed due to Denethor's strength of will, his integrity, and his right to the use of the stone.<sup id="cite_ref-pal_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pal-8">[8]</a></sup> But this caused even greater stress upon the steward's already weakened body, coupled by the apparent invincibility of Sauron shown him by the stone and the manner of his viewing<sup id="cite_ref-pal_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pal-8">[8]</a></sup> and aged prematurely.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg/300px-Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="261" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg/450px-Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/c0/Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg/600px-Anna_Kulisz_-_Looking_into_the_Stone.jpg 2x" data-file-width="900" data-file-height="784"/></a><figcaption><i>Looking into the Stone</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Anna_Kulisz" title="Category:Images by Anna Kulisz">Anna Kulisz</a></figcaption></figure>

Denethor, meanwhile, looked not only at [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), but also probably to [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), and extracted information from the wizard to his benefit, which Sauron was helpless to prevent. He also sought to surpass Thorongil and Gandalf in wisdom, and to keep an eye on them.<sup id="cite_ref-pal_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pal-8">[8]</a></sup>

In all debatable matters of importance domestic, or external, however, even Denethor held [Council](/wiki/Council_of_Gondor "Council of Gondor"), and at least listened to what the Lords of the Fiefs and the Captains of the Forces had to say.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> But he became blind to all struggles save that of him and Sauron, his people and Sauron's [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs). He mistrusted all his allies who were not under his own rule, and though he cared for his nation, his concerns were largely political; he feared and opposed any potential rivals in the nobility, regardless of their intent, and despised lesser men.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> He wore mail under his robes, to prevent himself from getting soft in his old age.<sup id="cite_ref-siege_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-11">[11]</a></sup>

### Stirring of trouble {#Stirring_of_trouble}

Faramir, his younger son, began to have dreams, speaking of [Imladris](/wiki/Rivendell "Rivendell") and [Isildur's Bane](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). Boromir convinced his father to send him instead of his brother. Boromir did not return from his quest; he was shot by [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai) on [Amon Hen](/wiki/Amon_Hen). Faramir and Denethor both heard the [horn of Gondor](/wiki/Great_Horn "Great Horn"). Several days later his horn was found by watchers below the [Entwash](/wiki/Entwash), cloven in two. It was then brought to Denethor. Desperate after the death of his heir, he looked into the *palantír* more often than usual and so it grew his despair.<sup id="cite_ref-A_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-5">[5]</a></sup>

Knowing of the coming strike of Mordor, Denethor called in the [Captains of the Outlands](/wiki/Captains_of_the_Outlands) to defend [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith). [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) began to fly over the city, out of view but still casting shadows of fear.<sup id="cite_ref-MT_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MT-12">[12]</a></sup>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/9/98/Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg/200px-Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="247" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/9/98/Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg/300px-Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/9/98/Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg/400px-Cor_Blok_-_Pippin_between_Gandalf_and_Denethor.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="1233"/></a><figcaption>&#34;<a href="/wiki/Peregrin_Took" title="Peregrin Took">Pippin</a> between <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> and Denethor&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Cor_Blok" title="Cor Blok">Cor Blok</a></figcaption></figure>

Thirteen days after Boromir's death, Mithrandir arrived with the [hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") [Peregrin Took](/wiki/Peregrin_Took). Denethor openly vented his rage at first, but at the same time was curiously attracted to the hobbit, a witness of his son's death, questioning him closely and suspiciously. When Peregrin offered his service in return for Boromir's sacrifice, Denethor was pleased and amused. After examining Pippin's [barrow-blade](/wiki/Daggers_of_Westernesse "Daggers of Westernesse"), he accepted the hobbit's service courteously. He then confronted Gandalf. While they supped, Gandalf told of the fall of [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard). Denethor hinted at his own abilities to see what happened in the world. He racked the hobbit with questions for an hour, and then gave his guests lodging.<sup id="cite_ref-MT_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MT-12">[12]</a></sup>

### Siege of Gondor {#Siege_of_Gondor}

Soon after, the [Rammas](/wiki/Rammas_Echor "Rammas Echor") was surrounded and Gondor placed under siege. Faramir returned from errands in [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien), and told the story of his encounter with [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins), two days before, and of his journey to Cirith Ungol. At the name of this place, Gandalf himself seems to be extremely uneasy.

A discussion on the matter followed between Denethor, Gandalf, and Faramir, in which Denethor revealed he had guessed more than was said by his son. He noticed that Faramir had looked at Mithrandir to understand if he was saying too much. Denethor knew about the [Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") and he was aware of Frodo's mission. He accused Faramir of condemning his people to certain death and told Gandalf that on this matter he would not have destroyed the ring, instead wishing to hide it without using it. Noticing his son's weariness, Denethor allowed Faramir to rest for the night. Denethor never said explicitly that he would have liked for Boromir to live and Faramir to die: when asked by Faramir, he said that he would have liked for their places to be exchanged, as if Boromir was in Ithilien he would have brought him a mighty gift (the Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-siege_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-11">[11]</a></sup>

In a council held [the next day](/wiki/11_March "11 March"), Faramir was sent to strengthen the garrison of [Osgiliath](/wiki/Osgiliath).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> The decision was not taken by Denethor himself, but by the Council, led by Denethor but to which Faramir took part, agreeing in the end with his father's will.

Faramir recovered the lost ground all the way back to the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), but soon faced fresh troops from [Minas Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul) and [Harad](/wiki/Harad), led by the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king), a master strategist and one of terrible presence. Faramir was driven back to the [Causeway Forts](/wiki/Causeway_Forts), but these fell, and Faramir was forced to retreat, having lost a third of his men. The retreat was dogged by the Nazgûl, and Faramir was almost killed by a wound from a southern arrow, thought to be a poisoned dart.<sup id="cite_ref-siege_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-11">[11]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/1/15/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg/220px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="220" height="121" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/1/15/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg/330px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/1/15/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg/440px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Pyre_of_Denethor.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="849" data-file-height="468"/></a><figcaption><i>The Pyre of Denethor</i> by <a href="/wiki/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann" title="Anke Eißmann">Anke Eißmann</a></figcaption></figure>

After [Imrahil](/wiki/Imrahil) had brought him the body of Faramir, nearly dead, Denethor gave in to despair and seemed not to be interested into anything but his son's life. While the outer defences of the [Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Pelennor_Fields) collapsed at many points, he looked into the *palantír*; he found the Enemy stronger than ever on all fronts and feared that the Ring was in his hands; he sees a black sky and Frodo captured in Mordor.

Denethor suddenly seemed to have aged by many years, and his will was snapped. He abandoned leadership of the city to Gandalf and Imrahil, staying by his son's side in the [White Tower](/wiki/Tower_of_Ecthelion "Tower of Ecthelion"), thinking that everything was lost for his family, his city, and his people. [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) had been cut off and could not come to their aid; the first circle was also in flames: the latter piece of information was the final trigger for Denethor's suicide.<sup id="cite_ref-siege_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-11">[11]</a></sup>

He prepared a pyre for him and Faramir in [Rath Dínen](/wiki/Rath_D%C3%ADnen). He was hampered in his aim by [Beregond](/wiki/Beregond), one of his own [Guards of the Citadel](/wiki/Guards_of_the_Citadel), and Gandalf, who removed Faramir from the pyre. When Faramir called his name, Denethor seemed willing to abandon his suicidal designs, but suddenly he raised up the *palantír* and prophesied the doom of Gondor, acknowledging that a new enemy was about to arrive: the pirates from Umbar. In a final speech to Gandalf, he said that he would have liked for things to be as they had always been in his life: to be a ruling Steward of a free city, ruling in peace and with his sons masters of their own will. But if he had to see his city in the hand of an illegitimate [King](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor "Kings of Gondor") and his son turned into the pupil of a [wizard](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards"), then he preferred to have nothing and to decide how to end his own life:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Better to burn sooner than late, for burn we must. Go back to your bonfire! And I? I will go now to my pyre. No tomb for Denethor and Faramir! No long slow sleep of death embalmed. We will burn like <a href="/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans" title="Pre-Númenóreans">heathen</a> kings before ever a ship sailed hither from the <a href="/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor" title="Númenor">West</a>. The West has failed. Go back and burn!
</p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

He took the white rod of his office and broke it on his knee, casting it into the flames, symbolising the end of his stewardship and the end of the rule of the Stewards. He then laid himself down on the table with the *palantír* on his breast and so perished.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

### Afterwards {#Afterwards}

Faramir did survive, and the city did not fall due to the timely appearance of the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) and the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) with Aragorn II.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Faramir kept the title of Steward, but not of Ruling Steward, for he recognised Aragorn's right to take the throne. Using [Athelas](/wiki/Athelas) to save the lives of Faramir, [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn), and [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck"), Aragorn was first acknowledged as King by the people of Minas Tirith before he could actually claim the title. Faramir's reaction to his father's death is not recorded, but it is said that the news of his death wasn't given to him during the first days of his recovery, in order not to grieve him.

## Description {#Description}

Denethor had a "carven"<sup id="cite_ref-Appearance_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appearance-4">[4]</a></sup> and lean face,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> "proud" bones and "skin like ivory". His nose was curved and long and his eyes were dark.<sup id="cite_ref-Appearance_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appearance-4">[4]</a></sup> He was tall<sup id="cite_ref-Tall_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tall-3">[3]</a></sup> and beardless.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

<b>1980: <a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)"><i>The Return of the King</i> (1980 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/William_Conrad" title="William Conrad">William Conrad</a> is the voice of Denethor. His role is essentially reduced to presaging the doom of the arrival of the Black Ships (as he has seen in the <i><a href="/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri" title="Palantíri">palantír</a></i>) to <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peregrin_Took" title="Peregrin Took">Pippin</a>, and then calling for his own execution.</dd></dl>

<b>1981: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1981 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The voice of Denethor is provided by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Vaughan" title="Peter Vaughan">Peter Vaughan</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>1992: <a href="/wiki/Der_Herr_der_Ringe_(1992_German_radio_series)" title="Der Herr der Ringe (1992 German radio series)"><i>Der Herr der Ringe</i> (1992 German radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Denethor is played by Heinz Schimmelpfenig.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers">The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Denethor, played by <a href="/wiki/John_Noble" title="John Noble">John Noble</a>, appears in a flashback in the film&#39;s <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers_(extended_edition)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (extended edition)">Extended Edition</a>. He appears at <a href="/wiki/Osgiliath" title="Osgiliath">Osgiliath</a> shortly after <a href="/wiki/Boromir" title="Boromir">Boromir</a> completes his successful <a href="/wiki/Sauron%27s_attack_on_Osgiliath" title="Sauron&#39;s attack on Osgiliath">defence</a> of the city against <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>&#39;s forces and tells Boromir of <a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring" title="The One Ring">the One Ring</a> being found, urging his son to go attend the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Elrond" title="Council of Elrond">Council of Elrond</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rivendell" title="Rivendell">Rivendell</a> and try to persuade them to bring it back to <a href="/wiki/Gondor" title="Gondor">Gondor</a> so as to use it in defence of their homeland against Sauron. Denethor also makes known his preference of Boromir over <a href="/wiki/Faramir" title="Faramir">Faramir</a>, which would further play out in the next film.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Denethor appears almost entirely devoid of hope and highly irrational after learning of Boromir&#39;s death. He demands his remaining son go on essentially a suicide mission to reclaim enemy-captured Osgiliath and stubbornly refuses to light the <a href="/wiki/Warning_beacons_of_Gondor" title="Warning beacons of Gondor">warning beacons of Gondor</a> to call for the aid of <a href="/wiki/Rohan" title="Rohan">Rohan</a> (Pippin lights it instead at Gandalf&#39;s behest). Denethor then claims <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden" title="Théoden">Théoden</a> betrayed him when Sauron&#39;s armies begin attacking <a href="/wiki/Minas_Tirith" title="Minas Tirith">Minas Tirith</a>). In the book, the danger of his madness is that it seems to follow a certain logic; Sauron <i>does</i> have vastly superior forces, all of which he has surely shown to the Steward in the <i>palantír</i>. His actions, however, do not immediately proclaim his insanity; the Osgiliath mission is less obviously suicidal, as the city has not yet been overrun, and the warning beacons have indeed been lit, although Denethor expects little help.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>When Faramir is brought back from Osgiliath on his horse into the throne room, seemingly dead, Pippin attempts to stop Denethor from cremating his son (and himself with him), who he is sure is still alive. After Denethor throws Pippin out, the Hobbit rushes back to the battle to find Gandalf and stop the wanton murder of Faramir. When Gandalf and Pippin return astride <a href="/wiki/Shadowfax" title="Shadowfax">Shadowfax</a>, Denethor snatches a torch and (after a cynical monologue about Sauron&#39;s power in the extended edition) lights the pyre himself. Gandalf rides in, throwing Denethor off the pyre. Pippin jumps in to pull Faramir off and douses the flames on his body. A maddened Denethor attacks Pippin, but just then, Shadowfax kicks him back onto the pyre. And it&#39;s from there that he sees Faramir showing signs of life. Consumed by madness and flames, Denethor screams and flees to the precipice of the citadel, throwing himself off it and plummeting to his death.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/P%C3%A1n_prste%C5%88ov_(2001-2003_Slovak_radio_series)" title="Pán prsteňov (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)"><i>Pán prsteňov</i> (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The voice of Denethor is provided by Marián Slovák.</dd></dl>

<b>2015: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Denethor&#39;s role in the game is relatively minor, as, by the time the player reaches <a href="/wiki/Minas_Tirith" title="Minas Tirith">Minas Tirith</a>, <a href="/wiki/Faramir" title="Faramir">Faramir</a> already lies wounded and the Steward&#39;s will is failing. One notable change is his betrothal of Boromir to <a href="/wiki/Loth%C3%ADriel" title="Lothíriel">Lothíriel</a> of Dol Amroth.</dd></dl>
