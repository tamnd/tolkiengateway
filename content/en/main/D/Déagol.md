**Déagol** was a [Stoor](../S/Stoors.md) [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) of the [Gladden Fields](../G/Gladden_Fields.md) that found [the One Ring](../T/The_One_Ring.md) and was murdered by [Sméagol](../S/Sméagol.md).

## History {#History}

Déagol was a friend and relative<sup id="cite_ref-L214_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-3">[3]</a></sup> of Sméagol, being "sharper-eyed but not so quick and strong".<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-4">[4]</a></sup>

One day Sméagol [had his birthday](../R/Ribadyan.md), Déagol gave a customary present (albeit begrudgingly, as he was a mean little soul),<sup id="cite_ref-L214_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-3">[3]</a></sup> which he claimed was more than what he could afford.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-4">[4]</a></sup> Later that day, though still very early in the morning, they went fishing in the [Gladden Fields](../G/Gladden_Fields.md) on a boat. When Sméagol took a break to nose about the banks, a great fish pulled Déagol to the bottom of the water. Upon seeing something gleaming in the river-bed, he abandoned his fishing equipment and seized the gleaming object. When he swam out, *"with weeds in his hair and a handful of mud"*, he saw that what he discovered was a *"[beautiful golden ring](../T/The_One_Ring.md)"*.

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/C/Cor_Blok_-_Sméagol_and_Déagol.jpeg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Cor_Blok_-_Sm%C3%A9agol_and_D%C3%A9agol.jpeg/200px-Cor_Blok_-_Sm%C3%A9agol_and_D%C3%A9agol.jpeg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="140" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Cor_Blok_-_Sm%C3%A9agol_and_D%C3%A9agol.jpeg/300px-Cor_Blok_-_Sm%C3%A9agol_and_D%C3%A9agol.jpeg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Cor_Blok_-_Sm%C3%A9agol_and_D%C3%A9agol.jpeg/400px-Cor_Blok_-_Sm%C3%A9agol_and_D%C3%A9agol.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1434"/></a><figcaption>&#34;<a href="../G/Gollum.md" title="Gollum">Sméagol</a> and Déagol&#34; by <a href="../C/Cor_Blok.md" title="Cor Blok">Cor Blok</a></figcaption></figure>

Yet, as he gloated over his finding, Sméagol watched him from nearby and silently approached him, desiring the ring. Sméagol, being greedy, thought that Déagol's gift to him was poor and insufficient,<sup id="cite_ref-L214_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-3">[3]</a></sup> and thus used his birthday as an excuse to claim the ring; and as Déagol refused to hand it over, Sméagol strangled him, and hid his body cleverly so that the others didn't find it.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-4">[4]</a></sup>

In the years following the murder of Déagol, Sméagol became haunted by his actions, making up the defence that it was his birthday and that Déagol should have given it to him. After Sméagol became **[Gollum](../G/Gollum.md)**, he repeated his defence over and over as he gnawed bones within darkness until he very nearly believed it himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Déagol* is a name that is based on the [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) word *deagol (digol)*, which means "secret" or "hidden".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> It is a translation of the "original" [Westron](../W/Westron.md) name *[Nahald](../N/Nahald.md)*, which means "secret" in the Northern tongues of the [Vales of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Nahald_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nahald-1">[1]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>Déagol appears in <a href="../P/Prologue%253A_A_Ring&#39;s_Tale.md" title="Prologue: A Ring&#39;s Tale">the Prologue</a>. While fishing in the <a href="../A/Anduin.md" title="Anduin">Great River Anduin</a> with <a href="../G/Gollum.md" title="Gollum">Sméagol</a>, he is pulled into the water by a large fish attatched to his rod. He lets go of his rod and spots <a href="../T/The_One_Ring.md" title="The One Ring">a ring</a> at the bottom. He takes it back to the surface and rubs it on his body, enamored by its beauty. Sméagol asks him for the Ring because &#34;it&#39;s my birthday, my love, and I wants it&#34;. Déagol tells him he has already given him more than he could afford and that he will keep the Ring because he found it. Gollum asks &#34;will you indeed, my love?&#34; and strangles Déagol to death, taking the ring for himself.</dd></dl>
