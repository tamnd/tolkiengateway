**Durin I**, or **Durin the Deathless** (known as such due to his longevity) was the eldest of the [Fathers of the Dwarves](../F/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves.md), the founder of the city of [Khazad-dûm](../K/Khazad-dûm.md), and the first [King of Durin's folk](../K/King_of_Durin's_folk.md).

## History {#History}

### Origin {#Origin}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="../F/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves.md" title="Fathers of the Dwarves">Fathers of the Dwarves</a></b></div></div>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aulë_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/e1/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="303" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/e1/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/e1/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_and_the_Seven_Fathers.jpg 2x" data-file-width="819" data-file-height="826"/></a><figcaption><a href="../T/Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a> - <i>Aulë and the Seven Fathers</i></figcaption></figure>

Durin was the eldest of the Seven [Fathers of the Dwarves](../F/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves.md) fashioned under a mountain by the [Vala](../V/Vala.md) [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) who yearned for the arrival of the [Children of Ilúvatar](../C/Children_of_Ilúvatar.md). The Vala instructed his creations in the [speech](../K/Khuzdul.md) he had devised for them. When [Ilúvatar](../I/Ilúvatar.md) pointed out that this was against His plans, and reprimanded him, and that Aulë, as a Vala, had no power to make animate creations. Aulë repented and was about to destroy his creation but Ilúvatar himself gave them independent life; and said that they shall sleep until after the coming of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), who were to be his Firstborn.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[4]</a></sup>

Aulë laid the sleeping fathers and their mates in deep places; Durin had none.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup> He was set to sleep under [Mount Gundabad](../M/Mount_Gundabad.md) in the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) in the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup>

### Reign {#Reign}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/7b/Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="218" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/7b/Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/7b/Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Durin_I_Discovers_the_Three_Peaks.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="930"/></a><figcaption><a href="../T/Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a> - <i>Durin I Discovers the Three Peaks</i></figcaption></figure>

When Durin awoke in the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), he journeyed south alone through the wild where no person ever wandered before, and gave name to "nameless hills and dells".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup> He came upon [Kheled-zâram](../K/Kheled-zâram.md) and looking upon [mysterious stars](../S/Seven_Stars.md) set [like a crown](Durin's_Crown.md) reflected in its surface, he took this as a sign and erected [Durin's Stone](Durin's_Stone.md) on the location of that event.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup> There, in the caves above the lake, he founded the city of [Khazad-dûm](../K/Khazad-dûm.md) and for many years, it was the greatest Dwarven city in Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[2]</a></sup>

Durin founded the [House of Durin](../H/House_of_Durin.md) and his people became known as [Durin's folk](Durin's_folk.md), or the [Longbeards](../L/Longbeards.md). His people were Dwarves that joined him from other kindreds west and east.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[9]</a></sup>

He lived to a great age, even by the measure of the Dwarves, until he was known as "the [Deathless](Deathless.md)"; however, he was not actually immortal, and died before the end of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) and his tomb was in [Khazad-dûm](../K/Khazad-dûm.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[2]</a></sup>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The Longbeards believed that Durin would return seven times, and each time he will again be named Durin and reign as King. According to the legend recorded in the [Red Book](../R/Red_Book.md), these returns would be through an heir born into his line, an individual held to be Durin reborn, with same appearance, character and name.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[2]</a></sup>

Durin would not actually reincarnate in the sense of him being born and reborn in a new body. Rather, his original body was preserved, and his spirit would return to it and be granted life again. He would share this trait along with the other fathers of the Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup>

[Durin's Axe](Durin's_Axe.md), a great heirloom of Durin's folk, may have been his personal weapon although the canonicity of this is not certain; it was apparently found when [Balin](../B/Balin.md) and [his colony](../B/Balin's_folk.md) came to Khazad-dûm in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2989](../T/Third_Age_2989.md).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In-universe, the name *Durin* was a word for "king" in the language of the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../M/Men_of_the_North.md" title="Men of the North">Men of the North</a> in the early [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-relations_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relations-15">[13]</a></sup>

In reality, *Durinn* is one of the Dwarfs in the *[Dvergatal](../V/Völuspá.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-IE_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IE-16">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Gould_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gould-17">[15]</a></sup> The name means "Sleepy"<sup id="cite_ref-Gould_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gould-17">[15]</a></sup> or "Sleeper"<sup id="cite_ref-IE_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IE-16">[14]</a></sup>. [Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) noted that "the Sleeper" is a good outer name for the Longbeards to give to their first king, who was prophesied to one day awake again from his sleep.<sup id="cite_ref-IE_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IE-16">[14]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2021: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/6c/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg/150px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="150" height="257" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/6c/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg/225px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/6/6c/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg/300px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Durin_I.jpg 2x" data-file-width="630" data-file-height="1080"/></a><figcaption>Durin I in <i>The Lord of the Rings Online</i></figcaption></figure>

<dl><dd>Durin I appears in several flashbacks telling the history of Gundabad. His early rule was marked by clashes with the servants of <a href="../M/Morgoth.md" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a>, which led Durin to search for a new home for his people to the south. After finding <a href="../M/Mithril.md" title="Mithril">true-silver</a> near <a class="mw-redirect" href="../K/Kheled-zâram.md" title="Kheled-zâram">Kheled-zâram</a>, Durin took the majority of his people there to found <a class="mw-redirect" href="../K/Khazad-dûm.md" title="Khazad-dûm">Khazad-dûm</a>, leaving the rest to be ruled by his closest friend Mótsog in Gundabad. Hearing no word from Durin for many long years, Mótsog grew jealous of him and proclaimed himself King in open rebellion.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Fearing an ambush in Gundabad where secret entrances were known only to Durin himself, Mótsog build for himself a new stronghold to the south, Biriz-zahar. A bloody civil war followed, in which dwarves slew one another. Eventually, Durin himself was captured by Mótsog and put to mockery. His Queen Ragna the Fierce led a daring rescue attempt, which was interrupted when Morgoth saw a chance to destroy the Longbeards at their weakest. <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">Sauron</a> send nine <a class="mw-redirect" href="Dragon.md" title="Dragon">dragons</a> and at least one <a class="mw-redirect" href="../B/Balrog.md" title="Balrog">balrog</a> to assault Biriz-zahar, as well as countless <a class="mw-redirect" href="../O/Orc.md" title="Orc">orcs</a>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Facing extinction, Durin convinced Mótsog to abandon the feud and to fight against the common foe. Through grievous losses, the dwarves won the day, killing five dragons and many thousands of orcs. Durin gave mercy to the few surviving rebels, though as penance they and their descendants were made to shear their beards for many generations after. The fate of Mótsog himself was not known, but it was believed in later ages that Durin slew the traitor.</dd></dl>
