The **Dark Land**, also referred to as the **South Land**, was a continent that lay in south-east [Arda](/wiki/Arda).<sup id="cite_ref-AmbarV_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AmbarV-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The Dark Land was created as a by-product of the [Battle of the Powers](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Powers), in which the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) overthrew [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) in his original fortress of [Utumno](/wiki/Utumno).<sup id="cite_ref-SM293_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM293-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SM305_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM305-3">[3]</a></sup> Originally, [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) was one landmass, set between the western sea of [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer) and the [East Sea](/wiki/East_Sea).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> This changed during the War; the inland [Sea of Ringil](/wiki/Sea_of_Ringil), originally set in the mid-south of Middle-earth, grew in size and "became a great sea flowing north-eastward and joining by straits both the Western and Eastern Seas."<sup id="cite_ref-SM293_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM293-2">[2]</a></sup> This event split Middle-earth into two landmasses, and the landmass to the south and east of the former Sea of Ringil<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup> was known as the Dark Land.<sup id="cite_ref-AmbarV_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AmbarV-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SM305_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM305-3">[3]</a></sup>

No inhabitants of the Dark Land were ever recorded. The Dark Land may have been part of the [Empty Lands](/wiki/Empty_Lands) that [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) "cast back" during the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-LR_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-6">[5]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The Dark Land is named only in an early map by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien"),<sup id="cite_ref-AmbarV_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AmbarV-1">[1]</a></sup> likely dating to the mid-1930s.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

[Fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") have suggested and discussed different theories of inspiration behind the Dark Land:

- the Dark Land as reminiscent of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria_(continent)" title="wikipedia:Lemuria (continent)">Lemuria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>
- the Dark Land as perhaps representing a combination of both Australia and Antarctica (because of its geographic position).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Although never fleshed out in much detail, a &#34;dark continent&#34; called Mórenorë is said to be situated south of Middle-earth, separated by the sea of Haragaer.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> A few glimpses of this remote continent, however, were provided:</dd></dl>

- A black cold-drake, Naikamil, fled from mountains in the south of Endor to Mórenorë after killing her mate.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>
- [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant), a monster of the Elder Days, is said to have "settled in the shadowy reaches of Morenórë, the Dark Continent",<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[note 2]</a></sup> according to tales of the Avari Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup>
- Ninko Goldmaster, a merchant with a shady past appearing as a non-player character in an adventure setting owned shares in ships travelling to legendary Mórenorë.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>

In his work *[Parma Endorion](/wiki/Parma_Endorion)*, [Michael Martinez](/wiki/Michael_Martinez) coined the term *Hyarmenor* to refer to the Dark Land.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[14]</a></sup>
