**Dimrill Dale** was a valley on the east side of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains), beneath the [Redhorn](/wiki/Caradhras "Caradhras"), [Silvertine](/wiki/Celebdil "Celebdil"), and [Cloudyhead](/wiki/Fanuidhol "Fanuidhol") peaks. It was at the eastern end of the [Redhorn Pass](/wiki/Redhorn_Pass) and was bordered by the [Dimrill Stair](/wiki/Dimrill_Stair). Dimrill Dale was the source of the [Silverlode](/wiki/Celebrant "Celebrant") river and at its southeast corner was [Mirrormere](/wiki/Mirrormere), a sacred lake to the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) of [Durin's Folk](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

During the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin" title="Durin">Durin the Deathless</a> wandered south to Dimrill Dale and founded the Kingdom of [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Moria "Moria") beneath the surrounding mountains. Thousands of years later, after Orcs had turned the Dwarven city into the "Black Pit" of [Moria](/wiki/Moria), the climactic battle of the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](/wiki/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs) was fought here. The [Battle of Azanulbizar](/wiki/Battle_of_Azanulbizar), which occurred in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2799](/wiki/Third_Age_2799 "Third Age 2799"),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> it ended as a defeat of the Orcs and their leader [Azog](/wiki/Azog). However, the Dwarves were unable to take back Moria because of the [Balrog](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane "Durin's Bane") that still dwelt there.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[3]</a></sup>

The [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) briefly rested in Dimrill Dale after escaping from Moria, following the loss of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Dimrill* means "overshadowed rills" referring to the rills that ran down the mountain-side.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The Dwarves named the valley **[Azanulbizar](/wiki/Azanulbizar)** in [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul).<sup id="cite_ref-names_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-names-6">[6]</a></sup> This name was lent to the [battle](/wiki/Battle_of_Azanulbizar "Battle of Azanulbizar") fought there.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-3">[3]</a></sup>

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") explored various possibilities regarding the exact interpretation of the elements occurring in the name: it is either *[Azanul](/wiki/Azanul)* + *[bizar](/wiki/Bizar "Bizar")* or <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Uzn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Uzn (page does not exist)">Azan</a>+<a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/%C3%9Bl" title="Ûl">ûl</a>+<a href="/wiki/Bizar" title="Bizar">bizar</a></i>, though the whole was to somehow express "Dimrill Dale". Tolkien stated that "the Common Speech form is an accurate translation: the valley of the dim (overshadowed) rills that ran down the mountainside".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In another point it is given as "Vale of Dim Streams" with three elements.<sup id="cite_ref-RS_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RS-8">[8]</a></sup>

The first interpretation says that *azan* "shadows, dimnesses", *[-ul](/wiki/-ul)* genitive marker and *bizar* "streams, rills"; in this interpretation it means "rills of shadows", and the word "dale" is understood (the full name being *[duban](/wiki/Duban "Duban") Azanulbizar*).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> According to the second interpretation, *bizar* means "dale" and *ul* "rill(s), streams".<sup id="cite_ref-RS_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RS-8">[8]</a></sup>

**Nanduhirion** was the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name for the valley;<sup id="cite_ref-names_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-names-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-10">[10]</a></sup> it contains the elements <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Nan" title="Nan">nan(d)</a></i> ("vale"), *[dû](/wiki/D%C3%BB "Dû")* ("dimness"), *[sîr](/wiki/S%C3%AEr "Sîr")* ("stream") and the ending *[-(i)on](/wiki/-nd "-nd")* ("region" or "land").<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1995: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a> — <a href="/wiki/Mirkwood_(MERP)" title="Mirkwood (MERP)">Mirkwood</a></i>:</b>

It was named Azanulinbar-dûm, often shortened to Azan, and was located near the source of the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Redwater" title="Redwater">Redwater</a>. The dwarves of the <a href="/wiki/Iron_Hills" title="Iron Hills">Iron Hills</a> Garrison have established a camp &#34;Mekhem-bizru&#34; outside the Great Gates, while the Elves deal with the outsiders in &#34;Echad Andestel&#34; outside Lothlorien proper.
