**Déor** was an <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people" title="wikipedia:English people">English</a> [minstrel](/wiki/Minstrels "Minstrels"),<sup id="cite_ref-Min_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Min-1">[1]</a></sup> and the father of [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in the text called *[Ælfwine of England](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine_of_England)* from *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Deo_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deo-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Déor came from somewhere in the south of [Lúthien](/wiki/Luthany "Luthany"), though he also spent some time in [Lionesse](/wiki/Lionesse) where he met his wife, whom he called [Éadgifu](/wiki/%C3%89adgifu).

Later, he moved to the city of [Kortirion](/wiki/Kortirion#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup> ruled by the [Prince of Gwar](/wiki/Prince_of_Gwar) called [Óswine](</wiki/%C3%93swine_(prince_of_Gwar)> "Óswine (prince of Gwar)"), for he wished to be close to the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), who still lived around the city since Óswine was friendly to them. There, Éadgifu gave birth to their son Ælfwine, who inherited her mother's unquenchable longing for the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-Deo_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deo-2">[2]</a></sup>

However, while Ælfwine was still a boy, a fierce people called the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Forodwaith_(The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Forodwaith (The Book of Lost Tales) (page does not exist)">Forodwaith</a> (i.e. <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings" title="wikipedia:Vikings">Vikings</a>) attacked Kortirion and laid siege to it, during which Déor died leading a "desperate sally" before the walls,<sup id="cite_ref-Deo_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Deo-2">[2]</a></sup> slain by [Orm](/wiki/Orm) the sea-captain of the Forodwaith.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Déor* is in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), meaning "brave, bold".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

In one occurrence, the character was called *Heorrenda* before being changed back to *Déor*.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The character of Déor was probably inspired by the Old English poem <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=D%C3%A9or_(poem)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Déor (poem) (page does not exist)">Déor</a></i>, which also features a minstrel as the eponymous main character. However, according to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), the only link between the two characters is their name.<sup id="cite_ref-Min_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Min-1">[1]</a></sup>
