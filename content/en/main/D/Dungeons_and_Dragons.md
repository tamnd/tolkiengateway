<i><b>Dungeons &amp; Dragons</b></i> is a popular fantasy role-playing game that was first published by [TSR](/wiki/TSR) in [1974](/wiki/1974). While unrelated to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s works, it took inspiration from it. Since [1997](/wiki/1997) it is owned and published by [Wizards of the Coast](/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast), who also publish the card game *[Magic: The Gathering](/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering)*.

Branching off from war games as the first published tabletop role-playing game, it paved the way for later games such as *[Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing)*. Until [2013](/wiki/2013), [Dragon Magazine](/wiki/Dragon_Magazine) served as one of its official periodicals.

## Inspiration from Tolkien {#Inspiration_from_Tolkien}

*Dungeons &amp; Dragons*, especially in its early days, borrowed liberally from various sources.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-Dragon95_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dragon95-2">[2]</a></sup> Its use of terms from Tolkien's works specifically came under scrutiny in [1977](/wiki/1977) when TSR was threatened with a lawsuit by Elan Merchandising or [Tolkien Enterprises](/wiki/Tolkien_Enterprises). Their primary objection concerned TSR's board game *[The Battle of the Five Armies](</wiki/Battle_of_the_Five_Armies_(board_game)> "Battle of the Five Armies (board game)")*, though they also targeted terminology within *Dungeons &amp; Dragons*, leading to some name changes in subsequent releases after the suit was settled:

- *[Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits")* to "[halfling](/wiki/Halflings "Halflings")"
- *[Ent](/wiki/Ents "Ents")* to "treant"
- *[Balrog](/wiki/Balrogs "Balrogs")* to "Type VI demon \[balor\]"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Other names, like [Elves](/wiki/Elves), [Half-elves](/wiki/Half-elven "Half-elven"), [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) and [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons), are so common, and not necessarily related to Tolkien's works, that they still stand.

## Adaptations {#Adaptations}

*[Adventures in Middle-earth](/wiki/Adventures_in_Middle-earth)* and its successor *[The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying)* are licensed role-playing games for 5th edition *Dungeons &amp; Dragons*.

Fans also adapted older editions to the setting as the *Eä d20 RPG*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
