**Dunharrow** or the **Hold of Dunharrow** was a fortified refuge of the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) in the [White Mountains](/wiki/White_Mountains)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> south of [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras).<sup id="cite_ref-Map_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Appearance {#Appearance}

Dunharrow was a level upland on the top of a high cliff on the eastern side of the [Harrowdale](/wiki/Harrowdale) hundreds of feet above the floor of the valley. On the other sides the upland was surrounded by three mountains, the [Írensaga](/wiki/%C3%8Drensaga) to the north, the [Starkhorn](/wiki/Starkhorn) to the south and the [Dwimorberg](/wiki/Dwimorberg) to the east.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Muster_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muster-3">[3]</a></sup>

In order to reach the refuge, a steep winding road known as the [Stair of the Hold](/wiki/Stair_of_the_Hold) had to be climbed up a cliff on the eastern side of the Harrowdale, which led through a cutting between walls of rock at the top to the "[Firienfeld](/wiki/Firienfeld)", a large grassy area for the encampment of soldiers and refuge-seekers.<sup id="cite_ref-Muster_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muster-3">[3]</a></sup>

A double row of unshaped standing stones ran over the centre of the Firienfeld from its western side near the top of the road to the east to the [Dimholt](/wiki/Dimholt),<sup id="cite_ref-Muster_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muster-3">[3]</a></sup> a wood of dark trees with a hollow place near the entrance to the [Paths of the Dead](/wiki/Paths_of_the_Dead) below the Dwimorberg.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0a/Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG/300px-Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="492" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/0a/Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG/450px-Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/0a/Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG/600px-Roger_Garland_-_Theoden%27s_army_on_the_road_to_Dunharrow.JPG 2x" data-file-width="739" data-file-height="1211"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Theoden&#39;s army on the road to Dunharrow&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Roger_Garland" title="Roger Garland">Roger Garland</a></figcaption></figure>

Dunharrow was built in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) during in the [Dark Years](/wiki/Dark_Years) before a ship came to the western shores of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and before the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) established the realm of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) by long-forgotten men whose name was lost and not remembered in legends or songs. Later no one in [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) knew if it had been built as a town, a secret temple or a tomb of kings or for some other purpose.<sup id="cite_ref-Muster_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muster-3">[3]</a></sup>

The [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) are known to have carved statues in their own likeness, which could watch over places and could be animated by their crafter to attack invaders in [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> It is mentioned that "no power or terror" was left in the Púkel-men on the winding road up to Dunharrow.<sup id="cite_ref-Muster_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muster-3">[3]</a></sup> At the end of the First Age most of the Drúedain had remained in the White Mountains where they were persecuted by Men who had arrived later from the East who had fallen back into the service of the Dark and the Drúedain were ultimately driven from the White Mountains to the [mountains](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAwaith_Iaur "Drúwaith Iaur") of [Andrast](/wiki/Andrast) and to [woods](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAadan_Forest "Drúadan Forest") in [Anórien](/wiki/An%C3%B3rien).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> As a consequence, it is possible that Dunharrow was built by the Drúedain and that they were driven from there or that they carved the Púkel-men in Dunharrow and that the standing stones and the Paths of the Dead were built by those later arrived Men in the service of the Dark.

When the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) came to the region to search for strong places where they could take refuge in times of dangers, king [Brego](/wiki/Brego) and his son [Baldor](/wiki/Baldor) went up the Stair of the Hold to Dunharrow and talked to an old man who sat on the threshold of the [Dark Door](/wiki/Dark_Door) who told them that the way was made by those who are [Dead](/wiki/Oathbreakers "Oathbreakers"), that is shut and kept by them until the time comes.<sup id="cite_ref-Baldor_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baldor-7">[7]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2569](/wiki/Third_Age_2569 "Third Age 2569") finished the building of [Meduseld](/wiki/Meduseld), the Golden Hall, in [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras). At the opening feast for the new hall<sup id="cite_ref-Baldor_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baldor-7">[7]</a></sup> Baldor vowed that he would walk the Paths of the Dead.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2570](/wiki/Third_Age_2570 "Third Age 2570"), Baldor went to Dunharrow, entered the Dark Door and did not return.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> It is possible that the Rohirrim assumed that Dunharrow could have been a temple, because they called it *Dúnharg* in their own language,<sup id="cite_ref-AppE_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppE-10">[10]</a></sup> which means temple on the hillside.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-11">[11]</a></sup>

When Rohan was invaded by the [Dunlendings](/wiki/Dunlendings) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2758](/wiki/Third_Age_2758 "Third Age 2758") some Rohirrim, including [Fréaláf](/wiki/Fr%C3%A9al%C3%A1f), took refuge in Dunharrow. Soon after the end of the [Long Winter](/wiki/Long_Winter) in [2759](/wiki/Third_Age_2759 "Third Age 2759") Fréaláf and a small company of men led a daring surprise raid from Dunharrow against the Dunlendings in spring during which they recaptured [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras) and killed [Wulf](/wiki/Wulf), the leader of the Dunlendings, in [Meduseld](/wiki/Meduseld).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Some [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) who were driven from the north in the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](/wiki/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs) traversed Rohan and sought refuge in the [White Mountains](/wiki/White_Mountains).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Those invaders were hunted down by [Brytta Léofa](/wiki/Brytta_L%C3%A9ofa). However some were seen still lingering near Dunharrow and killed King [Walda](/wiki/Walda) and his companions when they rode by mountain paths from Dunharrow in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2851](/wiki/Third_Age_2851 "Third Age 2851").<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

[Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli), [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas), and the [Grey Company](/wiki/Grey_Company) arrived at Dunharrow on [7 March](/wiki/7_March) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019") and then left at dawn the next day to take the Paths of the Dead. [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden) came to Dunharrow with his riders and [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") on [9 March](/wiki/9_March) and rode out for [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) on [the 10th](/wiki/10_March "10 March").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Dunharrow* is the modernized form of [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) ([Old English](/wiki/Old_English)) *Dúnharg*<sup id="cite_ref-AppE_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppE-10">[10]</a></sup> (or *Dūnhaerg*), which means "the heathen fane on the hillside".<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-11">[11]</a></sup> A fane is an archaic word meaning temple.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

The first element of the name is probably *dún* ("mountain", "hill").<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> The second element of the name is *haerg* ("temple"<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>), which was modernized as *harrow*, because it exists as an element in English place-names, such as *Harrow (on the Hill)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/ab/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="263" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/ab/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg/450px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/a/ab/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg/600px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Dunharrow.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="878"/></a><figcaption><i>Dunharrow</i> by <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a> (obsolete drawing)</figcaption></figure>

In an earlier draft version of the chapter [The King of the Golden Hall](/wiki/The_King_of_the_Golden_Hall) the name *Dunberg* was used for the refuge where the people of Rohan may long defend themselves, which was later called Dunharrow.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

In earlier versions of the chapter [The Muster of Rohan](/wiki/The_Muster_of_Rohan) the name Dunharrow was used as the name of the mountain, which was later called Starkhorn.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> In the first version there was a natural amphitheatre on the western side of the mountain and there were caves in the mountain, which had been made by forgotten men as their dwelling and holy place who had vanished and gone away and mingled with the people of Dunland and Lebennin. It is also mentioned that some folk, dark with grey eyes, in whose veins flowed the blood of the forgotten men still lived at Dunharrow.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> In a later version this concept is retained, a long forgotten folk, which had vanished and gone far away had dwelt there and made a dark temple.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> In the next version the it is mentioned that the ancient men of Dunharrow whose name was lost had not served Sauron and made a refuge that no enemy could conquer there.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> In the following version the Rohirrim only found one old man who spoke in a strange language that they could not understand when they came first to Dunharrow and wondered where the rest of his people were.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> In those versions the caves including a torchlit hall for feasts were later used by the Rohirrim.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

Tolkien had made an illustration of Dunharrow and the [Firienfeld](/wiki/Firienfeld), that was first published in *[The Lord of the Rings 1977 Calendar](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1977_Calendar)*. That illustration represented an earlier conception of the [Dark Door](/wiki/Dark_Door), that didn't fit the description in the published text. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) noted that the [Dimholt](/wiki/Dimholt) and the pillar of stone are absent. Tolkien himself had added the note 'No longer fits story'.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>

In a later version of the chapter the two lines of standing stones led to a dark wood called the *Firienholt* that climbed on the sides of a hill called the *Firien* with a huge doorway and caverns in the side of the hill, which no one had dared to enter in living memory.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> In an outline for book V the name *Firgen* is mentioned in brackets after *Firien* for the hill and the name *Halifirien* is provided as an alternative name. Dunharrow is the name of a cave in the hill and that cave is said to be a *haliern*.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> A *haliern* (*hálig-ern*) is an Old English word for a holy place or a sanctuary.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>

Tolkien made two other drawings of Dunharrow , which were published in [Sauron Defeated](/wiki/Sauron_Defeated) and also represented earlier conceptions that do not fit the description in the published text.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>
