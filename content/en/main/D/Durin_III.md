**Durin III** (fl. [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1600s](/wiki/Second_Age_1600 "Second Age 1600")) was the [King of Durin's folk](/wiki/King_of_Durin%27s_folk) who ruled the great [Dwarf](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves")-city of [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Khazad-d%C3%BBm) at the time of Sauron's [assault](/wiki/Sack_of_Eregion "Sack of Eregion") on [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion).

## History {#History}

Like all Durins after <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin" title="Durin">Durin I</a>, he was given the name of the first [Father of the Dwarves](/wiki/Seven_Fathers_of_the_Dwarves "Seven Fathers of the Dwarves") because he greatly resembled him in both appearance and manner. Indeed it was believed among the Dwarves that he was the reincarnation of Durin I, though whether this is possible is unclear.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-2">[2]</a></sup>

During his reign in the 16th century of the Second Age, the [Dwarves of Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Khazad-d%C3%BBm) had struck a rare friendship with the [Elves of Eregion](/wiki/Elves_of_Eregion). Around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1500](/wiki/Second_Age_1500 "Second Age 1500"), the Elves, guided by [Annatar](/wiki/Annatar), began forging the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> According to the Dwarves, [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor) and the Elven-smiths gave Durin the first of the [Seven Rings](/wiki/Seven_Rings) of the Dwarves (other traditions state that [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor) was said to have yielded the Seven - all the Seven - to [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) after torture and it was Sauron who gifted the ring to Durin).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-2">[2]</a></sup>

Their instructor in crafting the Rings, the wise [Annatar](/wiki/Annatar), was actually [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) who had come to corrupt the Elves through the Rings of Power. By [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1697](/wiki/Second_Age_1697 "Second Age 1697"), seeing that he could not control either the Elves nor the Dwarves through the Rings, he led a large army towards Eregion. In response, Durin sent a great force of Dwarves to the aid of [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor), but with the [fall of Eregion](/wiki/Sack_of_Eregion "Sack of Eregion"), his warriors were driven back and the [Doors of Durin](/wiki/Doors_of_Durin) were shut. Their actions were not in vain for it was by the aid of Durin that [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) was able to escape and establish the refuge of [Imladris](/wiki/Imladris) in the North.<sup id="cite_ref-Galadriel_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galadriel-3">[3]</a></sup>

Sauron's assault on Eregion was only the beginning of his campaign of conquest. His servants also invaded [Gundabad](/wiki/Gundabad) and the [Ered Mithrin](/wiki/Ered_Mithrin), cutting off communications between Durin III and the [Iron Hills](/wiki/Iron_Hills). Consequently, the [Alliance of Dwarves and Men](/wiki/Alliance_of_Dwarves_and_Men) came to an end during his reign.<sup id="cite_ref-relations_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relations-1">[1]</a></sup> Although [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion) was conquered, Durin III's people were too numerous and valiant, and his halls too deep and strong, to be taken by Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

One account states that the name *Durin* was a word for "king" in the language of the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Men_of_the_North" title="Men of the North">Men of the North</a> in the early [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-relations_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relations-1">[1]</a></sup>

Another account states that *Durinn* is one of the Dwarfs in the *[Dvergatal](/wiki/V%C3%B6lusp%C3%A1 "Völuspá")*. The name means "Sleepy".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>In the prologue, Durin III appears with the other six Lords receiving their <a href="/wiki/Seven_Rings" title="Seven Rings">Rings</a>. He is only identified as Durin on a <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Decipher_Card" title="Decipher Card">Decipher Card</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2021: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online:_Fate_of_Gundabad" title="The Lord of the Rings Online: Fate of Gundabad">The Lord of the Rings Online: Fate of Gundabad</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Durin III briefly appears in a flashback in the Second-age, sealing the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/West-gate" title="West-gate">West-gate</a> following the <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Eregion" title="Sack of Eregion">Sack of Eregion</a>. He also was noted to have convened the Fourth Dwarrowmoot of the Seven Houses at Mount Gundabad. The name of his Ring of Power is given as Angya, or Handórm in the dwarven tongue.</dd></dl>

<b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>King Durin III is portrayed by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Mullan" title="Peter Mullan">Peter Mullan</a> in the Amazon series. He is depicted as the king of <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Khazad-d%C3%BBm" title="Khazad-dûm">Khazad-dûm</a> and father to <a href="/wiki/Durin_IV" title="Durin IV">Prince Durin IV</a>. His first appearance in the series occurs during the <a href="/wiki/Adrift" title="Adrift">second episode</a>, when he speaks with <a href="/wiki/Elrond" title="Elrond">Elrond</a> who is visiting Khazad-dûm.</dd></dl>
