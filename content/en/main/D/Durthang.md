**Durthang** was an "old castle", by the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) "now one of the many [orc-holds](/wiki/Orc-holds "Orc-holds")" around the valley of [Udûn](</wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)> "Udûn (valley)").<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup> It stood "a few miles north" of [Carach Angren](/wiki/Carach_Angren) in the northern [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath) "high up" above the valley of [Udûn](</wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)> "Udûn (valley)") in the angle where a mountain spur branched away<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup> south-east to Carach Angren<sup id="cite_ref-map_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-map-2">[2]</a></sup>.

A road with "no wall or parapet along the edge"<sup id="cite_ref-begin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-begin-3">[3]</a></sup> ran "winding down from" Durthang and then turned east "along a shelf cut in the" southern side of the mountain spur, down into the [Plateau of Gorgoroth](/wiki/Plateau_of_Gorgoroth)<sup id="cite_ref-map_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-map-2">[2]</a></sup> and along the feet of the mountain spur on to Carach Angren.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

It is possible that Durthang was "originally a fortress of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor)"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-sauron_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sauron-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Foster_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foster-6">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-thainsbook_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thainsbook-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> that was built by "the road-builders of old" who made the road that led from Durthang to [Carach Angren](/wiki/Carach_Angren) in [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor)<sup id="cite_ref-built_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-11">[10]</a></sup>. The [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) had constructed several fortresses overlooking the entrance to Mordor in the aftermath of the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance) like the [Towers of the Teeth](/wiki/Towers_of_the_Teeth)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup> or the [Tower of Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Tower_of_Cirith_Ungol).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup> After the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) devastated Gondor in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1636](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636"),<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup> the watch on Mordor was neglected until, by [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1640](/wiki/Third_Age_1640 "Third Age 1640"),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> "the fortresses that guarded the passes were unmanned" because of a lack of troops.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> It is possible that this included Durthang.<sup id="cite_ref-sauron_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sauron-5">[4]</a></sup> It is possible that Durthang was later reoccupied and manned by troops from Gondor,<sup id="cite_ref-sauron_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sauron-5">[4]</a></sup> because in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1944](/wiki/Third_Age_1944 "Third Age 1944") when king [Ondoher](/wiki/Ondoher) of Gondor and both his sons fell in a battle "north of the [Morannon](/wiki/Black_Gate "Black Gate")"<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> by the [Wainriders](/wiki/Wainriders),<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> the Morannon and the Towers of the Teeth were still manned by troops from Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1980](/wiki/Third_Age_1980 "Third Age 1980") when the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) came to Mordor and gathered the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) there,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> it is possible that Orcs<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup> occupied Durthang.<sup id="cite_ref-sauron_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sauron-5">[4]</a></sup> At the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) Durthang was an [Orc-hold](/wiki/Orc-holds "Orc-holds").<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup>

On Sunday,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> [18 March](/wiki/18_March) of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-march_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-march-22">[21]</a></sup> [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) caught a glimpse of Durthang when they came to the road that led away from "that orc-tower" to the Isenmouthe.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup> After resting "under overhanging stone in sight of Durthang"<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup> until that evening,<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup> Frodo and Samwise began to head down the road, which they found was not very broad.<sup id="cite_ref-begin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-begin-3">[3]</a></sup> After traveling for twelve miles and deciding to take a rest for a few minutes,<sup id="cite_ref-begin_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-begin-3">[3]</a></sup> a company of Orcs "going at a great pace" from Durthang<sup id="cite_ref-journey_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-25">[24]</a></sup> passed them by.<sup id="cite_ref-built_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-11">[10]</a></sup> When one of the two "large fierce *uruks*" that lead them mistook the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) for deserters from the garrison at [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol), the [slave-driver](/wiki/Slave-drivers "Slave-drivers") forced them to march along with the company to the Black Gate.<sup id="cite_ref-built_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-11">[10]</a></sup> On [19 March](/wiki/19_March),<sup id="cite_ref-march_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-march-22">[21]</a></sup> after marching for "some miles" eastward near [Udûn](</wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)> "Udûn (valley)") in Mordor, they came to a "road-meeting" where "several companies came together" and clashed, each trying to get to the Black Gate first.<sup id="cite_ref-journey_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-25">[24]</a></sup> When "a troop of heavy-armed *uruks* from Barad-dûr charged into the [Durthang line](/wiki/Durthang_line)", Samwise saw the chance for him and Frodo to slip away in the confusion.<sup id="cite_ref-journey_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-25">[24]</a></sup> Between [25 March](/wiki/25_March)<sup id="cite_ref-march_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-march-22">[21]</a></sup> and [8 April](/wiki/8_April),<sup id="cite_ref-april_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-april-26">[25]</a></sup> some of the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Host_of_the_West" title="Host of the West">Host of the West</a><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup> ventured "into Mordor and destroyed the fortresses in the north of the land".<sup id="cite_ref-Field_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Field-28">[27]</a></sup> It is possible that Durthang was one of these fortresses, but its exact fate remains unknown.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Durthang* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin)<sup id="cite_ref-thang_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thang-29">[28]</a></sup> for "Dark press or oppression",<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup> from *[dûr](/wiki/D%C3%BBr "Dûr")* ("dark")<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup> + *[thang](/wiki/Thang "Thang")* ("oppression").<sup id="cite_ref-thang_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thang-29">[28]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The name *Durthang* first appeared as written alongside *Carach Angren* in the original draft of "[The Land of Shadow](/wiki/The_Land_of_Shadow)" chapter, to which [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) commented that, though the draft is "partly illegible", much of "the landscape was perfectly clear" in [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s eyes.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup> In this draft, Durthang was about "4 miles" away from the Isenmouthe and the stony road that lead from it "turned away somewhat northward" instead of east.<sup id="cite_ref-stony_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stony-33">[32]</a></sup> In the second draft, Durthang was described as being "perhaps ten miles" from the Isenmouthe and that the road descended "swiftly from Durthang" before leveling out to run "under the ridge" to the Isenmouthe.<sup id="cite_ref-stony_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stony-33">[32]</a></sup> This distance remained in the text "as late as the printer's typescript" before being changed to twenty miles.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup> In a struck-through part of *[The Chronology of The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Chronology_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Tolkien wrote that Frodo and Samwise slept "under stone in sight of Durthang" on [17 March](/wiki/17_March).<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup> In draft notes on the chronology, Tolkien originally wrote that Frodo and Samwise saw Durthang on 17 March and took "the road from Durthang" on the "night of March 17-18" before joining "the orc-company".<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[35]</a></sup> Tolkien changed this to that of the published version.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[36]</a></sup> Around [1948](/wiki/1948), Tolkien drew a <a class="external text" href="https://jrrtestate.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/710tiff108mb.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">draft</a> [map of Rohan, Gondor, and Mordor](/wiki/Map_of_Rohan,_Gondor,_and_Mordor "Map of Rohan, Gondor, and Mordor") while writing Book Five of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*;<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[37]</a></sup> this map contains a symbol for Durthang in the same location as on the re-drawn version by Christopher Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-artist_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artist-39">[38]</a></sup> It also seems to contain a faint label Durthang in the valley of Udûn to the right of its location.<sup id="cite_ref-artist_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artist-39">[38]</a></sup> In a draft of [Appendix B](/wiki/Appendix_B), Tolkien wrote that, after Sauron was defeated, the Host of the West entered Mordor and destroyed every Orc-hold.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[39]</a></sup> This likely included Durthang because it was described as an Orc-hold in "[The Land of Shadow](/wiki/The_Land_of_Shadow)" chapter of *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b><a href="/wiki/1987" title="1987">1987</a>-<a href="/wiki/1996" title="1996">96</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the <i><a href="/wiki/Gates_of_Mordor_(book)" title="Gates of Mordor (book)">Gates of Mordor</a></i> adventure module it is mentioned that Durthang was built by the Dúnedain of Gondor at the beginning of the Third Age after the fall of Sauron to keep evil things from re-entering Mordor and to monitor the interior of Mordor. It is mentioned that the Gondorian troops abandoned Durthang after the plague and that it was afterwards occupied by the immortal demonic werewolf Gaurhir and a garrison of orcs. The castle of Durthang consists of five interconnected towers with its spire surpassing a height of 200 feet. Durthang consists of a ground floor, three levels and caverns below the ground floor.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In the <a href="/wiki/Gorgoroth_(book)" title="Gorgoroth (book)">Gorgoroth</a> campaign module it is described that Sauron called the Ringwraith Hoarmurath of Dír to Mordor in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1640" title="Third Age 1640">1640</a> and that he met with seven of the other Ringwraiths except the Witch-king at the castle of Ostigurth and then occupied the abandoned castle Durthang and resided there until <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2000" title="Third Age 2000">2000</a> when he joined the other Ringwraiths in the siege of Minas Ithil. <figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/35/Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg/200px-Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/35/Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg/300px-Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/3/35/Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg/400px-Shadow_of_Mordor_-_Durthang.jpg 2x" data-file-width="505" data-file-height="370"/></a><figcaption>Durthang - <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor">Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor</a></i></figcaption></figure>

<dl><dd>Durthang is one of the strongholds in Udûn and is separated into four regions.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[45]</a></sup> In the bow quest, &#34;Shadows Under Siege&#34;, Durthang is described as having &#34;<a href="/wiki/Iron" title="Iron">iron</a> walls&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[46]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The <b>Durthang West</b> region has a large bridge, beneath which lies the ninth <a href="/wiki/Ithildin" title="Ithildin">Ithildin</a> rune: <i>i achortha ivorn</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-ithildin_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ithildin-48">[47]</a></sup> The Durthang West region also has a deep cave which contains the ninth artifact: the Serpentine Blade.<sup id="cite_ref-artifact_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artifact-49">[48]</a></sup> The Serpentine Blade was a deadly <a href="/wiki/Orkish" title="Orkish">Orcish</a> shiv dating to <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2942" title="Third Age 2942">2942</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-blade_50-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blade-50">[49]</a></sup> In one particular instance, an Uruk used it to stage a coup, probably in Durthang since it was found there, and rebel against the <a href="/wiki/Black_Hand" title="Black Hand">Black Hand</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-blade_50-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blade-50">[49]</a></sup> When Talion finds it, he ponders how Men can &#34;hope to stand against&#34; such hate-filled Uruks who are all too willing to backstab each other.<sup id="cite_ref-blade_50-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blade-50">[49]</a></sup> To this, <a href="/wiki/Celebrimbor" title="Celebrimbor">Celebrimbor</a> proclaims that in teaching &#34;the meaning of fear&#34; to the Uruks, they &#34;will give hope to Middle-earth&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-blade_50-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blade-50">[49]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The <b>Durthang Keep</b> is a giant ruin in which the northeast tower contains the tenth Ithildin rune: <i>Achared</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-ithildin_48-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ithildin-48">[47]</a></sup> The northwestern wing of the Durthang Keep region has a small cell containing the tenth artifact: the Rusted Horn.<sup id="cite_ref-artifact_49-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artifact-49">[48]</a></sup> The Rusted Horn was a <a href="/wiki/Mannish" title="Mannish">Mannish</a> horn dating to approximately <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Age_3430" title="Second Age 3430">3430</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-horn_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-horn-51">[50]</a></sup> In one particular instance, it was blown to herald a &#34;great victory&#34; during the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad" title="Battle of Dagorlad">Battle of Dagorlad</a> &#34;in defiance of the <a href="/wiki/Mouth_of_Sauron" title="Mouth of Sauron">Mouth of Sauron</a>&#34; and Sauron&#39;s &#34;assembled army of more than three hundred thousand Orcs&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-horn_51-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-horn-51">[50]</a></sup> It also &#34;serenaded the fallen dead...forever doomed to haunt the <a href="/wiki/Dead_Marshes" title="Dead Marshes">Dead Marshes</a>&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-horn_51-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-horn-51">[50]</a></sup> When Talion finds it, he reflected that, when <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Dirhael" title="Dirhael">Dirhael</a> once claimed &#34;that he saw ghosts on the Dead Marshes&#34;, he did not believe him.<sup id="cite_ref-horn_51-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-horn-51">[50]</a></sup> To this, Celebrimbor stated that Mordor summoned all the dead.<sup id="cite_ref-horn_51-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-horn-51">[50]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The <b>Durthang Outskirts</b> region contains a large bridge connecting it to the Durthang Keep region; beneath it is the eleventh Ithildin rune: <i>puigadol dagor</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-ithildin_48-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ithildin-48">[47]</a></sup> The Durthang Outskirts region also has another ruin which contains a small grotto with the twelfth artifact: the Grog Bowl.<sup id="cite_ref-artifact_49-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artifact-49">[48]</a></sup> The Grog Bowl is an Orcish bowl made &#34;in huge kilns&#34; from &#34;metal or wood&#34; dating to the year <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2942" title="Third Age 2942">2942</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bowl_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bowl-52">[51]</a></sup> To combat &#34;the old soldier&#39;s disease&#34;, it is beaten into the Uruks by their trainers &#34;to keep their grog bowls as clean as possible&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-bowl_52-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bowl-52">[51]</a></sup> When Talion finds it, he comments that, though it is harsh, the grog of the Uruks is effective medicine.<sup id="cite_ref-bowl_52-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bowl-52">[51]</a></sup> To this, Celebrimbor laments that Uruks truly were twisted parodies of his kin.<sup id="cite_ref-bowl_52-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bowl-52">[51]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The <b>Durthang Wastes</b> has a large ruin which contains a small cell with the twelfth Ithildin rune: <i>Tridaeol chith efuin</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-ithildin_48-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ithildin-48">[47]</a></sup> The Durthang Wastes region also has a cave which contains the eleventh artifact beyond a cracked wall: the Graven Idol Representing <a href="/wiki/Morgoth" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-artifact_49-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artifact-49">[48]</a></sup> The Graven Idol Representing Morgoth is a <a href="/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans" title="Númenóreans">Númenórean</a> artifact dating to approximately <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Age_3318" title="Second Age 3318">3318</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-idol_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-idol-53">[52]</a></sup> The Graven Idol Representing Morgoth &#34;was likely&#34; taken to Middle-earth by missionaries who sought to spread Morgoth&#39;s &#34;vile message&#34; and find victims for the &#34;sacrifices to strange gods&#34; held by &#34;priests and dark acolytes&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-idol_53-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-idol-53">[52]</a></sup> In one particular instance, a Númenórean criticized <a href="/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n" title="Ar-Pharazôn">Pharazôn</a> for giving them war and taxes after being sold many goods, but blamed it on the &#34;hideous thing&#34; of the Graven Idol Representing Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-idol_53-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-idol-53">[52]</a></sup> The Númenórean then mocked &#34;the <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">great chained wonder</a>&#34; to another Númenórean before joining the <a href="/wiki/Faithful" title="Faithful">Faithful</a> in trying to leave <a href="/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor" title="Númenor">Númenor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-idol_53-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-idol-53">[52]</a></sup> When Talion finds it, he notes that it depicts the first <a href="/wiki/Dark_Lord" title="Dark Lord">Dark Lord</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-idol_53-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-idol-53">[52]</a></sup> To this, Celebrimber adds that Sauron was his loyal servant.<sup id="cite_ref-idol_53-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-idol-53">[52]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="/wiki/2017" title="2017">2017</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_War" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of War">Middle-earth: Shadow of War</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Durthang appears on the world map in <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_War" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of War">Middle-earth: Shadow of War</a></i>, but is not visitable. However, it is the fortress of Udûn in <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_War_-_The_Mobile_Game&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of War - The Mobile Game (page does not exist)">Middle-earth: Shadow of War - The Mobile Game</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[53]</a></sup> In the ninth quest of the game, &#34;The Valley of Hell&#34;, Celebrimbor states that the Uruks of Durthang belong to the Dark Tribe.<sup id="cite_ref-questnine_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-questnine-55">[54]</a></sup> When one of the Nazgûl fled Minas Morgul, he took an unspecified <a href="/wiki/Ithil-stone" title="Ithil-stone">Relic</a> with him to the fortress of Durthang in Udûn.<sup id="cite_ref-questnine_55-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-questnine-55">[54]</a></sup> The Dark Tribe had reinforced Durthang since Talion and Celebrimbor were last there ten years before.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[55]</a></sup> In the eleventh quest of the game, &#34;The Skirmish&#34;, <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Baranor" title="Baranor">Baranor</a> states that Durthang was once an outpost of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">[56]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>It was originally called <b>Durthand</b>, and was the stronghold of an order known as the Thandrim. In a year, the sixty survivors surrendered to Ugrukhôr, one of Sauron&#39;s chief lieutenants, in exchange for safe passage through the Black Gate. Ugrukhôr let the Thandrim leave Mordor, only to have archers slay them all as soon as they passed the Black Gate, save one he spared to report these events to Gondor: Halannon the Survivor.</dd></dl>
