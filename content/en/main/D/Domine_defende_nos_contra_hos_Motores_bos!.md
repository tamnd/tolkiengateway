<i><b>Domine defende nos contra hos Motores bos!</b></i> is a shared title of a poem "in thirty-one octosyllabic couplets"<sup id="cite_ref-Other_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Other-2">[2]</a></sup> and a [Latin](../L/Latin.md) text that were written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) for *[The End of Bovadium](../T/The_End_of_Bovadium.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-3">[3]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Poem excerpt {#Poem_excerpt}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p><span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">Alas! that prayer was never heard:</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">Jove’s ears, maybe, were rendered surd;</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">for Motors prolific bred and teemed;</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">from near and far to Town they streamed,</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">hunting their prey with hooting shrill</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">from Iffley turn to Hinksey Hill.</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">Their stink above the steeples went;</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">stones were shaken and ears were rent</span><br/>
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">by din of wheels and engine-blare.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup></span>
</p>
</div>

## Background {#Background}

While it is untold when exactly Tolkien first wrote the poem "in thirty-one octosyllabic couplets" and its related [Latin](../L/Latin.md) text, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) speculated that the Latin text followed the poem, but was unsure how they both relate to *[The End of Bovadium](../T/The_End_of_Bovadium.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-CGL_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGL-3">[3]</a></sup> since they share details with earlier drafts.<sup id="cite_ref-Other_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Other-2">[2]</a></sup> In a book review by the <a class="external text" href="https://www.tolkienguide.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tolkien Collector&#39;s Guide</a>, it is suggested that Tolkien may have had the poem *From Iffley*, an early version of *[Valedictory](../V/Valedictory.md)*,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> in mind when writing *Domine defende nos contra hos Motores bos!* due to the mention of "from Iffley" in the poem. They noted in the review that "it is a stark change from the timelessness of the former to the smoke fumes of the" latter.<sup id="cite_ref-guidereview_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guidereview-1">[1]</a></sup>
