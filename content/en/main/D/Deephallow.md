**Deephallow** was a village on [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")'s eastern border, built on the banks of the River [Brandywine](/wiki/Brandywine), just north to the point where [Shirebourn](/wiki/Shirebourn) flowed into the river. Immediately across Brandywine was [Haysend](/wiki/Haysend), the southernmost point of [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) noted that "is not clear in etymology (not meant to be - not all names are!)".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

[David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) suggested that the name represents a speculative [Old Hobbitish](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") form \**Deophealh* "deep <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/haugh" title="wiktionary:haugh">haugh</a>". A haugh meant first "angle, corner, secluded place" and later "land by a river", consistent to the location of the village near the wedge formed by Brandywine and Shirebourn.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The same element is also seen in "[Woodhall](/wiki/Woodhall)".

## Other versions of the Legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_Legendarium}

Although Deephallow is never mentioned in the text, there was a mention of it in a draft of *[A Short Cut to Mushrooms](/wiki/A_Short_Cut_to_Mushrooms)*, as the place where the [Causeway](/wiki/Causeway) leads.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> <span></span>
