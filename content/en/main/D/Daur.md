<i><b>daur</b></i> means "a stop or pause" in [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-N9_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N9-1">[1]</a></sup>

The word also referred to a [Númenórean](../N/Númenor.md) linear measure equivalent to 5000 *[rangar](../R/Ranga.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> (See *[lár](../L/Lár.md)* for more details.)

## *Daur* in the Song of Praise {#Daur_in_the_Song_of_Praise}

The Sindarin word <i><b>Daur</b></i> appears in the [song of praise](../L/Long_live_the_Halflings!_Praise_them_with_great_praise!.md) of [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) and [Sam](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) at the [Field of Cormallen](../F/Field_of_Cormallen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

It has been suggested that *daur* is the [lenited](../L/Lenition.md) form of *[taur](../T/Taur.md#Noldorin)* ("lofty, noble" or "king"),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> however the reliability of this speculation has been contested as the syntax of the phrase does not seem to require lenition.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

When analyzing the song of praise, Tolkien derived *Daur* from *dāra* "wise" with a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) form *tāra*. This replaces an earlier version from *ndāra* with [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *nāra*. He also queried the possibility of *daur* coming from lenition of base *t*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
