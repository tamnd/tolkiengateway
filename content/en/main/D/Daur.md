<i><b>daur</b></i> means "a stop or pause" in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin).<sup id="cite_ref-N9_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N9-1">[1]</a></sup>

The word also referred to a [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor "Númenor") linear measure equivalent to 5000 *[rangar](/wiki/Ranga "Ranga")*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> (See *[lár](/wiki/L%C3%A1r "Lár")* for more details.)

## *Daur* in the Song of Praise {#Daur_in_the_Song_of_Praise}

The Sindarin word <i><b>Daur</b></i> appears in the [song of praise](/wiki/Long_live_the_Halflings!_Praise_them_with_great_praise! "Long live the Halflings! Praise them with great praise!") of [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") at the [Field of Cormallen](/wiki/Field_of_Cormallen).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

It has been suggested that *daur* is the [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") form of *[taur](/wiki/Taur#Noldorin "Taur")* ("lofty, noble" or "king"),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> however the reliability of this speculation has been contested as the syntax of the phrase does not seem to require lenition.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

When analyzing the song of praise, Tolkien derived *Daur* from *dāra* "wise" with a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) form *tāra*. This replaces an earlier version from *ndāra* with [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *nāra*. He also queried the possibility of *daur* coming from lenition of base *t*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
