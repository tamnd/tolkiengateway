The <b><i>Diplomatarium Islandicum</i> manuscripts</b> refers to [unpublished notes](../../index/U/Unpublished_material.md) by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) inserted in his copy of *[Diplomatarium Islandicum](../L/List_of_books_in_Tolkien's_library.md#Diplomatarium_Islandicum)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Rare_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rare-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Transcription {#Transcription}

"The appearance of the \_Ethiopians\_ at this point in the account is clearly, in part at least, due to a comparison of Psalm lxxiii with the book of Exodus. It is probable too that v. 16 of the psalm ("The day is thine, the night also is thine: thou hast prepared the light and the sun") was brought into relation with the mention, in Exod. xiii 21, of the pillars of cloud and fire immediately after \_Etham\_.

\_byrig\_ is probably plural, as noted above: 'the cities of the region of Etham'. See quot. in note p. 32. In any case, of course, \_Etham\_ (\_Æthan\_) is taken not as a city or place, but a region; \_Æthanes byrig\_ in O.E. does not mean 'the city of Etham', where city = Etham, but 'the city belonging to or in Etham'.

67 \_mearelandum ón\_ : a good example of a manuscript accent indicating emphasis and stress (here properly borne by the 'postponed' \_on\_ in archaic word order) and not quantity. The words render \_in extremis finibus solitudinis\_. \[Vulgate Exodus 13:20\]
