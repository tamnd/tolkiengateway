**Dor-en-Ernil** was a region in the south of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

Though its boundaries are not clearly established, it appears to have been part of - or perhaps synonymous with - the mountainous coastland fief known as [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> or the wider sense of [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Extending out from its western shores was a smaller mountainous peninsula on which was built Dol Amroth,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> the stronghold of the [Princes of Dol Amroth](/wiki/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Dor-en-Ernil* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name. It means "Land of the Prince"<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> analyzed as *[dor](/wiki/Dor "Dor")* ("land"), *[en](/wiki/En "En")* ("of") and *[ernil](/wiki/Ernil "Ernil")* ("prince").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The context in which the name was used suggests that the name referred to the [Princes of Dol Amroth](/wiki/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-Amroth14_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amroth14-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes that the name appears only in the [Map of Rohan, Gondor, and Mordor](/wiki/Map_of_Rohan,_Gondor,_and_Mordor), the label being on the other side of the mountains from Dol Amroth.<sup id="cite_ref-Amroth14_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amroth14-8">[8]</a></sup> The name does not appear in the [General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth). Christopher Tolkien put the label on the same location, but among the wider context in the newer map that he drew for the book *[Unfinished Tales of Númenor and Middle-earth](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales "Unfinished Tales")*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>Dor-en-Ernil was an area in the region of Central Gondor, located south of the <a href="/wiki/Ringl%C3%B3_Vale" title="Ringló Vale">Ringló Vale</a> and west of Lower <a href="/wiki/Lebennin" title="Lebennin">Lebennin</a>. During the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Ring" title="War of the Ring">War of the Ring</a>, it was attacked by <a href="/wiki/Corsairs_of_Umbar" title="Corsairs of Umbar">Corsairs of Umbar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Haradrim" title="Haradrim">Haradrim</a>. The fishing village of &#34;Gaerlond&#34; and the inland village of &#34;Tungobel&#34; were both burned, the lighthouse of &#34;Barad Rill&#34; was toppled, and the <a href="/wiki/Warning_beacons_of_Gondor" title="Warning beacons of Gondor">beacon</a> &#34;Dol Brannor&#34; was seized by the Enemy. The river <a href="/wiki/Gilrain" title="Gilrain">Gilrain</a> and the city of <a href="/wiki/Linhir" title="Linhir">Linhir</a> formed the eastern border. The hills to the west, between Dor-en-Ernil and the Havens of <a href="/wiki/Belfalas" title="Belfalas">Belfalas</a> were mostly inaccessible but were identified in dialogue as the &#34;Emyn Ernil&#34;.</dd></dl>
