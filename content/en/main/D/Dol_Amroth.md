**Dol Amroth** was a promontory, ruled as a hereditary princedom from its stronghold, situated on a peninsula in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) facing the [Bay of Belfalas](../B/Bay_of_Belfalas.md).

The emblem of the Prince of Dol Amroth was a silver-upon-blue banner, bearing a <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ship_and_the_Silver_Swan&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ship and the Silver Swan (page does not exist)">ship with a swan-prow</a> upon the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Description {#Description}

Its northern shore defined part of [Cobas Haven](../C/Cobas_Haven.md), the small bay into which the [Morthond](../M/Morthond.md) River flowed.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Upon the headland the [Princes of Dol Amroth](../P/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth.md) established a castle and thus Dol Amroth referred to this stronghold and to the neighbouring port-city, the chief city of the fief of [Belfalas](../B/Belfalas.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Minas_Tirith_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Minas_Tirith-4">[4]</a></sup> Within the walls of the city was the [Sea-ward Tower](../S/Sea-ward_Tower.md) or Tirith Aear,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> which had a bell that was rung for the benefit of mariners.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The first known settlers of [Belfalas](../B/Belfalas.md) were [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md) refugees from [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) in the late [First Age](../F/First_Age.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Amroth_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amroth-7">[7]</a></sup> and in the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) (after the [War of the Elves and Sauron](../W/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron.md)) the headland that would become Dol Amroth was settled by [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) and [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md); their company was increased by [Elves from Lórinand](../E/Elves_of_Lórinand.md).<sup id="cite_ref-GalCel_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GalCel-8">[8]</a></sup>

A noble [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) family of the [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md) settled in Belfalas before the Downfall and built a stronghold on the promontory. When the [Realms in Exile](../R/Realms_in_Exile.md) were formed, [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) gave their ruler the title of *[Prince](../P/Prince.md)*, and his realm became known as **[Dor-en-Ernil](Dor-en-Ernil.md)**.<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Amroth](../A/Amroth.md), the former [King of Lórien](../K/King_of_Lórien.md), was lost in the [Bay of Belfalas](../B/Bay_of_Belfalas.md) near the headland in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1981](../T/Third_Age_1981.md), and around that time the last Elves departed it. The promontory was now known as Dol Amroth, named as such after the lost King (the "Hill of Amroth").<sup id="cite_ref-GalCel_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GalCel-8">[8]</a></sup> The Númenóreans now occupied the city, which now probably belonged to Dor-en-Ernil,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and its first ruler became [Galador](../G/Galador.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-1">[1]</a></sup> Indeed, according to the tradition of his [house](../H/House_of_Dol_Amroth.md), [Galador](../G/Galador.md)'s mother was [Mithrellas](../M/Mithrellas.md), a [Silvan Elf](../S/Silvan_Elves.md) who had accompanied Amroth's beloved [Nimrodel](../N/Nimrodel.md) on her southward journey from [Lórien](../L/Lórien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-1">[1]</a></sup>

Being a coastal city, Dol Amroth was subject to occasional attacks by the [Corsairs of Umbar](../C/Corsairs_of_Umbar.md) for its fifteenth prince was slain in battle against these sea raiders in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2746](../T/Third_Age_2746.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In all, there were twenty-one [Princes of Dol Amroth](../P/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth.md) before the twenty-second, [Imrahil](../I/Imrahil.md), led a company of knights and a contingent of 700 men from the city to [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) during the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Minas_Tirith_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Minas_Tirith-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Population {#Population}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Princess_of_Dol_Amroth.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/3d/Ted_Nasmith_-_Princess_of_Dol_Amroth.jpg/250px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Princess_of_Dol_Amroth.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="210" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/3d/Ted_Nasmith_-_Princess_of_Dol_Amroth.jpg/375px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Princess_of_Dol_Amroth.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/3/3d/Ted_Nasmith_-_Princess_of_Dol_Amroth.jpg 2x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="336"/></a><figcaption><i>Princess of Dol Amroth</i> by <a href="../T/Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

The first known settlers of the promontory were [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) and [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md) along with many [Elves from Lórinand](../E/Elves_of_Lórinand.md) some time after [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [1701](../S/Second_Age_1701.md), apart from the [Sindarin settlement](../E/Edhellond.md) north of Dol Amroth established in the late [First Age](../F/First_Age.md). The Elves remained there until [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1981](../T/Third_Age_1981.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Amroth_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amroth-7">[7]</a></sup>

The [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md) family that had settled in Dol Amroth was said to be akin to [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-1">[1]</a></sup> and, according to the legend of [Mithrellas](../M/Mithrellas.md), their Princes had Elvish blood.<sup id="cite_ref-GalCel_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GalCel-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> Because of this, the [ruling house](../H/House_of_Dol_Amroth.md) and their kin were noble by blood and fair in face and mind.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

In addition to the rulers of Dol Amroth, the people of Dol Amroth were also of high blood being tall, grey-eyed, proud, and dark-haired, indicative of their high Númenórean blood.<sup id="cite_ref-Minas_Tirith_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Minas_Tirith-4">[4]</a></sup> In fact, when Prince [Imrahil](../I/Imrahil.md) arrived in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) with his <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Swan-knights&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Swan-knights (page does not exist)">swan-knights</a>, they "held themselves like lords in whom the race of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) ran true."<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-13">[13]</a></sup> Being mostly [Dúnedain of the South](Dúnedain_of_the_South.md), the inhabitants of Dol Amroth were also some of the few people of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) who knew and spoke [Sindarin](../G/Gondor_Sindarin.md) on a daily basis.<sup id="cite_ref-Men_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-14">[14]</a></sup> They were famous as the most skilful harp players in all of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), who played at the coronation of [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Dol Amroth* is [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) from *[dol](Dol.md)* ("hill") and the name of the King *[Amroth](../A/Amroth.md#Etymology)* ("Upclimber"),<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> hence "Hill of Amroth".
