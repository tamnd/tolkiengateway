**Ascar** was the northernmost of the rivers that flowed through [Ossiriand](/wiki/Ossiriand). It emerged near [Dolmed](/wiki/Dolmed), and the [Dwarf-road](/wiki/Dwarf-road_of_Beleriand "Dwarf-road of Beleriand") from the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) lay along its northern bank, until it met [Gelion](/wiki/Gelion) at [Sarn Athrad](/wiki/Sarn_Athrad).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After the murder of King [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) of Doriath the [Dwarves of Nogrod](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Nogrod) easily entered [Menegroth](/wiki/Menegroth) where they [ransacked](/wiki/Battle_in_the_Thousand_Caves "Battle in the Thousand Caves") and plundered the halls, retaking the [Nauglamír](/wiki/Nauglam%C3%ADr). The victorious Dwarves marched along the banks of the Ascar with their spoils. Suddenly, [Beren](/wiki/Beren), [Dior](/wiki/Dior) his son, and a host of [Laiquendi](/wiki/Green-elves "Green-elves") attacked the Dwarves. After the [Battle of Sarn Athrad](/wiki/Battle_of_Sarn_Athrad) Beren reclaimed the Nauglamír, but had the rest of the treasure of Doriath cast into the Ascar. From that time forth the river was renamed [Rathlóriel](/wiki/Rathl%C3%B3riel) ("Goldenbed").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Asgar* in [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) means "rushing" or "impetuous",<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> derived from [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") A-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=SKAR&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="SKAR (page does not exist)">SKAR</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [Eriol's](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine "Ælfwine") [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) translations, *Ascar* is referred to as *Baening*. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) tentatively interpreted the O.E. name as "river filled with bones (of the Dwarves)", but doubted that it was his father's intended meaning.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The name *Rathlóriel* means "Golden-bed".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> In the *[Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies")*, the name *Rath Loriel* is said to contain the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) word *[rath](/wiki/Rath#Noldorin "Rath")* ("coarse, river-bed") and *[lor-](/wiki/Glaur "Glaur")* ("gold").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In the early drafts of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, *Rathlóriel* was called *Rathlorion*. However, Tolkien considered in later writings changing the name of the river to *Rathmalad*<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>, "hastily pencilled" beneath *Rathloriel* on the [The Second 'Silmarillion' Map](/wiki/The_Second_%27Silmarillion%27_Map), and also *Rathmallen*<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> which appears only in the text *The Tale of Years* (entry "[503](/wiki/First_Age_503 "First Age 503")").
