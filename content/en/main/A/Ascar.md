**Ascar** was the northernmost of the rivers that flowed through [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md). It emerged near [Dolmed](../D/Dolmed.md), and the [Dwarf-road](../D/Dwarf-road_of_Beleriand.md) from the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) lay along its northern bank, until it met [Gelion](../G/Gelion.md) at [Sarn Athrad](../S/Sarn_Athrad.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After the murder of King [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md) of Doriath the [Dwarves of Nogrod](../D/Dwarves_of_Nogrod.md) easily entered [Menegroth](../M/Menegroth.md) where they [ransacked](../B/Battle_in_the_Thousand_Caves.md) and plundered the halls, retaking the [Nauglamír](../N/Nauglamír.md). The victorious Dwarves marched along the banks of the Ascar with their spoils. Suddenly, [Beren](../B/Beren.md), [Dior](../D/Dior.md) his son, and a host of [Laiquendi](../G/Green-elves.md) attacked the Dwarves. After the [Battle of Sarn Athrad](../B/Battle_of_Sarn_Athrad.md) Beren reclaimed the Nauglamír, but had the rest of the treasure of Doriath cast into the Ascar. From that time forth the river was renamed [Rathlóriel](../R/Rathlóriel.md) ("Goldenbed").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Asgar* in [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) means "rushing" or "impetuous",<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> derived from [root](../S/Sundocarme.md) A-<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=SKAR&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="SKAR (page does not exist)">SKAR</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [Eriol's](../_/Ælfwine.md) [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) translations, *Ascar* is referred to as *Baening*. [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) tentatively interpreted the O.E. name as "river filled with bones (of the Dwarves)", but doubted that it was his father's intended meaning.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The name *Rathlóriel* means "Golden-bed".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> In the *[Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)*, the name *Rath Loriel* is said to contain the [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) word *[rath](../R/Rath.md#Noldorin)* ("coarse, river-bed") and *[lor-](../G/Glaur.md)* ("gold").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In the early drafts of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*, *Rathlóriel* was called *Rathlorion*. However, Tolkien considered in later writings changing the name of the river to *Rathmalad*<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>, "hastily pencilled" beneath *Rathloriel* on the [The Second 'Silmarillion' Map](../T/The_Second_'Silmarillion'_Map.md), and also *Rathmallen*<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> which appears only in the text *The Tale of Years* (entry "[503](../F/First_Age_503.md)").
