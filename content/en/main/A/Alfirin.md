**Alfirin** was an [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) name for the flower [Men](/wiki/Men) called [simbelmynë](/wiki/Simbelmyn%C3%AB "Simbelmynë") (they also used the name [uilos](/wiki/Uilos "Uilos") for the same flower).<sup id="cite_ref-Note38_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note38-1">[1]</a></sup> The name comes from its habit of growing thickly on the tombs of [Men](/wiki/Men): it was found among the Kings' mounds in the [Barrowfield](/wiki/Barrowfield) of [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras), and also on the [Tomb of Elendil](/wiki/Tomb_of_Elendil). Tolkien provided a description of "an immortelle, but not dry and papery: simply a beautiful bell-like flower, running through many colours, but soft and gentle."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The white variety seems to have been most common.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Alfirin* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, said to mean "not dying". While first said to consist of *[alph](/wiki/Alph "Alph")* ("[swan](/wiki/Swans "Swans")") + *irin* (unglossed), [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") amended the compound to consist of *al-* ("not") + *fĭrin* ("mortal"; see also [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *[firin](/wiki/Firin "Firin")*).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In his comments in *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)*, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) points out that [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas)' description of "golden bells ... of *mallos* and *alfirin*", is not quite in harmony with other descriptions, and he suggests that this particular use of the name may refer to a different flower altogether.<sup id="cite_ref-Note38_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note38-1">[1]</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup> Alternatively, the golden flowers of [Lebennin](/wiki/Lebennin) might simply be a differently-coloured variety of the white *alfirin* commonly seen on Men's tombs.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 2]</a></sup>
