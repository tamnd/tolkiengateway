<i><b>athrad</b></i> (pl. *ethraid*) is a noun meaning "(river-)crossing, ford, way" in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC163_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC163-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It is composed of <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ath-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ath- (page does not exist)">ath-</a></i> "on both sides, across" and <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=R%C3%A2d&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Râd (page does not exist)">râd</a></i> "path, track".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
