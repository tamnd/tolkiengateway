The <i><b>Atanatárion</b></i> was a literary work dealing with the three "Great Tales" as written down in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) that were primarily concerned with the heroes of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) of the late [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Atan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atan-1">[1]</a></sup>

These three Great Tales, which were later preserved in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), were:<sup id="cite_ref-Atan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atan-1">[1]</a></sup>

1\) <i><b><a href="../L/Lay_of_Leithian.md" title="Lay of Leithian">Narn Beren ion Barahir</a></b></i> ("Tale of [Beren](../B/Beren.md) son of [Barahir](../B/Barahir.md)") or <i><b>Narn e·Dinúviel</b></i> ("Tale of the [Nightingale](../L/Lúthien.md)")

2\) *Narn e·mbar [Hador](../H/Hador.md)* ("Tale of the [House of Hador](../H/House_of_Hador.md)"), which consists of:

- a) <i><b><a href="../N/Narn_i_Chîn_Húrin_(tale).md" title="Narn i Chîn Húrin (tale)">Narn i·Chîn Húrin</a></b></i> ("Tale of the Children of [Húrin](../H/Húrin.md)") or <i><b>Narn e·’Rach <a href="../M/Morgoth.md" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a></b></i> ("Tale of the Curse of Morgoth")

- b) <i><b>Narn en·Êl</b></i> ("Tale of the [Star](../E/Eärendil.md)") or <i><b>Narn e·Dant <a href="../G/Gondolin.md" title="Gondolin">Gondolin</a> ar Orthad en·Êl</b></i> ("Tale of the [Fall of Gondolin](../F/Fall_of_Gondolin.md) and the Raising of the [Star](../S/Star_of_Eärendil.md)")

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The title *Atanatárion* is [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) for "([Legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md)) of the [Fathers of Men](Atanatári.md)", with the later part of the title forming a genitive plural of the term *Atanatar* ("of the Fathers of Men"), while the "Legendarium" part of the title is implied in the English gloss of the title of the work.<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-2">[2]</a></sup>

The [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) cognates of the title are <i><b>Nern in Adanath</b></i> and <i><b>Nern in Edenedair</b></i>, where the "Legendarium" part of the title is explicitly translated,<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-2">[2]</a></sup> with the term *Nern* being the plural of the noun <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Narn_(noun)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Narn (noun) (page does not exist)">narn</a></i> ("a tale").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Composition {#Composition}

[Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md) wrote about the *Atanatárion* and the titles of the three Great Tales on a small slip with the heading: *Memorandum*, some time in the [1950](../_/1950.md)'s. According to [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), he probably considered composing them in the form of three shorter stories as Appendices to *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Atan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atan-1">[1]</a></sup>
