The <i><b>Atanatárion</b></i> was a literary work dealing with the three "Great Tales" as written down in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) that were primarily concerned with the heroes of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) of the late [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Atan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atan-1">[1]</a></sup>

These three Great Tales, which were later preserved in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), were:<sup id="cite_ref-Atan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atan-1">[1]</a></sup>

1\) <i><b><a href="/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian" title="Lay of Leithian">Narn Beren ion Barahir</a></b></i> ("Tale of [Beren](/wiki/Beren) son of [Barahir](/wiki/Barahir)") or <i><b>Narn e·Dinúviel</b></i> ("Tale of the [Nightingale](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien "Lúthien")")

2\) *Narn e·mbar [Hador](/wiki/Hador)* ("Tale of the [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador)"), which consists of:

- a) <i><b><a href="/wiki/Narn_i_Ch%C3%AEn_H%C3%BArin_(tale)" title="Narn i Chîn Húrin (tale)">Narn i·Chîn Húrin</a></b></i> ("Tale of the Children of [Húrin](/wiki/H%C3%BArin)") or <i><b>Narn e·’Rach <a href="/wiki/Morgoth" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a></b></i> ("Tale of the Curse of Morgoth")

- b) <i><b>Narn en·Êl</b></i> ("Tale of the [Star](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil "Eärendil")") or <i><b>Narn e·Dant <a href="/wiki/Gondolin" title="Gondolin">Gondolin</a> ar Orthad en·Êl</b></i> ("Tale of the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin) and the Raising of the [Star](/wiki/Star_of_E%C3%A4rendil "Star of Eärendil")")

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The title *Atanatárion* is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) for "([Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium)) of the [Fathers of Men](/wiki/Atanat%C3%A1ri "Atanatári")", with the later part of the title forming a genitive plural of the term *Atanatar* ("of the Fathers of Men"), while the "Legendarium" part of the title is implied in the English gloss of the title of the work.<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-2">[2]</a></sup>

The [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) cognates of the title are <i><b>Nern in Adanath</b></i> and <i><b>Nern in Edenedair</b></i>, where the "Legendarium" part of the title is explicitly translated,<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-2">[2]</a></sup> with the term *Nern* being the plural of the noun <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Narn_(noun)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Narn (noun) (page does not exist)">narn</a></i> ("a tale").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Composition {#Composition}

[Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) wrote about the *Atanatárion* and the titles of the three Great Tales on a small slip with the heading: *Memorandum*, some time in the [1950](/wiki/1950)'s. According to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), he probably considered composing them in the form of three shorter stories as Appendices to *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Atan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atan-1">[1]</a></sup>
