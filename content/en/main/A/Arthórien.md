**Arthórien** was a region in eastern [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md).

## History {#History}

Arthórien was located between the rivers [Aros](Aros.md) and [Celon](../C/Celon.md), west of [Estolad](../E/Estolad.md) and south of [Himlad](../H/Himlad.md).<sup id="cite_ref-UT_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WJBeleriand_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJBeleriand-2">[2]</a></sup> Although what appears to be the same region is also called [Radhrim](../R/Radhrim.md),<sup id="cite_ref-WJBeleriand_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJBeleriand-2">[2]</a></sup> it is unknown if these two regions were the same (or if one was situated within the other).

After [Denethor](<../D/Denethor_(son_of_Lenwë).md>) was killed upon [Amon Ereb](Amon_Ereb.md) in the [First Battle of Beleriand](../F/First_Battle_of_Beleriand.md), some of the [Green-elves](../G/Green-elves.md) fled to Doriath, apparently seeking the protection of the [Girdle of Melian](../G/Girdle_of_Melian.md). These elves, who became known as the [Guest-elves](../G/Guest-elves.md), settled in Arthórien.<sup id="cite_ref-UT_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WJP1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJP1-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology and names {#Etymology_and_names}

On a map of Beleriand, the name <i><b>Garthúrian</b></i> (could possibly be read as *Garthúrien*, according to [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md)) is marked as another name for *Arthórien*.<sup id="cite_ref-WJBeleriand_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJBeleriand-2">[2]</a></sup> The name *Garthurian* occurs in early linguistic writings, said to be a "[Doriathrin](../D/Doriathrin.md) name of Doriath", meaning "Fenced or Hidden Realm/Kingdom". In the same manuscript, it is said that [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) *Arthoren*, *Arthurien* was a translation of *Garthurian*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> A somewhat different version is given in a "philological fragment of uncertain date", where it is written that "the Noldor often used the name *Arthúrien* for Doriath, though this is but an alteration of the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) *Garthúrian* 'hidden realm'".<sup id="cite_ref-WJBeleriand_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJBeleriand-2">[2]</a></sup>

Another name used for the region of Arthórien was *Eglamar*. This usage is noted as a mystery by Christopher Tolkien, as the name usually applies to other concepts (cf. <a class="mw-disambig" href="../E/Eglamar_(disambiguation).md" title="Eglamar (disambiguation)">Eglamar (disambiguation)</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-WJP1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJP1-3">[3]</a></sup>
