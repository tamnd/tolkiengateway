**Alina Maria<sup id="cite_ref-PO_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PO-1">[1]</a></sup> Helena Dadlez**<sup id="cite_ref-LG_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LG-2">[2]</a></sup> is a former employee of [George Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin "Allen & Unwin").

Polish-born<sup id="cite_ref-LG_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LG-2">[2]</a></sup> Alina Dadlez worked for the British publishers George Allen &amp; Unwin from the 1950s to the 1980s.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> She lived in London and was official naturalised as a British subject on [6 July](/wiki/6_July) [1959](/wiki/1959).<sup id="cite_ref-LG_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LG-2">[2]</a></sup> In the announcement of her naturalisation in <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_London_Gazette" title="wikipedia:The London Gazette">The London Gazette</a></i> ([18 September](/wiki/18_September) [1959](/wiki/1959)) her occupation is named "Departmental Manageress (Publishers)".<sup id="cite_ref-LG_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LG-2">[2]</a></sup>

At Allen &amp; Unwin, she worked at the department for foreign rights and permissions, where her employment status is described as a coordinator and later as a manager.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Alina Dadlez frequently corresponded with [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) concerning the translations of his work into foreign languages, such as Dutch, Polish or German.

Since at least [1987](/wiki/1987), Alina Dadlez worked for the London record label <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decca_Records" title="wikipedia:Decca Records">Decca Records</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Alina Dadlez was probably a member of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Social_and_Cultural_Association" title="wikipedia:Polish Social and Cultural Association">Polish Social and Cultural Association</a> (*Polski Ośrodek Społeczno-Kulturalny*) located in London, for her name is on the list of the donors for this association in [1969](/wiki/1969).<sup id="cite_ref-PO_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PO-1">[1]</a></sup>

Alina Dadlez is mentioned in the acknowledgement of [Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter)'s *[J.R.R. Tolkien: A Biography](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_A_Biography)*.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
