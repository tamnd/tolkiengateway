The **Ainulindalë** is the fourth chapter of the second section of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](../T/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings.md)*. It contains the first version of the [*Ainulindalë* (Rúmil's work)](<Ainulindalë_(Rúmil's_work).md>) written in the 1930s after the "[The Music of the Ainur](../T/The_Music_of_the_Ainur.md)", published in *[The Book of Lost Tales Part One](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One.md)*.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

<dl><dd><i>See synopsis given in &#34;<a href="../T/The_Music_of_the_Ainur.md#The_Music_of_the_Ainur" title="The Music of the Ainur">The Music of the Ainur</a>&#34;</i></dd></dl>

The narrative differences with the previous version are limited to the framework. Now Rúmil of [Tol Eressëa](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md) is [Rúmil](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Tirion).md>) of [Tûn](../T/Tûn.md), and [Eriol](../E/Eriol.md) is named by his [English](../O/Old_English.md) name *[Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md)*. Rúmil is still the one who told the story to Eriol, but it is written down as a proper work.

## Commentary {#Commentary}

This text follows directly the cosmological myth given in *The Book of Lost Tales*, but now as an independent work. Both the *[Sketch of the Mythology](../S/Sketch_of_the_Mythology.md)* and *[The Quenta](../T/The_Quenta.md)* ommited the creation of the world, but [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md) did not abandone it, but composed this new version with the Lost Tale in front of him, rephrasing most of it but without summarizing, unlike the rest of the mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-Ainulindale_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainulindale-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:155</sup>

Of this version, Tolkien made two manuscripts, which [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) names *Ainulindalë* A and *Ainulindalë* B. The first is extremely rough, full of changes at the moment of composition concerning the removal of the Lost Tales context. This only has the title *The Music of the Ainur* added later. The *Ainulindalë* B was probably made right after the previous one, being a handsome manuscript with just a few stilistic changes in the narrative. It includes the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) title *Ainulindalë* and the authorship of [Rúmil](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Tirion).md>), relating it with "The Gnomes Material" like *[The Ambarkanta](../T/The_Ambarkanta.md)* and *[The Lhammas](../T/The_Lhammas.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Ainulindale_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainulindale-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:154–5</sup>

Although every sentence was rewritten, the only remarkable difference with the *[Ainulindalë](Ainulindalë.md)* of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* is still the absence of the [Vision of Ilúvatar](../V/Vision_of_Ilúvatar.md) and the [creative word](../E/Eä.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ainulindale_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainulindale-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:166</sup>

Many years later, Tolkien wrote a new version in [1946](../_/1946.md), of which only a torn page survives.

In late 1940s, Tolkien lent manuscript B to Mrs. [Katharine Farrer](../K/Katharine_Farrer.md) (cf. [Letter 115](../L/Letter_115.md)), which he dubbed "Flat World Version" (along with a "[Round World](../R/Round_World.md) Version"). From these derive other versions of the *[Ainulindalë](<Ainulindalë_(Morgoth's_Ring).md>)*, explained and published in *[Morgoth's Ring](../M/Morgoth's_Ring.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
