The **Ainulindalë** is the fourth chapter of the second section of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](/wiki/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings)*. It contains the first version of the [*Ainulindalë* (Rúmil's work)](</wiki/Ainulindal%C3%AB_(R%C3%BAmil%27s_work)> "Ainulindalë (Rúmil's work)") written in the 1930s after the "[The Music of the Ainur](/wiki/The_Music_of_the_Ainur)", published in *[The Book of Lost Tales Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One)*.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

<dl><dd><i>See synopsis given in &#34;<a href="/wiki/The_Music_of_the_Ainur#The_Music_of_the_Ainur" title="The Music of the Ainur">The Music of the Ainur</a>&#34;</i></dd></dl>

The narrative differences with the previous version are limited to the framework. Now Rúmil of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) is [Rúmil](</wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)> "Rúmil (elf of Tirion)") of [Tûn](/wiki/T%C3%BBn), and [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol) is named by his [English](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") name *[Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine)*. Rúmil is still the one who told the story to Eriol, but it is written down as a proper work.

## Commentary {#Commentary}

This text follows directly the cosmological myth given in *The Book of Lost Tales*, but now as an independent work. Both the *[Sketch of the Mythology](/wiki/Sketch_of_the_Mythology)* and *[The Quenta](/wiki/The_Quenta)* ommited the creation of the world, but [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) did not abandone it, but composed this new version with the Lost Tale in front of him, rephrasing most of it but without summarizing, unlike the rest of the mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-Ainulindale_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainulindale-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:155</sup>

Of this version, Tolkien made two manuscripts, which [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) names *Ainulindalë* A and *Ainulindalë* B. The first is extremely rough, full of changes at the moment of composition concerning the removal of the Lost Tales context. This only has the title *The Music of the Ainur* added later. The *Ainulindalë* B was probably made right after the previous one, being a handsome manuscript with just a few stilistic changes in the narrative. It includes the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) title *Ainulindalë* and the authorship of [Rúmil](</wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)> "Rúmil (elf of Tirion)"), relating it with "The Gnomes Material" like *[The Ambarkanta](/wiki/The_Ambarkanta)* and *[The Lhammas](/wiki/The_Lhammas)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Ainulindale_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainulindale-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:154–5</sup>

Although every sentence was rewritten, the only remarkable difference with the *[Ainulindalë](/wiki/Ainulindal%C3%AB)* of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* is still the absence of the [Vision of Ilúvatar](/wiki/Vision_of_Il%C3%BAvatar) and the [creative word](/wiki/E%C3%A4 "Eä").<sup id="cite_ref-Ainulindale_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainulindale-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:166</sup>

Many years later, Tolkien wrote a new version in [1946](/wiki/1946), of which only a torn page survives.

In late 1940s, Tolkien lent manuscript B to Mrs. [Katharine Farrer](/wiki/Katharine_Farrer) (cf. [Letter 115](/wiki/Letter_115)), which he dubbed "Flat World Version" (along with a "[Round World](/wiki/Round_World) Version"). From these derive other versions of the *[Ainulindalë](</wiki/Ainulindal%C3%AB_(Morgoth%27s_Ring)> "Ainulindalë (Morgoth's Ring)")*, explained and published in *[Morgoth's Ring](/wiki/Morgoth%27s_Ring)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
