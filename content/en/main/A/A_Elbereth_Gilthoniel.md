**A Elbereth Gilthoniel** or **Aerlinn in edhil o Imladris** is an *[aerlinn](/wiki/Aerlinn "Aerlinn")* (poem) of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) addressed to [Varda](/wiki/Varda), sung in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and in iambic tetrameter. The first stanza, which is the only one known, rhymes as such: *aababcc*.

### Translation {#Translation}

<dl><dd><i>O Elbereth Starkindler<br/>(white) glittering slants down<br/>sparkling like jewels<br/>from [the] firmament [the] glory [of] the star-host!<br/>To-remote distance far-having gazed <br/>from [the] tree-tangled middle-lands,<br/><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Fanuilos" title="Fanuilos">Fanuilos</a>, to thee I will chant<br/>on this side of ocean, here on this side of the Great Ocean!</i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></dd></dl>

The rest of it is not saved, but it may be that *[Oh Snow-white](/wiki/Elven_Hymn_to_Elbereth "Elven Hymn to Elbereth")* is a [Westron](/wiki/Westron) translation or variation of this aerlinn. Variations of this hymn are sung throughout the book, such as the very last one written down:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...and as if in answer, from down below, coming up the road out of the valley, voices sang:
</p>
<dl><dd>A! Elbereth Gilthoniel!<br/>silivren penna míriel<br/>o menel aglar elenath,<br/>Gilthoniel, A! Elbereth!<br/>We still remember, we who dwell<br/>In this far land beneath the trees<br/>The starlight on the Western Seas.</dd></dl>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Frodo_Baggins" title="Frodo Baggins">Frodo Baggins</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Return of the King">The Return of the King</a></i>, &#34;<a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Grey_Havens" title="The Grey Havens">The Grey Havens</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

[Donald Swann](/wiki/Donald_Swann) wrote music for this song, which can be found in the book *[The Road Goes Ever On](</wiki/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book)> "The Road Goes Ever On (book)"),* where Tolkien gives also a [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) text of the poem in the [Mode of Beleriand](/wiki/Mode_of_Beleriand) given above;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> it is also found on the accompanying CD of the latest version.

A different musical version was recorded by the [Tolkien Ensemble](/wiki/Tolkien_Ensemble). The first version cited above can be found on their album *[A Night in Rivendell](/wiki/A_Night_in_Rivendell)* and takes the form of a chant sung by a female soloist. The second version can be found on the album *[At Dawn in Rivendell](/wiki/At_Dawn_in_Rivendell)*.

Another musical version was composed by the Russian Tolkienite band <a class="external text" href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79i12JMzzT4" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Aire and Saruman</a>.

[*Tolkien Journal* (Vol.2, No.1)](</wiki/Tolkien_Journal_(Vol.2,_No.1)> "Tolkien Journal (Vol.2, No.1)") 1966, included the poem and an English translation by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <span></span>
