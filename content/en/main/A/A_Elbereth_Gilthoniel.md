**A Elbereth Gilthoniel** or **Aerlinn in edhil o Imladris** is an *[aerlinn](Aerlinn.md)* (poem) of [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) addressed to [Varda](../V/Varda.md), sung in [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) and in iambic tetrameter. The first stanza, which is the only one known, rhymes as such: *aababcc*.

### Translation {#Translation}

<dl><dd><i>O Elbereth Starkindler<br/>(white) glittering slants down<br/>sparkling like jewels<br/>from [the] firmament [the] glory [of] the star-host!<br/>To-remote distance far-having gazed <br/>from [the] tree-tangled middle-lands,<br/><a class="mw-redirect" href="../F/Fanuilos.md" title="Fanuilos">Fanuilos</a>, to thee I will chant<br/>on this side of ocean, here on this side of the Great Ocean!</i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></dd></dl>

The rest of it is not saved, but it may be that *[Oh Snow-white](../E/Elven_Hymn_to_Elbereth.md)* is a [Westron](../W/Westron.md) translation or variation of this aerlinn. Variations of this hymn are sung throughout the book, such as the very last one written down:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...and as if in answer, from down below, coming up the road out of the valley, voices sang:
</p>
<dl><dd>A! Elbereth Gilthoniel!<br/>silivren penna míriel<br/>o menel aglar elenath,<br/>Gilthoniel, A! Elbereth!<br/>We still remember, we who dwell<br/>In this far land beneath the trees<br/>The starlight on the Western Seas.</dd></dl>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../F/Frodo_Baggins.md" title="Frodo Baggins">Frodo Baggins</a> in <i><a href="../T/The_Return_of_the_King.md" title="The Return of the King">The Return of the King</a></i>, &#34;<a class="mw-redirect" href="../T/The_Grey_Havens.md" title="The Grey Havens">The Grey Havens</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

[Donald Swann](../D/Donald_Swann.md) wrote music for this song, which can be found in the book *[The Road Goes Ever On](<../T/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book).md>),* where Tolkien gives also a [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) text of the poem in the [Mode of Beleriand](../M/Mode_of_Beleriand.md) given above;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> it is also found on the accompanying CD of the latest version.

A different musical version was recorded by the [Tolkien Ensemble](../T/Tolkien_Ensemble.md). The first version cited above can be found on their album *[A Night in Rivendell](A_Night_in_Rivendell.md)* and takes the form of a chant sung by a female soloist. The second version can be found on the album *[At Dawn in Rivendell](At_Dawn_in_Rivendell.md)*.

Another musical version was composed by the Russian Tolkienite band <a class="external text" href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79i12JMzzT4" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Aire and Saruman</a>.

[*Tolkien Journal* (Vol.2, No.1)](<../T/Tolkien_Journal_(Vol.2,_No.1).md>) 1966, included the poem and an English translation by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <span></span>
