**Atlantis-haunting**<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup> or **Atlantic complex**<sup id="cite_ref-Auden_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Auden-2">[2]</a></sup> were ways by which [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) referred to his recurring dreams of a towering, inescapable wave rising up out of the sea or sweeping over the landscape.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>This legend or myth or dim memory of some ancient history has always troubled me. In sleep I had the dreadful dream of the ineluctable Wave, either coming out of the quiet sea, or coming in towering over the green inlands. It still occurs occasionally, though now exorcized by writing about it. It always ends by surrender, and I awake gasping out of deep water. I used to draw it or write bad poems about it.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, <a href="/wiki/Letter_257" title="Letter 257">Letter 257</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Tolkien describes this dream in various letters, always as an ineluctable wave, sometimes rising up from the sea and sometimes passing over trees and fields<sup id="cite_ref-Auden_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Auden-2">[2]</a></sup>; sometimes dark and sometimes green and sunlit<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>. After learning that his son, [Michael](/wiki/Michael_Tolkien "Michael Tolkien"), had similar dreams without Tolkien ever having mentioned it to him, Tolkien wondered if he might have inherited it from his own parents in turn.<sup id="cite_ref-Auden_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Auden-2">[2]</a></sup> It was this dream that inspired the story of [Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor"), which Tolkien frequently referred to in his letters as the "Atlantis isle". After writing the story of the Downfall of Númenor, Tolkien noted that he ceased having the dream.<sup id="cite_ref-Auden_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Auden-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup>

The story appears in various places throughout Tolkien's writings. In the 1930s, Tolkien began a challenge with [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis) which resulted in a "time travel" story, *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)*;<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> he completed only four chapters, two of which take place in Atlantis, then known as [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor). The story takes into account "the traditions of the North Sea concerning the coming of corn and culture heroes, ancestors of kingly lines, in boats"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">[1]</a></sup> and father-son pairs who share the names of [Eädwine](/wiki/E%C3%A4dwine) and [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) through several versions and cognates. Tolkien imported elements from his [earlier](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales "The Book of Lost Tales") [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") into this, notably the villain character of [Thû](/wiki/Th%C3%BB)/[Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), who was responsible for Númenor's [Downfall](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> This story eventually developed into the *[Akallabêth](/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth)*, in which the island of Númenor is covered by waves and sinks into the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In a letter to W. H. Auden, Tolkien also wrote that he "bequeathed \[the dream\] to [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir)" in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Auden_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Auden-2">[2]</a></sup> In *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King),* Faramir tells [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn) that he often dreams of Númenor, "of the land of Westernesse that foundered, and of the great wave climbing over the green lands and above the hills, and coming on, darkness unescapable."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [1945](/wiki/1945), Tolkien revisited similar concepts and attempted to write the novel *[The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers)*; there, a character named [Alwin Arundel Lowdham](/wiki/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham) experiences lucid dreams about Atlantis, which he shares with the [Notion Club](/wiki/Notion_Club); through these dreams, he discovers much about the story of Númenor, and that he is a "reincarnation" of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
