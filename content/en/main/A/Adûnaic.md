**Adûnaic** ("Language of the West"), or the native name **Adûnayân**, was the **Númenórean** language, spoken by the [Men](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) during the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age).

## Origins and family {#Origins_and_family}

Adûnaic was derived from the [Hadorian](/wiki/House_of_Hador "House of Hador") tongue, related to the [Bëorian](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor")—collectively called [Taliska](/wiki/Taliska). It was more distantly related to the languages of the [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men) of the east, such as the Men of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) and the [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen).<sup id="cite_ref-men_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-men-1">[1]</a></sup> During the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) these languages were much influenced by [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [Avarin](/wiki/Avarin), but also by the languages of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar), as the [Elf-friends](/wiki/Elf-friends) spoke [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Despite it being a millennium or more after their separation, the Númenórean mariners who returned to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) could find common roots and words with the [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men) of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) (related to the Folks of Beor and Hador), and talk about simple matters.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> This is also partly due to the Númenórean longevity, and the slow change of their language.<sup id="cite_ref-atani_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the western districts of Númenor, the rustic folk still used another dialect, a last remnant of the Bëorian language.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Taliska was not related to the [Haladin](/wiki/Haladin) tongue at all, therefore the Númenóreans did not recognise the peoples of [Enedwaith](/wiki/Enedwaith) and [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath) as their distant kin, who spoke [unrelated](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean "Pre-Númenórean") languages.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Adûnaic was generally considered to be a language of less prestige than the [Elven tongues](/wiki/Elvish "Elvish"). Most locations of Númenor, and most of the lords and ladies of the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain), had also [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) or [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) names beside their native ones.<sup id="cite_ref-ak_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ak-8">[8]</a></sup> Even most commoners knew Sindarin to some degree.

Most of the House of Bëor had been killed after the [Dagor Bragollach](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> but even during the Second Age a Bëorian accent of Adûnaic still survived in parts of Númenor, most notably in [Emerië](/wiki/Emeri%C3%AB) and around the harbour of [Andúnië](/wiki/And%C3%BAni%C3%AB).

### Days of pride {#Days_of_pride}

About 2,000 years into the Second Age, the Númenóreans began to envy the immortality of the [Firstborn](/wiki/Firstborn), which extended to the languages of the Elves; the Kings and their [followers](/wiki/King%27s_Men "King's Men") used the Elven tongues less and less. Though the Kings and Queens had all taken their names in Quenya, after some time their supporters used their Adûnaic names to refer to them.

Eventually [Ar-Adûnakhôr](/wiki/Ar-Ad%C3%BBnakh%C3%B4r) took his name in Adûnaic and forbade anyone to speak the Elven tongues in his presence. Adûnaic was now the language of the royal court. Its supremacy was most strongly enforced by [Ar-Gimilzôr](/wiki/Ar-Gimilz%C3%B4r): he outlawed the use of Elvish anywhere in Númenor, which antagonized the few [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful) Númenóreans still living in the land.

However, his son Inziladûn took a Quenya name again, [Tar-Palantir](/wiki/Tar-Palantir), and repealed the ban on the Elven tongues and gave peace to the Faithful. His daughter [Míriel](/wiki/Tar-M%C3%ADriel "Tar-Míriel") would probably have continued his reforms, but her cousin [Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n "Ar-Pharazôn") seized power and, in addition, gave her an Adûnaic name (Ar-Zimraphel).<sup id="cite_ref-ak_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ak-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Decline {#Decline}

In Middle-earth, the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful) settlers (who controlled the shorelands of the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands) from [Lune](/wiki/Lune) to [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir)), neglected their native tongue and favored [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and devised names in it; no doubt as a reaction against the rebel Kings who banned the Elvish tongues. This was the case among the Faithful of high lineage who used Sindarin as their native tongue, and became a mark of Númenórean descent, while Adûnaic was left as the daily language of the illiterate, neglected to unheeded change. However some Númenóreans who had Sindarin as their mother tongue, learned this "vulgar" language when it was needed.<sup id="cite_ref-atani_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas), the Númenóreans didn't stop to use Adûnaic names, as for them the language didn't have the political connection to the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men) and the rebellion against the Valar; their ancestors had settled there before the division of their people (c. [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [2221](/wiki/Second_Age_2221 "Second Age 2221")).<sup id="cite_ref-NM_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-10">[10]</a></sup>

With the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) came the end of classical Adûnaic. The study and preservation of the language was neglected by the Exiles of Númenor, because they associated it with the rebellious and repressive Númenórean Kings.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

However, this debased tongue, spread from the early Númenórean sailors and colonists, continued to spread as their settlements increased in size and influence and met more peoples, who came under their rule. Those Men of different kinds used this language as a *lingua franca* among them. This process became of general importance after the [Realms in Exile](/wiki/Realms_in_Exile); from [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) to [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) the tongue became widely current, being adopted both by inhabitants and incomers, eventually becoming their native language, which they called [Westron](/wiki/Westron), widely spoken during the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-men_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-men-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-atani_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Adûnaic continued to survive, at least in names in [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas) and [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-NM_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-10">[10]</a></sup> Some members of the [House of Dol Amroth](/wiki/House_of_Dol_Amroth) were named in Adûnaic instead of Sindarin. It is probable that Prince [Imrazôr](/wiki/Imraz%C3%B4r), also known as "the Númenórean",<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-House_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-House-14">[14]</a></sup> has an Adûnaic name.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[note 1]</a></sup> The names of his descendants in the late Third Age, [Adrahil](/wiki/Adrahil) and [Imrahil](/wiki/Imrahil),<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> were also Adûnaic.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Structure {#Structure}

The language called "classical Adûnaic" was spoken at the time of the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor). It is not known at what time in the history of Númenor the classical form evolved from an earlier stage, nor when that language developed from the languages of the [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador) and [Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor"). However, the phonology and morphology of classical Adûnaic is fairly well documented, as is the phonology of its predecessor.<sup id="cite_ref-Structure_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Structure-19">[18]</a></sup>

(Ancient Adûnaic might be attested in the name *Agathurush* "[Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3)/Greyflood".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> The sound *sh* occurred in ancient Adûnaic but developed into *s* in the classical period.<sup id="cite_ref-Structure_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Structure-19">[18]</a></sup> This might suggest that ancient Adûnaic was spoken when [Aldarion](/wiki/Tar-Aldarion "Tar-Aldarion") discovered the mouth of Gwathló in the 730's.)

The consonants mostly have their classical sound values, with the following exceptions:

*mh* = voiceless "m"

*nh* = voiceless "n"

*ph* = bilabial "f"

*th* = as "th" in "thing"

*kh* = as "ch" in "Bach"

*v/w* = as "w" in "will"

*y* = as "y" in "yes"<sup id="cite_ref-Structure_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Structure-19">[18]</a></sup>

There are three basic vowels: *i* *a* *u*. These can be short or long; the long vowels are marked with a circumflex: *î* *â* *û*.<sup id="cite_ref-Vowels_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vowels-21">[20]</a></sup>

From older *ai* and *au* evolved *ê* and *ô*. These vowels are always long – short *e* and *o* does not occur.<sup id="cite_ref-Vowels_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vowels-21">[20]</a></sup> (Tolkien seems to omit the redundant circumflex on *ê* and *ô* in words like "[Ar-Zimraphel](/wiki/Ar-Zimraphel)"; Cf also the word *roth* "foam", which Tolkien gives the pronunciation key *róþ.*<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup>)

### Morphology {#Morphology}

Adûnaic words are based on roots that consist of one to three consonants and one characteristic vowel. Inflexions and derivations add infixes or affixes to these roots, but in all inflexions or derivations of a word the order of the consonants is consistent, and the characteristic vowel is always present (sometimes mutated into *ê* or *ô*).

This can be exemplified by the root GIMIL "star". From GIMIL is derived *gimli* "star", *gimil* "(all) the stars", *igmil* "star-shaped figure", *gimlu-nitîr* "star-kindler", *Gimlad* "Starwards", etc.<sup id="cite_ref-Structure_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Structure-19">[18]</a></sup>

#### Nouns {#Nouns}

##### Strong and weak nouns {#Strong_and_weak_nouns}

Adûnaic distinguishes between **strong** and **weak** nouns. Strong nouns are inflected by altering the final vowel of the root. Weak nouns are inflected by means of affixes.<sup id="cite_ref-Nouns_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nouns-23">[22]</a></sup>

##### Gender {#Gender}

Nouns have one of four different genders: **masculine**, **feminine**, **common** and **neuter**. The masculine and feminine genders designate beings of male and female sex, common gender designates beings of unspecified sex, and neuter designates inanimate nouns.<sup id="cite_ref-Nouns_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nouns-23">[22]</a></sup>

##### Case {#Case}

Nouns are inflected in three cases: **normal**, **subjective** and **objective**.

The subjective case is used to emphasize the subject of a sentence. In sentences with a noun in the subjective case, the copula can be omitted.

The objective case is used for the first noun in compounds, when that noun can be regarded as the object of the second noun.

The normal case is used in all other contexts.<sup id="cite_ref-Nouns_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nouns-23">[22]</a></sup>

##### Number {#Number}

Nouns have three numbers: **singular**, **dual** and **plural**. Singular is used for a single item. Only the singular can be inflected in the objective case. Plural is used for larger numbers. Dual is used for "natural pairs" such as parents, the sun and moon etc.<sup id="cite_ref-Nouns_23-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nouns-23">[22]</a></sup>

#### Verbs {#Verbs}

##### Tense {#Tense}

Verbs are inflected in four tenses: **aorist**, **past**, **continuative present** and **continuative past**.<sup id="cite_ref-Further_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Further-25">[24]</a></sup> Since several of these tenses have overlapping meanings, it is not entirely clear which tense is which.

The aorist tense is used as present, future, or "historic present or past in narrative".<sup id="cite_ref-Further_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Further-25">[24]</a></sup> Some verbs seem to form aorist by the suffix *-a*: *kalba* 'fell'<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> (*kalab* 'to fall'<sup id="cite_ref-Further_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Further-25">[24]</a></sup>).

The significance of the past tense depends on the significance of the aorist in the same context: if the aorist tens signifies the past, then the past tense signifies pluperfect ("remote past"). If the aorist signifies the future, then the past tense signifies future perfect (past in relation to the future).<sup id="cite_ref-Further_25-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Further-25">[24]</a></sup> Some verbs seem to form the past tense by doubling the final root consonant and adding the suffix *-a*: *kallaba* 'fell'.<sup id="cite_ref-Nouns_23-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nouns-23">[22]</a></sup>

Exactly how the continuative present and continuative past are used is not known. Some verbs seem to form the continuative present by changing the final root vowel to *u* and adding the suffix *-i*: *kalubi* 'lean over'.<sup id="cite_ref-night67_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-night67-27">[26]</a></sup> An example of the continuative past might be *azaggara* 'was warring' (*azgarâ* 'to wage war').<sup id="cite_ref-Further_25-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Further-25">[24]</a></sup>

##### Number {#Number_2}

Verbs agree in number with the noun they refer to. Plural verbs take the ending *-m*.

##### Person {#Person}

If the subject of a verb is a personal pronoun, it is expressed by a pronominal prefix. Pronominal prefixes are often used in combination with an independent noun, e.g. *bâr <u>u</u>kallaba* 'the lord \[<u>he</u>\] fell'.<sup id="cite_ref-Nouns_23-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nouns-23">[22]</a></sup> From the sources we can reconstruct an incomplete set of pronominal prefixes.

#### Adjectives {#Adjectives}

Very little is known about Adûnaic adjectives. We know that they show number agreement: adjectives that refer to plural nouns take the ending *-î*: <i>batîna lôkh<u>î</u></i> 'roads \[are\] crooked'.<sup id="cite_ref-night67_27-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-night67-27">[26]</a></sup>

If the noun referred to takes the subjective case, a copula is assumed (see example above). If the noun takes the normal case, the adjective is an attribute of the noun: *izindi batân* 'straight road'.<sup id="cite_ref-night67_27-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-night67-27">[26]</a></sup>

The adjective *burôda* is usually translated 'heavy'<sup id="cite_ref-night67_27-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-night67-27">[26]</a></sup> but once 'very heavy'.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup> This might imply that adjectives are compared by altering the root vowels.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Adûnaic* is an Anglicized name of the language. The Númenóreans called their language *Adûnayân*.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup> Later [Westron](/wiki/Westron) was called *Adûni* by its speakers.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Adûnaic has a faintly Semitic flavour.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup> The triconsonantal structure of the majority of the word-bases of Adûnaic is reminiscent of Semitic. However, the vocalic arrangements in the word-bases do not resemble Semitic much.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[32]</a></sup> Tolkien said the Númenórean language is based on [Hebrew](/wiki/Hebrew).<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup>
