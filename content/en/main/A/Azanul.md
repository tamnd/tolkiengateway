<i><b>azanul</b></i> is a [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) word that can mean the adjective "of the shadows, dim", or the while name "Dim-rill"

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word has the stem *azan* the plural form of <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Uzn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Uzn (page does not exist)">uzn</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> ([Root](/wiki/Sundocarm%C3%AB "Sundocarmë") Z-N).

It is possible that the following is the adjectival ending *[-ul](/wiki/-ul)*.

It is also possible that the full word is *Azanûl* meaning "Dim rill", containing the noun *[ûl](/wiki/%C3%9Bl "Ûl")* "Stream, rill".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Example {#Example}

- [Azanulbizar](/wiki/Azanulbizar)

<dl><dd>It can be analyzed as <i>(<a href="/wiki/Duban" title="Duban">duban</a>) Azanul-bizar</i> &#34;dim-rill (dale)&#34; or <i>Azan-ûl bizar</i> &#34;dim-rill dale&#34;</dd></dl>

## Other versions {#Other_versions}

Tolkien intended the word *Azanûl* to be the full name of that valley, but settled on *Azanulbizar*. The interpretation of the word, as well as that of the *[bizar](/wiki/Bizar "Bizar")* have changed. Tolkien experimented with various interpretations of the different elements, though the whole was to somehow express "Dimrill Dale". <span></span>
