**Amalion** was the name of a [tree](../T/Trees.md), which [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) regularly drew in illustrations.<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The name does not appear in any of the stories of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Carl F. Hostetter](../C/Carl_F._Hostetter.md) has suggested that the name *Amalion* derives from [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *amalya* ("rich, blessed"), from *amal* ("riches, blessing, bliss, good fortune"), related to a later form, *alam*, with the same meaning, and also *alam* ("[elm-tree](../E/Elms.md)") as in *[The Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other works {#Other_works}

[Wayne G. Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) have suggested that Amalion is related to the "Tree of Tales" in *[On Fairy-Stories](../O/On_Fairy-Stories.md)* and [Niggle](../N/Niggle.md)'s Tree in *[Leaf by Niggle](../L/Leaf_by_Niggle.md)*. A version of the tree was used as cover art for *[Tree and Leaf](../T/Tree_and_Leaf.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>
