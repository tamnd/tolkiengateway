**Amalion** was the name of a [tree](/wiki/Trees "Trees"), which [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") regularly drew in illustrations.<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The name does not appear in any of the stories of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium").

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Carl F. Hostetter](/wiki/Carl_F._Hostetter) has suggested that the name *Amalion* derives from [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *amalya* ("rich, blessed"), from *amal* ("riches, blessing, bliss, good fortune"), related to a later form, *alam*, with the same meaning, and also *alam* ("[elm-tree](/wiki/Elms "Elms")") as in *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other works {#Other_works}

[Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) have suggested that Amalion is related to the "Tree of Tales" in *[On Fairy-Stories](/wiki/On_Fairy-Stories)* and [Niggle](/wiki/Niggle)'s Tree in *[Leaf by Niggle](/wiki/Leaf_by_Niggle)*. A version of the tree was used as cover art for *[Tree and Leaf](/wiki/Tree_and_Leaf)*.<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>
