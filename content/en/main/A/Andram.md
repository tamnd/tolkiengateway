**Andram** ([S](../S/Sindarin.md). 'long wall'; [and](And.md) = long, [ram](../R/Ram.md) = wall) was a "dividing fall"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> that ran across [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) west to east, from beyond [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md) to the [Gates of Sirion](../G/Gates_of_Sirion.md) and ending at [Ramdal](../R/Ramdal.md), being three leagues in width from north to south.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The wall was pierced in two places: the gorge of Nargothrond, through which flowed the River [Narog](../N/Narog.md), and where the River [Sirion](../S/Sirion.md) ran underground from its falls south of the [Aelin-uial](Aelin-uial.md) to its reappearance at the [Gates of Sirion](../G/Gates_of_Sirion.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Its easternmost hill was [Amon Ereb](Amon_Ereb.md), which usually was not considered a part of Andram (indeed *Ramdal* meant "Wall's End").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

On [the Second 'Silmarillion' Map](../T/The_Second_'Silmarillion'_Map.md), Andram is depicted as a long chain of hills, from the Falls of Sirion in the west to Rhamdal in the east, with a bend (not appearing in the [Map of Beleriand and the Lands to the North](../M/Map_of_Beleriand_and_the_Lands_to_the_North.md)) in the middle. These hills are barely visible on the original map because they are drawn in pencil, but they appear very clearly on the map drawn by Christopher Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.
