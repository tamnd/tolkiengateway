**Andram** ([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"). 'long wall'; [and](/wiki/And "And") = long, [ram](/wiki/Ram "Ram") = wall) was a "dividing fall"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> that ran across [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) west to east, from beyond [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond) to the [Gates of Sirion](/wiki/Gates_of_Sirion) and ending at [Ramdal](/wiki/Ramdal), being three leagues in width from north to south.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The wall was pierced in two places: the gorge of Nargothrond, through which flowed the River [Narog](/wiki/Narog), and where the River [Sirion](/wiki/Sirion) ran underground from its falls south of the [Aelin-uial](/wiki/Aelin-uial) to its reappearance at the [Gates of Sirion](/wiki/Gates_of_Sirion).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Its easternmost hill was [Amon Ereb](/wiki/Amon_Ereb), which usually was not considered a part of Andram (indeed *Ramdal* meant "Wall's End").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

On [the Second 'Silmarillion' Map](/wiki/The_Second_%27Silmarillion%27_Map "The Second 'Silmarillion' Map"), Andram is depicted as a long chain of hills, from the Falls of Sirion in the west to Rhamdal in the east, with a bend (not appearing in the [Map of Beleriand and the Lands to the North](/wiki/Map_of_Beleriand_and_the_Lands_to_the_North)) in the middle. These hills are barely visible on the original map because they are drawn in pencil, but they appear very clearly on the map drawn by Christopher Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.
