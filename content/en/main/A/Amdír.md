**Amdír**, also called **Malgalad**, was the father of [Amroth](/wiki/Amroth) and a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindar "Sindar") [King of Lórien](/wiki/King_of_L%C3%B3rien) who was slain during the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad).<sup id="cite_ref-AaN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AaN-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Little is known of Amdír's early history.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup> [Aldarion](/wiki/Aldarion) is noted as visiting 'the country of [Amroth'](/wiki/Amroth "Amroth") (Amdír's son) on the Bay of Belfalas in about [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [730](/wiki/Second_Age_730 "Second Age 730"), which might provide an early reference to Amdír's family. However, it is unclear whether the land marked on the Third Age map as Dol Amroth was known by that name when Aldarion visited it.<sup id="cite_ref-Note_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note-3">[2]</a></sup>

Peace and obscurity in Amdír's realm lasted until the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and the subsequent return of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-AaN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AaN-1">[1]</a></sup> Though he answered the call of [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) to the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), he was not disposed to serve under the [High King of the Noldor](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor). Instead, he brought "*as large a force as he could muster*" and joined forces with the greater army of [Silvan Elves](/wiki/Silvan_Elves) under King [Oropher](/wiki/Oropher), the Sindarin-lord of the [Woodland Realm](/wiki/Woodland_Realm).<sup id="cite_ref-Princes_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Princes-4">[3]</a></sup>

Ill-equipped compared to the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) and highly independent, the Silvan Elves suffered heavy losses in the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad). It was there that Amdír and more than half his army perished, being cut off from the main host and driven into the [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes).<sup id="cite_ref-Princes_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Princes-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *[amdir](</wiki/Amdir_(Sindarin_word)> "Amdir (Sindarin word)")*, without the long *í*, is recorded as a word for "Hope", or "looking up", in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup> However, the name <i><b>Amdír</b></i> with a long *i* may rather possibly derive from a shortened form of Sindarin <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=D%C3%AEr&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Dîr (page does not exist)">dîr</a></i> (meaning an "adult male"), chiefly used as ending in proper names.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

The King of Lórien is called <i><b>Malgalad</b></i> in one text, detailing the fate of the Sindarin kings in the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad). [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) argues that, since they are contemporaries and both described as Kings that died in the Battle of Dagorlad, they are likely the same. It is unclear however which form was abandoned. *Malgalad* likely means "Gold-shine", from *[malt](/wiki/Malt "Malt")* ("gold, the metal)"<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup> and *[galad](/wiki/Galad "Galad")* ("light, radiance, glittering, reflection from jewels, glass or polished metal, or water)"<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> or "Golden-tree", from the outdated spelling of *[galadh](/wiki/Galadh "Galadh")*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>
