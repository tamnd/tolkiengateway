<i><b>A Secret Vice</b></i> is the title of a lecture written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) around the autumn of [1931](/wiki/1931).<sup id="cite_ref-RG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RG-1">[1]</a></sup> The lecture deals with constructed languages in general, and the relation of a mythology to its language. Tolkien contrasts auxiliary languages (like Esperanto) with artistic languages constructed for aesthetic pleasure.<sup id="cite_ref-MC6_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC6-2">[2]</a></sup>

The title (consisting of a phrase occurring in the lecture) was created by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) for the publication of the lecture manuscript in *[The Monsters and the Critics](/wiki/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Tolkien himself referred to the lecture as "A Secret Vice" in a letter.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [2016](/wiki/2016), it was published as a separate book *[A Secret Vice: Tolkien on Invented Languages](/wiki/A_Secret_Vice:_Tolkien_on_Invented_Languages)*, edited by [Dimitra Fimi](/wiki/Dimitra_Fimi) and [Andrew Higgins](/wiki/Andrew_Higgins). The book provides an introduction and commentaries to the essay, as well as some previously unseen drafts and notes.

## Summary {#Summary}

Tolkien begins by briefly discussing [Esperanto](/wiki/Esperanto) but states that the topic of his lecture rather concerns "secret" languages. As an example of such a language, he first recalls an incident where he overheard a man working on his own "secret grammar" while Tolkien was in the army in the [First World War](/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"). He then continues by mentioning and analysing two constructed languages: the children's play-languages [Animalic](/wiki/Animalic) and [Nevbosh](/wiki/Nevbosh). The next constructed language discussed is [Naffarin](/wiki/Naffarin), a more advanced, private language "partly overlapping the last stages of *Nevbosh*". Finally, Tolkien discusses the languages he created for his [mythology](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") and gives examples of his [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) poetry (with translations in English).<sup id="cite_ref-MC6_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC6-2">[2]</a></sup>

Included in the lecture are:

- a fragment in [Nevbosh](/wiki/Nevbosh)
- a fragment in [Naffarin](/wiki/Naffarin)
- the poem *[Oilima Markirya](/wiki/Oilima_Markirya)* (in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya))<sup id="cite_ref-PE16_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE16-5">[5]</a></sup>
- the poem *[Nieninque](/wiki/Nieninque)* (in Qenya)<sup id="cite_ref-PE16_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE16-5">[5]</a></sup>
- the poem *[Éarendel at the Helm](/wiki/%C3%89arendel_at_the_Helm)* (in Qenya)<sup id="cite_ref-PE16_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE16-5">[5]</a></sup>
- a fragment in [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) (beginning with "[Dir avosaith a gwaew hinar](/wiki/Dir_avosaith_a_gwaew_hinar)")<sup id="cite_ref-RG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RG-1">[1]</a></sup>
