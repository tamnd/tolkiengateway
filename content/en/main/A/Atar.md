<i><b>atar</b></i> (pl. *atari*) is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word meaning "father".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

- [CE](/wiki/CE) *atar*<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
- [Root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [ATA](/wiki/ATA)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other forms {#Other_forms}

- <i><b>atar</b>inya</i> = "my father"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
- <i><b>atar</b>emma</i> = "Our father"
- <i><b>atar</b>en</i> (dative) = "To father"<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

*Atar* can be compared to Greek/Latin *pater*.

In Celtic branch the Indoeuropean initial p- was lost thence we have forms similar to *atar*, like Irish *athair* (from Proto-Celtic <i>*ɸatīr</i>).

Note also Turkish *atto*. <span></span>
