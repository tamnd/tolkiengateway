**Atani** was a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name for [Men](/wiki/Men),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> and especially the [Men](/wiki/Men) of the [Three Houses](/wiki/Edain#The_Three_Houses "Edain") of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain). However it was seldomly applied to the Men east of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Atan* pl. *Atani* is glossed as "Second People" in the Silmarillion Index. (cf: *[atta](/wiki/Atta "Atta")* "two").

The equivalents of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) are *[Adan](/wiki/Adan)* pl. *Edain*.

The word appears in names such as [Núnatani](/wiki/N%C3%BAnatani), <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Hr%C3%B3atani&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Hróatani (page does not exist)">Hróatani</a>, [Atanalcar](/wiki/Atanalcar), [Atanamir](/wiki/Atanamir), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Atanatar" title="Atanatar">Atanatar</a> and [Atanatari](/wiki/Atanatari).

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In a draft to the text *[Of Dwarves and Men](/wiki/Of_Dwarves_and_Men)*, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) considered that the word is derived from the [language](/wiki/Taliska "Taliska") of the [Folk of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor"), but as [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) noted, it contradicted the final version of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
