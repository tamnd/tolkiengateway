**Amnon** was a [prophet](/wiki/Seer "Seer") of old according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*. His most known prophesy was "*Great is the [fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin")*", which was quoted again by [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon), King of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), at the time the event took place.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the first version of the [Doom of Mandos](/wiki/Doom_of_Mandos) appearing in *The Book of Lost Tales*, it is a servant of [Vefántur](/wiki/Vef%C3%A1ntur) who proclaims, among other misfortunes, the fall of Gondolin; and the [Noldoli](/wiki/Noldoli) remembered those words as the "Prophecies of Amnos" (*[Amnos](/wiki/Amnos)* being the place where they had heard them).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Later, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) wrote a couple of notes referring to the "Prophecy of Amnon", with the words "*Great is the fall of Gondolin. When the [lily of the valley](/wiki/Lothengriol "Lothengriol") withers then shall Turgon fade*", but it is unclear if here *Amnon* is a place or a person. As [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) suggests, the prophet Amnon could be the servant of Vefántur that proclaimed the doom of the Noldoli.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In the Bible, Amnon was the wicked eldest son of David. [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") was surely aware of this character (as he was a Catholic), and it is curious to speculate on his particular choosing of this name, as it was in his earlier works.
