**Arossiach**, the **Fords of Aros**, was the ford on the upper reaches of the River [Aros](/wiki/Aros), where the long and perilous road through the valley of [Nan Dungortheb](/wiki/Nan_Dungortheb) crossed eastward into the lands of [East Beleriand](/wiki/East_Beleriand).

## Geography {#Geography}

The River [Aros](/wiki/Aros) had its sources in two streams that flowed southward out of the hills of [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion). Where those streams met, an ancient east-west road crossed the upper reaches of the river at a ford. The ford marked the boundary between two regions. To the east was [Himlad](/wiki/Himlad), and the other lands held by the sons of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor). To the west was [Dor Dínen](/wiki/Dor_D%C3%ADnen), and beyond it the perilous valley of [Nan Dungortheb](/wiki/Nan_Dungortheb).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The Fords of Aros seem to date back to the days before the beginning of the [Years of the Sun](/wiki/Years_of_the_Sun), when the roads north of [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath) were safe to travel. After the coming of [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant), and the raising of the [Girdle of Melian](/wiki/Girdle_of_Melian), the lands west of the Arossiach became a haunted and dangerous place, and in the late [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) the Fords were only used by the bravest or most desperate travellers.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

[Aredhel](/wiki/Aredhel), after leaving her brother [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) and the city of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), desired to see the sons of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor). Denied entrance into [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), she took the road north of the Hidden Kingdom and crossed the Aros at the Arossiach on her journey into Himlad.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Arossiach* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name meaning "Fords of Aros", from *[Aros](/wiki/Aros)* + *[iach](/wiki/Iach "Iach")* ("ford").
