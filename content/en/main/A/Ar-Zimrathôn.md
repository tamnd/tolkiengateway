**Ar-Zimrathôn** was the son and heir to [Ar-Adûnakhôr](Ar-Adûnakhôr.md), and became the twenty-first [King of Númenor](../K/King_of_Númenor.md). His father was the first King to take his name in the [Adûnaic](Adûnaic.md) tongue, and Ar-Zimrathôn continued this tradition by doing the same, though the [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) form of his name, [Tar-Hostamir](../T/Tar-Hostamir.md), was recorded in the Scroll of Kings.<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Zimrathon_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Zimrathon-1">[1]</a></sup>

Ar-Zimrathôn held the Sceptre of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) for seventy-one years,<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Zimrathon_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Zimrathon-1">[1]</a></sup> and was succeeded by his son [Ar-Sakalthôr](Ar-Sakalthôr.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Sakalthor_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Sakalthor-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Zimrathôn* can mean "Collector of Jewels" or "Many Jewels" in [Adûnaic](Adûnaic.md), depending the reading of his Quenya name.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The meaning of the Adûnaic prefix *Ar-* is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "king" and is a prefixal form of *ârû* ("king") or derived from the same root.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

His [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) name, *Hostamir* can mean "Many Jewels" or "Collector of Jewels" depending if *[hosta](../H/Hosta.md)* is read as a noun ("many") or a verb ("gather, collect"); (*[mir](../M/Mir.md)*) meaning "jewel".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Like for all the rulers of Númenor whose name was inscribed in the Scroll of Kings, the prefix *tar-* ("high") was added to his name.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
