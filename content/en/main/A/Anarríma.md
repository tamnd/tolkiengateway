**Anarríma** was one of the constellations set in the heavens by [Varda](/wiki/Varda) to enlighten the awakening of the elves and gathered by Varda from among the ancient stars.<sup id="cite_ref-S_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-1">[1]</a></sup>

The making of the constellations (and of some new bright stars) happened 1000-1050 [Valian Years](/wiki/Valian_Years) after the first flowering of the Trees.<sup id="cite_ref-MR_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The elements of the name seem to be *[anar](/wiki/Anar "Anar")* ("sun") and *ríma* ("edge, hem, border"), thus: "Sun-border".<sup id="cite_ref-LR_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Anarríma is not identifiable with an actual constellation. The word *ríma* might refer to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_Borealis" title="wikipedia:Corona Borealis">Corona Borealis</a> (the Northern Crown) or the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Square_of_Pegasus" title="wikipedia:Great Square of Pegasus">Great Square of Pegasus</a>, easily recognized from its four bright stars.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Anarríma entered the mythology only in "[The Later *Quenta Silmarillion*](/wiki/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion "The Later Quenta Silmarillion")", composed after the completion of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, in early 1950s.<sup id="cite_ref-MR_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-2">[2]</a></sup> In the previous versions of the story, only [Valacirca](/wiki/Valacirca) and [Telumehtar](/wiki/Telumehtar) / [Menelmacar](/wiki/Menelmacar) are mentioned. <span></span>
