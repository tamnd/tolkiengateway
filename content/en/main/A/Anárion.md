**Anárion** was the second son of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and the brother of [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur).<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-1">[1]</a></sup> His son [Meneldil](/wiki/Meneldil) was the last man born in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) before its [Downfall](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor").<sup id="cite_ref-Gladden_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gladden-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Anárion, together with his father and brother, were the leaders of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful), the small minority of [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) that resisted the dark rule of [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n) and his councilor [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron). In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3319](/wiki/Second_Age_3319 "Second Age 3319"), when [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n) assailed the land of [Aman](/wiki/Aman),<sup id="cite_ref-SA_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-3">[3]</a></sup> nine ships of the Faithful escaped to the east (four for Elendil, three for Isildur, and two for Anárion).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> They were separated by the great storm that arose during the [Downfall](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor"). Elendil landed in northern [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) near [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) and there established the realm of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor). Isildur and Anárion, however, landed in the south and founded the realm of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320") about the [Mouths of Anduin](/wiki/Mouths_of_Anduin).<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-1">[1]</a></sup>

Together, Anárion and Isildur were chiefly responsible for the early ordering of Gondor, and their thrones were set side by side in the Great Hall of [Osgiliath](/wiki/Osgiliath). In the city of [Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Anor), Anárion established his house while Isildur set up his in [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil).

Sauron also survived the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and reentered [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) in [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320"). In [3429](/wiki/Second_Age_3429 "Second Age 3429"), Sauron attacked Gondor with a force great enough to [take Minas Ithil](/wiki/First_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil "First Fall of Minas Ithil") and force Isildur to flee. Whilst Isildur, his wife, and his sons sailed from Gondor seeking Elendil’s aid, Anárion defended Osgiliath and was able to drive his forces back into the mountains. Yet Sauron gathered greater strength and Anárion knew that without succor Gondor would fall.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-3">[3]</a></sup>

Aid did arrive when Elendil, Isildur, and the [Men of Arnor](/wiki/Men_of_Arnor), in league with the [High King of the Noldor](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor), [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad), formed the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) and marched south. Anárion led the [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) to the [plain](/wiki/Dagorlad "Dagorlad") between [Cirith Gorgor](/wiki/Cirith_Gorgor) and the [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes) and fought in the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad) alongside the alliance. The allies entered [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) and laid the [Siege of Barad-dûr](/wiki/Siege_of_Barad-d%C3%BBr), but Anárion was slain by a thrown rock in [3440](/wiki/Second_Age_3440 "Second Age 3440").<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

[Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Anor) and its attached land of [Anórien](/wiki/An%C3%B3rien) were named following the heraldic meaning of his name.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Anárion had four children, the youngest of whom was a son, [Meneldil](/wiki/Meneldil).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 1]</a></sup> It is said that he was well-pleased at the departure of Isildur and his sons.<sup id="cite_ref-Gladden_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gladden-2">[2]</a></sup> After the [Disaster of the Gladden Fields](/wiki/Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields), Meneldil became the first independent [King of Gondor](/wiki/King_of_Gondor) and the two realms in exile were estranged.

Anárion's war-helm, which was also used as a crown, was crushed when he was killed. The war-helm of [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) was used for the [crowning](/wiki/Crown_of_Gondor "Crown of Gondor") of the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

The [Line of Anárion](/wiki/House_of_An%C3%A1rion "House of Anárion") lasted over two thousand years, their members being known as *Anárioni*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup> In honor of the founding co-rulers, when [Rómendacil II](/wiki/R%C3%B3mendacil_II) built the [Argonath](/wiki/Argonath) he had the Pillars of the Kings carved in the likenesses of Anárion and Isildur.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> Yet the male line of Anárion dwindled and finally perished when King [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur) died childless. From that time forth the [Ruling Stewards](/wiki/Stewards_of_Gondor "Stewards of Gondor") ruled in the name of the House of Anárion.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>

<a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Elessar" title="Elessar">Elessar</a>, the Heir of Isildur who would rule the reunited kingdoms, was also a descendant of Anárion through [Fíriel](/wiki/F%C3%ADriel), daughter of [Ondoher](/wiki/Ondoher).

*Anárion* is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name. Although its meaning is not glossed, it is ostensibly a compound of [anar](/wiki/Anar "Anar") ("sun") and the patronymic suffix [-ion](/wiki/-ion) ("son of"), therefore having the meaning "Son of the Sun".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*, in Section VII "[The Heirs of Elendil](/wiki/The_Heirs_of_Elendil)", [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) explained that there were three manuscripts in the textual history of this section. Manuscript A contained the information about the Southern Line of Gondor, and from its condition it appeared to be the first stage of composition. Manuscript B added the Northern Line, and began as a fair copy of A for the Southern line but became heavily amended. Manuscript B led to Manuscript C, a fine copy (but with later emendations), which led to a final typescript D. In the earlier Manuscripts A and B, Anárion's date of birth was [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3209](/wiki/Second_Age_3209 "Second Age 3209") instead of [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3219](/wiki/Second_Age_3219 "Second Age 3219"). It was Isildur who was born in 3219 instead of 3209 and thus Anárion was the *older* of Elendil's two sons.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-5">[5]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>In the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King_(Extended_Edition)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King (Extended Edition)">Extended Edition</a>, Denethor refers to himself as being &#34;Steward of the House of Anárion&#34; before he lights his pyre.
