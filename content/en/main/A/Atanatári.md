**Atanatári** ([Q](/wiki/Quenya "Quenya"), pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[aˌtanaˈtaːri]</span>; singular **Atanatar**, pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[aˈtanatar]</span>) is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) term which means "Fathers of [Men](/wiki/Men)", and is used to describe the forefathers of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Its use is not exactly clear: sometimes it is used to refer to the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), other times it is only applied to [Bëor](/wiki/B%C3%ABor), [Marach](/wiki/Marach) and contemporaries, and in yet other places it is used to refer to those peoples the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) are descended from.

If the third meaning is adopted, it can be said that the so-called [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) (the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim), [Men](/wiki/Men) of [Dale](/wiki/Dale), etc.) are also descendants from the *Atanatári*, like the *[Edain](/wiki/Edain)* (or later [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain)).

The [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) form of the word is <i><b><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Edenedair" title="Edenedair">Edenedair</a></b></i> (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[eˈdeneda͡ɪr]</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> <span></span>
