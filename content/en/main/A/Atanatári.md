**Atanatári** ([Q](../Q/Quenya.md), pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[aˌtanaˈtaːri]</span>; singular **Atanatar**, pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[aˈtanatar]</span>) is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) term which means "Fathers of [Men](../M/Men.md)", and is used to describe the forefathers of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Its use is not exactly clear: sometimes it is used to refer to the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), other times it is only applied to [Bëor](../B/Bëor.md), [Marach](../M/Marach.md) and contemporaries, and in yet other places it is used to refer to those peoples the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) are descended from.

If the third meaning is adopted, it can be said that the so-called [Middle Men](../M/Middle_Men.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) (the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md), [Men](../M/Men.md) of [Dale](../D/Dale.md), etc.) are also descendants from the *Atanatári*, like the *[Edain](../E/Edain.md)* (or later [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md)).

The [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) form of the word is <i><b><a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Edenedair.md" title="Edenedair">Edenedair</a></b></i> (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[eˈdeneda͡ɪr]</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> <span></span>
