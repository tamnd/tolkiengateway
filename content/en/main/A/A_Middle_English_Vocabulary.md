<i><b>A Middle English Vocabulary</b></i> is a glossary of [Middle English](../M/Middle_English.md) words complied by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md), published in [1922](../_/1922.md).

It was intended to be published together with <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Kenneth_Sisam&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Kenneth Sisam (page does not exist)">Kenneth Sisam</a>'s *[Fourteenth Century Verse &amp; Prose](../F/Fourteenth_Century_Verse_&_Prose.md)* in [1921](../_/1921.md), but it was not finished in time, so it was published separately in [1922](../_/1922.md), which became Tolkien's first published book.

The *Vocabulary* contains some 4,740 entries with nearly 6,800 definitions, running to 167 pages.<sup id="cite_ref-a_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-a-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Appearance {#Appearance}

*A Middle English Vocabulary* was published on [May 11](../_/11_May.md), [1922](../_/1922.md), to be a supplement to Sisam's *Verse &amp; Prose* published the year before.<sup id="cite_ref-Biblio_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Biblio-2">[2]</a></sup> The separate binding is unpaged, and titled "Glossary", rather than "Vocabulary", on the title page.<sup id="cite_ref-Lee_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lee-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Publication {#Publication}

Sisam was Tolkien's old tutor of English Language and Literature, and also involved the *[Oxford English Dictionary](../O/Oxford_English_Dictionary.md)*, working on several words under the letter *S*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> After that section was published, Oxford University Press asked him to write a compilation of fourteenth century verse and prose. By 1919, it turned out Sisam did not have time to compile the glossary, so Tolkien was asked to do it. The project, originally planned to be little else than a glossary, quickly became a much larger work. Tolkien was given time off from his regular work on the OED, and even continued working on the glossary after his transfer to [Leeds University](../L/Leeds_University.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ring_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ring-5">[5]</a></sup> The publication had been delayed due to private matters, and Tolkien was glad when it was finally finished. Sisam's book was, in the end, published without the glossary.<sup id="cite_ref-L6_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L6-6">[6]</a></sup> Later editions of Sisam's book did include Tolkien's glossary.<sup id="cite_ref-Biblio_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Biblio-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Method {#Method}

Tolkien gained praise for his glossary, which was unlike most others. Whereas it was a trend of the days to suggest (often questionable) etymologies of rare and obscure words, Tolkien chose to devote his time to the different meanings of common words, such as *to*, *he* and *habben* ("to have"). His Vocabulary is filled with textual references,<sup id="cite_ref-Lee_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lee-3">[3]</a></sup> and much of his findings elaborated on the OED, pre-dating words by sometimes a century. Some of these suggestions were taken into account in later editions of the OED.<sup id="cite_ref-Ring_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ring-5">[5]</a></sup>
