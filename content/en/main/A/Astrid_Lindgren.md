**Astrid Anna Emilia Lindgren** ([14 November](/wiki/14_November) [1907](/wiki/1907) – [28 January](/wiki/28_January) [2002](/wiki/2002)) was a Swedish author and screenwriter.

Lindgren wrote three children's books in the genre of fantasy: *[Mio, min Mio](/wiki/Mio,_min_Mio)* (1954; *Mio, my Son*), *Bröderna Lejonhjärta* (1973; *The Brothers Lionheart*), and *Ronja Rövardotter* (1981; *Ronia the Robber's Daughter*). The first two novels might be caracterized as stories of fantasy reminiscent of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, where the heroes return home after a perilous adventure. And the latter, *Ronia the Robber's Daughter*, has been described as a work reminding of the genre <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_fantasy" title="wikipedia:High fantasy">High fantasy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the late 1940s, when [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s *The Hobbit* was translated to Swedish as *[Hompen](/wiki/Hompen)*, Lindgren was responsible for the department of Children's literature at Rabén &amp; Sjögren (the publishers of the translation).<sup id="cite_ref-Lena_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lena-2">[2]</a></sup>

However, apparently the works of Tolkien did not have a direct influence on Lindgren's own works. While she has been attributed saying that Tolkien had a great impact on her work,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> other informed sources claim that Lindgren in several interviews denied any influence by Tolkien and that she had not even read *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-Lena_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lena-2">[2]</a></sup>
