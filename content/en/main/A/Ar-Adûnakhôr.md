**Ar-Adûnakhôr** was the son of King [Tar-Ardamin](../T/Tar-Ardamin.md) and the twentieth [King of Númenor](../K/King_of_Númenor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Adûnakhôr was born in a time when most [Númenóreans](../N/Númenóreans.md) already had stopped using the [Elven tongues](../E/Elvish.md) generations before, although it was still customary for the [Kings of Númenor](../K/Kings_of_Númenor.md) to take their royal names in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md).

When Ar-Adûnakhôr succeeded his father, however, he was the first King to break with tradition and took his title in [Adûnaic](Adûnaic.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppAr-Adunakhor_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppAr-Adunakhor-4">[4]</a></sup> Tar-Herunúmen, a name in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) was still recorded in the [Scroll of Kings](../S/Scroll_of_Kings.md), because the kings feared that a break with this ancient custom could cause evil to befall.<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

His names were perceived as blasphemous and ill-omened<sup id="cite_ref-AppAr-Adunakhor_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppAr-Adunakhor-4">[4]</a></sup> by the [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md) Númenóreans, because they mean "Lord of the West", a title that the Faithful only used for one of the [Valar](../V/Valar.md), especially [Manwë](../M/Manwë.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup>

During his reign the Elven-tongues were no longer used, nor permitted to be taught, but were maintained in secret by the Faithful. After that elven ships from [Tol Eressëa](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md) only came seldom and secretly to the west shores of Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup>

Númenórean settlers in the north of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) (between the [Gulf of Lune](../G/Gulf_of_Lune.md) and the city of [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md)) refused to participate in the rebellion against the Valar and were joined by many exiles of the Faithful who fled from persecution by Ar-Adûnakhôr and the later Kings of Númenor. During the reign of Ar-Adûnakhôr the term "[Middle Men](../M/Middle_Men.md)" originated from these settlers.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

He was succeeded to the throne by his son, [Ar-Zimrathôn](Ar-Zimrathôn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Zimrathon_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Zimrathon-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Etymology and names {#Etymology_and_names}

*Adûnakhôr* is an [Adûnaic](Adûnaic.md) name that means "Lord of the West".<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup> It can be analyzed as *[adûn](Adûn.md)* "West" ([S.](../S/Sindarin.md) *dûn*) and *khôr* "Lord" ([Q.](../Q/Quenya.md) *heru*). It is said that as a title, it signifies [Manwë](../M/Manwë.md). Although the name was taken in defiance of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md), ironically its elements are said to be of [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) origin.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> See also *[ar-](Ar.md#Adûnaic)*.

*Herunúmen* is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) name that means "Lord of the West".<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Adunakhor_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Adunakhor-1">[1]</a></sup> Ostensibly a compound of [heru](../H/Heru.md) ("lord") and [númen](../N/Númen.md) ("west").<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> Like for all the rulers of Númenor whose name was inscribed in the Scroll of Kings, the prefix *[tar](../T/Tar.md)-* ("high") was added to his name.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
