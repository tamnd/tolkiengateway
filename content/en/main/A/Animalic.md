**Animalic** was a private language invented by [Mary Incledon](/wiki/Mary_Incledon) and <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Marjorie_Incledon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Marjorie Incledon (page does not exist)">Marjorie Incledon</a>, [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s young cousins, during the first years of the 1900's.<sup id="cite_ref-MC6_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC6-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-B2_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B2-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> It was probably the first introduction of artificial languages to Tolkien.

## Fragment {#Fragment}

Tolkien, then about ten years old, was taught some of the language which his cousins used 'fluently to the dismay of bystanders'. Tolkien says he was 'never fully instructed in it, nor a proper Animalic-speaker', but he cites by memory in his lecture *[A Secret Vice](/wiki/A_Secret_Vice)* the only surviving fragment of the Animalic: **Dog nightingale woodpecker forty**, meaning "you are an ass". **Donkey** was the Animalic word for numeral "forty", while **forty** had the converse meaning. In general, Animalic was almost entirely constructed 'out of English animal, bird, and fish names'.<sup id="cite_ref-MC6_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC6-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-B2_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B2-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SV9_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SV9-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Otter? {#Otter?}

Fimi and Higgins propose that Tolkien assumed (or was given) an Animalic name **Otter** for himself. In the year 1909 he wrote under the title *[Book of the Foxrook](/wiki/Book_of_the_Foxrook)* of his notebook in Esperanto *Privata al LUTTRO* 'Private to OTTER'. According to the editors, "if this was Tolkien's name in Animalic, it is significant given the fact that in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)* Tolkien's fictional informant is the Anglian mariner [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol), whose original name was *[Ottor](/wiki/Ottor)*, an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) form of *otter*".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Characterization {#Characterization}

It appears that the Animalic was a rather awkward language, Tolkien himself calls it "crude (in some ways) in the extreme" and characterized by "a complete absence of phonematic invention which at least in embryo is usually an element in all such constructions.<sup id="cite_ref-MC6_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC6-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SV9_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SV9-4">[4]</a></sup>

## After Animalic {#After_Animalic}

Later on, Tolkien developed another, more sophisticated language, [Nevbosh](/wiki/Nevbosh), with Mary Incledon, Marjorie having lost her interest in such activities.<sup id="cite_ref-B2_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B2-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> After that Tolkien began constructing languages of his own, the first of these being [Naffarin](/wiki/Naffarin).<sup id="cite_ref-B2_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B2-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
