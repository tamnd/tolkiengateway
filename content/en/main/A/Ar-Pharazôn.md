> *"Ar-Pharazôn the Golden was the proudest and most powerful of all the Kings, and no less than the kingship of the world was his desire."* —*[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, [Appendix A](/wiki/Appendix_A)

**Ar-**Pharazôn **the Golden** was the twenty-fifth and last [King](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor "King of Númenor") of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> He was the son of [Gimilkhâd](/wiki/Gimilkh%C3%A2d), who was the younger brother of the twenty-fourth King, [Tar-Palantir](/wiki/Tar-Palantir). Ar-Pharazôn's willful rule, and his great pride, led directly to the [world-changing](/wiki/Bent_World "Bent World") [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and the founding of the [realms in exile](/wiki/Realms_in_exile "Realms in exile") of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In his youth, Pharazôn and [Amandil](/wiki/Amandil), the [Lord of Andúnië](/wiki/Lords_of_And%C3%BAni%C3%AB "Lords of Andúnië") and later the leader of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful) Númenóreans, were dear friends. After some time, Pharazôn departed from Númenor to participate in the wars in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) that the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men) were waging against [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron). While in Middle-earth, he became a great captain and commander of the Númenórean armies. Pharazôn did not return to Númenor until a few years before the death of Tar-Palantir, when he heard of his own father's early death. During his time there, Pharazôn was generous with the wealth he had acquired in Middle-earth, and the hearts of the people were turned toward him.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup> Like his father, Pharazôn became the leader of the rebellion against his uncle the King's reforms, who attempted to return to the ways of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### King of Númenor {#King_of_Númenor}

He remained on the island until Tar-Palantir died. At that time, Pharazôn took the King's daughter [Míriel](/wiki/Tar-M%C3%ADriel "Tar-Míriel") as his wife, much against her will, and against the laws of Númenor which prohibited first cousins from marrying. Pharazôn then changed her name to Ar-Zimraphel.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup> Thus he usurped the throne and, taking the [Sceptre of Númenor](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor) into his own hand, proclaimed himself King Ar-Pharazôn in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3255](/wiki/Second_Age_3255 "Second Age 3255").<sup id="cite_ref-SA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-4">[4]</a></sup>

Soon after Ar-Pharazôn's unlawful ascent to power, he heard news that Sauron had been assailing the Númenórean settlements in Middle-earth since his return to the island. His captains explained to him that the fallen [Maia](/wiki/Maia) wished to drive the Númenóreans back to the [Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer") whence they had come, and declare himself King of Men. They also told Ar-Pharazôn that Sauron wished to destroy Númenor itself, if possible.

#### Ar-Pharazôn humbles Sauron {#Ar-Pharazôn_humbles_Sauron}

Ar-Pharazôn became very angry upon hearing this news, and decided that he himself would claim the title of King of Men instead, and make Sauron his servant. To accomplish this end, he spent five years preparing a great force.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup> In [3261](/wiki/Second_Age_3261 "Second Age 3261") he launched the Númenórean fleet and landed at the [Haven of Umbar](/wiki/Umbar "Umbar").<sup id="cite_ref-SA_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-4">[4]</a></sup> From there, Ar-Pharazôn and his army marched north to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). Their splendour and might was so great that Sauron's own servants deserted him. Therefore, since he perceived that he could not defeat the Númenóreans through strength of arms, Sauron changed into his fair form and humbled himself before Ar-Pharazôn. The King stripped Sauron of his titles and decreed that he would be taken as a hostage to Númenor, to keep his servants from harassing the Númenóreans in Middle-earth.

Sauron acted as if this decision dismayed him, but it actually furthered his plans to destroy Ar-Pharazôn and the Númenóreans in retaliation for this humiliation. Sauron thus allowed himself to be brought to Númenor by Ar-Pharazôn's army, but as soon as he set foot on the island he began corrupting the minds of the Númenóreans, beginning with their proud King. Sauron flattered Ar-Pharazôn and used his knowledge to help the King gain anything he desired. Ar-Pharazôn responded to this flattery and made Sauron his chief adviser three years after his coming to Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup>

<h4><span id="Sauron.27s_teachings"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Sauron&#39;s_teachings">Sauron&#39;s teachings</span></h4>

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/a7/Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg/200px-Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="282" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/a7/Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg/300px-Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/a/a7/Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg/400px-Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1_-_Sauron_and_Ar-Pharazon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="847"/></a><figcaption><i>Sauron and Ar-Pharazôn</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Janka_Late%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1" title="Category:Images by Janka Latečková">Janka Latečková</a></figcaption></figure>

Sauron taught the people of Númenor many things, and in most of his speeches he spoke against the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), whom most Númenóreans already feared and disliked. Eventually, however, the Maia's teachings began to focus more upon [Darkness](/wiki/Darkness). He told his listeners that "Darkness alone is worshipful, and the Lord thereof may yet make other worlds to be gifts to those that serve him, so that the increase of their power shall find no end." These words reached the ears of Ar-Pharazôn, and he spoke to Sauron in secret in order to learn more about the Lord of Darkness. Sauron told the King that this Lord was [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth"), and that [Eru Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar") was only a lie devised by the Valar to keep [Men](/wiki/Men) from rebelling against them. The Maia thus himself lied to Ar-Pharazôn, and said that by serving Melkor, the "Giver of Freedom", the King would escape from these untruths of the Valar, and become stronger than them.

At this time Ar-Pharazôn began to worship the Darkness and Melkor its Lord. He practised this new religion in secret at first, but soon displayed it openly, and most of his people followed him. The King made ascending the [Meneltarma](/wiki/Meneltarma) (which the worshippers of Ilúvatar had formerly done, in order to make offerings on its summit) a crime punishable by death. Sauron then advised Ar-Pharazôn to destroy [Nimloth](/wiki/Nimloth_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Nimloth of Númenor"), the White Tree of Númenor, which was the last symbol of the Númenóreans' ancient friendship with the Valar and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves). Ar-Pharazôn was reluctant to do this, because he had heard of Tar-Palantir's prophecy that the Line of Kings would end when the Tree perished.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup>

#### Years of tyranny {#Years_of_tyranny}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/2/25/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg/300px-Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="375" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/2/25/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg/450px-Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/2/25/Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg/600px-Joel_Kilpatrick_-_Corrupting_Counsel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="2000"/></a><figcaption><i>Corrupting Counsel</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Joel_Kilpatrick" title="Category:Images by Joel Kilpatrick">Joel Kilpatrick</a></figcaption></figure>

However, when a disguised man (actually [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur)) stole a fruit from Nimloth upon hearing Sauron's advice to the King, Ar-Pharazôn assented to the Tree's destruction. It was also at this time that Ar-Pharazôn dismissed his erstwhile friend Amandil from his [Council](/wiki/Council_of_the_Sceptre "Council of the Sceptre"), though the King and his supporters did not otherwise harm Amandil or his family. After the White Tree was felled, Sauron had it burned in a mighty [Temple](/wiki/Temple) that the Númenóreans had built in [Armenelos](/wiki/Armenelos) on his orders. The King and his followers then used this Temple for the worship of Melkor. They captured members of the Faithful and sacrificed them in the fires of the altar, in the hope that Melkor would release them from [Death](/wiki/Gift_of_Men "Gift of Men"). The Númenóreans did the same to the Men of Middle-earth who lived near their settlements, and thus Ar-Pharazôn became the greatest tyrant that the world had seen since [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth), though Sauron actually ruled all from behind the throne.

But Ar-Pharazôn and the Númenóreans did not escape death by this means, and eventually the King grew old and was in great fear of the end of his life and going out into the Darkness that he had worshipped. Ar-Pharazôn became wrathful and desperate, and at last Sauron gave the King his final advice. The Maia told Ar-Pharazôn that by warring against the Valar and conquering [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor), their land in [the West](/wiki/Aman "Aman"), he could gain everlasting life.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup> Ar-Pharazôn feared to wage such a war, but his desperation prompted him to begin building a [Great Armament](/wiki/Great_Armament) in [3310](/wiki/Second_Age_3310 "Second Age 3310") to attack Valinor.<sup id="cite_ref-SA_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-4">[4]</a></sup>

While this Armament was being constructed, the Valar sent warnings to the Númenóreans in the form of terrible thunderstorms and clouds shaped like [Eagles](/wiki/Eagles), and the ground began to groan and shake. But these signals only caused the King and his people to harden their hearts yet more, and Ar-Pharazôn (prompted by Sauron) proclaimed, "The [Lords of the West](/wiki/Valar "Valar") have plotted against us. They strike first. The next blow shall be ours!"<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup>

#### War against the Valar {#War_against_the_Valar}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/2/2d/John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg/180px-John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="180" height="181" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/2/2d/John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg/270px-John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/2/2d/John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg/360px-John_Howe_-_The_Fleet_of_Ar-Pharazon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="534" data-file-height="538"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/John_Howe" title="John Howe">John Howe</a> - <i>Fleet of Ar-Pharazôn</i></figcaption></figure>

Nine years after the preparations for war began, the Great Armament was completed. Ar-Pharazôn journeyed to the west of Númenor where the fleet was anchored, and boarded his mighty ship [Alcarondas](/wiki/Alcarondas). He sat upon his throne in full armour and signalled for the raising of the anchors. The fleet left Númenor just before sundown, and passed into the forbidden seas of the West.

Because no one from the Great Armament ever returned to mortal lands, Ar-Pharazôn's fate remains uncertain. It is said in the *[Akallabêth](/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth)* that the fleet passed [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) and anchored near the coast of Valinor. Upon seeing the splendour of [Taniquetil](/wiki/Taniquetil), Ar-Pharazôn became afraid and nearly gave the order to retreat. In the end, however, his pride caused him to go ashore, and a great part of his army followed him. Ar-Pharazôn led his troops to the Elven city of [Tirion](/wiki/Tirion), and they set up an encampment around it. Then the King proclaimed that the land of the Valar was his, if none would challenge him for it.

At this time, the Valar called upon Eru for aid, and relinquished their authority over [Arda](/wiki/Arda). Eru broke the world and made it round, so that Men could never again sail to Valinor. In this catastrophe, Ar-Pharazôn's fleet was pulled into the chasm that opened between the [Blessed Realm](/wiki/Blessed_Realm) and mortal lands, and Númenor sank beneath the Sea. Ar-Pharazôn himself (along with the soldiers that had followed him to Valinor) is said to have been buried underground, and lies in the [Caves of the Forgotten](/wiki/Caves_of_the_Forgotten) until the [Last Battle](/wiki/Final_Battle "Final Battle").<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Though Ar-Pharazôn was the last King of Númenor, the Line of [Elros](/wiki/Elros) survived through [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) the son of Amandil, and his two sons, [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) and [Anárion](/wiki/An%C3%A1rion). They managed to escape the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) that Ar-Pharazôn's actions had caused, and later created the kingdoms of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).

Sometime before his death at the end of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), Elendil wrote down some of the history of Númenor, culminating in the tale of its destruction. Much of this document was focused on the effects of the reign of Ar-Pharazôn and Sauron's teachings to the Númenóreans. Elendil's work was called the *[Akallabêth](/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth)*, and its retelling of Ar-Pharazôn's life was one of the only parts of Númenor's history that remained widely known after the Downfall.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Many years after the Downfall, the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor) erected a great pillar at Umbar as a commemoration of Ar-Pharazôn. Even though Elendil's people suffered greatly because of the evil that Sauron caused in Númenor as a result of Ar-Pharazôn's deeds, his descendants still wished to honour the King and his army for the humbling of Sauron. When Sauron retook Umbar from the Gondorians, he in turn had this monument to his defeat destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Pharazôn* means "Golden" in [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), and is derived from the word *[pharaz](/wiki/Pharaz "Pharaz")*, 'gold'.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Had Ar-Pharazôn taken a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name, he would have ruled as <i><b>Tar-Calion</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> This title does not appear to be a direct translation of his Adûnaic name, since it means "Son of Light" (from *[cala](/wiki/Cala "Cala")* 'light', and *[-ion](/wiki/-ion)*, the masculine patronymic).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Like all the other Kings of Númenor who took their royal names in Adûnaic, Pharazôn added the prefix *[ar](/wiki/Ar "Ar")* ('high', 'King') to his name when he seized the Sceptre.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In the [*Etymologies*](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies"), [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") noted the name <i><b>Tar-kulu</b></i> (in the entry for [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [KUL](/wiki/KUL)), which editors [Carl F. Hostetter](/wiki/Carl_F._Hostetter) and [Patrick H. Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_H._Wynne) suggest could be a formerly unattested name of Ar-Pharazôn.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In earlier versions of the *Akallabêth*, Ar-Pharazôn summoned Sauron to Númenor instead of taking an army to Middle-earth and humbling him. The elements of Morgoth-worship and human sacrifice were not originally parts of his rule, either; in the first stories of the Downfall, Ar-Pharazôn merely built the Temple (on top of the Meneltarma, however) for future use and allowed Sauron to watch the people from it.

Alternate stories of Ar-Pharazôn's life written by Tolkien include a version in which Míriel was actually attracted to him, and their marriage was consensual. She gave him most of her power voluntarily. This remarkable textual development existed even in some of the latest revisions of the *Akallabêth*.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

In the earliest drafts of the *Akallabêth*, Ar-Pharazôn was named **Angor**<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> later replaced by **Tar-kalion**<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> and then **Tarkalion**.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>
