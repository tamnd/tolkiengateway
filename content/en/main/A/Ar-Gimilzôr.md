**Ar-Gimilzôr** was the twenty-third [King](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor "King of Númenor") of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor)<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Gimilzor_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Gimilzor-1">[1]</a></sup> and the grandfather of [Tar-Míriel](/wiki/Tar-M%C3%ADriel) and of [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n), the last King.<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Palantir_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Palantir-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Ar-Gimilzôr was a great enemy of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful). He completely outlawed the use of the [Elven tongues](/wiki/Elvish "Elvish"). In addition, he did not permit the Eldar to come to Númenor and punished those who still welcomed the Elven ships<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Gimilzor_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Gimilzor-1">[1]</a></sup> that still came secretly from [Eressëa](/wiki/Eress%C3%ABa) to the western shores of Númenor where most of the Faithful lived, because he considered these Elves to be the "spies of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar)" and hoped to keep his deeds and counsels hidden from the Valar. Eventually, Ar-Gimilzôr's distrust of the Faithful caused him to exile all the Faithful that he could identify to the east of Númenor near the haven of [Rómenna](/wiki/R%C3%B3menna). There he kept them under surveillance.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-4">[4]</a></sup>

Because of Ar-Gimilzôr's harsh treatment of them, many of the Faithful emigrated to the northern shores of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) where they could speak to the Eldar in the kingdom of [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-4">[4]</a></sup>

In addition to his heavy-handed policies against the Faithful, Ar-Gimilzôr completely neglected traditions long held even by the rebellious Kings before him, such as the tending of the [White Tree](/wiki/Nimloth_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Nimloth of Númenor") of Númenor. The beautiful Tree, a symbol of the ancient friendship between Númenor and [the West](/wiki/Undying_Lands "Undying Lands"), fell into a decline. Ar-Gimilzôr was also the first of the Kings to never ascend the [Meneltarma](/wiki/Meneltarma) and display reverence to [Eru](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar").<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-4">[4]</a></sup>

Ar-Gimilzôr wed a woman "renowned for her beauty",<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-4">[4]</a></sup> whose name was [Inzilbêth](/wiki/Inzilb%C3%AAth) and who was a descendant of [Tar-Calmacil](/wiki/Tar-Calmacil).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Gimilzor_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Gimilzor-1">[1]</a></sup> He did not know at the time that she herself was of the Faithful, and thus later in their marriage "there was small love between them". Inzilbêth passed on her beliefs to their elder son [Inziladûn](/wiki/Inzilad%C3%BBn), whereas their second son [Gimilkhâd](/wiki/Gimilkh%C3%A2d) was much more like his father. Ar-Gimilzôr would thus have preferred to see the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre) go to the hand of his younger son, but he could not change the laws of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), and was succeeded by [Inziladûn](/wiki/Inzilad%C3%BBn), who took the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name of [Tar-Palantir](/wiki/Tar-Palantir).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Gimilzor_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Gimilzor-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Gimilzôr* is an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name. Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "Star-foam" and is a compound of *gimil* ("stars") and *zôr* ("foam"), because *Gimilzor* was the Adûnaic name of Elros.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> The meaning of the Adûnaic prefix *Ar-* is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "king" and is a prefixal form of *ârû* ("king") or derived from the same root.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

His [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name was *Telemnar*. Its meaning is not glossed, but Paul Strack suggests it means "Silver Fire" and is a compound of an assimilated form of *telpë (telep-)* ("silver") and *nár* ("fire").<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Like for all the rulers of Númenor whose name was inscribed in the Scroll of Kings, the prefix *tar-* ("high") was added to his name.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
