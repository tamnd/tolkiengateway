**A Long-expected Party** is the title of the opening chapter of *[The Return of the Shadow](../T/The_Return_of_the_Shadow.md)*, the sixth book of *[The History of Middle-earth](../T/The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)* series by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md). It is also the title of the first chapter in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, but in this volume Christopher provides the *history* of how that first chapter was written.

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

As explained in the Forward, [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) wrote the early chapters of *The Lord ofthe Rings* in successive phases.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> In the First Phase the professor wrote four different versions of A Long-expected Party, which are published in this chapter (later in the Second Phase a fifth version is provided<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>). For each of the four versions a short introduction is given, followed by the text (as far as it went), with notes and commentary thereafter. At the end of the chapter Christopher highlights the differences between the versions (and the story as eventually published),<sup id="cite_ref-Fourth_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fourth-3">[3]</a></sup> Tolkien's musings on the further direction of the story,<sup id="cite_ref-Brewing_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brewing-4">[4]</a></sup> and details of communications with [Allen and Unwin](Allen_and_Unwin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The fascinating parts of this chapter for readers are the surprising differences between the four versions and the final result. Below is a short listing of some of the differences:

- **Version I**: Bilbo gives the party and he is 70 years old. There is no sign of [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) or any heir and Bilbo does not use the [One Ring](../O/One_Ring.md) to disappear. The final words of his speech announce that he plans to get married. A narrator frankly explains that the story of the party was just to explain what Bilbo had been doing before going on to the real story, the tale of one of his descendants. The reason for Bilbo's departure is a flare-up of his Tookish side and the expiration of his wealth, with no mention of his Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
- **Version II**: Bilbo gives the party and he is 71 years old. [Elves](../E/Elves.md), [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md), and even [Men](../M/Men.md) from [Dale](../D/Dale.md) arrive at [Bag End](../B/Bag_End.md) with goods for the party. Gandalf appears with his fireworks. Bilbo uses his Ring to disappear but only after stepping down so that no one noticed his going.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
- **Version III**: Bingo Baggins, the son of Bilbo and Primula Brandybuck, gives the party at age 72. Bilbo and Primula disappeared when Bilbo was 111 years old and Bingo was but 39. Gaffer Gamgee makes his appearance. It is Bingo whose Tookish side has flared up and his disappearance is just a joke before he goes off on a lark rather than as part of a grim adventure.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
- **Version IV**: Bilbo is not married and adopts his cousin Bingo Bolger. Bilbo disappears from the Shire at age 111 and Bingo Bolger-Baggins gives the party when he is 72 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-Fourth_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fourth-3">[3]</a></sup>

After the text of the four versions there is a section containing Tolkien's notes about the brewing story. The impetus for Bilbo's departure is lingering lust for dragon gold, with nothing about the ring except for a few questions in Tolkien's mind.<sup id="cite_ref-Brewing_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brewing-4">[4]</a></sup> Throughout the four versions and in the subsequent notes the names of the characters are in a state of flux.
