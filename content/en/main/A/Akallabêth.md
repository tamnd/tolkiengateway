<b><i>Akallabêth</i>: The Downfall of Númenor</b> is the fourth part of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, edited by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) from [his father](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s later texts.

It tells the story of the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) after the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), the descendants of those [Men](/wiki/Men) who aided the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) in their fight against [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) during the late [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), turned by degrees against the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), and were later corrupted by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron). The *Akallabêth* was written by [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) near the end of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) and was preserved in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

At the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), the [Edain](/wiki/Edain), who alone among the race of [Men](/wiki/Men) were loyal defenders of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) during their war against [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth), were given a new land of their own in reward for their efforts, free from the troubles and sadness of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). It was located in the middle of the [Great Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer"), between the western shores of Middle-earth and the eastern shores of [Aman](/wiki/Aman), where the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) lived.<sup id="cite_ref-akallabeth_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/ad/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="440" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/ad/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/a/ad/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Eagles_of_Manw%C3%AB.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1090" data-file-height="1600"/></a><figcaption><i>The Eagles of Manwë</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

The Men of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) were [forbidden](/wiki/Ban_of_the_Valar "Ban of the Valar") by the Valar to sail westward so far that they could no longer see the island, so the majority of their voyaging was eastward and in time they returned to the shores of Middle-Earth. The Númenóreans established remote colonies in Middle-earth and made contact with the [Men of Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle_Men "Middle Men") and taught them many crafts and skills. They made alliance with [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) and the Elves and aided them in the [war](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron "War of the Elves and Sauron") against Sauron where he was defeated by the power of the Númenóreans.

Over time many began to speak openly of their envy of the Eldar's immortality and in defiance of the Ban. The people of Númenor became split between the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men), those loyal to the King and prone to oppose the restraints upon Númenor, and the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful), those who remained true to their friendship with the Eldar and loyalty to the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-akallabeth_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-akallabeth-2">[2]</a></sup> During this time, the long life of the Númenóreans began to decrease as the Shadow rose. Their joy for life and good will departed, but their power increased.

Sauron re-emerged to challenge the power of Númenor in Middle-earth and their colonies along the shores were assailed by him and his armies. The king, [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n), responded and came with a great host to Middle-earth and bade Sauron to come before him and swear fealty. To the surprise of many, Sauron did as he was asked. But the King was not content with his show of obedience, and brought Sauron back to Númenor as a hostage. Sauron gave the impression that this was against his will, but in truth it was exactly what he wanted. Sauron exploited his power to corrupt the King to his will. Soon he became his adviser, and most of the Númenóreans obeyed his will and turned to the worship of Morgoth.

Sauron convinced the King, who was now in his twilight years, to assail Aman in order to gain immortality. Thus Ar-Pharazôn led his [Great Armament](/wiki/Great_Armament) and landed on Aman. However, as this was done, the Valar appealed to [Eru Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar") and he destroyed the Great Armament. Ar-Pharazôn and his host were buried under hills, and the whole of Númenor sank under the Great Sea. [Arda](/wiki/Arda) was made spherical and Aman was put beyond it, out of the reach of mortal Men.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="440" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/0d/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Ships_of_the_Faithful.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1090" data-file-height="1600"/></a><figcaption><i>The Ships of the Faithful</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

Just a few people still uncorrupted by Sauron managed to escape the catastrophe; they fled Númenor by ship. This group of Faithful Númenóreans was led by [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) the Tall and his two sons, [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) and [Anárion](/wiki/An%C3%A1rion). They landed in Middle-earth, where the followers of Elendil established two kingdoms which came to be known as the realms of Exile: [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in the south, and [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in the north. Some of the King's Men, enemies of Elendil, who were in Middle-earth at the time of the [Downfall](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor") established other realms in exile to the south; of these the Haven of Umbar was the chief. Sauron, although greatly diminished and bereft of shape, had survived the Downfall and returned to Middle-earth to continue troubling its inhabitants.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The study of Númenor's history was suppressed in the realms of Exile because it was seen as a vain pursuit, "breeding only useless regret". Only one story from the former home of the Dúnedain remained generally known: the cautionary tale of the pride of Ar-Pharazôn and his "impious armada", which corresponds to the last half of the *Akallabêth*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Akallabêth* is an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name. It means "(she that has) Downfallen<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> or "The Downfallen".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It is derived from *[kalab](/wiki/Kalab "Kalab")* ("fall down"<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>).<sup id="cite_ref-PM_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-8">[8]</a></sup>

The Quenya translation of this word is *[Atalantë](/wiki/Atalant%C3%AB)*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> It is derived from the verbal stem *[talat-](/wiki/Talat- "Talat-")* ("slipping, sliding, falling down").<sup id="cite_ref-PM_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

*Akallabêth* or *Atalantë* was the name for the island of Númenor after its Downfall. The Exiles became reluctant to speak of the land by any other name, or indeed much at all; but at times "they turned towards the West in the desire of their hearts" and remembered Akallabêth, their former home.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Development {#Development}

The *Akallabêth* originated with *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)*, a time-travel story that Tolkien wrote due to a conversation with [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis) about writing their own science fiction. Lewis produced *[Out of the Silent Planet](/wiki/Out_of_the_Silent_Planet)* however Tolkien abandoned *The Lost Road*, having only written two introductory chapters and two chapters of Númenor in the end, as he was more interested in writing his own version of the Atlantis legend. As Tolkien was writing *The Lost Road* he also produced another manuscript, an outline which was closely related to the last two chapters of *The Lost Road* about Númenor and its downfall. This was followed by another, more finished, manuscript titled *[The Fall of Númenor (chapter)](</wiki/The_Fall_of_N%C3%BAmenor_(chapter)>)*. These can be said to be the true germ of the *Akallabêth* itself, written in the mid-to-late thirties.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Tolkien began to develop the tale of Númenor's Downfall further when writing *[The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers)* in the 1940s, about a fictional discussion group called the [Notion Club](/wiki/Notion_Club) (a reference to the [Inklings](/wiki/Inklings)) where its members discuss [Alwin Arundel Lowdham](/wiki/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham)'s dreams of Atlantis and Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> At this time there were major developments to the legend in *[The Drowning of Anadûnê](/wiki/The_Drowning_of_Anad%C3%BBn%C3%AA)*. A large amount of the wording, especially in the later versions of the text, is retained in the *Akallabêth*.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes in essays, which he named sketches written by his father that he was developing two traditions concerning Númenor's history, each with its own retelling of the Downfall: *The Fall of Númenor*, a more Elvish version, and a "Mannish" form, *The Drowning of Anadûnê*.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> These texts can be found in Volume IX of *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)*, *[Sauron Defeated](/wiki/Sauron_Defeated)*.

Tolkien's final revisions of the *Akallabêth* were published in the last volume of *The History of Middle-earth*, *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*. It was from this version that Christopher got the document that he published in *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. Since the latest version of the *Akallabêth* contained references to [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), Christopher removed all references to him, as he believed that his father abandoned the framing device of Ælfwine and [Pengolodh](/wiki/Pengolodh) because there was no suggestion of it in his father's latest writings of *[The Quenta Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion "The Later Quenta Silmarillion")* before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>
