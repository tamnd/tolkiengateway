**Angainor** was the chain used to contain [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) in the [Halls of Mandos](/wiki/Halls_of_Mandos). It was forged by the [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) and held Melkor for [three ages](/wiki/Chaining_of_Melkor "Chaining of Melkor") (around 2,900 solar years).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> At the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), Melkor was again bound with Angainor, and his [Iron Crown](/wiki/Iron_Crown) was made into a collar.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Angainor* contains [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *[anga](/wiki/Anga "Anga")* ("iron").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, the chain is called <i><b>Angaino</b></i> ("the oppressor") and its making by Aulë is more detailed: he first made a special metal called <i><b><a href="/wiki/Tilkal" title="Tilkal">tilkal</a></b></i>, and mixing it with six metals he forged the chain Angaino, the two manacles Vorotemnar and the four fetters Ilterendi. Aulë put Vorotemnar on [Melko](/wiki/Melko)'s hands and two Ilterendi on each of his ankles.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In later revisions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") which [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) never finalized, Melkor is never chained by the Valar as he willingly surrenders to them as part of a treacherous plot, though Tulkas clanks Angainor behind Melkor's back occasionally as he is escorted back to Valinor.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In [Norse Mythology](/wiki/Norse_Mythology), <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleipnir" title="wikipedia:Gleipnir">Gleipnir</a> is the binding that holds the mighty wolf <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenrir" title="wikipedia:Fenrir">Fenrir</a>, which was forged by dwarves with six different materials.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
