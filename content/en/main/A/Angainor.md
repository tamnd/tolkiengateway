**Angainor** was the chain used to contain [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md) in the [Halls of Mandos](../H/Halls_of_Mandos.md). It was forged by the [Vala](../V/Valar.md) [Aulë](Aulë.md) and held Melkor for [three ages](../C/Chaining_of_Melkor.md) (around 2,900 solar years).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> At the end of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), Melkor was again bound with Angainor, and his [Iron Crown](../I/Iron_Crown.md) was made into a collar.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Angainor* contains [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *[anga](Anga.md)* ("iron").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*, the chain is called <i><b>Angaino</b></i> ("the oppressor") and its making by Aulë is more detailed: he first made a special metal called <i><b><a href="../T/Tilkal.md" title="Tilkal">tilkal</a></b></i>, and mixing it with six metals he forged the chain Angaino, the two manacles Vorotemnar and the four fetters Ilterendi. Aulë put Vorotemnar on [Melko](../M/Melko.md)'s hands and two Ilterendi on each of his ankles.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In later revisions of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) which [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md) never finalized, Melkor is never chained by the Valar as he willingly surrenders to them as part of a treacherous plot, though Tulkas clanks Angainor behind Melkor's back occasionally as he is escorted back to Valinor.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In [Norse Mythology](../N/Norse_Mythology~40840.md), <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleipnir" title="wikipedia:Gleipnir">Gleipnir</a> is the binding that holds the mighty wolf <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenrir" title="wikipedia:Fenrir">Fenrir</a>, which was forged by dwarves with six different materials.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
