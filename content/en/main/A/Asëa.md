<i><b>asëa</b></i> is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word which also constitutes the first element of the plant name *[asëa aranion](Asëa_aranion.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE49_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE49-2">[2]</a></sup> A signification given of *asëa* is "name of plant '[*athelas*](Athelas.md)'".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning and etymology of *asëa* is a matter of debate. In [1998](../_/1998.md), William Cloud Hicklin claimed to have "ongoing discussion about the origins of \[the word [athelas](Athelas.md)\]" with [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), and wrote in a forum message that:

"<i>a very late note [by Tolkien] (1970 or later) says that Asea [...] was the name in Quenya, regularly adapted and compounded with -las in Sindarin. The plant was known to the medical loremasters of the Noldor. The root is *ATHAYA, &#39;helpful, kindly, beneficial.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Hicklin's assertion has attracted a widespread attention on the Internet,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and was reprinted in the journal *[Tyalië Tyelelliéva](../T/Tyalië_Tyelelliéva.md)* [No. 15](../T/Tyalië_Tyelelliéva_15.md) (p. 31).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Hicklin's suggested etymology has also been picked up by [Helge Fauskanger](../H/Helge_Fauskanger.md) in his "Quenya-to-English wordlist", where *asëa* is said to be an adjective meaning "beneficial, helpful, kindly" derived from root ATHAYA.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 1]</a></sup>

Another mention of the existence of an unpublished etymology of *athelas*/*asëa* occurred in *[The Lord of the Rings: A Reader's Companion](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_A_Reader's_Companion.md)* ([2005](../_/2005.md)/[2008](../_/2008.md)), where the authors referred to a claim by [Arden R. Smith](Arden_R._Smith.md). Here it was written that the first element in *athelas* is connected with Quenya *asëa*.<sup id="cite_ref-RC183_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC183-12">[11]</a></sup>

With the publication of [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s manuscript "Words, Phrases &amp; Passages in *The Lord of the Rings*" in [2007](../_/2007.md), an etymology of *athelas*/*asëa* was published, and editor [Christopher Gilson](../C/Christopher_Gilson.md) wrote in a comment that this etymology is the source of the claim in *[The Lord of the Rings: A Reader's Companion](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_A_Reader's_Companion.md)*.

Since Tolkien's manuscript date from the late 1950s and early 1960s (and does not include any mention of the root ATHAYA), Hicklin's claim of an unpublished etymology dating from the 1970s remains unverified in published sources.

In his analysis of the Quenya words in "Words, Phrases &amp; Passages in *The Lord of the Rings*", Petri Tikka has tentatively suggested that *asëa* could mean "foil" (gloss followed by a question mark).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[note 2]</a></sup>
