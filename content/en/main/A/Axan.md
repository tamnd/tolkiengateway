<i><b>axan</b></i> (pl. <i><b>axani</b></i>) is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) noun meaning "law, rule, commandment, primarily proceeding from [Eru](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar")". The term refers to laws established by Ilúvatar that lesser beings are directed to follow but can choose to disobey. The *axani* contrast with the *[únati](/wiki/%C3%9Anat "Únat")*, which are physical laws that are impossible to break. [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) refused to accept any *axani* as binding upon him, but much to his frustration, he was unable to violate the *únati*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It is said to derive from [Valarin](/wiki/Valarin) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Aka%C5%A1%C3%A2n&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Akašân (page does not exist)">akašân</a></i>, meaning "He says", with "He" referring to Eru.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

*axan* is similar to the ancient Greek word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anax_(Greek)" title="wikipedia:Anax (Greek)">ἄναξ</a></i> (*anax*) meaning "ruler, lord, king". The Valarin form *akašân*, in the meantime, resembles the word <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/akasha" title="wikipedia:akasha">akasha</a>, the aether in Hindu cosmology and mysticism. It is not known whether Tolkien was familiar with either term.
