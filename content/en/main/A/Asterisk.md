The **asterisk** is a symbol used in historical linguistics. [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) made use of asterisks in his texts about [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) languages.

## Usage {#Usage}

In historical linguistics, an asterisk immediately before a word indicates that the word is not directly attested, but has been reconstructed on the basis of other linguistic material (see also: <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/comparative_method" title="wikipedia:comparative method">comparative method</a>). The asterisk shows that this form of the word is hypothetical and lacks historical validation.

Asterisks are therefore used mostly in the reconstruction of unwritten or prehistoric languages, deduced by comparison between other languages.

For example, the [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) word *endleofan* suggests a Proto-Germanic form was \**ainlif*. The Proto-Germanic people did not have a writing system. Therefore *ainlif* is unattested, which is indicated with an asterisk.

## Elvish {#Elvish}

Tolkien used asterisks when writing about the history of his [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) languages to show that some forms are unattested and existed before the established alphabets of, for example, [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) or the [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md). As such, an asterisk placed before a word indicates that the form is hypothetical. As noted by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), such a marking means that the form is "*deduced to have existed from later, recorded forms*". Asterisks are used most usually in [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

For example, Tolkien gives \*<i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Lukti%C3%AAn%C3%AA&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Luktiênê (page does not exist)">luktiênê</a></i> as the ancient form of the name *[Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md)*.

In [fandom](../F/Fandom.md), even the asterisked forms of Tolkien's invented words are considered "valid" and as canonical as the un-asterisked words.

## Fandom {#Fandom}

Students of Tolkien's languages use asterisks when the form and meaning of a word can be deduced from Tolkien's existing writings.

For example the word \**corma* is a hypothetical word believed to mean "ring". It can be seen only as part of the word <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Cormacolindo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Cormacolindo (page does not exist)">cormacolindo</a>,</i> which means "[Ring-bearer](../R/Ring-bearer.md)". The longer word's form makes it reasonable to assume that the word "corma" exists in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md). Since the word is never written clearly by Tolkien, however, it is considered hypothetical and marked with an asterisk.

Asterisks are also used to show ancient forms of attested words. The [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) word <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Hobas&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Hobas (page does not exist)">hobas</a></i> from [Root](../S/Sundocarmë.md) [KHOP](../K/KHOP.md) points to an [Old Sindarin](../O/Old_Sindarin.md) intermediate \**khopasse*. Tolkien never wrote this word, but the rules of Sindarin etymology makes this form the only possibility. Proper referencing requires an asterisk to make clear that it is not a word given by Tolkien.

Students also sometimes employ a double asterisk \*\* to indicate a mistaken hypothetical form. A mistaken form would be the proposed Primitive Quendian word \*\**aladâ* meaning "tree", instead of the correct form of the word \**galadâ*. They both use [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *[alda](Alda.md),* but the former uses [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) \**aladh* instead of the proper *[galadh](../G/Galadh.md)*. In this instance, the double asterisk could also clarify the behavior of *g* in the two languages.

[Tolkien Gateway](../T/Tolkien_Gateway.md) has entries only for attested canonical words. Asterisked forms will be given as examples but will not have links to their own entries. <span></span>
