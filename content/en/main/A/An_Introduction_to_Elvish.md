<i><b>An Introduction to Elvish and to Other Tongues and Proper Names and Writing Systems of the Third Age of the Western Lands of Middle-Earth as Set Forth in the Published Writings of Professor John Ronald Reuel Tolkien</b></i> was a book by [Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) that discussed the [languages](../L/Languages.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).

The book comprises various articles written by members of the [Mythopoeic Society](../M/Mythopoeic_Society.md) and its publication was authorized by the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Mythopoeic_Linguistic_Fellowship&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mythopoeic Linguistic Fellowship (page does not exist)">Mythopoeic Linguistic Fellowship</a> (a forerunner of the [Elvish Linguistic Fellowship](../E/Elvish_Linguistic_Fellowship.md)).

Authors to the articles include: Jim Allan, [Bill Welden](../B/Bill_Welden.md), [Paula Marmor](../P/Paula_Marmor.md), [Christopher Gilson](../C/Christopher_Gilson.md), <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Lise_Menn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lise Menn (page does not exist)">Lise Menn</a>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Laurence_J._Krieg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Laurence J. Krieg (page does not exist)">Laurence J. Krieg</a> and others.

Discussing the earliest stages of [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) suggested this book as a reference for the vocabulary of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) of the later stages.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Structure {#Structure}

### Eldarin tongues {#Eldarin_tongues}

The first articles offer analyses of the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) texts published. Extrapolation of a sketchy grammar is offered based on the available data, as well as meaning of the names found in the books.

There is also a chapter that goes on to discuss the relationship of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) and analyze a possible "[Proto-Eldarin](../C/Common_Eldarin.md)" through comparative linguistics.

These chapters are followed by glossaries pointing at possible real-word similarities.

An article written from Tolkien's [secondary world](../S/Secondary_world.md) perspective, explains how [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) possibly influenced the Indo-European languages.

### Other tongues {#Other_tongues}

More obscure languages like [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md), [Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md), "[Adûnaic](Adûnaic.md) tongues" and more obscure ones ([Westron](../W/Westron.md), [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md), [Entish](../E/Entish.md), "[Valinorean](../V/Valinorean.md)") are also briefly discussed. Their sources are primarily information as given in the [Appendices](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Appendices.md).

### Personal names {#Personal_names}

A part of the book is dedicated to [tolkiennymy](../T/Tolkiennymy.md) and provides etymologies of the [Old English](../O/Old_English.md), [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md) and [Norse](../N/Norse.md) names and other words which represent [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md) languages ([Westron](../W/Westron.md) and [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md)); possible influences from British and Celtic folklore are pointed out.

There is also a "Baby-book" with all the known [Germanic](../G/Germanic.md), [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md) and other real-world ("translated") Hobbit names by category and gender, along with their etymologies.

### Writing systems {#Writing_systems}

An extensive section with analyses of the [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) and [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md) in which aims to be more clear and readable presentation of the information of [Appendix E](Appendix_E.md), followed by theoretical and structural background, with a possible history of their evolution through time.

Examples of English-tengwar texts used by [fans](../T/Tolkien_fandom.md) are given, with analyses and commentaries.

Also, a chapter compares <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lodwick" title="wikipedia:Francis Lodwick">Francis Lodwick</a>'s "Universal Alphabet" with Tengwar.

## Sources and validity {#Sources_and_validity}

The book was compiled shortly before *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* was published therefore its material was only the works published during Tolkien's lifetime: *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*, *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](../T/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil.md)*, *[The Road Goes Ever On](<../T/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book).md>)* and *[Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings](../G/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*

The book was complete in [1977](../_/1977.md) but publication was halted for a year. *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* was published in the meantime which included new material that (in few points) obsoleted the theories of the book. A year later, and while the book was still in a hiatus, Jim Allan wrote about those points in the postscript of his Foreword; he points out that updating the text by incorporating the new information would not be possible, and encourages critical comparison by the reader.<sup id="cite_ref-post_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-post-2">[2]</a></sup>

Since 1977, a great amount of material was published in the *[History of Middle-earth](../T/The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)* series, not to mention magazines such as *[Vinyar Tengwar](../V/Vinyar_Tengwar.md)* and *[Parma Eldalamberon](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon.md)*. While fans still appear to consent that the book is one of the best and more serious works, new material have rendered the theories incomplete or outdated. However, some of the parts of the book, like the one concerning real-world names and the writing systems, are still considered to provide useful information.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Proto-Eldarin {#Proto-Eldarin}

In their articles, [Christopher Gilson](../C/Christopher_Gilson.md) and [Bill Welden](../B/Bill_Welden.md) attempt to shed light to the common ancestor of Quenya and Sindarin, dubbed "Proto-Eldarin" and investigate the sound changes from the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) to the end of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md). The authors claim to have seen some unpublished writings by Tolkien, which may explain how the Proto-Eldarin reconstructions correctly end in long vowels. Of course, with limited material (*[The Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)* and similar works where Tolkien discusses [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) and [roots](../S/Sundocarme.md) were not published until 10 years later) there were some implausible reconstructions. For example, the words *[vilya](../V/Vilya.md)* and *[gwai](../G/Gwaew.md)* are treated as cognates, derived from Proto-Eldarin \*\**wigyā*.

The theories also described the evolution of Sindarin through words of palatal-final consonants (*-ᶅ, -ɲ, -ᶉ*) which sometimes caused [umlaut](../I/I-affection.md). For example \**winjā* &gt; \*\**weiɲ* (written <i>wein<sup>y</sup></i>) &gt; <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gwain&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gwain (page does not exist)">-wain</a></i>. *The Etymologies* and other works have revealed the evolution of Sindarin through [Common Telerin](../C/Common_Telerin.md) and [Old Noldorin](../O/Old_Noldorin.md) where no palatalisation occurs; a word such as \**winjā* would evolve to \**winia* and umlauts are the result of a much later [i-affection](../I/I-affection.md).

The authors generally assume that Proto-Eldarin lost its final sounds before the Quenya stage. For example, Quenya *[alda](Alda.md)* has been wrongly attributed to a Proto-Eldarin word \*\**galdar*, with loss of *-r*; the plural form \*\**galdari* produced Quenya plural *aldar*. In the [Sindarin branch](../C/Common_Telerin.md), the form \*\**gald* produced Sindarin *[galadh](../G/Galadh.md)* through <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Epenthesis&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Epenthesis (page does not exist)">epenthesis</a> (called *svarabhakti* in the book).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The analysis also correctly supposed the existence of initial nasal stops, such as *mb-* which evolved to Quenya *m-* and Sindarin *b-*. Ironically, the foreword of the book mentions that the theory was deemed as incompatible to new Elvish data found in the recent *Silmarillion*, and suggested that the evidence for the nasal stops was only a result of coincidence.<sup id="cite_ref-post_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-post-2">[2]</a></sup> However, later sources such as *The Etymologies* confirmed that initial nasal stops indeed existed in the Primitive Quendian stage (cf. words like *ndōro*, *mbandō*, *ñgōlē*).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
