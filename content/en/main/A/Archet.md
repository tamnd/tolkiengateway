**Archet** was a village in [Bree-land](../B/Bree-land.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Sign_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sign-1">[1]</a></sup> It was located northeast of [Bree](../B/Bree.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Sign_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sign-1">[1]</a></sup> north of the [East Road](../E/East_Road.md) north of [Combe](../C/Combe.md) among the trees probably on the southern<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> edge of the [Chetwood](../C/Chetwood.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Sign_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sign-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When [Aragorn](Aragorn.md), [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md), [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md), [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) and [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) left the [Prancing Pony](../P/Prancing_Pony.md) inn in Bree in the morning of [30 September](../_/30_September.md), [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> even many inhabitants from Archet were crowded on the road in Bree to watch their departure.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> On that day Aragorn and the [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) left the East Road and turned to the north in the direction of Archet and on the next day turned right before they reached Archet to pass it on the east through the Chetwood.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-2">[2]</a></sup>

When [Frodo and his companions](../T/Travellers.md) returned to Bree on [28 October](../_/28_October.md), [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Barliman Butterbur told them that Bree had been raided early in the year by [ruffians](../R/Ruffians.md) from the south during the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md). Barliman Butterbur believed that they had been let into Bree by Harry Goatleaf and Bill Ferny, which had been on the side of the ruffians and which had fled Bree with them after they had lost the fight against the inhabitants of Bree, but three Men and two Hobbits from Bree were killed. The defeated ruffians fled to the woods beyond Archet and the wilds where they lived as robbers so that travelling on the road was no longer safe. As a result the inhabitants of Breeland locked up their dwellings early, kept watchers all around the hedge and guarded the gates with a lot of men during the night. He also informed them that the Bree-landers had not understood how much the Rangers of the North had done for their safety, because after they all left (to fight in the War of the Ring in the south) wolves had howled around the fences in the winter and there were dark shapes in the woods, dreadful things that make the blood run cold when he thought of them.<sup id="cite_ref-Homeward_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Homeward-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Archet* is an "English place-name of [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md) origin.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomenclature_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomenclature-8">[8]</a></sup>

The name *Archet* has been modelled on a relic of a "British" (i.e. Celtic) name that was chosen for its meaning, because the survival of traces of the older language of the Bree-men resembled the survival of Celtic elements in England. The element *chet* means "wood".<sup id="cite_ref-Nomenclature_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomenclature-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) stated that the name *Archet* was descended from British <i>*ar(e)cait-</i> and that [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md) *argoed* means "trees" or "edge of forest".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Mark T. Hooker](../M/Mark_T._Hooker.md) suggests that the element <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ar-#Old_Irish" title="wiktionary:ar-">Ar-</a></i> is a prefix meaning "nearby", found in several [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md) place-names. The meaning of *Archet* would thus be "near the woods", which Hooker notes describes its geographic position "*on the edge of the Chetwood*".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

[David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) suggests that *Archet* means "by the wood" and that it is equivalent to Welsh *Argoed*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

The real-life [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md) place-name <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argoed" title="wikipedia:Argoed">Argoed</a></i> means "by a wood".
