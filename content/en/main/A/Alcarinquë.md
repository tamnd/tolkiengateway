**Alcarinquë** was a name of one of the [stars](/wiki/Stars "Stars") in [Arda](/wiki/Arda).<sup id="cite_ref-SI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SI-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the distant past, at the time of the awakening of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), [Varda](/wiki/Varda) added many brilliant stars to the heavens using the shimmering dews of [Telperion](/wiki/Telperion) the Silver Tree.<sup id="cite_ref-Captivity_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Captivity-2">[2]</a></sup> Among these were the familiar star-patterns we still know today, and other lights that were not truly stars. Among these was *Alcarinquë* - the planet we know as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter" title="wikipedia:Jupiter">Jupiter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MRS_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MRS-3">[3]</a></sup>

*Alcarinquë* would likely have been the brightest object in the skies at that time. When Varda created Alcarinquë and the other new lights the Elves first awoke.<sup id="cite_ref-Captivity_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Captivity-2">[2]</a></sup> It was not until the Elves had been to [Aman](/wiki/Aman) and the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) were returning to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) that the Moon and the Sun were created<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and Venus - the [Silmaril](/wiki/Silmarils "Silmarils") of [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) - was not placed in the heavens until much later.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Alcarinquë* (or *Alkarinque*) is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name meaning "The Glorious",<sup id="cite_ref-SI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SI-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MRS_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MRS-3">[3]</a></sup> likely derived from the Quenya adjective <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Alcarinqua&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Alcarinqua (page does not exist)">alcarinqua</a></i> ("glorious").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
