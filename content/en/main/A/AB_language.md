The **AB language** refers to a medieval literary dialect first identified by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) in the article "[Ancrene Wisse and Hali Meiðhad](/wiki/Ancrene_Wisse_and_Hali_Mei%C3%B0had)" (published in [*Essays and Studies by Members of the English Association*, 14](</wiki/Essays_and_Studies_(1929)> "Essays and Studies (1929)")).

## History {#History}

Tolkien had found that the [Middle English](/wiki/Middle_English) spelling habits of the manuscript <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ancrene_Wisse_(manuscript)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ancrene Wisse (manuscript) (page does not exist)">Ancrene Wisse</a></i> (i.e., "A") showed great similarities to the manuscripts of the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Katherine_Group&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Katherine Group (page does not exist)">Katherine Group</a> (i.e., "B"). This lead Tolkien to propose the existence of a standard literary language (the AB language) in the West Midland area of England in the 13th century. Tolkien's further investigations of the AB language led him to publish his [Ancrene Wisse](/wiki/Ancrene_Wisse) in 1962.

According to [Arne Zettersten](/wiki/Arne_Zettersten), Tolkien's research on the AB language was perhaps his most important academic contribution, influencing the work of his student and colleague [Simonne d'Ardenne](/wiki/Simonne_d%27Ardenne) and Zettersten himself, as well as contemporary research on Middle English.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
