**Berelach** was the son of [Borlas](/wiki/Borlas) of [Pen-arduin](/wiki/Pen-arduin),<sup id="cite_ref-Lach_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lach-1">[1]</a></sup> and the grandson of [Beregond](/wiki/Beregond).<sup id="cite_ref-New_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-New-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Berelach was the youngest child and only son of Borlas. During his youth, he was a friend of [Saelon](/wiki/Saelon).<sup id="cite_ref-New_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-New-2">[2]</a></sup>

Berelach served in the [King's Ships](/wiki/King%27s_Ships), and for several years he was stationed at [Harlond](</wiki/Harlond_(Gondor)> "Harlond (Gondor)"). He sometimes visited his father at [Pen-arduin](/wiki/Pen-arduin) to ease his grief and loneliness, often accompanied by [Saelon](/wiki/Saelon).

During the year [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [100](/wiki/Fourth_Age_100 "Fourth Age 100"), his visits became short and not very often, as he had been given a high command, and thus spent many of his days at sea. But even when he was on land, his duty often kept him within [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir).<sup id="cite_ref-Lach_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lach-1">[1]</a></sup>

When Berelach had last visited his father before leaving again in [April](/wiki/April) of [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [103](/wiki/Fourth_Age_103 "Fourth Age 103"), he told him of a recent incident in which a few Shipmen and a small vessel of the Fleet had disappeared. Berelach makes the assumption that the shipmen must have left without leave on some task of their own and drowned. Berelach reasons that the shipmen were unskilled and deserved their fate, serving as evidence that the King's Ships have too few skilled sailers and that fish are more profitable.<sup id="cite_ref-New_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-New-2">[2]</a></sup>

Later in the month of [June](/wiki/June), Borlas quoted Berelach's tale to Saelon, only for Saelon to contradict Berelach by saying that the Shipmen were skilled sons of fishermen and that there had been no storms along the coast for a long time.<sup id="cite_ref-New_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-New-2">[2]</a></sup>

It is unknown whether it was Berelach or Saelon who spoke the truth of the tale.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Berelach* is in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), however its meaning is unclear. According to [Paul Strack](/wiki/Paul_Strack), the name might contain the element *[lach](/wiki/Lach "Lach")* ("leaping flame").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the first manuscript of the story (*Manuscript A*), Berelach's name was originally written as **Berthil**, before being changed to **Bergil**,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and then finally to *Berelach*, the current form (*Manuscript B* and ‘’Manuscript C*).*
