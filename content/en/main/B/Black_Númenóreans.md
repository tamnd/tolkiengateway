The **Black Númenóreans** emerged from the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men) party;<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup> they were cruel oppressors and overlords over the primitive [Men](/wiki/Men) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). Since they were colonising the continent, they survived the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) but swiftly diminished.

## Background {#Background}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="/wiki/King%27s_Men" title="King&#39;s Men">King&#39;s Men</a></b></div></div>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r398211">.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote{position:relative;margin:0.5rem 0;padding:0.75rem 1rem;box-shadow:var(--tg-border-color-base)0px 0px 0px 1px inset;border-radius:var(--tg-border-radius-medium);background:var(--tg-color-surface-2);font-size:var(--tg-font-size-small);line-height:var(--tg-line-height-xx-small);display:flex;gap:0.5rem;align-items:center}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote-icon{display:flex;align-items:center}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote-icon .mw-file-element{display:block}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote-icon .mw-file-element[src$=".svg"]{filter:var(--filter-invert);opacity:0.5}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote.tg-hatnote--red{background:var(--background-color-destructive-subtle);color:var(--color-emphasized)}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote.tg-hatnote--yellow{background:var(--background-color-warning-subtle);color:var(--color-emphasized)}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote.tg-hatnote--green{background:var(--background-color-success-subtle);color:var(--color-emphasized)}</style>

The [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans)' power and knowledge had grown throughout the course of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), and became increasingly preoccupied with the limits placed on their happiness—and eventually their power—by mortality, the purpose of which they began to question. They started fearing the [Gift of Men](/wiki/Gift_of_Men) and attempted to delay it or recall life. This growing wish to escape death made most of the Númenóreans envious of the immortal [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar), who they had come to physically resemble. The Eldar sought ever to remind the [Men of Númenor](/wiki/Men_of_N%C3%BAmenor) however, that death was a gift of the One God, [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar), to all men, and the will of Ilúvatar could not be gainsaid.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup>

During the reign of the Númenórean kings [Tar-Ciryatan](/wiki/Tar-Ciryatan) and [Tar-Atanamir](/wiki/Tar-Atanamir), who were both greedy for wealth, the Númenóreans formed two parties, the party of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful), who remained faithful to the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and maintained their friendship with the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and the party of the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men), who followed the policy of the King to oppose the [Ban of the Valar](/wiki/Ban_of_the_Valar) that forbade to sail to [Aman](/wiki/Aman) and did not maintain the friendship with the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The King's Men sailed far away to the south of Middle-earth and established lordships and strongholds<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> in Umbar, Harad and many other places on the coasts of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-6">[6]</a></sup> The Númenóreans in the service of Tar-Ciryatan and Tar-Atanamir oppressed the men of Middle-earth and levied heavy tribute from the men of the coasts and their ships brought back metals and gems from Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Three of the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl") were Númenóreans lords corrupted by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup> Over 1000 years<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> before the [Downfall](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor"), they served [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), being enslaved to his will.

## History {#History}

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

#### Origin {#Origin}

Many of the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men) who had sailed to the shores and seaward regions in the south of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and established fortresses and dwellings during [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)'s sojourn in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) were already bent to the will of Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-9">[9]</a></sup> As a consequence, the King's Men hated the followers of Elendil. Umbar was a stronghold of the King's Men.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup>

#### After the Downfall of Númenor {#After_the_Downfall_of_Númenor}

The King's Men were afterwards called the Black Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup>

When Sauron returned to Middle-earth after the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) many of the Black Númenóreans served him still. Two Black Númenóreans, [Herumor](/wiki/Herumor) and [Fuinur](/wiki/Fuinur) rose to power among the [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim) in the lands south of Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-9">[9]</a></sup>

As [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) was preparing to go to [war](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance "War of the Last Alliance") against the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and [Exiles](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Exiles of Númenor"), he gathered great strength of his servants from the south, including many Black Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-9">[9]</a></sup>

### Decline {#Decline}

After the fall of Sauron in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), the race of the Black Númenóreans "dwindled" swiftly or "became merged with the [Men](/wiki/Men) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth)".<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup> However, their descendants retained control over Umbar and inherited their hatred of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup>

Although the race of the Black Númenóreans had dwindled, a few notable figures were Black Númenóreans in later times. One of these was [Queen Berúthiel](/wiki/Ber%C3%BAthiel "Berúthiel"), the "nefarious, solitary, and loveless" wife of [Gondor's](/wiki/Gondor "Gondor") King [Tarannon Falastur](/wiki/Tarannon_Falastur), who ruled from [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [830](/wiki/Third_Age_830 "Third Age 830") until [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [913](/wiki/Third_Age_913 "Third Age 913") who had her exiled from Gondor and her name erased from the [Book of the Kings](/wiki/Book_of_the_Kings).<sup id="cite_ref-Interview_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Interview-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [933](/wiki/Third_Age_933 "Third Age 933"),<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> [King](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor "Kings of Gondor") [Eärnil I](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnil_I) captured [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar), driving the lords of Umbar into exile.<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-14">[14]</a></sup> 82 years later, in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1015](/wiki/Third_Age_1015 "Third Age 1015"),<sup id="cite_ref-Ciryandil_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ciryandil-15">[15]</a></sup> these lords led the [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim) in a siege of Umbar<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-14">[14]</a></sup> during which King [Ciryandil](/wiki/Ciryandil) of Gondor, the son of Eärnil I, was killed.<sup id="cite_ref-Ciryandil_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ciryandil-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[note 1]</a></sup>

Another more notable Black Númenórean was the [Mouth of Sauron](/wiki/Mouth_of_Sauron). He "entered the service of the [Dark Tower](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr "Barad-dûr") when it first rose again" and learned great sorcery and had forgotten his own name.<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-17">[16]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[note 2]</a></sup> He mocked the army of [King Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") in front of the [Morannon](/wiki/Morannon).<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-17">[16]</a></sup>

By *c.* [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050")<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[18]</a></sup> the settlements of the Men of Númenor in the south "beyond Umbar" had been "absorbed" or had become hostile and parts of Sauron's dominions, because they had been established by Men of Númenor who had already been corrupted by Sauron in Númenor. Sauron was probably most active in Near Harad and Far Harad, because those regions were a source for man-power for him, but the people of Harad could probably also be converted more easily to resist against Sauron than those of other regions in the south.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[19]</a></sup> It is not known, whether "absorbed" meant that the settlements were conquered by other Men of Middle-earth, that the Men of Númenor in those settlements intermarried with other people from Middle-earth or that the settlements were absorbed by the sea during the floods that were caused by the drowning of Númenor on the shores of Middle-earth.

## Culture {#Culture}

The Black Númenóreans were "enamoured of evil knowledge" and worshipped [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron).<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-17">[16]</a></sup> They hated the followers of Elendil and their realm Gondor. After the fall of Sauron in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance) their race "dwindled" swiftly or "became merged with the Men of Middle-earth".<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup> It is not known, whether this dwindling meant that their lifespan and knowledge dwindled like that of the Dúnedain of Gondor or that their numbers dwindled.

It is possible that [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir) was referring to the King's Men or the Black Númenóreans, when he said that most of the Men of Númenor who settled far and wide on the shores and seaward regions of Middle-earth fell into eviles and follies: many became enamoured of the Darkness and of black arts, some were fully idle and lived at ease and some fought each other until they were conquered by the wild men when they were weak.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[20]</a></sup>

**1982 - 1997:** [Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing)

<dl><dd>A tree with the kindreds of Men lists Umbareans, Saruleans, Elornans, Ciryedain and Mirëdorans as subbranches of the Black Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[21]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II:_The_Rise_of_the_Witch-king" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Black Númenóreans inhabit the land of <a href="/wiki/Angmar" title="Angmar">Angmar</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Witch-king" title="Witch-king">Witch-king</a> turns their allegiance to him to help fight against <a href="/wiki/Arnor" title="Arnor">Arnor</a>. The Black Númenóreans are playable units as well as their counterparts, Dark Rangers and Thrall Masters.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Black Númenóreans are portrayed in service of the different Lieutenants of the Enemy. The <i>Angmarim</i> inhabit the lands surrounding <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Carn_Dum" title="Carn Dum">Carn Dum</a> in Angmar and serve <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Mordirith" title="Mordirith">Mordirith</a> and later <a href="/wiki/Amarthiel" title="Amarthiel">Amarthiel</a>. Much later, <i>Umbarrim</i> Black Númenóreans make appearance in <a href="/wiki/Dol_Guldur" title="Dol Guldur">Dol Guldur</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mirkwood" title="Mirkwood">Mirkwood</a>. When the lands of <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Mordor</a> are explored following the downfall of <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>, Black Númenórean cultists known as <i>Mordorrim</i> are shown to be among its inhabitants.</dd></dl>

<b>2011: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_War_in_the_North" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Aganda%C3%BBr" title="Agandaûr">Agandaûr</a>, the game&#39;s main antagonist, is a Black Númenórean<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[22]</a></sup> as is the minor character <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfrun&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Wulfrun (page does not exist)">Wulfrun</a>, lieutenant of <a href="/wiki/Carn_D%C3%BBm" title="Carn Dûm">Carn Dûm</a>.</dd></dl>
