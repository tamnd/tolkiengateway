**Bucklebury** was the chief village of [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland), lying in the shadow of [Buck Hill](/wiki/Buck_Hill), seat of the [Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland). It was built about a mile east of the banks of the River [Brandywine](/wiki/Brandywine).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The [Bucklebury Ferry](/wiki/Bucklebury_Ferry) was a passage across the [Brandywine](/wiki/Brandywine) between Bucklebury and the [Marish](/wiki/Marish).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name means Buck-burg, or Buck-town (*buck* always means male goat or deer).

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") notes that it's "Buck*le*bury" rather than plain "Buckbury" because the word is either an alteration of earlier *Bucken-bury* (Bucken being archaic plural) or a reduction of <i><b>Buckl</b>and</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-nomen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>

Based on this, [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) has suggested that Bucklebury represents a possible [Old Hobbitish](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") form \**Buccanburh* "[Bucca](/wiki/Bucca)'s burg".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <span></span>
