The **Black Speech** was the [language](../L/Languages.md) of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md).

## History {#History}

This speech was created by [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) during the [Dark Years](../D/Dark_Years.md) to be the sole language of all the servants of Mordor, replacing the many different varieties of [Orkish](../O/Orkish.md) and other languages used by his servants. When Sauron was overthrown at the end of the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) the ancient "pure" form was forgotten by all but the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md). When Sauron returned it was once again made the official language of [Barad-dûr](Barad-dûr.md). However, a more "debased" form was used by the soldiery of [Barad-dûr](Barad-dûr.md) at the end of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The only example given of "pure" Black Speech is the [inscription](../R/Ring-inscription.md) upon [the One Ring](../T/The_One_Ring.md):<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Ash nazg durbatulûk, ash nazg gimbatul,<br/>
ash nazg thrakatulûk, agh burzum-ishi krimpatul.
</p>
</blockquote>

When translated into English, these words form the lines:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>One Ring to rule them all, One Ring to find them,<br/>
One Ring to bring them all and in the darkness bind them.
</p>
</blockquote>

These are the first two lines from the end of a verse about the [Rings of Power](../R/Rings_of_Power.md).

Many Orkish dialects had adopted words from it.<sup id="cite_ref-Trans_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trans-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Commenting on the Ring Inscription, Tolkien explains:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The Black Speech was not intentionally modeled on any style, but was meant to be self ­consistent, very different from Elvish, yet organized and expressive, as would be expected of a device of Sauron before his complete corruption. It was evidently an agglutinative language, and the verbal system must have included pronominal suffixes expressing the object, as well as those indicating the subject. [...] I have tried to play fair linguistically, and it is meant to have a meaning and not to be a mere casual group of nasty noises, though an accurate translation would even nowadays only be printable in the higher and artistically more advanced forms of literature. According to my taste such things are best left to Orcs, ancient and modern.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

Tolkien linguist [Helge Fauskanger](../H/Helge_Fauskanger.md) has noted a theory proposed by Russian historian Alexander Nemirovski, that the Black Speech, according to the shape of words, agglutination and grammar, shares many similarities with the ancient Mesopotamian language <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurrian_language" title="wikipedia:Hurrian language">Hurrian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2001-03: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Linguist <a href="../D/David_Salo.md" title="David Salo">David Salo</a> used what little is known of the Black Speech to create enough of a language for use in the films.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> This is usually referred to by <a class="mw-redirect" href="../T/Tolkienists.md" title="Tolkienists">Tolkienists</a> as neo-Black Speech.</dd></dl>

<b>2012-14: <a href="../T/The_Hobbit_(film_series).md" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the 2013 film, <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_The_Desolation_of_Smaug.md" title="The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug">The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug</a></i>, <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> showed a promissory note to <a href="../T/Thorin.md" title="Thorin">Thorin</a>. The note that promised payment for Thorin&#39;s head was written in Black Speech.</dd></dl>
