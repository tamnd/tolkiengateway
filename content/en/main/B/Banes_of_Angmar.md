In [Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing), the **Banes of Angmar** were a collective term for the magical blights, epidemics, diseases, and curses that afflicted [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1636](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636") and [Fo.A.](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age") [15](/wiki/Fourth_Age_15 "Fourth Age 15"). Unable to defeat the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain) on the battlefield, the [Witch-king of Angmar](/wiki/Witch-king_of_Angmar) turned to these methods instead.

While successful in slowly wasting away the Northern Kingdom, the Banes also depleted the forces of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) itself. The true toll of the Banes would be paid much later: the environmental degradation left much of Eriador desolate, despite the fall of Angmar, with the land only being healed under [King Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn").

## Known Banes {#Known_Banes}

While the term was also often used to encompass all the supernatural tactics, such as the sending of the [Barrow-wights](/wiki/Barrow-wights), scholars tended to focus on plagues and afflictions. A point of contention between Arthadan and Gondorian scholars was whether the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) was part of these.

### Amok Curse {#Amok_Curse}

A rune-spell that mentally weakened its victims, and ensorcelled them to pass the rune to others. It appeared in [Fornost](/wiki/Fornost) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_1781&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 1781 (page does not exist)">1781</a> via forged leather-bound letters allegedly sent from [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), and heavily affected the Seers of Fornost. Cursed documents still showed up in treasure hordes by the time of War of the Ring.

### Bloodeye Ravish {#Bloodeye_Ravish}

Aimed at hampering the Dúnadan cavalry, the Bloodeye ravish was spread by lice. It made horses clumsy and visually impaired, and in later stages prone to fury. It decimated half the horses in Eriador between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_1968&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 1968 (page does not exist)">1968</a>–<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_1970&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 1970 (page does not exist)">1970</a>, though its spread to the herds of Angmar kept the Witch-king from pressing the advantage. Its continued presence among wild animals was one of the reasons that kept the [Rangers of the North](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North) on foot.

### Crop and Herd Blights {#Crop_and_Herd_Blights}

These agricultural blights were frequently introduced by the Angmarrim, but plants and animals quickly developed resistances. While [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain) was generally able to keep its people fed, the remaining blights were able to spread more easily after its fall.

### Hound-fire {#Hound-fire}

A form of rabies meant to defeat the Arthedan-led dog-packs that hunted down Orcs and Wolves. It was spread through magically contaminated blood, which was smuggled in sausages in the 1710s. While many dogs had to destroyed, the strongest packs were resistant. This Bane also backfired, for through ticks it wiped out half of Angmar's war-wolves. Afterwards, Hound-fire was carried by asymptomatic foxes and skunks.

### Red Flux {#Red_Flux}

Possibly specifically targeted at Hobbits, who were the most prosperous folk of Arthedain at the time, the Red Flux was an influenza-like illness which slew half of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")'s population in the winters of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_1889&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 1889 (page does not exist)">1889</a>-[1891](/wiki/Third_Age_1891 "Third Age 1891") because of their overcrowded smials. It was likely spread by red-eyed undead creatures who wandered the Shire, leading to Hobbits becoming wary of strangers and starting to build above ground. The following year, it struck Fornost, where it was blamed on dark priests.

### Stream Fever {#Stream_Fever}

A mold from the [Under-deeps](/wiki/Under-deeps) which was put into Eriador's waterways. It weakened the immune system of herbivores, leading to entire herds being wiped out. As a thing from the Under-deeps, it could not abide the sun. By the 25th century of the Third Age, Hobbits had figured out a way to permanently cleanse streams, which under King Elessar was deployed throughout all of Eriador.

### Wood-Blights {#Wood-Blights}

From [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_1860&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 1860 (page does not exist)">1860</a> onward, hostile minor tree-spirits spread fungal agents that weakened trees, which contributed to the decline of Arthedain's forests. <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> <span></span>
