The **Bree Calendar** is the calendar used by the [Bree-folk](Bree-folk.md) during the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).

Some of the month names were different from those of the [Shire Calendar](../S/Shire_Calendar.md), which evolved independently. Those names were however also used in the [Eastfarthing](../E/Eastfarthing.md) of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When the [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) were still a [wandering](../W/Wandering_Days.md) people, their calendaric unit was not a 'week' , but a 'month', governed more or less by the [Moon](../M/Moon.md). In their old calendar, the new year began after harvest. This can be seen in the name of the month [Winterfilth](../W/Winterfilth.md) meaning "filling (of the year) before winter". However, through contact with alien peoples (perhaps the [Dúnedain of Arnor](../D/Dúnedain_of_Arnor.md)) they adopted the notion of weeks.

The Bree calendar is obviously a [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbit.md) localization of the [King's Reckoning](../K/King's_Reckoning.md) using [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md) month-names and several minor alterations to fit their customs. It is not known when the Calendar was introduced, but the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Bree_Reckoning&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Bree Reckoning (page does not exist)">Bree Reckoning</a> begun at [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1300](../T/Third_Age_1300.md) (when Hobbits arrived in [Bree](Bree.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The Bree Calendar followed that of the Shire in a couple of reforms, such as the shortening of the two medial months and the introduction of the [Lithedays](../L/Lithe.md). The Bree Calendar also followed [Thain](../T/Thain.md) [Isengrim II](../I/Isengrim_Took_II.md)'s reform, which put the [Mid-year's Day](../M/Mid-year's_Day.md) outside the reckoning of weekdays, so that every year started and ended at the same day of the week. <span></span>
