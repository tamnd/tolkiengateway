The **Book of the Foxrook** is a manuscript by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), likely composed around [10 June](/wiki/10_June) [1909](/wiki/1909) (this date was added to the title-page).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

It consists of a 16-page notebook containing "*the key to a secret code consisting of a rune-like phonetic alphabet and a sizable number of ideographic symbols*".<sup id="cite_ref-Seven_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seven-2">[2]</a></sup> The invented script is called *[Privata Kodo Skauta](/wiki/Privata_Kodo_Skauta)* ([Modern English](/wiki/Modern_English): "Private Scout Code").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The book also contains comments on [Esperanto](/wiki/Esperanto).<sup id="cite_ref-Seven_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seven-2">[2]</a></sup>

The existence of this manuscript was apparently first mentioned by [Humphrey Carpenter](/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter) in his *[J.R.R. Tolkien: A Biography](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_A_Biography)* (p. 37).<sup id="cite_ref-Seven_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seven-2">[2]</a></sup> A page from the Book of the Foxrook was reproduced in the exhibition catalogue *[J.R.R. Tolkien: Life and Legend](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_Life_and_Legend)*. An analysis and transcription of the same page appeared in the article "Tolkien and Esperanto" (cf. pp. 29–34), published in [*SEVEN*, Volume 17](/wiki/SEVEN_17 "SEVEN 17").

The Book of the Foxrook is kept at the [Bodleian Library](/wiki/Bodleian_Library) (Family papers 1/29(3), fol. 1r).<sup id="cite_ref-Seven_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seven-2">[2]</a></sup>
