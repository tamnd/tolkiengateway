**Brytta Léofa** ([Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) [2752](../T/Third_Age_2752.md) – [2842](../T/Third_Age_2842.md), aged 90 years) was the eleventh [King of Rohan](../K/King_of_Rohan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Brytta_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brytta-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Brytta was the son of [Fréaláf Hildeson](../F/Fréaláf.md) and the grandnephew of king [Helm](../H/Helm.md) of Rohan. He was the grandson of the King's sister [Hild](../H/Hild.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Helm_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helm-2">[2]</a></sup>

During the reign of Helm, when Brytta was still a child, Rohan was invaded by the [Dunlendings](../D/Dunlendings.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2758](../T/Third_Age_2758.md) and conquered the capital of [Edoras](../E/Edoras.md). The survivors were enslaved and others fled to the valleys of the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md). Brytta's father, Fréaláf, fled with many others to the mountain refuge at [Dunharrow](../D/Dunharrow.md). King Helm retreated with his surviving forces to the [Hornburg](../H/Hornburg.md) and was besieged by the invaders.<sup id="cite_ref-Helm_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helm-2">[2]</a></sup>

A short time afterwards the [Long Winter](../L/Long_Winter.md) came and covered Rohan in snow. The cold and famine that followed caused great suffering for both the Rohirrim and the invaders, and King Helm with his sons lost their lives. Soon after the end of the winter, Fréalaf led a small company from Dunharrow, liberated Edoras by surprise and killed the Dunlending leader [Wulf](../W/Wulf.md). Before the end of [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2759](../T/Third_Age_2759.md) the Dunlendings were driven from Rohan.<sup id="cite_ref-Helm_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helm-2">[2]</a></sup>

Fréaláf was crowned as the new King of Rohan. The wizard [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md) came to the crowning bringing gifts and took up residence in the Gondorian fortress of [Isengard](../I/Isengard.md), with the permission of Steward [Beren](<Beren_(Steward_of_Gondor).md>) of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Frealaf_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Frealaf-3">[3]</a></sup>

Brytta became king after the death of his father in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2798](../T/Third_Age_2798.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

During his reign Rohan was still recovering from the war with the [Dunlendings](../D/Dunlendings.md) during which many Rohirrim had died and many of their cattle and horses had been lost.<sup id="cite_ref-Frealaf_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Frealaf-3">[3]</a></sup> Brytta was generous and helped those in need. His people called him Léofa, because he was loved by all.<sup id="cite_ref-Brytta_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brytta-1">[1]</a></sup>

His rule saw a new trouble, as the [final battle](Battle_of_Azanulbizar.md) of the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](../W/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs.md) caused great numbers of [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) to flee from the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md), trying to make a home in the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md) in the south of Rohan.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> During his rule the Rohirrim hunted down the Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-Brytta_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brytta-1">[1]</a></sup>

Brytta was the longest-lived king of the Second Line (from Fréaláf to [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md))<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and ruled for 44 years until his death in 2842, at which it was believed that Rohan was free of the Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-Brytta_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brytta-1">[1]</a></sup> He was buried in the second mound on the east-side of the Barrow-field,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> on the left side of the road that approached Edoras<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> from the north-west.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Brytta was succeeded by his son [Walda](../W/Walda.md) at his death.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Brytta* means "bestower", "distributor", "prince", or "lord" in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md).<sup id="cite_ref-RC_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-11">[11]</a></sup> In poetry it is frequently found in epithets for a prince or lord. For instance, in line 352 of [Beowulf](<Beowulf_(poem).md>), the Danish king Hrothgar is called *béaga bryttan*, "giver of rings". Brytta's nickname Léofa means "beloved".

*Léofa* is derived from Old English *leof*, which means "loved", "beloved", or "dear".<sup id="cite_ref-RC_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-11">[11]</a></sup>
