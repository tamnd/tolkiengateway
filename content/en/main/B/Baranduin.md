The **Baranduin** was a river in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador). To the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), the **Brandywine** (as they called it) was the boundary between the known and unknown, and even those who lived in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) on the immediate opposite shore were considered "peculiar".

## History {#History}

[Elendil](/wiki/Elendil)'s [people](/wiki/Arnorians "Arnorians") dwelt about the course of the Baranduin.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the realm of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) was broken up in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861"), the river south of the [Brandywine Bridge](/wiki/Brandywine_Bridge) served as part of the border between [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain) and [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

When [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") was founded, the river was its eastern border. In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2340](/wiki/Third_Age_2340 "Third Age 2340") [Gorhendad Oldbuck](/wiki/Gorhendad_Oldbuck) crossed the river and founded [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall), and Baranduin now separated the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing) from [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland).

[Primula](/wiki/Primula_Brandybuck "Primula Brandybuck") and [Drogo Baggins](/wiki/Drogo_Baggins), parents of [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"), were lost along the river in a boating accident in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2980](/wiki/Third_Age_2980 "Third Age 2980"). As of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), [Merry Brandybuck](/wiki/Merry_Brandybuck) knew of no instances in which horses had crossed the river by swimming.

## Course {#Course}

Flowing out of [Lake Evendim](/wiki/Lake_Evendim) in northern [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), the river flowed eastward for about 60 miles before turning generally southward; after about another 120 miles it flowed through the easternmost reaches of the Shire, forming that land's eastern border, except for [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland), which lies east of it. Its only major crossings in the Shire are the [Brandywine Bridge](/wiki/Brandywine_Bridge) (originally *Bridge of Stonebows*) on the [East Road](/wiki/East_Road) and the [Bucklebury Ferry](/wiki/Bucklebury_Ferry). The [Girdley Island](/wiki/Girdley_Island) is just above the Brandywine Bridge.

Skirting the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest) to the south, the river then looped south-westward, crossing an old road at [Sarn Ford](/wiki/Sarn_Ford) and flowed to the north of the depopulated region of [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath) before flowing into the [Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer") to the north of the forested region of [Eryn Vorn](/wiki/Eryn_Vorn).

## Tributaries {#Tributaries}

No tributares of the Baranduin are described except those near or in the Shire:

- [The Water](/wiki/The_Water) - central Shire, from the northwest
- The [Stock-brook](/wiki/Stock-brook) - arises in the Woody End
- River [Shirebourn](/wiki/Shirebourn) - rises in Green-Hill country, tributary is Thistle Brook
- [Withywindle](/wiki/Withywindle) - from the Old Forest
- River [Norbourn](/wiki/Norbourn) - in the [Northfarthing](/wiki/Farthings "Farthings")

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Baranduin* was [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "golden-brown river", from *[baran](</wiki/Baran_(Sindarin)> "Baran (Sindarin)")* and *[duin](/wiki/Duin "Duin")*.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) of the Shire originally gave it the punning name <i><b>Branda-nîn</b></i>, meaning "border water" in original [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) [Westron](/wiki/Westron). This was later punned again as *[Bralda-hîm](/wiki/Bralda-h%C3%AEm)* meaning "heady ale" (referring to the colour of its water), which Tolkien renders into English as <i><b>Brandywine</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-4">[4]</a></sup>

The word *Brandywine* both resembles the original Elvish name *Baranduin*, and provides the Hobbitish meaning adequately.

The word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/brandywine" title="wiktionary:brandywine">brandywine</a></i> was actually the archaic English word for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/brandy" title="wikipedia:brandy">brandy</a> as imported from the Dutch <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/brandewijn" title="wiktionary:brandewijn">brandewijn</a></i>. [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) noted that it represents a possible [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) \**baernedwin*, meaning "burned wine", which would resemble quite closely the original Elvish *Baranduin*,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> making [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) *Brandywine* a legitimate corruption of S. *Baranduin*.
