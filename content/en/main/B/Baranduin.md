The **Baranduin** was a river in [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md). To the [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md), the **Brandywine** (as they called it) was the boundary between the known and unknown, and even those who lived in [Buckland](Buckland.md) on the immediate opposite shore were considered "peculiar".

## History {#History}

[Elendil](../E/Elendil.md)'s [people](../A/Arnorians.md) dwelt about the course of the Baranduin.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the realm of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) was broken up in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [861](../T/Third_Age_861.md), the river south of the [Brandywine Bridge](Brandywine_Bridge.md) served as part of the border between [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md) and [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

When [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) was founded, the river was its eastern border. In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2340](../T/Third_Age_2340.md) [Gorhendad Oldbuck](../G/Gorhendad_Oldbuck.md) crossed the river and founded [Brandy Hall](Brandy_Hall.md), and Baranduin now separated the [Eastfarthing](../E/Eastfarthing.md) from [Buckland](Buckland.md).

[Primula](../P/Primula_Brandybuck.md) and [Drogo Baggins](../D/Drogo_Baggins.md), parents of [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md), were lost along the river in a boating accident in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2980](../T/Third_Age_2980.md). As of [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md), [Merry Brandybuck](../M/Merry_Brandybuck.md) knew of no instances in which horses had crossed the river by swimming.

## Course {#Course}

Flowing out of [Lake Evendim](../L/Lake_Evendim.md) in northern [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md), the river flowed eastward for about 60 miles before turning generally southward; after about another 120 miles it flowed through the easternmost reaches of the Shire, forming that land's eastern border, except for [Buckland](Buckland.md), which lies east of it. Its only major crossings in the Shire are the [Brandywine Bridge](Brandywine_Bridge.md) (originally *Bridge of Stonebows*) on the [East Road](../E/East_Road.md) and the [Bucklebury Ferry](Bucklebury_Ferry.md). The [Girdley Island](../G/Girdley_Island.md) is just above the Brandywine Bridge.

Skirting the [Old Forest](../O/Old_Forest.md) to the south, the river then looped south-westward, crossing an old road at [Sarn Ford](../S/Sarn_Ford.md) and flowed to the north of the depopulated region of [Minhiriath](../M/Minhiriath.md) before flowing into the [Sea](Belegaer.md) to the north of the forested region of [Eryn Vorn](../E/Eryn_Vorn.md).

## Tributaries {#Tributaries}

No tributares of the Baranduin are described except those near or in the Shire:

- [The Water](../T/The_Water.md) - central Shire, from the northwest
- The [Stock-brook](../S/Stock-brook.md) - arises in the Woody End
- River [Shirebourn](../S/Shirebourn.md) - rises in Green-Hill country, tributary is Thistle Brook
- [Withywindle](../W/Withywindle.md) - from the Old Forest
- River [Norbourn](../N/Norbourn.md) - in the [Northfarthing](../F/Farthings.md)

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Baranduin* was [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) for "golden-brown river", from *[baran](<Baran_(Sindarin).md>)* and *[duin](../D/Duin.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) of the Shire originally gave it the punning name <i><b>Branda-nîn</b></i>, meaning "border water" in original [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) [Westron](../W/Westron.md). This was later punned again as *[Bralda-hîm](Bralda-hîm.md)* meaning "heady ale" (referring to the colour of its water), which Tolkien renders into English as <i><b>Brandywine</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-4">[4]</a></sup>

The word *Brandywine* both resembles the original Elvish name *Baranduin*, and provides the Hobbitish meaning adequately.

The word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/brandywine" title="wiktionary:brandywine">brandywine</a></i> was actually the archaic English word for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/brandy" title="wikipedia:brandy">brandy</a> as imported from the Dutch <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/brandewijn" title="wiktionary:brandewijn">brandewijn</a></i>. [David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) noted that it represents a possible [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) \**baernedwin*, meaning "burned wine", which would resemble quite closely the original Elvish *Baranduin*,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> making [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) *Brandywine* a legitimate corruption of S. *Baranduin*.
