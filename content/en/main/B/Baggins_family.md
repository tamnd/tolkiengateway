The **Baggins family** were a remarkable and rich [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") family from [The Shire](/wiki/The_Shire) which included two [Ring-bearers](/wiki/Ring-bearers): [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins").

## History {#History}

The Baggins clan traced their origin to the first recorded Baggins, one *Balbo Baggins*, who was born in or near Hobbiton in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1167](/wiki/Third_Age_2767 "Third Age 2767").

The Baggins family lived all around in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), mostly in or near the town of [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton). They were seen as well to do and respectable, as well as very predictable, for it was said one could tell what a Baggins would say on any occasion without even the bother of asking him.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-1">[1]</a></sup> They had many connections to other aristocratic Hobbit families, like the [Brandybucks](/wiki/Brandybucks) and the [Tooks](/wiki/Tooks); other branches were the [Sackville-Bagginses](/wiki/Sackville-Baggins_Family "Sackville-Baggins Family") and the [Chubb-Bagginses](/wiki/Chubb-Baggins_Family "Chubb-Baggins Family").

Although generally speaking they never had any adventures or did anything unexpected,<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-1">[1]</a></sup> its members with [Took](/wiki/Took_Family "Took Family") blood were more adventurous. A notable example was [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) who set out on the quest for [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain") with [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) the Grey and thirteen [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves); during his absence he was presumed dead and the headship of the family was in doubt, with [Otho](/wiki/Otho_Sackville-Baggins "Otho Sackville-Baggins") of the Sackville-Baggins branch having the ambition of being head of two major families, until Bilbo returned alive.<sup id="cite_ref-fr1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fr1-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-l214_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l214-3">[3]</a></sup> He was seen as odd or queer, but also extremely rich.

Bilbo adopted his "nephew" [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins), who inherited the *[smial](/wiki/Smials "Smials")* of [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) (which did not sit well with Otho<sup id="cite_ref-fr1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fr1-2">[2]</a></sup>) after Bilbo left. Frodo himself was involved in the quest which ended the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).

Other Bagginses include the descendants of [Posco Baggins](/wiki/Posco_Baggins), as well as [Peregrin Took](/wiki/Peregrin_Took) and [Meriadoc Brandybuck](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck), who had maternal Baggins blood.

In [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1421](/wiki/Third_Age_3021 "Third Age 3021") both Bilbo and Frodo went over [the Sea](/wiki/The_Sea "The Sea") but it was still impossible to presume death, so when [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) was elected [Mayor of the Shire](/wiki/Mayor_of_the_Shire), he established a rule of succession and inheritance in such situations. Presumably [Ponto Baggins II](/wiki/Ponto_Baggins_II) became the head of the family.<sup id="cite_ref-l214_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l214-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name Baggins is a translation in English of the actual [Westron](/wiki/Westron) name *[Labingi](/wiki/Labingi)*, which was believed to be related to the Westron word <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Laban&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Laban (page does not exist)">laban</a></i>, "bag". The name is associated with **[Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End)**.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-12">[8]</a></sup>

The name Baggins is translated in most translations of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, often keeping the "bag" or "sack" meaning:<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[9]</a></sup>

- In the Breton translation it is *Sac'heg*.
- In the Cornish translation it is *Baggyn*.
- In the Czech translation it is *Pytlík*.
- In the Dutch translation it is *Balings*.
- In the Estonian translation it is *Paunaste*.
- In the Finnish translation it is *Reppuli*.
- In the French translation it is *Sacquet* in most books, but *Bessac* in the new *Hobbit* translation.
- In the Frisian translation it is *Balsma*.
- In the German translation the family name is *Beutlin*.
- In the Hungarian translation it is *Zsákos*.
- In the Norwegian translation it is *Lommelun*.
- In one of the Polish translations it is *Bagosz*.
- In the Portuguese translation it is *Bolseiro*.
- In the Slovak translation it is *Bublík*.
- In the Slovene translation it is *Bisagin*.
- In the Spanish translation it is *Bolsón*.
- In the Swedish translation it is *Secker*; *Bagger* in the old translation by Åke Ohlmarks.
