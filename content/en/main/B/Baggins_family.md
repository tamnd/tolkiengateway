The **Baggins family** were a remarkable and rich [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) family from [The Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) which included two [Ring-bearers](../R/Ring-bearers.md): [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) and [Bilbo](Bilbo_Baggins.md).

## History {#History}

The Baggins clan traced their origin to the first recorded Baggins, one *Balbo Baggins*, who was born in or near Hobbiton in [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1167](../T/Third_Age_2767.md).

The Baggins family lived all around in [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md), mostly in or near the town of [Hobbiton](../H/Hobbiton.md). They were seen as well to do and respectable, as well as very predictable, for it was said one could tell what a Baggins would say on any occasion without even the bother of asking him.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-1">[1]</a></sup> They had many connections to other aristocratic Hobbit families, like the [Brandybucks](Brandybucks.md) and the [Tooks](../T/Tooks.md); other branches were the [Sackville-Bagginses](../S/Sackville-Baggins_Family.md) and the [Chubb-Bagginses](../C/Chubb-Baggins_Family~66345.md).

Although generally speaking they never had any adventures or did anything unexpected,<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-1">[1]</a></sup> its members with [Took](../T/Took_Family.md) blood were more adventurous. A notable example was [Bilbo Baggins](Bilbo_Baggins.md) who set out on the quest for [Erebor](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md) with [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) the Grey and thirteen [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md); during his absence he was presumed dead and the headship of the family was in doubt, with [Otho](../O/Otho_Sackville-Baggins.md) of the Sackville-Baggins branch having the ambition of being head of two major families, until Bilbo returned alive.<sup id="cite_ref-fr1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fr1-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-l214_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l214-3">[3]</a></sup> He was seen as odd or queer, but also extremely rich.

Bilbo adopted his "nephew" [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md), who inherited the *[smial](../S/Smials.md)* of [Bag End](Bag_End.md) (which did not sit well with Otho<sup id="cite_ref-fr1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fr1-2">[2]</a></sup>) after Bilbo left. Frodo himself was involved in the quest which ended the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md).

Other Bagginses include the descendants of [Posco Baggins](../P/Posco_Baggins.md), as well as [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) and [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md), who had maternal Baggins blood.

In [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1421](../T/Third_Age_3021.md) both Bilbo and Frodo went over [the Sea](../T/The_Sea.md) but it was still impossible to presume death, so when [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) was elected [Mayor of the Shire](../M/Mayor_of_the_Shire.md), he established a rule of succession and inheritance in such situations. Presumably [Ponto Baggins II](../P/Ponto_Baggins_II.md) became the head of the family.<sup id="cite_ref-l214_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l214-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name Baggins is a translation in English of the actual [Westron](../W/Westron.md) name *[Labingi](../L/Labingi.md)*, which was believed to be related to the Westron word <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Laban&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Laban (page does not exist)">laban</a></i>, "bag". The name is associated with **[Bag End](Bag_End.md)**.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-12">[8]</a></sup>

The name Baggins is translated in most translations of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, often keeping the "bag" or "sack" meaning:<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[9]</a></sup>

- In the Breton translation it is *Sac'heg*.
- In the Cornish translation it is *Baggyn*.
- In the Czech translation it is *Pytlík*.
- In the Dutch translation it is *Balings*.
- In the Estonian translation it is *Paunaste*.
- In the Finnish translation it is *Reppuli*.
- In the French translation it is *Sacquet* in most books, but *Bessac* in the new *Hobbit* translation.
- In the Frisian translation it is *Balsma*.
- In the German translation the family name is *Beutlin*.
- In the Hungarian translation it is *Zsákos*.
- In the Norwegian translation it is *Lommelun*.
- In one of the Polish translations it is *Bagosz*.
- In the Portuguese translation it is *Bolseiro*.
- In the Slovak translation it is *Bublík*.
- In the Slovene translation it is *Bisagin*.
- In the Spanish translation it is *Bolsón*.
- In the Swedish translation it is *Secker*; *Bagger* in the old translation by Åke Ohlmarks.
