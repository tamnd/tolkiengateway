**Bucklandish** was the dialect of [Westron](/wiki/Westron) spoken by the [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders).<sup id="cite_ref-Preface_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Preface-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Due to their relative isolation and different route during their [Wandering Days](/wiki/Wandering_Days), the [Stoors](/wiki/Stoors) (the ancestors of the [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders)) borrowed peculiar linguistic manners from the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin) and the [Angle](/wiki/Angle) of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> These linguistic features, with their [Dunlendish](/wiki/Dunlendish) background, were later retained in [The Shire](/wiki/The_Shire) and became characteristics of Bucklandish.

The [Stoors](/wiki/Stoors), who settled in regions like [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland), spoke a dialect that reflected their [Dunlendish](/wiki/Dunlendish) background. The dialect of the Stoors influenced Bucklandish, and many [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders), including the [Brandybucks](/wiki/Brandybucks), retained peculiar names derived from this earlier language.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

*[Bombadil](/wiki/Bombadil)* is said to be a Bucklandish name, added by Hobbit chroniclers to Tom Bombadil's many older ones. It is, like many names of the Bucklanders, untranslatable.<sup id="cite_ref-Preface_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Preface-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien said to the Dutch Translator, [Max Schuchart](/wiki/Max_Schuchart), that there were "'[Celtic](/wiki/Celtic)' elements in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and [East-farthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing "Eastfarthing") names."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Stoors have [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) elements in their names reflecting their [Pre-Númenórean](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) background (via the Dunlendings).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Tolkien mentioned that the survival of traces of the older language of the Stoors and the [Bree-men](/wiki/Bree-landers "Bree-landers") resembled the survival of Celtic elements in England.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Portrayals in adaptations {#Portrayals_in_adaptations}

<b>2001: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck" title="Meriadoc Brandybuck">Meriadoc Brandybuck</a> is described as being the linguistic &#34;oddball&#34; of the group: his accent is noticeably distinct from the other Hobbits seen on-screen, something of an invention between actor <a href="/wiki/Dominic_Monaghan" title="Dominic Monaghan">Dominic Monaghan</a> and the dialect coaches, to reflect his unique origin.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>In keeping with Tolkien&#39;s usage of Celtic for non-<a href="/wiki/Westron" title="Westron">Westron</a> elements, the Stoor characters in Dunland are often seen with <a href="/wiki/Welsh" title="Welsh">Welsh</a> names.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>
