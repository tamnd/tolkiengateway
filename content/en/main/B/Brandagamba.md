<i><b>Brandagamba</b></i> is the original [Westron](/wiki/Westron) version of the [hobbit-name](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") translated into English as **[Brandybuck](/wiki/Brandybuck)**.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It contains the element *[branda](/wiki/Branda "Branda")-* 'borderland' (referring to [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) being on the eastern edge of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")), but also evokes *[Branda-nîn](/wiki/Branda-n%C3%AEn)*, 'border-water' (the early [hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish "Hobbitish") name for the [Brandywine](/wiki/Brandywine) river).<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-1">[1]</a></sup>

The second half of the compound, <i>-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gamba&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gamba (page does not exist)">gamba</a></i>, is a re-occurrence from the older name *[Zaragamba](/wiki/Zaragamba)* (or *[Oldbuck](/wiki/Oldbuck_Family "Oldbuck Family")* in translation), in the sense of a male deer or ram.

The river Branda-nîn was later jokingly altered to *[Bralda-hîm](/wiki/Bralda-h%C3%AEm)*, 'heady ale'. According to Tolkien, "only a very bold hobbit would have ventured to call the [Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland) *braldagamba* in his hearing".<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Note on translation {#Note_on_translation}

While "[Marchbuck](/wiki/Marchbuck_Family "Marchbuck Family")" would be a literal translation of the name, it would lose the traditional connection with Brandywine, apparent in the actual Brandagamba-Branda-nîn. [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") rather translated the name as "[**Brandy**buck](/wiki/Brandybuck "Brandybuck")" in order to retain this connection.<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-1">[1]</a></sup>
