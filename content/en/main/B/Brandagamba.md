<i><b>Brandagamba</b></i> is the original [Westron](../W/Westron.md) version of the [hobbit-name](../H/Hobbits.md) translated into English as **[Brandybuck](Brandybuck.md)**.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It contains the element *[branda](Branda.md)-* 'borderland' (referring to [Buckland](Buckland.md) being on the eastern edge of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md)), but also evokes *[Branda-nîn](Branda-nîn.md)*, 'border-water' (the early [hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) name for the [Brandywine](Brandywine.md) river).<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-1">[1]</a></sup>

The second half of the compound, <i>-<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gamba&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gamba (page does not exist)">gamba</a></i>, is a re-occurrence from the older name *[Zaragamba](../Z/Zaragamba.md)* (or *[Oldbuck](../O/Oldbuck_Family.md)* in translation), in the sense of a male deer or ram.

The river Branda-nîn was later jokingly altered to *[Bralda-hîm](Bralda-hîm.md)*, 'heady ale'. According to Tolkien, "only a very bold hobbit would have ventured to call the [Master of Buckland](../M/Master_of_Buckland.md) *braldagamba* in his hearing".<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Note on translation {#Note_on_translation}

While "[Marchbuck](../M/Marchbuck_Family.md)" would be a literal translation of the name, it would lose the traditional connection with Brandywine, apparent in the actual Brandagamba-Branda-nîn. [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) rather translated the name as "[**Brandy**buck](Brandybuck.md)" in order to retain this connection.<sup id="cite_ref-AppF_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppF-1">[1]</a></sup>
