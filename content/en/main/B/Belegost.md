**Belegost** was one of two great underground [Dwarven](../D/Dwarves.md) [cities](../D/Dwarf_realms.md) in the [Blue Mountains](Blue_Mountains.md), the other being [Nogrod](../N/Nogrod.md), that prospered during the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md). It was home to the [Dwarves of Belegost](../D/Dwarves_of_Belegost.md).

## Geography {#Geography}

Belegost was delved in the eastern side of the north-central part and of the Blue Mountains northeast of [Mount Dolmed](../D/Dolmed.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Sindar_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sindar-4">[4]</a></sup> where the Dwarf-road of [Beleriand](Beleriand.md) crossed into [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md),<sup id="cite_ref-MI_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MI-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> guarding one of the only passes over the Blue Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> not far from lake [Nenuial](../N/Nenuial.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-10">[10]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Belegost was founded during the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) when the western [Fathers of the Dwarves](../F/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves.md) awoke from beneath the [Blue Mountains](Blue_Mountains.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In [Y.T.](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) [1250](../Y/Year_of_the_Trees_1250.md) the Dwarves of both Nogrod and Belegost became known to the [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md), and though friendship between the Elves and Dwarves was cool, they had much profit of each other and [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md) welcomed them.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Around [Y.T.](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) [1300](../Y/Year_of_the_Trees_1300.md), the Dwarves of Belegost were called upon by Thingol to aid in the building of [Menegroth](../M/Menegroth.md). The Dwarves laboured long on this, though they were eager for new works and did so gladly; they ever demanded a price for their work, but considered themselves paid between receiving the wisdom of [Melian](../M/Melian.md) and many fair pearls, including [Nimphelos](../N/Nimphelos.md), from Thingol.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

During the late [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), [Azaghâl](../A/Azaghâl.md) was the [Lord of Belegost](../L/Lord_of_Belegost.md) until he was killed in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](../N/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad.md).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

At the end of the First Age, Belegost was ruined in the [War of Wrath](../W/War_of_Wrath.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-16">[16]</a></sup> Around the [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [40](../S/Second_Age_40.md) many Dwarves migrated from Belegost to [Khazad-dûm](../K/Khazad-dûm.md).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> and brought much lore and craft with them.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-16">[16]</a></sup> However, some Dwarves remained on the eastern side of the Blue Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Belegost* is [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) for "Great Fortress",<sup id="cite_ref-Belegost_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Belegost-18">[18]</a></sup> from *[beleg](<Beleg_(word).md>)* ("great") + *[os(t)](../O/Ost.md)* ("fortress").<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> It was the translation of the original [Dwarvish](../D/Dwarvish.md) name into Sindarin.<sup id="cite_ref-Belegost_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Belegost-18">[18]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The original name in [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md) was <i><b>Gabilgathol</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Belegost_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Belegost-18">[18]</a></sup> [Paul Strack](../P/Paul_Strack.md) suggests that it contains the elements *[gabil](../G/Gabil.md)* ("great") and *[gathol](../G/Gathol.md)* ("fortress").<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

Unlike other names of *The Silmarillion*, the text also gives us an English name, which was possibly from [Westron](../W/Westron.md): <i><b>Mickleburg</b></i> ("Great Fortress").<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> *Mickle* is a root meaning "big"; see also [Michel Delving](../M/Michel_Delving.md). It is notable that <i><a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Miklagar%C3%B0r" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Miklagarðr</a></i>, also meaning "Great city", was the [Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md) name for Constantinople.

The [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) cognate was <i><b>Túrosto</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

On the Eastward Extension of [the First 'Silmarillion' Map](../T/The_First_'Silmarillion'_Map.md), the [Dwarf-road](../D/Dwarf-road_of_Beleriand.md) crosses the Blue Mountains below Mount Dolmed, then turns to the south and continues until the edge of the map with the direction "Southward in East feet of Blue Mountans are Belegost and Nogrod".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> In the second version of [the Earliest *Annals of Beleriand*](../T/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Beleriand.md)<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> and in [the Later *Annals of Beleriand*](../T/The_Later_Annals_of_Beleriand.md),<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> Belegost was in the east of the Blue Mountains and far south of Beleriand.

The position of Belegost was later moved by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) to the north of Mount Dolmed on a photocopy of [the Second 'Silmarillion' Map](../T/The_Second_'Silmarillion'_Map.md).<sup id="cite_ref-MI_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MI-6">[6]</a></sup> This corresponds with its position in the *[Quenta Silmarillion](<../Q/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road).md>)* from [1937](../_/1937.md),<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> in the revised version of [the Later *Quenta Silmarillion*](../T/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion.md),<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> and in the *[Grey Annals](../G/Grey_Annals.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>

The position of Belegost is indicated on the southern slopes of the eastern spur of the part of the Blue Mountains, which is south of the Gulf of Lune on the [First Map of *The Lord of the Rings*](../F/First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) that was drawn by J.R.R. Tolkien, on the version of this map that was redrawn by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) and published in *[The Treason of Isengard](../T/The_Treason_of_Isengard.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> and on [the 1943 Map of *The Lord of the Rings*](../T/The_1943_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md), but not on any subsequent maps.<sup id="cite_ref-MI_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MI-6">[6]</a></sup> Christopher Tolkien does not explain why this position of Belegost was not retained on the subsequent maps, but it is possible that his father realised that it was different from the position that he indicated on the photocopy of the Second Silmarillion map.

Although the locations of Belegost and Mount Dolmed are not indicated on the *[General Map of Middle-earth](../G/General_Map_of_Middle-earth.md)* and on the map of *[The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age](../T/The_West_of_Middle-earth_at_the_End_of_the_Third_Age.md)*, Mount Dolmed can be identified as the westernmost mountain in the middle of the part of the [Blue Mountains](Blue_Mountains.md), which is north of the [Gulf of Lune](../G/Gulf_of_Lune.md) by using the position of Mount Dolmed relative to the positions of the Hill of Himring and the highland of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../T/Taur-nu-Fuin.md" title="Taur-nu-Fuin">Taur-nu-Fuin</a> on the *[Map of Beleriand and the Lands to the North](../M/Map_of_Beleriand_and_the_Lands_to_the_North.md)* and determining the position of Belegost and Mount Dolmed relative to the positions of their respective remains after the War of Wrath, the island of [Himling](../H/Himring.md) and the island of [Tol Fuin](../T/Tol_Fuin.md),<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> on [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md)'s original map of The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup> This location is on a similar latitude as lake Nenuial.

## Portrayals in adaptations {#Portrayals_in_adaptations}

In *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](../T/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth.md)* Belegost was consciously placed far south of Nogrod and far south of Mount Dolmed on the map of eastern Beleriand<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup> based on the location of Belegost on the redrawing by Christopher Tolkien of the First Map of The Lord of the Rings, which shows the Ered Luin after the War of Wrath, although Karen Wynn Fonstad was aware of its different position by Christopher Tolkien on his Map of Beleriand and the Lands to the North in the Silmarillion and of the changes in the narrative relating to the Dwarf-road and Belegost in [The Shaping of Middle-earth](../T/The_Shaping_of_Middle-earth.md) that led Christopher Tolkien to place Belegost on his map.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup> In addition, the dwarven name of Belegost is incorrectly spelled as **Gabilgathod**.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup>
