The **Barrow-wights** were [undead](../U/Undead.md) creatures,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> dead bones animated<sup id="cite_ref-Bombadil_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bombadil-2">[2]</a></sup> by [evil spirits](../E/Evil_spirits.md)<sup id="cite_ref-evil_spirits_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-evil_spirits-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> that inhabited barrows<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow-wights_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-wights-6">[6]</a></sup>.

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/R/Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/1a/Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg/300px-Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="394" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/1a/Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg/450px-Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/1a/Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg/600px-Richard_Svensson_-_The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_part_4.jpg 2x" data-file-width="616" data-file-height="809"/></a><figcaption><i>The Adventures of Tom Bombadil part 4</i> by Richard Svensson</figcaption></figure>

The Barrow-wights were evil spirits,<sup id="cite_ref-evil_spirits_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-evil_spirits-3">[3]</a></sup> although their true nature is unknown; it is not known if they were perverted [Maiar](../M/Maiar.md) ([Úmaiar](../_/Úmaiar.md)) or [spirits](../F/Fëa_and_hröa.md) of [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md), [Elves](../E/Elves.md), or [Men](../M/Men.md).

It seems that Barrow-wights animated bones and that rings clinked on cold fingers and gold chains clinked when they walked in the [Barrows](Barrows.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Bombadil_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bombadil-2">[2]</a></sup> A barrow-wight could also animate a detached arm and make it walk on its fingers.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-7">[7]</a></sup>

Outdoors at night a Barrow-wight appeared like a tall dark figure with very cold eyes that were lit a pale light seeming to come from a remote distance. It had an icy touch that froze the bones of its victim and seemingly caused the victim to lose consciousness. It spoke with a deep and cold voice and used an incantation that tried to immobilize its victims. It arranged its victims to lie on their backs, clothed them in white, placed circlets on their heads, chains around their waists and rings on their fingers, swords by their sides and shields at their feet. In addition, a long sword was placed across the necks of the victim and it seemed that a Barrow-wight intended to use the sword to kill its victims.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-7">[7]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After most of the people of [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md) had died in the [Great Plague](../G/Great_Plague.md) in the days of King [Argeleb II](../A/Argeleb_II.md) of [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md),<sup id="cite_ref-evil_spirits_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-evil_spirits-3">[3]</a></sup> the [Witch-king](../W/Witch-king.md) of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) sent<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> evil spirits from [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) and [Rhudaur](../R/Rhudaur.md) to inhabit the deserted [Barrow-downs](Barrow-downs.md) in [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md)<sup id="cite_ref-evil_spirits_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-evil_spirits-3">[3]</a></sup>.

One of them occupied the cairn of the [last prince of Cardolan](../L/Last_prince_of_Cardolan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> The spirits stirred the dead bones in the mounds and haunted them.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-I7_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-I7-12">[12]</a></sup>

According to [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) verse, [Tom Bombadil](../T/Tom_Bombadil.md) escaped a Barrow-wight on some occasion, using his enchanting incantations.<sup id="cite_ref-AB1_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AB1-13">[13]</a></sup> Perhaps his authority was sourced by the inherent power he had on this region of the world, not the spirits themselves.

During the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) ([September](../S/September.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md)) the [Black Riders](../N/Nazgûl.md) entered Cardolan around September 24. Their chief, the [Witch-king](../W/Witch-king.md), moved to [Andrath](../A/Andrath.md) and visited the Barrow-downs, where he stayed for three days in order to rouse the Barrow-wights. According to the rare manuscript *[The Hunt for the Ring: Time Scheme - Black Riders](../T/The_Hunt_for_the_Ring.md)*, the [Witch King](../W/Witch_King.md) empowered the Barrow-wights and slew the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../R/Ranger.md" title="Ranger">Rangers</a> in order to trap the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../R/Ring.md" title="Ring">Ring</a>-bearer - a strategy that would almost work; he left on September 27.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

On 28 September [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md))<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md), [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md), [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) and [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) were trapped in the [Barrow-downs](Barrow-downs.md) by the [spells](../M/Magic.md) of the Barrow-wights, and were nearly slain by the creatures. They were saved in the last minute by Tom, who seemed to have had complete authority over them.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

According to [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md), the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) knew the Barrow-wights by many names.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> While these names are not recorded (except for the general denomination "evil spirits"), the creatures were also called "**Barrow-dwellers**" in Hobbit lore.<sup id="cite_ref-AB1_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AB1-13">[13]</a></sup> Often, they were also referred to by the shortened form "**Wights**".<sup id="cite_ref-LotR_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LotR-19">[19]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Barrow-wight* is a combination of the word *[barrow](Barrows.md)* and the word *[wight](../W/Wights.md)*. A *barrow* is a grave-mound.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow-wights_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-wights-6">[6]</a></sup> A *wight* meant originally and chiefly a supernatural, preternatural, or unearthly being.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Due to his inspiration from *Hrómundar saga Gripssonar*, during the writing of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* (see *[The History of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_History_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*) Tolkien at first foresaw a link between the wights and the [Ringwraiths](../N/Nazgûl.md), initially describing the Black Riders as horsed Wights, but the suggestion that they were the same kind of creatures was dropped in the published work. In the final work there remained a link between them: the wights were now spirits sent by the Witch-king.

In notes with a sketch of a narrative for an earlier version of the chapter In the House of Tom Bombadil, J.R.R. Tolkien stated that the Barrow-wights are related to the Black-riders and asked if the Black-riders are actually horsed Barrow-wights.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> In addition, Elrond said that the Barrow-wights were closely akin to the (Black) Riders in an earlier version of the chapter The Council of Elrond.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> In a rejected draft of a later version of the chapter The Council of Elrond, Elrond did not only say that the Elves of Rivendell ("we") knew of the Barrow-wights by many names, but also said that there were "others elsewhere, wherever the men of Númenor sought dark knowledge under the shadow of death in Middle-earth" and they were "akin to the" Ringwraiths.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup>

## Other writings {#Other_writings}

The character Tídwald, appearing in [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s poem "[The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm's Son](../T/The_Homecoming_of_Beorhtnoth.md)", accuses Torhthelm of fancying "*barrow-wights and bogeys*".<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>

<b>1994: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Remnants haunting their own tombs, the Barrow Wights are a type of greater Undead Beings. They draw energy from living beings, sacrificing their victims.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2001-2007: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game">The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Although the Barrow-wights did not appear in <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> films</a>, <a href="../D/Decipher.md" title="Decipher">Decipher</a> did produce a card depicting a Barrow-wight.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <a class="mw-redirect" href="../P/Pán_prsteňov_(2001-2003_Slovak_radio_series).md" title="Pán prsteňov (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)"><i>Pán prsteňov</i> (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The voice of the barrow-wight that temporarily imprisons Frodo and his three companions is provided by Vladimír Jedľovský.</dd></dl>

<b>2002-5: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Barrow-wights (not to be confused with the <a href="../G/Ghosts.md" title="Ghosts">ghost</a>-type <i>wights</i> appearing in the same game) are corpses of Men animated by evil spirits.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007-: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="../S/Sambrog.md" title="Sambrog">Sambrog</a> is a Wight-lord of the Barrow-downs. Barrow Wights are not just confined to the Barrow Downs, but have risen in many parts of Middle-earth mainly around the decaying ruins of Arnor. Wights can be found haunting the darkest corners of Mirkwood to even the Elven ruins which dot Ered Luin. A clan of warriors in Dunland has even gone as far as trying to raise their own undead army.</dd></dl>

<b>2011: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_War_in_the_North.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="../E/Eradan_(video_game_character).md" title="Eradan (video game character)">Eradan</a>, <a href="../A/Andriel.md" title="Andriel">Andriel</a>, and <a href="../F/Farin_(video_game_character).md" title="Farin (video game character)">Farin</a> encounter Barrow-wights during their trek through the Barrow-downs. The boss battle of the level is the Barrow-wight Lord.</dd></dl>

<b>2024: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Rings_of_Power.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>: <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power_-_Season_2&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power - Season 2 (page does not exist)">Season 2</a></b>

<dl><dd>In episode 4, <i><a href="../E/Eldest_(episode).md" title="Eldest (episode)">Eldest</a></i>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Vorohil&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Vorohil (page does not exist)">Vorohil</a>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Daemor&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Daemor (page does not exist)">Daemor</a>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Camnir&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Camnir (page does not exist)">Camnir</a>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=R%C3%ADan_(Selina_Lo)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rían (Selina Lo) (page does not exist)">Rían</a>, <a href="../E/Elrond.md" title="Elrond">Elrond</a> and <a href="../G/Galadriel.md" title="Galadriel">Galadriel</a> encounter Barrow-wights in the <a href="Barrow-downs.md" title="Barrow-downs">Barrow-downs</a>. Vorohil hears something which he claims was &#34;almost like a song. Or the memory of a song&#34;. Daemor is suddenly grabbed by chains which pull him down a barrow. The wights chant <i><a href="../C/Cold_be_hand_and_heart_and_bone,.md" title="Cold be hand and heart and bone,">Cold be hand and heart and bone</a></i> and emerge from the ground. The <a href="../E/Elves.md" title="Elves">Elves</a> engage in battle with the Wights but find their weapons ineffective. Elrond orders Camnir to help him open a Barrow. Galadriel is pulled by the chains of a wight toward a Barrow while Vorohil tries in vain to help. Elrond, with the sword he took from the Barrow, strikes the wight hand about to grab Galadriel and it dissolves. He gives Galadriel a sword and the other Elves find other weapons from Barrows which they use to defeat the wights. After the battle is over, Elrond tells them that &#34;According to lore, only the blades with which they were buried will return such creatures to rest&#34;. Camnir says that they&#39;ve been buried for over a thousand years. Vorohil remarks &#34;it is safe to say that something has awoken them&#34;. Galadriel says that <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">someone</a>, not something, awoke the wights.</dd></dl>
