<i><b>Beowulf</b></i> is the conventional title of an [Anglo-Saxon](../O/Old_English.md) epic poem.

## Tolkien and *Beowulf* {#Tolkien_and_Beowulf}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel&#39;s_Mother.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/9/99/John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel%27s_Mother.jpg/200px-John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel%27s_Mother.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="139" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/9/99/John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel%27s_Mother.jpg/300px-John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel%27s_Mother.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/9/99/John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel%27s_Mother.jpg/400px-John_Howe_-_Beowulf_Battles_Grendel%27s_Mother.jpg 2x" data-file-width="534" data-file-height="371"/></a><figcaption><a href="../J/John_Howe.md" title="John Howe">John Howe</a> - <i>Beowulf Battles Grendel&#39;s Mother</i></figcaption></figure>

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) was a prominent *Beowulf* scholar. His *[Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics](Beowulf%253A_The_Monsters_and_the_Critics.md)* was a turning point in the modern study of the poem, moving the focus from its historical to its literary significance.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien also left two substantial unpublished manuscripts concerning *Beowulf*. The first, a more substantial version of the previously mentioned critical essay, was edited by [Michael D.C. Drout](../M/Michael_D.C._Drout.md) and published as *[Beowulf and the Critics](Beowulf_and_the_Critics.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The second is a partial poetic and full prose translation of the epic, including commentary.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The latter was a minor media sensation on its re-discovery in 2003 and was also to be prepared for publication by Drout, but as of 2010 this was not forthcoming.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

On 22 May 2014, HarperCollins published [Beowulf: A Translation and Commentary](Beowulf%253A_A_Translation_and_Commentary.md), taken from the unpublished manuscript kept at the [Bodleian Library](Bodleian_Library.md) in [Oxford](../O/Oxford.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

An excerpt from *Beowulf*, rendered by Tolkien in [Valmaric script](../V/Valmaric_script.md), was published with commentary in [*Parma Eldalamberon* 14](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_14.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> A similar excerpt from *Beowulf*, rendered by Tolkien in his <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Qenya_Alphabet&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Qenya Alphabet (page does not exist)">Qenya Alphabet</a>, was published with commentary in [*Parma Eldalamberon* 20](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_20.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Influence on the legendarium {#Influence_on_the_legendarium}

Tolkien looked highly upon *Beowulf*, and it both directly and indirectly influenced his own [imaginative work](../L/Legendarium.md). The episode in *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* where Bilbo steals a cup from [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md)'s hoard, for example, is a conscious homage to a similar theft in *Beowulf*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
