<i><b>Battle Games in Middle-earth</b></i> was a fortnightly <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partwork" title="wikipedia:Partwork">partwork</a> dedicated to [The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game](/wiki/Middle-earth_Strategy_Battle_Game "Middle-earth Strategy Battle Game"), published by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=DeAgostini&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="DeAgostini (page does not exist)">DeAgostini</a> using material and models from [Games Workshop](/wiki/Games_Workshop). 91 regular issues (called *Packs*) and three movie specials were released.<sup id="cite_ref-FAQ_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FAQ-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Latham_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Latham-2">[2]</a></sup> The magazine ran from [2002](/wiki/2002) to [2006](/wiki/2006).

Outside of the English-speaking world, the magazine was localized in several different languages.<sup id="cite_ref-FAQ_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FAQ-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Contents {#Contents}

Each Pack came with a miniature. Some issues also contained paints, cardboard cut-out characters, or other components.<sup id="cite_ref-Latham_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Latham-2">[2]</a></sup>

The Packs themselves generally contained one or more articles of the following series:

- Guide to Middle-earth, which were short write-ups about characters and lore
- Playing the Game, which introduced rules piece-meals
- Battle Game, which featured battle scenarios
- Painting Workshop, which were painting guides for included miniatures
- Modelling Workshop, which were guides on how to build scenery and terrain

## Reception and impact {#Reception_and_impact}

Alan and Michael Perry, Games Workshop sculptors whose work was sometimes packaged with issues, found that it encouraged new people into the miniature gaming hobby.<sup id="cite_ref-Interview_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Interview-3">[3]</a></sup>

After the partwork's conclusion, [Mark Latham](/wiki/Mark_Latham), an editor and writer, and Glenn More, a graphic designer, were moved to [White Dwarf](/wiki/White_Dwarf), which is [Games Workshop](/wiki/Games_Workshop)'s magazine. This is a prospect fans found exciting, for they found White Dwarf to lack depth in its coverage of Lord of the Rings content.<sup id="cite_ref-GWOnline_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GWOnline-4">[4]</a></sup>
