**Barad-dûr**, also known as the **Dark Tower**, was the chief fortress of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), on the [Plateau of Gorgoroth](/wiki/Plateau_of_Gorgoroth) in [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). Known in [Black Speech](/wiki/Black_Speech) as <i><b><a class="mw-selflink-fragment" href="#Etymology">Lugbúrz</a></b></i>, the Eye of Sauron kept watch over [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) from its highest tower.

## Description {#Description}

Barad-dûr was built upon the end of a long southwestern spur of the [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui) in the northern part of the Plateau of Gorgoroth. It stood about 30 miles east of [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) and about 100 miles southeast of the [Black Gate](/wiki/Black_Gate). From Barad-dûr a road led northwest to the Black Gate.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Another road, [Sauron's Road](/wiki/Sauron%27s_Road), led from Barad-dûr's huge western gate west to an entrance in the eastern mountain side of Mount Doom from which a tunnel led to the [Sammath Naur](/wiki/Cracks_of_Doom "Cracks of Doom").<sup id="cite_ref-Mount_Doom_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mount_Doom-2">[2]</a></sup>

Barad-dûr was built upon a mighty mountain-throne above immeasurable pits.<sup id="cite_ref-Tower_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tower-3">[3]</a></sup> It was black and immeasurably strong with wall upon wall, battlement upon battlement<sup id="cite_ref-Breaking_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Breaking-4">[4]</a></sup> and towers as tall as hills.<sup id="cite_ref-Tower_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tower-3">[3]</a></sup> It had gaping gates of steel and adamant and contained great courts, dungeons and windowless prisons.<sup id="cite_ref-Tower_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tower-3">[3]</a></sup> The topmost tower had an iron crown and contained<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> the [Window of the Eye](/wiki/Window_of_the_Eye) from which Sauron looked over the land.<sup id="cite_ref-Mount_Doom_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mount_Doom-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### First Building {#First_Building}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Alan_Lee_-_The_building_of_Barad-dur.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/6a/Alan_Lee_-_The_building_of_Barad-dur.jpg/300px-Alan_Lee_-_The_building_of_Barad-dur.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="536" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/6a/Alan_Lee_-_The_building_of_Barad-dur.jpg/450px-Alan_Lee_-_The_building_of_Barad-dur.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/6/6a/Alan_Lee_-_The_building_of_Barad-dur.jpg 2x" data-file-width="486" data-file-height="869"/></a><figcaption><i>The building of Barad-dûr</i> by <a href="/wiki/Alan_Lee" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

Around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1000](/wiki/Second_Age_1000 "Second Age 1000") Sauron chose Mordor as a land to turn it into a stronghold and began to buid the Barad-dûr, because he was alerted by the growing power of the Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Around 600 years later he secretly forged the [One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") in the fires of [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom), completed the construction of Barad-dûr and strengthened its foundations with the power of the One Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

During the following years Sauron was able to consolidate his power and extend it into the east.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-8">[8]</a></sup> However, in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3261](/wiki/Second_Age_3261 "Second Age 3261"), [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n), king of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), landed at the [Haven of Umbar](/wiki/Umbar "Umbar") with a great host and marched north towards Mordor. Their splendour and might was so great that Sauron humbled himself before the king and came to Númenor as a hostage.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

The year after the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3319](/wiki/Second_Age_3319 "Second Age 3319") Sauron's spirit secretly returned to Barad-dûr.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> There he worked a terrible shape for himself, took up again the One Ring and prepared for war against the Eldar and the Exiles of Númenor, who had established their realms in [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-8">[8]</a></sup> He succeeded in [taking](/wiki/First_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil "First Fall of Minas Ithil") [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil), but in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3430](/wiki/Second_Age_3430 "Second Age 3430") [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) formed the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) and defeated Sauron four years later in the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad). After that they passed into Mordor and began the [Siege of Barad-dûr](/wiki/Siege_of_Barad-d%C3%BBr).<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> The siege lasted for seven years, Gil-galad and Elendil were slain and in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441") Sauron was finally defeated.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) son of Elendil cut the One Ring from Sauron's hand and took it for his own instead of destroying it. The Dark Tower was finally leveled but its foundations remained, because they had been strengthened with the power of the One Ring and could not be destroyed as long as that existed.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

### Reconstruction {#Reconstruction}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/1/1a/John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg/300px-John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="302" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/1/1a/John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg/450px-John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/1/1a/John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg/600px-John_Howe_-_The_Dark_Tower_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="828" data-file-height="834"/></a><figcaption><i>The Dark Tower</i> by <a href="/wiki/John_Howe" title="John Howe">John Howe</a></figcaption></figure>

Around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050") Sauron had returned and secretly made a stronghold at [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) did not discover that Sauron was the master of Dol Guldur until he entered it in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2850](/wiki/Third_Age_2850 "Third Age 2850"), and in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941") the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) decided to attack the fortress and Sauron, who had already made plans for an attack against Dol Guldur, abandoned it. The next year Sauron secretly returned to Mordor, which the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) had prepared for him, and began to rebuild Barad-dûr in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2951](/wiki/Third_Age_2951 "Third Age 2951").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> After that Sauron stayed in Barad-dûr and conducted his war on the free people of Middle-Earth from there.

On [March 25](/wiki/25_March "25 March"), [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019") the ring-bearer [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) succeeded in destroying the One Ring, although it was the creature [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) who actually held the Ring as it fell into the [Cracks of Doom](/wiki/Cracks_of_Doom). With the Ring destroyed Barad-dûr ultimately collapsed to ruin and Sauron was finally defeated.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>A brief vision he had of swirling cloud, and in the midst of it towers and battlements, tall as hills, founded upon a mighty mountain-throne above immeasurable pits; great courts and dungeons, eyeless prisons sheer as cliffs, and gaping gates of steel and adamant: and then all passed. Towers fell and mountains slid; walls crumbled and melted, crashing down; vast spires of smoke and spouting steams went billowing up, up, until they toppled like an overwhelming wave, and its wild crest curled and came foaming down upon the land. And then at last over the miles between there came a rumble, rising to a deafening crash and roar; the earth shook, the plain heaved and cracked, and Orodruin reeled.
</p>

*Barad-dûr* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> It means "Dark Tower".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> It is a compound of *[Barad](/wiki/Barad)* (a great towering building, (fort, city, castle) tower)<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-19">[19]</a></sup> and the adjective *[dûr](/wiki/D%C3%BBr "Dûr")* ("dark" (with evil implications")).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

*Lugbúrz* was the name of Barad-dûr in the [Black Speech](/wiki/Black_Speech), composed of the Black Speech words *lûg* ("fortress", "lock-up", "prison") and *[búrz](/wiki/B%C3%BArz "Búrz")* ("dark").<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

The [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) versions of *Barad-dûr* are *Taras Lúna* ("Dark Tower") and *Lúnaturco* ("Dark Stronghold").<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-19">[19]</a></sup> They are never used within the narration.

<b>1980: <a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)"><i>The Return of the King</i> (1980 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Barad-dûr is portrayed as a simple fortress. During a <a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring" title="The One Ring">ring</a>-induced dream, <a href="/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee" title="Samwise Gamgee">Sam</a> dreams of taking the Ring and storming the Tower, but his common sense gains the upper hand in time.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1981: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1981 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Barad-dûr is one of the first sites visited. After the <a href="/wiki/Witch-king" title="Witch-king">Witch-king</a> captures <a href="/wiki/Gollum" title="Gollum">Gollum</a> snooping on the edge of Mordor, the <a href="/wiki/Mouth_of_Sauron" title="Mouth of Sauron">Mouth of Sauron</a> questions the creature in the Dark Tower.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2001-03: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>Lord of the Rings</i> movies</a> by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Jackson" title="Peter Jackson">Peter Jackson</a>, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Taylor" title="Richard Taylor">Richard Taylor</a> and his design team built a 9 foot high miniature (&#34;<a href="/wiki/Bigatures" title="Bigatures">big-ature</a>&#34;) of Barad-dûr for use in the film.  Using the size scale for the model implemented for the films, the Dark Tower is  depicted as being over 1500 meters (5,000 feet) tall.</dd>
<dd><i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">The Return of the King</a></i> also shows Barad-dûr as clearly visible from the <a href="/wiki/Black_Gate" title="Black Gate">Black Gate</a> of Mordor. Even granting its enormous size, it was located one hundred miles away and to the east of the Gate, and behind the inner mountain ridges of <a href="/wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)" title="Udûn (valley)">Udûn</a> so <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a>&#39;s army would probably not have been able to see it. In the film version, the geography of Mordor seems generally to have been compressed somewhat, perhaps for artistic reasons related to rendering such complex stories in a visual medium. In the case of the Black Gate scene, having Barad-dûr visible from the Gate means that the army can see the Eye of Sauron staring at them.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Tower is briefly visible in the opening scene. <a href="/wiki/Galadriel" title="Galadriel">Galadriel</a> narrates a vision from her <a href="/wiki/Mirror_of_Galadriel" title="Mirror of Galadriel">Mirror</a>. It is only referred to as &#34;The Dark Tower of Mordor&#34;, and it is the base of the <a href="/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl" title="Nazgûl">Ringwraiths</a> (as opposed to <a href="/wiki/Minas_Morgul" title="Minas Morgul">Minas Morgul</a>). The Eye of Sauron is portrayed as a fiery eye at the top, but is only visible for a split second.</dd></dl>

<b>2004: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Barad-dûr is shown in the introduction scene. It&#39;s design is similar to that of the tower in the movies. It does not play a role in any of the campaigns, and does not appear on any of the maps.</dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Like in its predecessor, the tower of Barad-dûr plays no active role in this game. In the &#34;War of the Ring&#34; mode, it can be seen in the territory of Mordor, but is absent on the map itself.</dd></dl>

<b>2017: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>With the destruction of the One Ring, the main tower of Barad-dûr had collapsed, but the foundations remain standing and teeming with Sauron&#39;s servants. The dungeons of Barad-dûr, the &#34;Mordath&#34;, have been exposed to the sky above through the cracks in the earth.</dd></dl>
