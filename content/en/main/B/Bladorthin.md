**Bladorthin** was a great <a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/King" title="King">King</a> of some realm of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), sometime in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

Questions about whether Bladorthin was a [Man](/wiki/Men "Men") or an [Elf](/wiki/Elves "Elves"), or precisely where his kingdom lay, must remain within the realm of speculation.

## History {#History}

Sometime during the mid- to late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), Bladorthin ruled a kingdom which maintained armies and had a trade relationship with the [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") [Kingdom under the Mountain](/wiki/Kingdom_under_the_Mountain).<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

Bladorthin charged the [Dwarves of Erebor](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Erebor) with making multiple spears for his armies. These spears were to each consist of "a thrice-forged head" with a shaft cunningly lined with [gold](/wiki/Gold "Gold"). While the spears were forged, the Dwarves never delivered them and Bladorthin never payed for them. As a consequence, the spears remained in Erebor.<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941") during the [Quest of Erebor](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor), long after Bladorthin had passed away, [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin_II "Thorin II") and [Balin](/wiki/Balin) pondered on whether or not Bladorthin's order was still intact.<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff), noting that [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") never explained the meaning of *Bladorthin*, identifies the name as "clearly [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) (or perhaps [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin))". The Gnomish element *blador* "probably applies to wide open country" (cf. [Bladorinand](/wiki/Bladorinand), an early name of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand)), whereas the element *-thin* likely has the meaning of "grey" (as in <i><a href="/wiki/Thingol#Etymology" title="Thingol"><b>Thin</b>gol</a></i>). This would give the translation "the Grey Country", "Grey Plains Fay", or "Grey Master of the Plains".<sup id="cite_ref-HH_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HH-2">[2]</a></sup>

Like Rateliff, [Andreas Möhn](/wiki/Andreas_M%C3%B6hn) also identified the name as Gnomish, however, he claimed that *blador* meant "wide earth, flat earth" and interpreted the whole name as "wide earth-grey" or "Grey One of the wide lands". He also speculated that the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) form of *Bladorthin* may have been <i><b>Palursindo</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Möhn_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Möhn-3">[3]</a></sup>

Unlike them, [Michael L. Martinez](/wiki/Michael_Martinez "Michael Martinez") did not believe that *Bladorthin* was Gnomish, noting not only that the element *-blad* only appeared in *Bladorion*, a name which Tolkien abandoned, but also that the element is similar in sound to "Blaed(a)", an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name for "renowned".<sup id="cite_ref-endorion_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-endorion-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the early drafts of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, Bladorthin was the name of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) (who would later be assigned the colour "grey" in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*) for much of the narrative until Tolkien decided to change it late in the writing process. A connection can still be seen with the element *-thin* with the probable meaning "grey" (see [above](#Etymology)).<sup id="cite_ref-HH_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HH-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Speculation {#Speculation}

- [Robert Foster](/wiki/Robert_Foster) suggests Bladorthin was an Elven king whose premature death prevented the trade. The speculation that his death was premature narrows down the time of his death between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1999](/wiki/Third_Age_1999 "Third Age 1999") (the establishment of the [Kingdom under the Mountain](/wiki/Kingdom_under_the_Mountain)) and [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2770](/wiki/Third_Age_2770 "Third Age 2770") (its destruction by [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug)).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
- [J.E.A. Tyler](/wiki/J.E.A._Tyler) also interprets his death as premature.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>
- [Michael L. Martinez](/wiki/Michael_Martinez "Michael Martinez") also interprets his death as premature, and suggests that he was a [King of Dale](/wiki/King_of_Dale), perhaps an ancestor of [Girion](/wiki/Girion).<sup id="cite_ref-endorion_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-endorion-4">[4]</a></sup>
- [Douglas A. Anderson](/wiki/Douglas_A._Anderson) in *[The Annotated Hobbit](/wiki/The_Annotated_Hobbit)* suggests he was a [mortal](/wiki/Gift_of_Il%C3%BAvatar "Gift of Ilúvatar") [Man](/wiki/Men "Men").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
- An article on the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Tolkienwiki&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tolkienwiki (page does not exist)">Tolkienwiki</a> suggests not only that his death was premature but that his kingdom was destroyed by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), presumably located near the [Sea of Rhûn](/wiki/Sea_of_Rh%C3%BBn).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
- [Andreas Moehn](/wiki/Andreas_Moehn) counters most of the common theories, notably the interpretation that his death was premature. As *The Hobbit* does not mention his death as such, most probable is that Smaug's [descent](/wiki/Sack_of_Erebor "Sack of Erebor") on the [Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2770](/wiki/Third_Age_2770 "Third Age 2770") was what prevented the trade. Therefore he must died sometime later. He also rejects the theory that his kingdom was related to Dale, and locates it in [Dorwinion](/wiki/Dorwinion). In addition, he also suggests that Bladorthin may have been related to the Kings Thorin mentioned who used to send for the smiths of Erebor, rewarding "even the least skillful most richly"<sup id="cite_ref-unexpected_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-unexpected-8">[8]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-Möhn_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Möhn-3">[3]</a></sup>
