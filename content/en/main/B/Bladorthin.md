**Bladorthin** was a great <a class="mw-disambig" href="../K/King.md" title="King">King</a> of some realm of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), sometime in the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

Questions about whether Bladorthin was a [Man](../M/Men.md) or an [Elf](../E/Elves.md), or precisely where his kingdom lay, must remain within the realm of speculation.

## History {#History}

Sometime during the mid- to late [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), Bladorthin ruled a kingdom which maintained armies and had a trade relationship with the [Dwarven](../D/Dwarves.md) [Kingdom under the Mountain](../K/Kingdom_under_the_Mountain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

Bladorthin charged the [Dwarves of Erebor](../D/Dwarves_of_Erebor.md) with making multiple spears for his armies. These spears were to each consist of "a thrice-forged head" with a shaft cunningly lined with [gold](../G/Gold.md). While the spears were forged, the Dwarves never delivered them and Bladorthin never payed for them. As a consequence, the spears remained in Erebor.<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md) during the [Quest of Erebor](../Q/Quest_of_Erebor.md), long after Bladorthin had passed away, [Thorin](../T/Thorin_II.md) and [Balin](Balin.md) pondered on whether or not Bladorthin's order was still intact.<sup id="cite_ref-inside_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inside-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md), noting that [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) never explained the meaning of *Bladorthin*, identifies the name as "clearly [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) (or perhaps [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md))". The Gnomish element *blador* "probably applies to wide open country" (cf. [Bladorinand](Bladorinand.md), an early name of [Beleriand](Beleriand.md)), whereas the element *-thin* likely has the meaning of "grey" (as in <i><a href="../T/Thingol.md#Etymology" title="Thingol"><b>Thin</b>gol</a></i>). This would give the translation "the Grey Country", "Grey Plains Fay", or "Grey Master of the Plains".<sup id="cite_ref-HH_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HH-2">[2]</a></sup>

Like Rateliff, [Andreas Möhn](../A/Andreas_Möhn.md) also identified the name as Gnomish, however, he claimed that *blador* meant "wide earth, flat earth" and interpreted the whole name as "wide earth-grey" or "Grey One of the wide lands". He also speculated that the [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) form of *Bladorthin* may have been <i><b>Palursindo</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Möhn_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Möhn-3">[3]</a></sup>

Unlike them, [Michael L. Martinez](../M/Michael_Martinez.md) did not believe that *Bladorthin* was Gnomish, noting not only that the element *-blad* only appeared in *Bladorion*, a name which Tolkien abandoned, but also that the element is similar in sound to "Blaed(a)", an [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) name for "renowned".<sup id="cite_ref-endorion_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-endorion-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the early drafts of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*, Bladorthin was the name of [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) (who would later be assigned the colour "grey" in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*) for much of the narrative until Tolkien decided to change it late in the writing process. A connection can still be seen with the element *-thin* with the probable meaning "grey" (see [above](#Etymology)).<sup id="cite_ref-HH_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HH-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Speculation {#Speculation}

- [Robert Foster](../R/Robert_Foster.md) suggests Bladorthin was an Elven king whose premature death prevented the trade. The speculation that his death was premature narrows down the time of his death between [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1999](../T/Third_Age_1999.md) (the establishment of the [Kingdom under the Mountain](../K/Kingdom_under_the_Mountain.md)) and [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2770](../T/Third_Age_2770.md) (its destruction by [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
- [J.E.A. Tyler](../J/J.E.A._Tyler.md) also interprets his death as premature.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="../../help/R/References.md" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>
- [Michael L. Martinez](../M/Michael_Martinez.md) also interprets his death as premature, and suggests that he was a [King of Dale](../K/King_of_Dale.md), perhaps an ancestor of [Girion](../G/Girion.md).<sup id="cite_ref-endorion_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-endorion-4">[4]</a></sup>
- [Douglas A. Anderson](../D/Douglas_A._Anderson.md) in *[The Annotated Hobbit](../T/The_Annotated_Hobbit.md)* suggests he was a [mortal](../G/Gift_of_Ilúvatar.md) [Man](../M/Men.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
- An article on the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tolkienwiki&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tolkienwiki (page does not exist)">Tolkienwiki</a> suggests not only that his death was premature but that his kingdom was destroyed by [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md), presumably located near the [Sea of Rhûn](../S/Sea_of_Rhûn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
- [Andreas Moehn](../A/Andreas_Moehn.md) counters most of the common theories, notably the interpretation that his death was premature. As *The Hobbit* does not mention his death as such, most probable is that Smaug's [descent](../S/Sack_of_Erebor.md) on the [Lonely Mountain](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2770](../T/Third_Age_2770.md) was what prevented the trade. Therefore he must died sometime later. He also rejects the theory that his kingdom was related to Dale, and locates it in [Dorwinion](../D/Dorwinion.md). In addition, he also suggests that Bladorthin may have been related to the Kings Thorin mentioned who used to send for the smiths of Erebor, rewarding "even the least skillful most richly"<sup id="cite_ref-unexpected_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-unexpected-8">[8]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-Möhn_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Möhn-3">[3]</a></sup>
