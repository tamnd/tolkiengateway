**bizar** in [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) means "dale, valley"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> or "a small stream (running down from a spring)"<sup id="cite_ref-RC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-2">[2]</a></sup> in which case it would be form *bizâr*.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Root](/wiki/Sundocarm%C3%AB "Sundocarmë") B-Z-R<sup id="cite_ref-RC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions {#Other_versions}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") explored various possibilities regarding the exact interpretation of the elements occurring in the name *[Azanulbizar](/wiki/Azanulbizar)*; it was to somehow mean ["Dimrill Dale"](/wiki/Dimrill_Dale "Dimrill Dale").

In one interpretation, *Azanulbizar* means "The rills of the shadows", whereas the word "dale" is understood (the full name being *[duban](/wiki/Duban "Duban") Azanulbizar*, but *duban* "dale, valley" is left out so that the place was simply called *Azanulbizar*).

In another, the whole phrase "Dimrill Dale" fits into the word *Azanulbizar* since the *bizar* part that means "dale", and "rill(s)" or "streams" corresponds to the middle element *[ûl](/wiki/%C3%9Bl "Ûl")*. <span></span>
