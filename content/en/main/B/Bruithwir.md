**Bruithwir** was the father of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor), according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Bru_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bru-1">[1]</a></sup>

Fëanor's father in the later version of the legendarium, [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB), was already in existence but was unrelated to Fëanor.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On the last day of the feast of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Double_Mirth&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Double Mirth (page does not exist)">Double Mirth</a>, [Melko](/wiki/Melko) went to [Sirnúmen](/wiki/Sirn%C3%BAmen), where many of the [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes) lived at that time, and killed those guarding the treasures; then he proceeded to kill Bruithwir and seized their most glorious jewels, including the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils).<sup id="cite_ref-Bru_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bru-1">[1]</a></sup>

His tomb was known as the [Mound of the First Sorrow](/wiki/Mound_of_the_First_Sorrow), which was so named because of all the sorrow that Bruithwir's death caused afterwards.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Bruithwir* is clearly [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish), but its meaning is unclear. He was also called *Bruithwir go-Maidros* ("Bruithwir son of Maidros"), with *[go-](/wiki/Go- "Go-")* being a Gnomish prefix, meaning "son of".<sup id="cite_ref-Mar_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mar-4">[4]</a></sup>

An earlier name for the character was *Maron*.<sup id="cite_ref-Mar_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mar-4">[4]</a></sup>

In a slightly later text, there appears the name <i><b>Felëor</b></i> (changed from *Felegron*), as an additional name of Bruithwir.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
