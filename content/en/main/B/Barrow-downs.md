The **Barrow-downs** or <i><b>Tyrn Gorthad</b></i> were a series of treeless hills east of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md), behind the [Old Forest](../O/Old_Forest.md), and south-west of the village of [Bree](Bree.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> They were called the Barrow-downs, because many of the hills were crowned with [barrows](Barrows.md) (burial mounds). Standing stones stood on some of the green mounds. The Barrow-downs were a treeless country that was covered with grass and turf.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the late [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) the ancestors of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) built many of the barrows in the Barrow-downs before they migrated west to [Beleriand](Beleriand.md).<sup id="cite_ref-ArgelebII_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ArgelebII-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) many Men that were descended from the ancestors of the Edain, primarily from the [Folk of Beor](../H/House_of_Beor.md) and partly from the [Folk of Hador](../H/House_of_Hador.md) lived near [Lake Evendim](../L/Lake_Evendim.md), in the [North Downs](../N/North_Downs.md), in the [Weather Hills](../W/Weather_Hills.md) and in the land between.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> It is not known if they lived as far south as the Barrow-downs at that time. During the [Dark Years](../D/Dark_Years.md) Men who were related to the [Dunlendings](../D/Dunlendings.md) had migrated from the valleys of the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md) to the empty lands as far north as the Barrow-downs.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

When [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) escaped to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) from the [Downfall of Númenor](../D/Downfall_of_Númenor.md) and established the [Realms in Exile](../R/Realms_in_Exile.md) in [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [3320](../S/Second_Age_3320.md)<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> the Barrow-downs became a part of the kingdom of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The Barrow-downs were revered by the [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md) of Arnor, because many of the barrows had been built by their forefathers in the First Age, and they buried their lords and Kings there.<sup id="cite_ref-ArgelebII_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ArgelebII-3">[3]</a></sup>

After the division of the kingdom of Arnor into the kingdoms of [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md), [Rhudaur](../R/Rhudaur.md) and [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [861](../T/Third_Age_861.md)<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> the Barrow-downs became a part of the kingdom of [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1409](../T/Third_Age_1409.md),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> a large army from the kingdom of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) invaded the kingdom of Cardolan. The Dúnedain of Cardolan were defeated and Cardolan was ravaged, but a part of the Dúnedain of Cardolan defended the Barrow-downs or fled into the safety of the [Old Forest](../O/Old_Forest.md). The forces of Angmar were eventually defeated by [Elves](../E/Elves.md) from [Lindon](../L/Lindon.md), [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) and [Lothlórien](../L/Lothlórien.md) and subdued for a while.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

Around [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1636](../T/Third_Age_1636.md)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> the [Great Plague](../G/Great_Plague.md) spread to Cardolan from the South-west and most of the population of Cardolan died. The [Witch-king](../W/Witch-king.md) sent<sup id="cite_ref-Other_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Other-13">[13]</a></sup> [evil spirits](Barrow-wights.md) from Angmar and Rhudaur to the Barrow-downs, which inhabited the deserted barrows.<sup id="cite_ref-ArgelebII_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ArgelebII-3">[3]</a></sup> It is possible that the terror of the Barrow-wights prevented any attempt to resettle the Barrow-downs.<sup id="cite_ref-Araval_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Araval-14">[14]</a></sup>

Around September 24 [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md) the [Black Riders](../N/Nazgûl.md) entered Cardolan.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-15">[15]</a></sup> Their chief, the [Witch-king](../W/Witch-king.md), moved to [Andrath](../A/Andrath.md), visited the Barrow-downs, roused the Barrow-wights to be on the watch<sup id="cite_ref-Other_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Other-13">[13]</a></sup> in order to trap the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../R/Ring.md" title="Ring">Ring</a>-bearer and left on September 27.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-15">[15]</a></sup>

On [28 September](../_/28_September.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md), [Sam](../S/Sam.md), [Pippin](../P/Pippin.md) and [Merry](../M/Merry.md) were trapped in the downs by a [wight](Barrow-wights.md), probably in the same mound which contained the grave of the [last prince of Cardolan](../L/Last_prince_of_Cardolan.md)<sup id="cite_ref-ArgelebII_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ArgelebII-3">[3]</a></sup> (it is likely that Merry saw his last memories in his dreams, because he mentioned the "men of [Carn Dûm](../C/Carn_Dûm.md)").<sup id="cite_ref-RC_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-15">[15]</a></sup> They were rescued by [Tom Bombadil](../T/Tom_Bombadil.md), who amassed the barrow’s mighty hoard and gave them four special [swords](../D/Daggers_of_Westernesse.md). Tom took a brooch set with blue stones for [Goldberry](../G/Goldberry.md) and said that it was long ago worn by a fair woman on her shoulder and that he and Goldberry would not forget her.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrow_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrow-2">[2]</a></sup> It is possible that the brooch had belonged to the wife of or a woman from the family of the [last prince of Cardolan](../L/Last_prince_of_Cardolan.md), because it was in the barrow that contained his grave.<sup id="cite_ref-RS|Wight_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RS|Wight-17">[17]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

A "barrow" (or "berrow"; from English beorg, berg, 'hill, mound') not to be confused with the wheeled vehicle, is a tumulus or other prehistoric grave-mound.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-18">[18]</a></sup>

In topography, a "down" is a low-lying hill, from the [Anglo-Saxon](../O/Old_English.md) *dún* meaning "hill".<sup id="cite_ref-ASD_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASD-19">[19]</a></sup> In the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="wikipedia:United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a>, a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downland" title="wikipedia:Downland">down</a> is a gently-rolling chalk hill in Southern <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a> (seen especially in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Downs" title="wikipedia:North Downs">North</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs" title="wikipedia:South Downs">South Downs</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-OD_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OD-20">[20]</a></sup>

The name therefore would represent an earlier [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) form *Beorga Dune* "downs of barrows".<sup id="cite_ref-etym_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-etym-21">[21]</a></sup>

<i><b>Tyrn Gorthad</b></i> was the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name of the Barrow-downs. *Tyrn Gorthad* is a compound of the plural<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> *tyrn* of <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Torn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Torn (page does not exist)">torn</a></i> ("burial mound") and <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gorthad&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gorthad (page does not exist)">gorthad</a></i> ("wraith, spirit of dead").<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> In one manuscript, the name *Tyrn Goerthaid* was used by [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> *Goerthaid* seems to be the [plural](../S/Sindarin.md#Plural) of *gorthad* with <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Affection&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Affection (page does not exist)">affected</a> vowels.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earlier versions of the story, the barrows were said to belong to the men who “fought against the evil foes” when “the world was still young”. The cairn where the Hobbits were trapped was said to be the barrow of a prince of this kingdom who died during the war (the identification of the buried prince with the [last prince](../L/Last_prince_of_Cardolan.md) and the occurrence of Cardolan, Angmar and Carn Dûm are only in subsequent versions).<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

Also, in the earlier versions of Tom Bombadil’s statement regarding the blue-jewelled brooch he took for Goldberry, and the mysterious lady that once wore it, he mentioned that “they shall not forget” the kings, the warriors, the children and the fair maidens of the past, suggesting they were interred there and Bombadil had met them long ago.<sup id="cite_ref-RS|Wight_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RS|Wight-17">[17]</a></sup>

In an earlier version of what would later become Appendix A King Araval of Arthedain tried to resettle Cardolan, but the Barrow-wights terrified anybody who attempted to live near the Barrow-downs.<sup id="cite_ref-Araval_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Araval-14">[14]</a></sup>
