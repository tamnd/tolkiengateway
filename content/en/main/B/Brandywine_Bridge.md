The **Brandywine Bridge**, also known as the **Bridge of Baranduin**,<sup id="cite_ref-SDEpi_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SDEpi-1">[1]</a></sup> was the bridge on the [East Road](../E/East_Road.md) crossing the River [Baranduin](Baranduin.md); it marked the eastern border of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The Hobbits measured distances from the east-end of the Brandywine Bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-UI_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Originally called the **Bridge of Stonebows**,<sup id="cite_ref-UI_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-3">[3]</a></sup> it was built during the days of power of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md). [Marcho](../M/Marcho.md) and [Blanco](Blanco.md) with their [Hobbit](../H/Hobbit.md) followers passed that bridge to settle in [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md). The [Kings of Arnor](../K/Kings_of_Arnor.md) required them to keep the Great Bridge in repair (as well as all other bridges and roads).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) and his companions did not use the bridge when they left the Shire in [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md). However, on their return trip they came to the bridge on [30 October](../_/30_October.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> To their surprise they found the bridge blocked on both ends with two spiked gates. Despite a sign proclaiming "No admittance between sundown and sunrise" the four hobbits made their way over the bridge, after dispatching [Bill Ferny](Bill_Ferny.md). They stayed the night at the guard-house before moving on to rouse the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

In [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [15](../F/Fourth_Age_15.md) King [Elessar](../A/Aragorn.md) rode to the Brandywine Bridge and greeted his old friends (but did not cross into the Shire for he himself had forbidden men to enter the land in an edict of [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [6](../F/Fourth_Age_6.md)). At this meeting he bestowed the [Star of the Dúnedain](../S/Star_of_the_Dúnedain.md) on [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) and made Sam's daughter [Elanor](../E/Elanor_Gardner.md) a maid of honour to Queen [Arwen](../A/Arwen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

"Stonebow" is from Old English *stánbogan*, meaning a stone arch. The only quotations for the word, in the Oxford English Dictionary and the online Bosworth Toller Dictionary of Old English, are from *Beowulf*, where it appears twice, at lines 2545 and 2718, describing the lair of the dragon who kills the hero.

In [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md), the name of the bridge was <i><b>i Varanduiniant</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-SDEpi_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SDEpi-1">[1]</a></sup> *[i](../I/I.md)* is the singular definite article "the", which causes the initial *b-* in *[Baranduin](Baranduin.md#Etymology)* to take the [lenited](../L/Lenition.md) form *v-* (i.e., *Varanduin*); the final element *[iant](../I/Iant.md)* means "bridge".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The [Bucklebury Ferry](Bucklebury_Ferry.md) was said to be *twenty* miles south of the Bridge by [Merry](../M/Merry.md)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> but this was corrected to *ten* in the [50th Anniversary Edition](<../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(50th_Anniversary_Edition).md>).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
