**Bree-hill** was the most prominent point in the [Bree-land](Bree-land.md), on the slopes of which stood the villages of [Bree](Bree.md) itself (to the southwest) and [Staddle](../S/Staddle.md) (to the southeast).

There were many families of hobbits in Bree; and they claimed to be the oldest settlement of Hobbits in the world, founded long before the Brandywine was crossed and the Shire colonized. Though more lived in Staddle there were many [Hobbits](Bree-hobbits.md) in Bree, living on the higher slopes of the hill, above the houses of the Men.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

On its southern slopes there were flourishing growths of [pipe-weed](../P/Pipe-weed.md), which was probably brought to the north along the [Greenway](../G/Greenway.md) and the [Bree-hobbits](Bree-hobbits.md) claimed they were the first actual smokers of the leaf. Being an ancient cross-road, the "[art](../S/Smoking.md)" spread from there to other wanderers and races. [Tobold Hornblower](../T/Tobold_Hornblower.md) probably discovered the leaf there, introducing it to [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The southern slopes contained the fields called [Southlinch](../S/Southlinch.md), which produced a weed variety of that name.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Bree-hill* is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tautological_place_names" title="wikipedia:List of tautological place names">tautological place name</a>, because it means "hill-hill"<sup id="cite_ref-RC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-4">[4]</a></sup>.

The element *Bree* is a British (i.e. [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md)) word for "hill"<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>. [Wayne Hammond](../W/Wayne_Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) suggest that the name *Bree* is derived from the [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md) word *bre*, which means "hill".<sup id="cite_ref-RC_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-4">[4]</a></sup> [Tom Shippey](../T/Tom_Shippey.md) also points out that *Bree* means "hill" in Welsh.<sup id="cite_ref-Road_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Road-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) mentioned to also look at Brill and that it was derived from bree + hill when he mentioned that [Chetwood](../C/Chetwood.md) was a compound of Celtic and English elements that both mean "wood".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Tom Shippey mentions that J.R.R. Tolkien was inspired by the town of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brill,_Buckinghamshire" title="wikipedia:Brill, Buckinghamshire">Brill, Buckinghamshire</a>, which is located on a hill ten miles from [Oxford](../O/Oxford.md), when he chose the names *Bree* and *Bree-hill*.<sup id="cite_ref-Road_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Road-6">[6]</a></sup> [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) confirmed that the name *Bree* is based on *Brill* in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="wikipedia:Buckinghamshire">Buckinghamshire</a>, which his father knew well.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
