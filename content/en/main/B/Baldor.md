**Baldor the Hapless** was the prideful son of [Brego](/wiki/Brego) and grandson of [Eorl the Young](/wiki/Eorl "Eorl").

## History {#History}

With his father, Baldor explored [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan), and came to [Harrowdale](/wiki/Harrowdale). They found the [Paths of the Dead](/wiki/Paths_of_the_Dead). Surprised by a riddle from an old man that forbade them passage, he became curious. At the inauguration of [Meduseld](/wiki/Meduseld) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2569](/wiki/Third_Age_2569 "Third Age 2569"), he vowed to pass the [Dark Door](/wiki/Dark_Door). He did so a year later, and was never seen again alive.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Early March [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"), [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and the [Grey Company](/wiki/Grey_Company) found the golden-clad knight. His sword was broken and notched, and his legs ruptured.<sup id="cite_ref-Grey_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grey-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Baldor* means "prince" or "ruler" in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) and is closely related to *bald*,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> which means "bold"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Although in the final version there is no confirmation, all the early manuscripts state that the skeleton found in the Paths was Baldor's. In the original drafts, [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) recognises Baldor<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and says: "*Here lies Baldor, son of Brego*".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

Then Tolkien himself explains in a note:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Men_of_Darkness" title="Men of Darkness">Men of Darkness</a> built temples, some of great size, usually surrounded by dark trees, often in caverns (natural or delved) in secret valleys of mountain-regions; such as the dreadful halls and passages under the Haunted Mountain beyond the Dark Door (Gate of the Dead) in Dunharrow. The special horror of the closed door before which the skeleton of Baldor was found was probably due to the fact that the door was the entrance to an evil temple hall to which Baldor had come, probably without opposition up to that point. But the door was shut in his face, and enemies that had followed him silently came up and broke his legs and left him to die in the darkness, unable to find any way out.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond" title="Wayne G. Hammond">Wayne G. Hammond</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christina_Scull" title="Christina Scull">Christina Scull</a> (eds), <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_A_Reader%27s_Companion" title="The Lord of the Rings: A Reader&#39;s Companion">The Lord of the Rings: A Reader&#39;s Companion</a></i>, &#34;<a href="/wiki/The_Last_Debate" title="The Last Debate">The Last Debate</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>
