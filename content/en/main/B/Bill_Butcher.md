**Bill Butcher** was a [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) inhabitant of the town of [Delving](../D/Delving.md) who appeared in *[Perry-the-Winkle](../P/Perry-the-Winkle.md)*, a nursery rhyme<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> by [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md)<sup id="cite_ref-preface_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-preface-3">[3]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the poem, Bill Butcher was accompanied by his dog [Grip](<../G/Grip_(Bill_Butcher's_dog).md>) when he threw a knife upon seeing the [Lonely Troll](../L/Lonely_Troll.md) peeping over the stalls of the market-place.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is possible that Bill Butcher may have been one of the [Shire-hobbits](../S/Shire-hobbits.md) who travelled to <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Faraway&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Faraway (page does not exist)">Faraway</a> in order to demand food from the Lonely Troll.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In the first version of the poem in early [1928](../_/1928.md),<sup id="cite_ref-commentary_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-commentary-4">[4]</a></sup> which was entitled *The Bumpus*, the butcher was unnamed, "threw his knives", and had no dog.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> No changes to the character occurred in the second version on [29 March](../_/29_March.md),<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> in which Tolkien revised the poem as part<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> of his *[Tales and Songs of Bimble Bay](../T/Tales_and_Songs_of_Bimble_Bay.md)* series. The third version also retained the same details in relation to the butcher.<sup id="cite_ref-commentary_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-commentary-4">[4]</a></sup>

In early [1961](../_/1961.md),<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> when Tolkien revised the poem for inclusion within *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](../T/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil.md)*, the unnamed butcher was accompanied by his dog (instead of "Harry and his father"), threw a single knife, and was given the name **Bill butcher**. In a later revision, Tolkien capitalized the word butcher in Bill's name<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup>
