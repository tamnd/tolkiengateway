**Beleriand** was the region of north-western [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) during the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md). Originally, the name belonged only to the area around the [Bay of Balar](Bay_of_Balar.md), but in time the name was applied to the entire land.

## Geography {#Geography}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/H/Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/36/Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg/250px-Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="167" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/36/Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg/375px-Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/36/Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg/500px-Hope_Maps_-_Beleriand_Map.jpg 2x" data-file-width="6000" data-file-height="4000"/></a><figcaption><i>Beleriand Map</i> by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Hope_Maps.md" title="Category:Images by Hope Maps">Hope Maps</a></figcaption></figure>

In the First Age, Beleriand was among the westernmost lands of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-western_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-western-1">[1]</a></sup> Beleriand had a long western shoreline with the Great Sea [Belegaer](Belegaer.md), including the [Bay of Balar](Bay_of_Balar.md) in its south-west. The Ered Luin ([Blue Mountains](Blue_Mountains.md)) formed the eastern border of Beleriand, separating it from [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md).<sup id="cite_ref-western_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-western-1">[1]</a></sup> To the south, Beleriand "had no sure boundaries; for there were pathless forests in those days between the unpeopled shores and the lower waters of \[the river\] [Gelion](../G/Gelion.md)."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> North of Beleriand were the highland regions of [Hithlum](../H/Hithlum.md),<sup id="cite_ref-hithlum_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hithlum-4">[4]</a></sup> [Dorthonion](../D/Dorthonion.md), and [Lothlann](../L/Lothlann.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Some people considered the land of [Nevrast](../N/Nevrast.md) to be part of Beleriand, while others considered it part of Hithlum.<sup id="cite_ref-hithlum_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hithlum-4">[4]</a></sup> Beleriand stretched about 550 miles broad from [Eglarest](../E/Eglarest.md) on its western shore to the Blue Mountains in the east.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">[6]</a></sup>

The River [Sirion](../S/Sirion.md), the chief river of Beleriand, running north to south, divided it into [West Beleriand](../W/West_Beleriand.md) and [East Beleriand](../E/East_Beleriand.md). Crossing it east to west was a series of hills and a sudden drop in elevation known as [Andram](../A/Andram.md), the Long Wall. (The river sank into the ground at the *Fens of Sirion*, and re-emerged below the Andram at the *Gates of Sirion*.) To the east of the Long Wall, was the River [Gelion](../G/Gelion.md) and its six tributaries draining the Ered Luin, in [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md) or the Land of Seven Rivers. The River [Brithon](Brithon.md) and the River [Nenning](../N/Nenning.md) were the two lesser rivers of the western land of [Falas](../F/Falas.md).

By [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [587](../F/First_Age_587.md), Beleriand was mostly destroyed and submerged under the sea from the [War of Wrath](../W/War_of_Wrath.md) of the [Valar](../V/Valar.md) against [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md). Only a small section of East Beleriand remained, and was known as [Lindon](../L/Lindon.md).

In addition, fulfilling a prophecy, the graves of [Túrin Turambar](../T/Túrin.md) and [Morwen](../M/Morwen.md) survived as the island [Tol Morwen](../T/Tol_Morwen.md). Likewise part of [Dorthonion](../D/Dorthonion.md) became [Tol Fuin](../T/Tol_Fuin.md), and the [Hill of Himring](../H/Hill_of_Himring.md) became the island of Himling. All of these together were also mentioned as the Western Isles.<sup id="cite_ref-Quenta_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quenta-7">[7]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:332</sup>

*Beleriand* translates to "The Country of [Balar](../O/Ossë.md)" with the ending *[-ian(d)](../_/-ian.md)* for places and countries. The [OS](../O/OS.md) form could have been \**Balariandë* (*a* [slid](../I/I-affection.md) to *e* because of the following *i*).

The element *Balar* refers to [Ossë](../O/Ossë.md), who often dwelt at the shores (although this refers to the earlier [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) when Ossë and [Uinen](../U/Uinen.md) were [Valar](../V/Valar.md) of the sea, not [Maiar](../M/Maiar.md) of Ulmo).<sup id="cite_ref-ety_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ety-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> It was from Balar that the Teleri expanded and "colonised" the region of Beleriand, hence the name.<sup id="cite_ref-ety_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ety-9">[8]</a></sup>

### Nomenclature {#Nomenclature}

The name *Beleriand* originally applied only to the region around the [Bay of Balar](Bay_of_Balar.md) whence the name, before expanded to refer to the wider region. Properly *Beleriand* does not refer to the [Northlands](../N/Northlands.md) such as [Anfauglith](../A/Anfauglith.md), or the eastern lands of [Thargelion](../T/Thargelion.md); [Nevrast](../N/Nevrast.md) and [Dorthonion](../D/Dorthonion.md) are "grey areas".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> However the term *Beleriand* is sometimes used collectivelly in [fandom](../F/Fandom.md) to include all those submerged lands.

## Relative location to Eriador and within Middle-earth {#Relative_location_to_Eriador_and_within_Middle-earth}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/M/Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/f/f3/Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png/300px-Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="232" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/f/f3/Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png/450px-Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/f/f3/Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png/600px-Map_of_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png 2x" data-file-width="2040" data-file-height="1576"/></a><figcaption>Map of Beleriand and Eriador by <a href="../C/Christopher_Gilson.md" title="Christopher Gilson">Christopher Gilson</a>, published in <i><a href="../P/Parma_Eldalamberon.md" title="Parma Eldalamberon">Parma Eldalamberon</a></i>, <a href="../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_6.md" title="Parma Eldalamberon 6">issue 6</a> (<a href="../_/1983.md" title="1983">1983</a>).</figcaption></figure>

The [map of Beleriand as published in *The Silmarillion*](../M/Map_of_Beleriand_and_the_Lands_to_the_North.md) (1977) shows only a portion of the region, with few common points to the better-known [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) [Westlands](../W/Westlands.md) maps of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*; the only explicit common references are the [Blue Mountains](Blue_Mountains.md), which formed the boundary between Beleriand and [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md), and the information that in the Third Age, [Lindon](../L/Lindon.md) was part of Ossiriand that survived Beleriand's destruction at the end of the First Age.

In [1979](../_/1979.md), [Charles Noad](../C/Charles_Noad.md)<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> made an attempt to reconstruct a general map of Beleriand and Eriador. Using only the few distance indications from the text of *The Silmarillion*, Noad's very early attempt is remarkable as it proves very accurate despite being published before *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)* (where the island of Himling was first depicted, with Tol Fuin mentioned westwards) and *[The History of Middle-earth](../T/The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)*.

In [1989](../_/1989.md), [Ronald E. Kyrmse](../R/Ronald_Kyrmse.md)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> independently proposed his own map reconstruction, aligning the isle of [Himling](../H/Himling.md) with the hill of [Himring](../H/Himring.md). Kyrmse also identified [Dolmed](../D/Dolmed.md) on the early map of Beleriand as being the same as a mountain on the *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)* map (depicted slightly apart of the mountain range and north of the wooden area in [Forlindon](../F/Forlindon.md)), proving that Dolmed still existed in the Third Age (being too far north from the Gulf of Lune to have been destroyed). He might therefore have been the first to discover that Dolmed survived the destruction of Beleriand at the end of the First Age.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/S/Sage_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador_collage.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Sage_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador_collage.png/300px-Sage_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador_collage.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="307" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/3/39/Sage_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador_collage.png 1.5x" data-file-width="367" data-file-height="375"/></a><figcaption>How <a href="../K/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad.md" title="Karen Wynn Fonstad">Karen Wynn Fonstad</a> envisioned Beleriand relative to Eriador. The rectangle marks the region shown in the <i>The Silmarillion</i> map (canon); the rest, including the <a href="../T/Thangorodrim.md" title="Thangorodrim">Thangorodrim</a>, are Fonstad&#39;s extrapolations.</figcaption></figure>

In [1991](../_/1991.md), [Karen Wynn Fonstad](../K/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad.md) faced the same problems when she attempted to make a world-map of [Arda](../A/Arda.md). She found a solution by combining an early map for *The Lord of the Rings* (published in *[The Treason of Isengard](../T/The_Treason_of_Isengard.md)*)<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> and an early map for the Silmarillion (published in *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](../T/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings.md)*),<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> both being prototypes for the published maps by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md). She aligned [Tol Fuin](../T/Tol_Fuin.md) with <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../T/Taur-nu-Fuin.md" title="Taur-nu-Fuin">Taur-nu-Fuin</a> and [Himling](../H/Himling.md) with [Himring](../H/Himring.md) and filled the rest of the outline with some inspiration from the [only world-map](../../file/J/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Ambarkanta_Map_V.png.md) by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup> She brought the coast near that of [Belfalas](Belfalas.md), continued the course of [Gelion](../G/Gelion.md) to the south-west, extended the south-western tip to emphasize the Bay of Balar, and assumed that area as forested, a continuation of [Taur-im-Duinath](../T/Taur-im-Duinath.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Atlas_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atlas-20">[19]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:3</sup> While the [Iron Mountains](../I/Iron_Mountains.md) do not appear in the canonical map, Fonstad drew them as a continuation to her imagined range of [Iron Hills](../I/Iron_Hills.md)—[Grey Mountains](../G/Grey_Mountains.md)—[Mountains of Angmar](../M/Mountains_of_Angmar.md).

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/D/Didier_Willis_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/8/86/Didier_Willis_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png/300px-Didier_Willis_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="155" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/8/86/Didier_Willis_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png/450px-Didier_Willis_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/8/86/Didier_Willis_-_Beleriand_and_Eriador.png 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="309"/></a><figcaption>Reconstructed location of Beleriand at the appropriate scale and position relative to <a href="../E/Eriador.md" title="Eriador">Eriador</a>, as drawn by <a href="../D/Didier_Willis.md" title="Didier Willis">Didier Willis</a></figcaption></figure>

In the early 1990s, [Didier Willis](../D/Didier_Willis.md) independently made the same attempt using the map from *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)* that shows Himling; the second map of Beleriand, which was published in *[The War of the Jewels](../T/The_War_of_the_Jewels.md)* and indicates distances;<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> and the draft map of the lands east of the Blue Mountains published in *The Treason of Isengard*, which also indicates distances. Quite similar to Noad and Kymrse's earlier attempts, Willis' interpretation shows a slightly smaller Beleriand than Fonstad's and places the First Age [Bay of Balar](Bay_of_Balar.md) closer to the Third Age [Gulf of Lune](../G/Gulf_of_Lune.md).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> Not knowing Kyrmse's earlier interpretation, Willis also independently recognized [Dolmed](../D/Dolmed.md) on the early map of Beleriand and criticized Fonstad and [Robert Foster](../R/Robert_Foster.md) who suggested the opposite.<sup id="cite_ref-Atlas_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atlas-20">[19]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:34</sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/M/Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_(2).png.md"><img alt="Beleriand with the appropriate scale and at the correct position (considering both the late maps of Beleriand and Middle-earth and The Nature of Middle-earth distance scale), as edited by Projeto Tolkien." src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/ee/Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_%282%29.png/300px-Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_%282%29.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="180" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/ee/Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_%282%29.png/450px-Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_%282%29.png 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/ee/Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_%282%29.png/600px-Middle-earth_and_Beleriand_Map_%282%29.png 2x" data-file-width="3603" data-file-height="2160"/></a><figcaption>Beleriand with the appropriate scale and at the correct position (considering both the late maps of Beleriand and Middle-earth and The Nature of Middle-earth distance scale), as edited by <a href="https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/User:ProjetoTolkien" title="User:ProjetoTolkien">Projeto Tolkien</a>.</figcaption></figure>

In 2021, a manuscript from c. 1959 was published posthumously in *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)*, in which Tolkien considers Beleriand to be about 550 miles broad from Eglarest to the Blue Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">[6]</a></sup> When we equate this new found distance scale for Beleriand with the already known distance scale for the late Third Age Middle-earth (which appears in the map itself), both the Blue Mountains and Tol Himling and Himring are perfectly aligned, which means this is most likely the aproximate size and location of Beleriand when compared to the late Third Age Middle-earth (which is also a bit smaller than Fonstad's recreation).

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The earliest name in the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) was <i><b>Broseliand</b></i> (emended from *Broceliand*).<sup id="cite_ref-Broseliand_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Broseliand-24">[23]</a></sup> The name is not glossed, but in the [First 'Silmarillion' Map](../T/The_First_'Silmarillion'_Map.md) it is said that it is the English name for "all the lands watered by Sirion south of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup> While writing [The Lay of Leithian](../T/The_Lay_of_Leithian.md), Tolkien considered several names to replace *Broseliand*, which also remain unglossed but [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) tried to explain:<sup id="cite_ref-Broseliand_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Broseliand-24">[23]</a></sup>

- *Golodhinand* and *Noldórinan*, both related to the [Gnomes](../G/Gnomes.md) in the stems *[Golodh](../G/Golodh.md)* and *[Noldo](../N/Noldo.md)*.
- *Bladorinand*, related to *Bladorwen*, the name for [Palúrien](../P/Palúrien.md) as "the wide earth, Mother Earth".
- *Belaurien*, related to *Belaurin*, the [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) form of *Palúrien*.
- *Geleriand*, *Arsiriand*, *Lassiriand* and *[Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md)*, Christopher is not able to explain.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The early names *Broceliand*/*Broseliand* affirms that Tolkien was inspired by the legendary forest of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broc%C3%A9liande" title="wikipedia:Brocéliande">Brocéliande</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Broseliand_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Broseliand-24">[23]</a></sup>
