<i><b>Bilbo&#39;s Last Song (at the Grey Havens)</b></i> is a poem written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien). It is sung by [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) at the [Grey Havens](/wiki/Grey_Havens) as he is about to leave [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). Chronologically this places it at the very end of *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)*, the last volume of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, although it was written later than the books and never included in them.

The poem first appeared as posters (60×40 cm) in November [1974](/wiki/1974), illustrated by [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes), published by Allen &amp; Unwin.

The first book edition was published in [1990](/wiki/1990), illustrated by the same artist. It is a 32-page full colour picture-book, each double page containing two lines of the poem, with almost fifty of Baynes's paintings.

The poem was included in the second edition of *[The Road Goes Ever On](</wiki/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book)> "The Road Goes Ever On (book)")*, set to music by [Donald Swann](/wiki/Donald_Swann), in [1978](/wiki/1978).

## Background {#Background}

It was originally a composition in Old Norse, entitled *Vestr um haf* ("West over sea"), written as early as 1920s. In [1968](/wiki/1968), after [Joy Hill](/wiki/Joy_Hill), Tolkien's secretary, rediscovered it in a pile of books, Tolkien gave it to her as a gift for her assistance in the setting up of his new office.

After Tolkien's death in [1973](/wiki/1973), Hill showed the poem to [Donald Swann](/wiki/Donald_Swann), who liked the poem so much that he set it to music and included it in the second edition of *[The Road Goes Ever On](</wiki/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book)> "The Road Goes Ever On (book)")* in [1978](/wiki/1978).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The poem was also illustrated by [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes), and published as a poster on [26 November](/wiki/26_November) [1974](/wiki/1974).

The song was included in the BBC Radio 4 adaptation of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* (1981), with music by Stephen Oliver. The first verse is chanted by John Le Mesurier as Bilbo, the second omitted, and the third sung by a boy soprano.

In 1996 the song was recorded by the [Dutch Tolkien Society](/wiki/Unquendor "Unquendor") band [The Hobbitons](/wiki/The_Hobbitons), with permission from the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate), for their CD *J.R.R. Tolkien's Songs from Middle-earth*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The copyright of the poem was given by Tolkien to Hill together with the poem itself. Upon her death in 1991, Hill gave the copyright to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Paraclete" title="wikipedia:Order of the Holy Paraclete">Order of the Holy Paraclete</a>. Baynes's illustrations for *Bilbo's Last Song* were not included in the bequeathing to the Order, but were given the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams-Exeter_Programme_at_Oxford" title="wikipedia:Williams-Exeter Programme at Oxford">Williams-Exeter Programme at Oxford</a> upon her death in 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## The poem {#The_poem}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p><b>Bilbo&#39;s Last Song</b><br/>
<small>(At the Grey Havens)</small><br/>
<br/>
Day is ended, dim my eyes,<br/>
but journey long before me lies.<br/>
Farewell, friends! I hear the call.<br/>
The ship&#39;s beside the stony wall.<br/>
Foam is white and waves are grey;<br/>
beyond the sunset leads my way.<br/>
Foam is salt, the wind is free;<br/>
I hear the rising of the Sea.<br/>
<br/>
Farewell, friends! The sails are set,<br/>
the wind is east, the moorings fret.<br/>
Shadows long before me lie,<br/>
beneath the ever-bending sky,<br/>
but islands lie behind the Sun<br/>
that I shall raise ere all is done;<br/>
lands there are to west of West,<br/>
where night is quiet and sleep is rest.<br/>
<br/>
Guided by the Lonely Star,<br/>
beyond the utmost harbour-bar<br/>
I&#39;ll find the havens fair and free,<br/>
and beaches of the Starlit Sea.<br/>
Ship, my ship! I seek the West,<br/>
and fields and mountains ever blest.<br/>
Farewell to Middle-Earth at last.<br/>
I see the Star above your mast!
</p>
</div>
