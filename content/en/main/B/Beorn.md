**Beorn** was a [Skin-changer](/wiki/Skin-changers "Skin-changers"), a man who could assume the form of a huge black [bear](/wiki/Bears "Bears"). He aided [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) during their [Quest of Erebor](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor).

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/09/Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg/300px-Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="185" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/09/Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg/450px-Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/09/Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg/600px-Jef_Murray_-_At_the_Carrock.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="492"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Jef_Murray" title="Jef Murray">Jef Murray</a> - <i>At the Carrock</i></figcaption></figure>

His origins lay in the distant past, and [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) suspected he and his people had originally come from [the mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains "Misty Mountains"), until the [Orcs of the Misty Mountains](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains) drove them away. He lived with his tame [horses](/wiki/Horses "Horses") in a [wooden house](/wiki/Beorn%27s_Hall "Beorn's Hall") between the [Great River](/wiki/Anduin "Anduin") and [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood).

Beorn named stone eyots in the Great River "carrocks"; the one closer to his home he named "the [Carrock](/wiki/Carrock)" and created some steps that led from its base to the flat top. While Gandalf knew him, Beorn did not know Gandalf; but he knew his fellow wizard, [Radagast](/wiki/Radagast). Once Gandalf saw him as a huge black bear sitting all alone on the top of the Carrock watching the moon sinking towards the Misty Mountains, and heard him growl in the tongue of bears "The day will come when [they](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains "Orcs of the Misty Mountains") will perish and I shall go back!".<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-1">[1]</a></sup>

During the [Quest of Erebor](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor), Beorn received Gandalf, [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) and the thirteen [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) and gave the Dwarves and Bilbo help in their quest. As Gandalf knew that he disliked guests, he presented the dwarves gradually, while narrating his adventures in an exciting way. Beorn agreed to host them and provided them [ponies](/wiki/Ponies "Ponies") until they reached the Mirkwood. To be sure about his ponies, Beorn stalked the company in the form of a bear, seen by Gandalf and Bilbo, and before Gandalf left them, insisted that the Dwarves should leave the ponies behind, as promised, as they had been always under their master's watch.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-1">[1]</a></sup>

Beorn heard news of the [Dragon](/wiki/Dragons "Dragons") [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug)'s death in [Esgaroth](/wiki/Lake-town "Lake-town").<sup id="cite_ref-H14_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H14-2">[2]</a></sup>

In the [Battle of Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies), Beorn appeared transformed into a giant black bear, rescued [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin) from the [Goblins](/wiki/Orcs "Orcs") and killed their leader, [Bolg](/wiki/Bolg).<sup id="cite_ref-H18_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H18-3">[3]</a></sup>

Wenzel">David T. Wenzel</a></figcaption></figure>

After Thorin's burial, Beorn followed Bilbo, Gandalf and [Thranduil](/wiki/Thranduil) on their way back to the west. Beorn once more hosted Bilbo and Gandalf and other [Woodmen](/wiki/Woodmen) who came to celebrate [Yule](/wiki/Yule).<sup id="cite_ref-H18_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H18-3">[3]</a></sup>

Eventually, he became a "great chief" in the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin), and it is said that his descendants also were skin-changers, able to take the shape of a bear.<sup id="cite_ref-H18_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H18-3">[3]</a></sup> His people became known as the [Beornings](/wiki/Beornings),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and they helped defend [Thranduil](/wiki/Thranduil)'s kingdom of northern Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>. Beorn presumably died some time before the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) began<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and was succeeded by his son [Grimbeorn the Old](/wiki/Grimbeorn "Grimbeorn").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Character &amp; Appearance {#Character_&_Appearance}

Beorn was a great strong black-haired man with huge arms and a great beard.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-1">[1]</a></sup> He was generally benevolent and hated goblins and wargs; but he was also a loner and distrustful of travelers and beggars. He was never polite, and became easily angry. He never invited people into his house and his very few friends, who lived a good way away, came no more than a couple to his house at a time. Added to this, Beorn was not fond of [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves). Although fierce to his enemies, once convinced of the others' goodwill, he was welcoming, generous and helpful. He did not eat any meat, as he could talk with his animals, who helped him. His grove was occupied by huge [bees](/wiki/Bees "Bees"). Beorn's diet was mainly honey and cream.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In early manuscripts of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, the name of the character that would become Beorn is *Medwed*. Medwed's ability to change shape to a bear was due to an enchantment, perhaps of his own.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> It has been suggested that *Medwed* is a Slavic name<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> with <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/medved" title="wiktionary:medved">medved</a></i> being a common Slavic word for "bear".

It should be noted that <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/medv%C4%9Bd%D1%8C" title="wiktionary:Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/medvědь"><i>medved</i></a> is a taboo word for "bear" with the literal sense being "honey-eater"; the same etymology is constructed by J.R.R. Tolkien for the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) word <i><a class="external text" href="https://eldamo.org/content/words/word-2314741507.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">megli</a></i> (*mad-* "eat" + *glî* "honey"; thus "honey eater"),<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> suggesting that Tolkien was aware of the slavic word.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

It has been suggested that the character Beorn was influenced by the Norse *[Saga of Hrólfr Kraki](/wiki/Hr%C3%B3lfs_saga_kraka "Hrólfs saga kraka")*.<sup id="cite_ref-RME73_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RME73-20">[20]</a></sup> In the saga appears a great bear defending <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hr%C3%B3lfr_Kraki" title="wikipedia:Hrólfr Kraki">Hrólfr Kraki</a>, and also a man cursed to being transformed into a bear during nighttime. The work was well-known to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), as one of his students and friend, Stella Miller, made [a translation of the saga](/wiki/The_Saga_of_Hrolf_Kraki "The Saga of Hrolf Kraki") dedicated to Tolkien, [E.V. Gordon](/wiki/E.V._Gordon) and C.T. Onions;<sup id="cite_ref-AH164_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AH164-14">[14]</a></sup> but even more importantly as Tolkien himself wrote an unpublished short story of the tale, entitled *[Sellic Spell](/wiki/Sellic_Spell)*.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

Another suggested inspiration is the character of *[Beowulf](</wiki/Beowulf_(poem)> "Beowulf (poem)")*, "*whose name is commonly explained as Beowulf = 'bees' wolf' = honey-eater = bear, and one who breaks swords, rips off arms and cracks ribs with ursine power and clumsiness.*"<sup id="cite_ref-RME73_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RME73-20">[20]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>Beorn is a Character, with the Home Site Beorn&#39;s House.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game)" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (2003 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the GBA version, Beorn is visited after Thorin and Company depart from the Carrock. In the console and PC versions, Beorn appears only during the Battle of Five Armies and the ending cutscene, both times in the shape of a black bear.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2012-14: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (film series)</a></b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Mikael_Persbrandt" title="Mikael Persbrandt">Mikael Persbrandt</a> portrays Beorn.<sup id="cite_ref-AFTONBLADETCasting_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AFTONBLADETCasting-25">[25]</a></sup> In <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Desolation_of_Smaug" title="The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug">The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug</a></i>, while in bear form, he picks up on the <a href="/wiki/Thorin_and_Company" title="Thorin and Company">Company&#39;s</a> trail and chases them into his <a href="/wiki/Beorn%27s_Hall" title="Beorn&#39;s Hall">home</a>. He stands guard overnight, thwarting the plans of <a href="/wiki/Azog" title="Azog">Azog</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Orcs" title="Orcs">Orc</a> pack to attack. The next morning, he lends the Company some of his <a href="/wiki/Horses" title="Horses">horses</a> in order to get to <a href="/wiki/Mirkwood" title="Mirkwood">Mirkwood</a> before the Orcs. In <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i>, he arrives with <a href="/wiki/Radagast" title="Radagast">Radagast</a> on the back of one of the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Eagles" title="Eagles">Eagles</a> late in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies" title="Battle of Five Armies">battle</a>, and leaps down to the ground, transforming into his bear form in midair. Along with the rest of the Eagles, he helps decimate the remaining Orc army. He is also present during the funeral of Thorin, Fili and Kili.</dd></dl>

<b>2019: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Beorn makes a brief cameo in a flashback sequence where he saved a <a href="/wiki/Woodmen" title="Woodmen">Woodmen</a> child from <a href="/wiki/Gollum" title="Gollum">Gollum</a> the child-snatcher.</dd></dl>
