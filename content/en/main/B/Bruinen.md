**Bruinen** or **Loudwater** was a loud river in eastern [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md).

## Course {#Course}

The river began as two streams that flowed from the western slopes of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md). The northern stream had its source near the [High Pass](../H/High_Pass.md). The southern stream flowed through the deep valley of [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md). The two streams joined a few miles southwest of Rivendell and then flowed southwest until the Loudwater flowed into the river [Mitheithel](../M/Mitheithel.md). The Bruinen had only one known ford, the [Ford](../F/Ford_of_Bruinen.md) near Rivendell.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is certain that the southern stream was called Bruinen or Loudwater, because [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md)'s room in Rivendell "looked south across the ravine of the Bruinen"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and Frodo "walked along the terraces above the loud-flowing Bruinen"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>. It is probable that the northern stream was also called the Bruinen or Loudwater, because Rivendell lay in the lands east of Loudwater<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-4">[4]</a></sup> and the southern stream in the valley of Rivendell flows almost straight from east to west while the northern stream makes a turn to the flow to the southwest north of Rivendell so that the valley of Rivendell lies east from this section of the northern stream before it joins the southern stream<sup id="cite_ref-Map_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-1">[1]</a></sup>.

## History {#History}

After the founding of the realm of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) in [3320](../S/Second_Age_3320.md),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> the Loudwater formed its eastern border.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [861](../T/Third_Age_861.md),<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> when Arnor was divided into the splinter-kingdoms of [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md), [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md) and [Rhudaur](../R/Rhudaur.md), the river was the eastern border of Rhudaur.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1150](../T/Third_Age_1150.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA1150_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1150-9">[9]</a></sup> the [Stoors](../S/Stoors.md), one of the three kinds of [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md), left their homes at the bank of the river [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) and migrated to the west,<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-10">[10]</a></sup> crossed the Misty Mountains over the [Redhorn Pass](../R/Redhorn_Gate.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA1150_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1150-9">[9]</a></sup> and followed the course of the Loudwater southwards and settled between [Tharbad](../T/Tharbad.md) and the borders of [Dunland](../D/Dunland.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-10">[10]</a></sup>.

When [Thorin](../T/Thorin.md) and company went to reclaim [Erebor](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md), they crossed the Bruinen after their adventure with the [Trolls](../T/Trolls.md), before they long paused at Rivendell.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

At the beginning of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) was carried on [Glorfindel](../G/Glorfindel.md)'s horse towards the Ford of Bruinen, with the [Ringwraiths](../N/Nazgûl.md) in hot pursuit. At the Ford Frodo, poisoned by a [Morgul-wound](../M/Morgul-wound.md), made his stand, and defied the [Lord of the Nazgûl](../W/Witch-king.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-12">[12]</a></sup> This lured the Ringwraiths into the Bruinen,<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-12">[12]</a></sup> and Elrond commanded the river to form a great flood and [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) turned the shape of some of the waves into the form of white riders on white horses<sup id="cite_ref-Meetings_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meetings-13">[13]</a></sup>. This flood carried away the the horses and the Ringwraiths, buying the [Fellowship of the Ring](../F/Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md) some time.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-12">[12]</a></sup> Gandalf was convinced that the horses of the Ringwraiths had been killed in the river, but that the Ringwraiths had survived.<sup id="cite_ref-Meetings_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meetings-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Tolkien translated the name as **Loudwater**.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> [Patrick H. Wynne](../P/Patrick_H._Wynne.md) suggested that *Bruinen* is Sindarin and linked the first element *brui* ("noisy") to the second element of the word *[Ulumúri](../U/Ulumúri.md)*, and several other [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) and [Goldogrin](../G/Gnomish.md) words with some connection to "great sound". The second element is *[nen](../N/Nen.md)* ("water").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

[Wayne Hammond](../W/Wayne_Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) note that Loudwater is an old name for the River Wye in Buckinhamshire.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) pencilled in the name "Bruinen or Loudater" near the river north of the house of Elrond and "Merrill" near the tributary that flowed just south of the house of Elrond on the [Map of Wilderland](../M/Map_of_Wilderland.md) in one of his copies of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>
