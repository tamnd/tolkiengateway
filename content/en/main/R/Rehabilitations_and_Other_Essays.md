<i><b>Rehabilitations and Other Essays</b></i> is a collection of essays by [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis).

The essay "The Alliterative Metre" includes a mention of an unpublished text by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien): "Professor Tolkien will soon, I hope, be ready to publish an alliterative poem". It remains unknown to which poem Lewis is referring, although suggestions have included "[The Fall of Arthur](/wiki/The_Fall_of_Arthur)", "[The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm's Son](/wiki/The_Homecoming_of_Beorhtnoth_Beorhthelm%27s_Son)", "[The Lay of the Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)", or poems included in *[The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún](/wiki/The_Legend_of_Sigurd_and_Gudr%C3%BAn)*.<sup id="cite_ref-JF_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JF-1">[1]</a></sup>

The essay also includes a poem (p. 122) about himself and Tolkien "in a Berkshire bar" where they meet a man claiming to have seen a [dragon](/wiki/Dragons "Dragons").<sup id="cite_ref-JF_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JF-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup> Tolkien commented on the poem in his [letter 300](/wiki/Letter_300 "Letter 300").
