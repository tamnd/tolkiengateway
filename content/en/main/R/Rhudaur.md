**Rhudaur** was the hilly kingdom that originated from the break-up of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861").

## Geography {#Geography}

Rhudaur was a region in the north-east of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), lying between the [Ettenmoors](/wiki/Ettenmoors), the [Weather Hills](/wiki/Weather_Hills), and the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains). The land between the rivers [Loudwater](/wiki/Mitheithel "Mitheithel") and [Hoarwell](/wiki/Hoarwell), forming [the Angle](/wiki/Angle "Angle"), where the [Trollshaws](/wiki/Trollshaws) were located,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was also a part of Rhudaur.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Flight_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-3">[3]</a></sup>

Rhudaur was characterized by dreary and rising hills, dark trees with twisted roots hanging off of cliffs, and gloomy weather. Rhudaur was a sombre, threatening, and unfriendly country.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-3">[3]</a></sup>

Upon many heights and ridges were ancient stone walls, ruined towers, and ominous, evil-looking castles.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When the [Exiles of Númenor](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor) established [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), they spread out throughout [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) and many [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) settled the hills of Rhudaur.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861") Arnor's tenth King, Eärendur, died.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Due to dissensions between his sons the realm was split into [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), Rhudaur and [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan). There was often strife among the three kingdoms, because Rhudaur and Cardolan desired to possess the Weather Hills and the land westwards towards Bree, especially [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop). This was due to the fact that the tower on Weathertop contained the most important *[palantír](/wiki/Amon_S%C3%BBl-stone "Amon Sûl-stone")* of the North and the other two palantíri of the North were also held by Arthedain. While the line of [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) continued in Arthedain, it soon failed in Rhudaur and Cardolan.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-2">[2]</a></sup>

The first [Stoor](/wiki/Stoors "Stoors") [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) moved to the [Angle](/wiki/Angle) in Rhudaur around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1150](/wiki/Third_Age_1150 "Third Age 1150").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

During the reign of King [Argeleb I](/wiki/Argeleb_I) of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), which started in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1349](/wiki/Third_Age_1349 "Third Age 1349"),<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Argeleb claimed the lordship over all former Arnor, because no descendants of Isildur remained in Cardolan and Rhudaur, but Rhudaur resisted his claim. In Rhudaur an evil lord of the [Hill-men](/wiki/Hill-men) who had a secret alliance with Angmar had seized power and there were only few Dúnedain in Rhudaur. As a consequence, Argeleb fortified the Weather Hills, which were on the border between Arthedain and Rhudaur.<sup id="cite_ref-Argeleb_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Argeleb-8">[8]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1356](/wiki/Third_Age_1356 "Third Age 1356"),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> Argeleb was killed in a battle with Rhudaur and Angmar. Around this time the Stoors left the Angle and some of them returned to [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion).<sup id="cite_ref-Argeleb_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Argeleb-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1409](/wiki/Third_Age_1409 "Third Age 1409") Angmar attacked and ravaged [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and surrounded and destroyed [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop). Angmar defeated the Dúnedain of Cardolan and Arthedain and the [last prince of Cardolan](/wiki/Last_prince_of_Cardolan "Last prince of Cardolan") (later interred in the Barrow-downs)<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> and King Arveleg of Arthedain were killed. Rhudaur was occupied by evil men who were subject to Angmar. The Dúnedain that had remained in Rhudaur were killed or fled westwards.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In the days of [Argeleb II](/wiki/Argeleb_II), the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) sent [wights](/wiki/Wights "Wights") from [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) and Rhudaur to inhabit the deserted mounds of the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

When war brought the North-kingdom to an end, all the evil Men who inhabited Rhudaur were killed, but a shadow remained in the land. During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), Rhudaur was completely uninhabited.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Rhudaur* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name. According to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) it means "Troll shaw" and contains the element <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Rh%C3%BB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rhû (page does not exist)">rhû</a></i> ("evil, wicked"). [Christopher Gilson](/wiki/Christopher_Gilson) suggests in his editorial annotation within brackets that the second element is *[taur](/wiki/Taur "Taur")* ("forest").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Shaw is an archaic word meaning "thicket, small wood, copse, or grove".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> The Trollshaws were located in Rhudaur.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

J.R.R. Tolkien's translation was published in [Parma Eldalamberon 17](/wiki/Parma_Eldalamberon_17) in 2007. [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) suggested in 2004 and [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) suggested in 2005 that *Rhudaur* is a Sindarin name, which means "eastern wood" or "east forest", respectively, and that it is a compound of *[rhu-](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn "Rhûn")* ("east") and *[taur](/wiki/Taur "Taur")* ("forest").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become the Annals of the Kings and Rulers mentions that an evil people who are workers of sorcery and subjects of Angmar kill the remnants of the Dúnedain in Rhudaur and build dark forts in the hills.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

An earlier manuscript version of the the Tale of Years of the Third Age mentions that the Witch-king destroyed the remmants of the Dúnedain that lived in Rhudaur and that an evil people from the north that was much given to sorcery lived in Rhudaur for a long time.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>
