A **reading order** is the order in which Tolkien's works are read.

## Description {#Description}

New [Tolkien readers](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") are usually puzzled with the complexity of the works and the best order in which to read the works. The most famous works take place late in the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium")'s history, with hints to the backstory. The stories that comprise the background are relatively complex and have no obvious links to the most famous works. Furthemore, the stories-chapters-essays overlap each another, or take place simultaneously, differing only in the extensiveness of the details.

Hardcore fans read the works more than once. For example one can read *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* first, then *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and sometime later *The Lord of the Rings* again, in light of the backstory seen in *The Hobbit*.

There can be various approaches:

## Publication history {#Publication_history}

This can be one of the most common approaches.

- *The Hobbit*

<dl><dd>People can start with <i>The Hobbit</i>, as an easy and lighthearted fantasy story, which <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a> published first, without having much backstory and historical details in mind.</dd></dl>

- *The Lord of the Rings*

<dl><dd>This is the natural sequel of <i>The Hobbit</i> and completes the story. However it has many allusions to the greater world and ancient history of <a href="/wiki/Arda" title="Arda">Arda</a>.</dd></dl>

- *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*

<dl><dd>Published after Tolkien&#39;s death, it provides the cosmogony and earlier stories of Arda. It can give the reader insight about the world they already know.</dd></dl>

- *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)* / *[The Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)* / *[Beren and Lúthien](/wiki/Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien)* / *[The Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin)*

<dl><dd>These books provide additional and extensive details about several aspects of the greater history.</dd></dl>

## Rough chronological order {#Rough_chronological_order}

A less common approach is to read the saga in chronologically accurate order:

- *The Silmarillion*

*The Silmarillion* describes the beginnings of Tolkien's world.

- *The Children of Húrin*, *Beren and Lúthien* and *The Fall of Gondolin*

These books give an extended version of the Great Tales of the Elder Days, featured as a part of *The Silmarillion*.

- *Unfinished Tales*

Parts of this book give insight about [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) (mentioned in *The Silmarillion*) and early [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), with elements that will play their role in *The Lord of the Rings*.

- *The Hobbit*

Although directly unrelated to *The Silmarillion*, it is the next big story.

- *The Lord of the Rings*

The conclusion of the story of *The Hobbit* as well as things mentioned in *The Silmarillion*.
