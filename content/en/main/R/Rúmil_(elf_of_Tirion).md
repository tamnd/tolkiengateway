**Rúmil** was a [Noldorin](../N/Noldor.md) sage of [Tirion](../T/Tirion.md),<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">[1]</a></sup> one of the [Lambengolmor](../L/Lambengolmor.md) ("Loremasters")<sup id="cite_ref-WJ_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ-2">[2]</a></sup> of the Noldor and first deviser of written characters.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Rúmil was notable for inventing a writing system that was fitting for the recording of speech and song, some for graving upon metal or stone, others for drawing with brush or with pen.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">[3]</a></sup>

His alphabet was called both *Tengwar of Rúmil* ("Letters of Rúmil")<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">[4]</a></sup> and *[Sarati](../S/Sarati.md)*, as each letter was called a *sarat*.<sup id="cite_ref-WJ_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The Tengwar of Rúmil was not used in Middle-earth and was later expanded and perfected by [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md)<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">[3]</a></sup> as the [Tengwar of Fëanor](../T/Tengwar.md) ("Letters of Fëanor"), more commonly known as simply Tengwar.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">[4]</a></sup>

Based on when Rúmil's work in the [Annals of Valinor](../A/Annals_of_Valinor.md) ends, it was suggested that he was one of the Noldor who initially joined the [Exile](../E/Exile.md), but then returned to Valinor with [Finarfin](../F/Finarfin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:99</sup>

Rúmil continued his studies from Aman for many thousands of years beyond the [fall of Gondolin](../F/Fall_of_Gondolin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Lhammas_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lhammas-7">[7]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:154</sup> He continued to document the evolution of language among the three clans in Aman,<sup id="cite_ref-Lhammas_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lhammas-7">[7]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:145</sup> as well as what he had learned of languages from [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), such as [Dwarvish](../K/Khuzdul.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Lhammas_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lhammas-7">[7]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:144</sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Works {#Works}

Rúmil was said to be the author of various works, some of which would find [great acknowledgment](../T/The_Silmarillion.md) after they were translated into [Westron](../W/Westron.md) by [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md). These include:

- *[Ainulindalë](<../A/Ainulindalë_(Rúmil's_work).md>)*<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>
- *[Annals of Valinor](../A/Annals_of_Valinor.md)*
- *[Lhammas](../L/Lhammas.md)*
- <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Lhammasethen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lhammasethen (page does not exist)">Lhammasethen</a></i>
- *[Ambarkanta: The Shape of the World](../A/Ambarkanta.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>
- <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=I_Equessi_Rumilo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="I Equessi Rumilo (page does not exist)">i Equessi Rumilo</a></i>

[Pengolodh](../P/Pengolodh.md) of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md) continued and completed much of Rúmil's work.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The only thing known about the name *Rúmil* is that it is [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) speculated that it is likely to be connected with the [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) words *rû* and *rûm* ("secret, mystery"), *ruim* ("secret, mysterious"), *rui* ("whisper") and/or *ruitha* ("to whisper").<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:345</sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Book of Lost Tales {#Book_of_Lost_Tales}

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*, Rúmil was the [Gnome](../G/Gnome.md) Door-ward of [Mar Vanwa Tyaliéva](../C/Cottage_of_Lost_Play.md) and the narrator of many tales including *[The Music of the Ainur](../T/The_Music_of_the_Ainur.md)*, *[The Coming of the Valar and the Building of Valinor](../T/The_Coming_of_the_Valar_and_the_Building_of_Valinor.md)*, and *[The Flight of the Noldoli](../T/The_Flight_of_the_Noldoli.md)* (along with [Lindo](../L/Lindo.md)).

He is described as old in appearance and grey of locks.<sup id="cite_ref-link_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-link-13">[12]</a></sup> The character [Littleheart](../L/Littleheart.md) referred to him as "Rúmil the Sage"<sup id="cite_ref-valar_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-valar-14">[13]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:82</sup> and [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) described him as "the garrulous and whimsical philologist of [Kortirion](../K/Kortirion.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:61</sup>

In the distant past ("a lifetime gone") Rúmil was a resident of [Kôr](../K/Kôr.md).<sup id="cite_ref-link_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-link-13">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:60</sup> In the [Great Lands](../G/Great_Lands.md), long before the [Fall of Gondolin](../F/Fall_of_Gondolin.md), he was a slave under [Melko](../M/Melko.md).<sup id="cite_ref-link_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-link-13">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:59</sup> He was not one of the first Elves as, when describing the [Music of the Ainur](../M/Music_of_the_Ainur.md), he claims his knowledge of it originated from [Manwë](../M/Manwë.md) whispering it to "the fathers of my father in the deeps of time".<sup id="cite_ref-music_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-music-16">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:66</sup>

Rúmil was very learned in languages. During his days in Kôr he learned much of the "secret tongue" [of the Valar](../V/Valarin.md) by the goodness of [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md). He was aware of the distinctions between the various [Elven tongues](../E/Elvish.md) of the Teleri (later [Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium)), Noldoli, [Solosimpi](../S/Solosimpi.md), and the [Inwir](../I/Inwir.md) (the royal clan *within* the Teleri). While a slave under Melko, he lightened his thraldom by learning the speech of all monsters and goblins. He further conned even the speeches of beasts including voles, mice, and beetles. He worried at whiles over the tongues of [Men](../M/Men.md), but disliked them due to their propensity to change and of being a "hard stuff whereof to labour songs or tales".<sup id="cite_ref-link_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-link-13">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:59–60</sup>

Rúmil does not appear directly in *[The Book of Lost Tales: Part Two](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales%253A_Part_Two.md)*, however he and his work, "the book of Rúmil", is frequently cited in the Appendix defining names.

In a later draft of [Meril-i-Turinqi's](../M/Meril-i-Turinqi.md) *"[The Chaining of Melko](../T/The_Chaining_of_Melko.md)"*, Tolkien apparently intended to replace Rúmil with <i><b>Evromord the Door-ward</b></i> of Mar Vanwa Tyaliéva.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>

### The Lost Road {#The_Lost_Road}

In the 1930's version of the *[Ainulindalë](<../A/Ainulindalë_(Lost_Road).md>)*, [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md) (Eriol) still heard the story of the Music of the Ainur from Rúmil’s own lips in Tol Eressëa.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup>
