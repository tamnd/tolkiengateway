**Rhûnic**<sup id="cite_ref-merc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merc-1">[1]</a></sup>, also called **Rhûnnic**<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>, was a language spoken by the [Easterling](../E/Easterlings.md) mercenaries<sup id="cite_ref-merc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merc-1">[1]</a></sup> known as the [Gaudrim](../G/Gaudrim.md) in *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Rings_of_Power.md)*.

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/300px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/450px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/600px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4371" data-file-height="2126"/></a><figcaption>A summary of Tolkien’s attested human languages</figcaption></figure>

Tolkien never refers to any "Rhûnic" language or people, but uses the broader category of "[Easterlings](../E/Easterlings.md)", and the existence of Easterling languages is confirmed even though such languages were not fleshed out by Tolkien (outside of some names like [Khamûl](../K/Khamûl.md)). Rhûnic, while allegedly based on one of Tolkien’s languages<sup id="cite_ref-merc_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merc-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-fof_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fof-3">[3]</a></sup>, was developed for *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Rings_of_Power.md)*. In the words of the series composer [Bear McCreary](../B/Bear_McCreary.md)<sup id="cite_ref-merc_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merc-1">[1]</a></sup>:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>For this series, we always strive to ensure that music sung by soloists or choirs communicates meaning in text that is relevant to the culture the music represents. So, what language would the music of Rhûn be sung in? Rhûn is a large, mostly unexplored area in Tolkien’s published works, left intentionally vague. Tolkien scholars working on our series dug through Tolkien’s unpublished notes and used them to build the foundation of a Rhûnic language, one that has never been heard before by fans. Helping to expand one of Tolkien’s lesser-known languages into a vibrant musical culture was a true honor.
</p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

It is possible that Rhûnic is based on J. R. R. Tolkien’s Hungarian-based language called [Mágol](../M/Mágol.md), for which most of existent material remains unpublished.<sup id="cite_ref-fof_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fof-3">[3]</a></sup>

<br> Note that **Â** only appears in [Brânk](../B/Brânk.md) while **Û** and **KH** only appear in [Khamûl](../K/Khamûl.md), **the latter of which may not be included in Rhûnic**.

**Y** only appears in the word **DYÚL**, so It might not represent a separate phoneme, but rather change the **D** sound into one that’s similar to the Czech and Slovak **Ď** or the Hungarian **GY**.

It is also possible that the **N** in the words **NÍ** and <b>*NIM</b> is pronounced as /ɲ/, the same way the **N** in the word <i><b>n</b></i>ew is in some dialects of English.
