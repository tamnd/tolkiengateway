**Rushey** was a small village south of the [Marish](/wiki/Marish), in the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire").<sup id="cite_ref-Part_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Part-1">[1]</a></sup> It was built on relatively stable ground amidst the soggy fens of the Marish, hence the name.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup> The [Causeway](/wiki/Causeway) traversed the village, connecting it to the villages of [Stock](/wiki/Stock) to the north and [Deephallow](/wiki/Deephallow) to the south.<sup id="cite_ref-Part_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Part-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name means "Rush-isle". [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) has suggested an [Old Hobbitish](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") etymology from \**Ryscieg*.<sup id="cite_ref-Salo_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Salo-3">[3]</a></sup> <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ey#Old_Norse" title="wiktionary:ey">-ey</a></i> or *-y* in English place-names has the sense "small island", as seen in [Girdley](/wiki/Girdley).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>

It was called so as "*a 'hard' among the fens of the Marish*"<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup> forming an isolated "island" in the midst of the soggy ground; this nomenclature is also seen in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Ely" title="wikipedia:Isle of Ely">Isle of Ely</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Salo_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Salo-3">[3]</a></sup>

The name is spelled as "Rushy" on [A Part of the Shire](/wiki/A_Part_of_the_Shire).<sup id="cite_ref-Part_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Part-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Salo_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Salo-3">[3]</a></sup>
