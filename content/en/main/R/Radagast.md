**Radagast the Brown**, also known as **Hrávandil** and **Aiwendil**, was a [Maia](/wiki/Maia) who was sent to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) as one of the [Guardians](/wiki/Five_Guardians "Five Guardians") to protect the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) against [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) at the beginning of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) and later as one of the [Wizards](/wiki/Wizards) to contest the will of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). Chosen for his later mission by the Valië [Yavanna](/wiki/Yavanna), he had a strong affinity for animals. Radagast dwelt, for a time, at [Rhosgobel](/wiki/Rhosgobel) on the western eaves of [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood), near the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields) and the [Great River](/wiki/Anduin "Anduin") in the Third Age.

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Jef_Murray_-_Mage_of_Rhosgobel.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Jef_Murray_-_Mage_of_Rhosgobel.jpg/300px-Jef_Murray_-_Mage_of_Rhosgobel.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="490" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Jef_Murray_-_Mage_of_Rhosgobel.jpg/450px-Jef_Murray_-_Mage_of_Rhosgobel.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/3/3b/Jef_Murray_-_Mage_of_Rhosgobel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="490" data-file-height="800"/></a><figcaption><i>Mage of Rhosgobel</i> by <a href="/wiki/Jef_Murray" title="Jef Murray">Jef Murray</a></figcaption></figure>

The [Maia](/wiki/Maia) Aiwendil was an [Ainu](/wiki/Ainu) who existed before [Time](/wiki/Time), and one of the [spirits](/wiki/Spirits "Spirits") who descended to [Arda](/wiki/Arda) in order to serve the [Valar](/wiki/Valar).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Aiwendil was associated with the Valië [Yavanna](/wiki/Yavanna), the Queen of earth.<sup id="cite_ref-IstariTables_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IstariTables-4">[4]</a></sup>

In the beginning of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), after the Vala [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB) discovered the primeval [Elves](/wiki/Elves) at [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen), Aiwendil was sent by the Valar as one of five other great spirits of the Maiar as a [Guardian](/wiki/Five_Guardians "Five Guardians") to Cuiviénen to protect the Elves from the forces of [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor). He was known there by the name *Hrávandil*. It is unclear in which shape he came there, how long he remained or if he accompanied the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) on the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

After the defeat of [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth), the Valar grew concerned about the growing power of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). Manwë summoned a council of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), and it was decided that they would send Maiar as emissaries to Middle-earth to aid the [Free Peoples](/wiki/Free_Peoples). [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) chose [Curumo](/wiki/Saruman "Saruman") and Manwë chose [Olórin](/wiki/Gandalf "Gandalf"). Yavanna subsequently begged Curumo to take Aiwendil with him,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Curumo was obliged to do so to please Yavanna.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The Valar forbade them to match Sauron's power with power or to use force or fear to dominate the Elves and [Men](/wiki/Men)<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>, such as to reveal themselves in majestic forms or open displays of power<sup id="cite_ref-UTIstari_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTIstari-2">[2]</a></sup>. Instead, they were asked to advise and persuade Elves and Men and were sent in weak and humble real bodies that resembled old [Men](/wiki/Men). As a consequence, they could experience fear, pain, weariness, hunger, thirst and could be killed. However, due to their spirits they aged only slowly.<sup id="cite_ref-UTIstari_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTIstari-2">[2]</a></sup>

In c. [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1000](/wiki/Third_Age_1000 "Third Age 1000"), Aiwendil who was clothed in earthen brown, Curumo, and Olórin arrived upon the shores of Middle-earth, having the form of old [Men](/wiki/Men), whom the peoples called [Wizards](/wiki/Wizards).<sup id="cite_ref-UTIstari_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTIstari-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Travels in Middle-earth {#Travels_in_Middle-earth}

When the Wizards took their mission they roamed Middle-earth, but Radagast was never much of a traveller.<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP256_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP256-9">[9]</a></sup> There is not much to be told about his early journeys, but by the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) he eventually settled down and dwelt, for a time at least, at [Rhosgobel](/wiki/Rhosgobel) near the borders of [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood)<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP256_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP256-9">[9]</a></sup> near its southern end,<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-10">[10]</a></sup> probably on its western side south of the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields) and opposite of the [East Bight](/wiki/East_Bight)<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>. He received his name Radagast from the [Men of the Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Men_of_the_Vales_of_Anduin).<sup id="cite_ref-IstariNote4_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IstariNote4-12">[12]</a></sup> Living on the western borders of Mirkwood, it can be assumed that the Wizard held watch against the [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) of [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur) that slowly engulfed the forest. It is likely that he became acquainted with the inhabitants of that region. He was friends with animals, especially birds, including the [Great Eagles](/wiki/Eagles "Eagles").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Although the neighboring [Beorn](/wiki/Beorn) was unsociable, he used to see him from time to time, and he considered Radagast "not a bad fellow" for a Wizard.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-10">[10]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2851](/wiki/Third_Age_2851 "Third Age 2851"), the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) met once more and, after that, Saruman began to search near the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields) for the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> Radagast decided to aid his search with birds and beasts who acted as spies hoping that Saruman's actions would help watch and hinder Sauron. Radagast did this in good faith, knowing nothing of Saruman's real ambitions to keep the Ring for himself.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-15">[15]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Angelo_Montanini_-_Radagast.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/48/Angelo_Montanini_-_Radagast.jpg/300px-Angelo_Montanini_-_Radagast.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="255" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/48/Angelo_Montanini_-_Radagast.jpg/450px-Angelo_Montanini_-_Radagast.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/4/48/Angelo_Montanini_-_Radagast.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="425"/></a><figcaption><i>Radagast</i> by <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Montanini" title="Angelo Montanini">Angelo Montanini</a></figcaption></figure>

By the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) Radagast did not dwell any more in Rhosgobel. In summer [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018") Saruman told Radagast that he was willing to help Gandalf, and sent the Brown Wizard to seek him out at once. Radagast did not know much of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) but sought for [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), knowing that he would find Gandalf nearby.<sup id="cite_ref-FRCouncil_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRCouncil-16">[16]</a></sup>

Indeed, on [Midsummer's Day](/wiki/Mid-year%27s_Day "Mid-year's Day"),<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> Radagast was sitting on the side of the [Greenway](/wiki/Greenway) with his horse near [Bree](/wiki/Bree) when Gandalf found him on his way to the village. Radagast warned Gandalf that the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) were abroad, disguised as riders in black, and that they were seeking news of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"). He also gave him Saruman's invitation and agreed to help Gandalf by getting beasts and birds to send news to [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc). With that he rode away back towards Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-FRCouncil_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRCouncil-16">[16]</a></sup>

By sending Gandalf to Orthanc, Radagast unwittingly had him captured. Saruman's message proved to be a trap for Gandalf who was imprisoned in Orthanc, but still he did not believe that Radagast was also a part of Saruman's plans. Indeed, it was thanks to Radagast that Gandalf was able to escape from the pinnacle of Orthanc upon the wings of [Gwaihir](/wiki/Gwaihir).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

Radagast's actions during and after the rest of the War are not recorded. After the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond), many scouts were sent out from [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) to many different locations. Some passed over the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and eventually came to [Rhosgobel](/wiki/Rhosgobel), but they found that Radagast was not there.<sup id="cite_ref-FRSouth_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRSouth-19">[19]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

Radagast was associated with the [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Yavanna](/wiki/Yavanna),<sup id="cite_ref-IstariTables_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IstariTables-4">[4]</a></sup> who loved plants<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> and had planted the seeds for plants in [Arda](/wiki/Arda)<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>. He was perhaps a member of the "people" of Yavanna.<sup id="cite_ref-IstariTables_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IstariTables-4">[4]</a></sup>

Radagast had much less power and wisdom than [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) or [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> Gandalf esteemed him as "a worthy Wizard"<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP257_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP257-23">[23]</a></sup>, but Saruman scorningly gave him the bynames "**the Bird-tamer**", "**the Simple**" and "**the Fool**"<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP258_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP258-24">[24]</a></sup>.

Radagast was "a master of shapes and changes of hue".<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP257_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP257-23">[23]</a></sup> He fell in love with the beasts and birds of Middle-earth<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> and knew much about herbs and animals<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP257_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP257-23">[23]</a></sup>. Beasts and birds were his friends.<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP257_23-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP257-23">[23]</a></sup> Gandalf, however, held greater knowledge of birds and beasts than Radagast, as well as greater respect and affection received from them. Radagast found beasts and birds easier to deal with and interacted very little with [Elves](/wiki/Elves) or [Men](/wiki/Men).<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC.245_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC.245-27">[27]</a></sup>

Radagast only had short, curling, light brown hair on his chin (instead of a full beard) when he appeared in the body of a man in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Beards_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beards-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In a very late note written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) in [1972](/wiki/1972) the name is said to be derived from the [Men of the Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Men_of_the_Vales_of_Anduin) and that its meaning is "not now clearly interpretable".<sup id="cite_ref-IstariNote4_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IstariNote4-12">[12]</a></sup>

In a manuscript written by Tolkien in [1954](/wiki/1954), the name *Radagast* is said to be "in the tongue of Númenor of old" and to mean "tender of beasts".<sup id="cite_ref-UTIstari_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTIstari-2">[2]</a></sup> [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) suggests that it is possible that Tolkien did not mean classical [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), but [Westron](/wiki/Westron), which was descended from Adûnaic.<sup id="cite_ref-HHO_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HHO-28">[28]</a></sup>

John D. Rateliff and [Douglas A. Anderson](/wiki/Douglas_A._Anderson) suggest the name of the [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic) chieftain <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radagaisus" title="wikipedia:Radagaisus">Radagaisus</a></i>, whose name (as noted by [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo)) is rendered *Rhadagast* in some works from the 18th and 19th century (e.g., *King Alfred's Anglo-Saxon Version of Boethius' De Consolatione Philosophiae*, translated by J.S. Cardale in 1829) as a source of inspiration. They also suggest the name of the Slavic god <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radegast_%28god%29" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><i>Radegast</i></a> (mentioned by the eleventh-century german historian Adam of Bremen's Gesta Hamburgensis Ecclesiae Pontificum and nineteenth-century german writer Jacob Grimm in Teutonic Mythology) or *Redigast*.<sup id="cite_ref-HHO_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HHO-28">[28]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-30">[30]</a></sup> John D. Rateliff also suggests that it may be an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name, which means "Spirit of the Road" and consistings of the components *rad* ("road") and *gast* ("spirit", "angel", "human being"). John D. Rateliff suggests that the name of the Gothic king is the most convincing source of inspiration, because of J.R.R. Tolkien's interest in the Gothic language and his knowledge of Gothic history.<sup id="cite_ref-HHO_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HHO-28">[28]</a></sup> [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) mentions that Edward Gibbon's *Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire* stayed in Tolkien's mind and that *Radagaisus* is included in its index.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>

Mark Fisher suggests the old [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name *rudugást* ("red-brown spirit") from *rudu* ("red" or "red brown")<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> and *gást* ("spirit")<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> as a source for the name Radgast.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup>

[Jason Fisher](/wiki/Jason_Fisher) suggests the [Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse) word *ráðgast* ("to take counsel") as a source for the name Radagast.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup>

The name *Aiwendil* is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) for "Lover of Birds".<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup> It is obviously derived from *[aiwë](/wiki/Aiw%C3%AB "Aiwë")* ("a small bird") + *[-ndil](/wiki/-ndil)* ("devoted to").<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup>

Another Quenya name was *Hrávandil*, used when he was sent to [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen) with the other [Guardians](/wiki/Five_Guardians "Five Guardians"), which according to [Carl Hostetter](/wiki/Carl_Hostetter) most likely means "Wild beast friend", from *hravan* ("wild beast") and *-ndil* ("friend").<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup>

As one of the [Wizards](/wiki/Wizards) sent to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), he was known as "Radagast **the Brown**".<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP256_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP256-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP258_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP258-24">[24]</a></sup> It is possible that he received this byname, because of the earthen brown colour of the clothes that he wore when he arrived in Middle-earth in c. [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1000](/wiki/Third_Age_1000 "Third Age 1000"). The bynames of Saruman and Gandalf, "the White"<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[39]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP258_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP258-24">[24]</a></sup> and "the Grey"<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[40]</a></sup>, respectively,<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP258_24-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP258-24">[24]</a></sup> matched the colour of their clothes and the other two Wizards who wore sea-blue clothes were called "the Blue Wizards"<sup id="cite_ref-UTIstari_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTIstari-2">[2]</a></sup>.

[Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), when talking to [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), mocked Radagast by calling him "Radagast the Bird-tamer", "Radagast the Simple", and "Radagast the Fool".<sup id="cite_ref-CouncilP258_24-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CouncilP258-24">[24]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

From the first drafts of *The Hobbit*, [Bladorthin](/wiki/Gandalf "Gandalf") identifies Radagast as a fellow wizard and as his "cousin". [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) notes that, at this stage in the development of [Tolkien's](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") there was no reason why a wizard could not have a cousin. Rateliff also suggests that it is likely that Tolkien considered explaining Gandalf's absence (following the departure of Thorin and Company from Beorn's house) by saying that he went to visit Radagast (who lived close by) to plan the attack on the [Necromancer](/wiki/Sauron "Sauron").<sup id="cite_ref-HHMedwed_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HHMedwed-41">[41]</a></sup>

Early in the process of writing *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, it is clear that [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") envisaged some role for Radagast in the tale.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[42]</a></sup> He eventually decided that he would use Radagast as the means of getting Gandalf to Isengard.<sup id="cite_ref-TIC1.130-140_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TIC1.130-140-43">[43]</a></sup>

Initially Gandalf describes Radagast as his "cousin",<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[44]</a></sup> as he did in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*,<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-10">[10]</a></sup> but in a subsequent draft he becomes his "kinsman".<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[45]</a></sup> In the final version Gandalf merely says that Radagast is "one of my order".<sup id="cite_ref-FRCouncil_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRCouncil-16">[16]</a></sup>

Tolkien initially called him "Radagast **the Grey**", but in pencil he changed this to "Brown" and subsequently Saruman refers to him as "Radagast the Brown".<sup id="cite_ref-TIC1.130-140_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TIC1.130-140-43">[43]</a></sup>

When Tolkien finished writing the story up till Moria, he made notes on the future story development; therein he considered Gandalf evicting Saruman from Isengard and handing it over to Radagast after the fall of Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[46]</a></sup>

<b>2014: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Radagast first appears when he comes in Dol Guldur during the <a href="/wiki/Attack_on_Dol_Guldur" title="Attack on Dol Guldur">attack</a> by the <a href="/wiki/White_Council" title="White Council">White Council</a> to take the weakened Gandalf to safety. After they escape, Gandalf requests a horse, takes Radagast&#39;s staff and also commands him to summon &#39;bird and beast&#39; for the impending <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies" title="Battle of Five Armies">battle</a>. He arrives with the Eagles late in the battle along with <a href="/wiki/Beorn" title="Beorn">Beorn</a>. They demolish the Orc forces and effectively help end the Battle of Five Armies.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Donald_Gee" title="Donald Gee">Donald Gee</a> provided the voice of Radagast. He is introduced much earlier than in the book because his meeting with Gandalf is given chronologically.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/P%C3%A1n_prste%C5%88ov_(2001-2003_Slovak_radio_series)" title="Pán prsteňov (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)"><i>Pán prsteňov</i> (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)</a>:</b>

### Games {#Games}

<b>1987-: <i><a href="/wiki/Mithril_Miniatures" title="Mithril Miniatures">Mithril Miniatures</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Radagast has been issued in a couple of different versions: figure LR3 &#34;Radagast the Brown&#34; is seen with a cat and an owl;<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[48]</a></sup> an older version of the figure portrays Radagast without beard and with a different bird.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[49]</a></sup> There is also a &#34;Radagast Mounted&#34; (MS539), where Radagast (again without beard) is portrayed mounted on a horse.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[50]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1988: <i><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_War_in_Middle_Earth" title="J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s War in Middle Earth">J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s War in Middle Earth</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Radagast is a non-playable character in this game.</dd></dl>

<b>2001-: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Strategy_Battle_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game">The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The hero figure <a href="/wiki/File:SBG_-_Radagast.jpeg" title="File:SBG - Radagast.jpeg">Radagast the Brown</a>, is a user of subtle magics, in contrast to the more overt kinds used by Gandalf and Saruman. However, he has some unique powers nonetheless.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">[51]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2011-2010: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game">The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>A Decipher card was made by <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Weta_Workshop" title="Weta Workshop">Weta</a>, with Weta&#39;s John Harding posing as Radagast.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[52]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007-: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the distant past Radagast, &#34;Master of Shapes&#34;, had taught the ancestors of the <a href="/wiki/Beornings" title="Beornings">Beornings</a> the art of skin-changing. During his stay in <a href="/wiki/Rhosgobel" title="Rhosgobel">Rhosgobel</a>, Radagast had come across <a href="/wiki/Gollum" title="Gollum">Gollum</a> who was stealing and eating the babies of the <a href="/wiki/Woodmen" title="Woodmen">Woodmen</a> and banished him from the <a href="/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin" title="Vales of Anduin">Vales of Anduin</a>, causing Gollum to crawl inside the caverns of the <a href="/wiki/Misty_Mountains" title="Misty Mountains">Misty Mountains</a>. In early <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_3018" title="Third Age 3018">3018</a>, Radagast, on his way to see <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a>, visits <a href="/wiki/Grimbeorn" title="Grimbeorn">Grimbeorn</a> and bids him to send one of his children with a message for <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bree-land" title="Bree-land">Bree-land</a>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In the present, Radagast is first found in Ost Guruth, a small settlement of men in the <a href="/wiki/Lone-lands" title="Lone-lands">Lone-lands</a> north of the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Great_Road" title="Great Road">Great Road</a>. He is friendly to the local people, the Eglain, and helps them to combat the rise of evil in the swamps of Agamaur.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">[53]</a></sup> After the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Ring" title="War of the Ring">War of the Ring</a>, he tasks the player with ways of helping the living creatures around them. Radagast eventually settles back in Rhosgobel now that evil thatis departing the forest.</dd></dl>

<b>2011: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_War_in_the_North" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The characters <a href="/wiki/Eradan" title="Eradan">Eradan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Farin" title="Farin">Farin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Andriel" title="Andriel">Andriel</a> travel to Mirkwood in search of Radagast and arrive just in time to rescue him from the Spider Queen, Saenathra. He thanks them for the rescue and provides them with information about the Dragon Urgost.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[54]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2012-: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_Strategy_Battle_Game" title="The Hobbit Strategy Battle Game">The Hobbit Strategy Battle Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Radagast, based on <a href="/wiki/Peter_Jackson" title="Peter Jackson">Peter Jackson</a>&#39;s <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i></a>, is a hero figurine in two different versions: the standard model &#34;Radagast the Brown with <a href="/wiki/Sebastian" title="Sebastian">Sebastian</a>&#34;,<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">[55]</a></sup> and a limited model sold with the box <i>The Hobbit: Escape from Goblin Town</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[56]</a></sup></dd></dl>
