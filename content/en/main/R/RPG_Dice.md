**RPG Dice** are a trademark-friendly way of referring to the sorts of polyhedral dice used to play *[Dungeons and Dragons](../D/Dungeons_and_Dragons.md)*, and its *Lord of the Rings* adaptations *[Adventures in Middle-earth](../A/Adventures_in_Middle-earth.md)* and *[The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying.md)*.

The typical set generally consists of a four-sided die (d4), a six-sided die (d6), an eight-sided die (d8), a ten-sided die (d10), a twelve-sided die (d12), and a twenty-sided die (d20). Often, a second d10 with different numbering is added so one can roll up to 100 with both.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup>

The starter set for *The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying* comes with a translucent red set of such dice, though with two twenty-sided dice instead of two ten-sided dice. These come engraved with the [Eye of Sauron](../E/Eye_of_Sauron.md) and Gandalf's rune.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup> This is a convention taken from *[The One Ring](<../T/The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game).md>)* role-playing game, which used a [smaller subset of dice](../T/The_One_Ring_Dice_Sets.md).

## Q Workshop {#Q_Workshop}

While not affiliated with either roleplaying game system requiring such dice, Q Workshop did release several sets of themed dice under license from [New Line Cinema](../N/New_Line_Cinema.md) in [2026](../_/2026.md). These all have the standard set of seven, with two ten-sided dice. Many were also released as sets containing exclusively six-sided dice.<sup id="cite_ref-qw_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-qw-3">[2]</a></sup>
