**Roäc** (b. [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2788](/wiki/Third_Age_2788 "Third Age 2788")<sup id="cite_ref-H15_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H15-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup>) was the chief of the [ravens](/wiki/Ravens "Ravens") of [Ravenhill](/wiki/Ravenhill) near [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain").<sup id="cite_ref-H15_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H15-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Roäc was the son of [Carc](/wiki/Carc), and as such "*a descendant of those* \[ravens\] *that had remained on the hill even after Smaug had driven out their old allies, the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves)"*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

While [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) and [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) were keeping watch on Erebor, an old [thrush](/wiki/Thrushes "Thrushes") summoned Roäc. When he met Thorin and Company in [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), Roäc was marked by age, being 153 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-H15_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H15-1">[1]</a></sup>

Being able to speak [Westron](/wiki/Westron), the raven delivered the news that [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) had been killed. Roäc agreed to bring a message to [Dáin II Ironfoot](/wiki/D%C3%A1in_Ironfoot "Dáin Ironfoot") from Thorin, a call for help in guarding the treasures of Erebor in the wake of war.<sup id="cite_ref-H15_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H15-1">[1]</a></sup> However, Roäc also gave a piece of advice to Thorin, warning him that "*the treasure is likely to be your death*".<sup id="cite_ref-H16_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H16-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Roäc* is likely an <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomatopoeia" title="wikipedia:Onomatopoeia">onomatopoeic</a> name.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

In [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) (especially Irish) mythology, ravens are often associated with warfare and the battleground. In [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) mythology, ravens figure as the army of King Arthur's knight Owain.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

In *[The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún](/wiki/The_Legend_of_Sigurd_and_Gudr%C3%BAn)*, a raven and a finch speak five of the verses,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup> reminiscent of Roäc and the old thrush.
