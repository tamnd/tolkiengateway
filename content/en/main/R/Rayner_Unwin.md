**Rayner Stephens Unwin** [CBE](/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire") ([23 December](/wiki/23_December) [1925](/wiki/1925) – [23 November](/wiki/23_November) [2000](/wiki/2000)) was chairman of the [George Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_and_Unwin "Allen and Unwin"). He was the son of Sir [Stanley Unwin](/wiki/Stanley_Unwin), co-founder of the publishing firm.

## Biography {#Biography}

Rayner was the second son of Sir [Stanley Unwin](/wiki/Stanley_Unwin) after <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Severn" title="wikipedia:David Severn">David</a>. His second cousin was Harold "Chris" Unwin, who later would serve in the Royal Air Force with [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) and would become close friends.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

He reviewed [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* at age 10 and without his praise of the book it might never have been published.

Tolkien appreciated young Rayner's taste and considered him a good critic. While still planning on a sequel to *The Hobbit* in [1938](/wiki/1938), he wanted to send him the drafts; being uncertain about the [first chapter](/wiki/A_Long-expected_Party "A Long-expected Party") of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, he thought Rayner's opinion would encourage him to follow the story.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Rayner did not enjoy the next chapters as they had too much "hobbit talk".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In the summer of [1944](/wiki/1944), Unwin was reading English at [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford) as a naval cadet.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the meantime, Tolkien resented that Stanley Unwin would not wait to publish his *[Silmarillion](/wiki/Silmarillion)*, and while writing *The Lord of the Rings* he hoped that both would be published together. He also hoped that he would reach an agreement with London publisher <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Collins (page does not exist)">Collins</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> The Unwins were concerned about the resulting size of *The Lord of the Rings*, while Tolkien insisted that it would not be cut. Tolkien also insisted that it belonged to the same saga as *The Silmarillion* and would not be published separately.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Asked his opinion on these issues by his father, Rayner wrote that despite Tolkien's connecting the two works, he felt that the *Lord of the Rings* could be a self-contained story even without *The Silmarillion*. He proposed to have Tolkien or one of his sons edit *The Lord of the Rings* accordingly, incorporating parts of *The Silmarillion* as needed; otherwise they could just publish *The Lord of the Rings* and reject *The Silmarillion* later. Although this letter was not intended to be seen by Tolkien, Stanley sent it,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> disappointing Tolkien, who eventually turned to Collins.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-9">[9]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxx</sup>

Ultimately Tolkien was not able to reach an agreement with Collins either. In late [1951](/wiki/1951), Rayner wrote to Tolkien, and again the [next year](/wiki/1952 "1952") enquiring about *[Errantry](/wiki/Errantry)* and his publishing process (*The Lord of the Rings* remained unpublished). They exchanged letters with kind and warm words, with Tolkien humbly acknowledging his poor behavior and expressing a desire for compromise.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Rayner expressed his interest in reading *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Silmarillion* again and promising to discuss the latter at a more appropriate time. This agreement seemed to end their dispute and enabled Tolkien to revise *The Lord of the Rings* once more.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-9">[9]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxxi</sup>

After reading the newly revised book, Rayner telegraphed his father in [November](/wiki/November) [1952](/wiki/1952) saying that *The Lord of the* *Rings* was the work of a genius but would be risky to publish. Stanley replied: "If you believe it is a work of genius, *then* you may lose a thousand pounds".<sup id="cite_ref-rc_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-9">[9]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxxii</sup>

While the book was being prepared for publication in the summer of [1953](/wiki/1953), Rayner discussed various details with Tolkien, such as the titles for each of the three volumes,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> as it was eventually decided. In his own suggestions, he erroneously proposed *The Lord of the Rings* applying to the first volume, without an overall title for the complete work. He also ultimately chose *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)* for the final volume (instead of *[The War of the Ring](/wiki/The_War_of_the_Ring)*), despite Tolkien thinking that it gave away the plot.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-9">[9]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxxiii-xxxiv</sup> That summer he also visited Tolkien in his house (obviously [76 Sandfield Road](/wiki/76_Sandfield_Road)), and the visit was so hasty that Tolkien neglected to offer him a refreshment.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Rayner Unwin and [Norman Davis](/wiki/Norman_Davis) (Tolkien's successor) planned to commemorate Tolkien's 70th birthday on [January 3](/wiki/January_3) [1963](/wiki/1963).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Tolkien's and Rayner's friendship remained deep, which Tolkien compared to that of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) and [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> At several points Tolkien asked Rayner to stop calling him "Professor", which gave an impression of pedantry, and call him "Ronald".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-l334_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l334-16">[16]</a></sup>

In [1968](/wiki/1968), Rayner Unwin followed in his father's footsteps as chairman of the publishing firm; Tolkien lamented that he didn't have enough free time to spend time together any more.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> On [28 March](/wiki/28_March) [1972](/wiki/1972) Tolkien received the [Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) at Buckingham Palace; Unwin honoured him with a dinner at the Garrick Club and his firm had arranged their ([John](/wiki/John_Tolkien "John Tolkien")'s and [Priscilla](/wiki/Priscilla_Tolkien "Priscilla Tolkien")'s) stay at Brown's Hotel in London.<sup id="cite_ref-l334_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l334-16">[16]</a></sup>

Rayner had a son, Merlin Unwin ([May](/wiki/May) [1954](/wiki/1954)) the "second Merlin" Tolkien knew, agreeing it was more appropriate a name than "[Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)";<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> and a daughter, Camilla Unwin, who in [1969](/wiki/1969) wrote to Tolkien asking about "the purpose of life" for a school project. Tolkien wrote a long letter, which in the same time was too short for that question.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

He retired in [1985](/wiki/1985) and passed away in 2000.
