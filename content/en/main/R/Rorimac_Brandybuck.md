**Rorimac Brandybuck**, also known as **Old Rory** and **Goldfather**, was a [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") and [Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland).

## History {#History}

Rorimac was the oldest son of [Gorbadoc Brandybuck](/wiki/Gorbadoc_Brandybuck). He married [Menegilda Goold](/wiki/Menegilda_Goold) and had two sons, [Saradoc](/wiki/Saradoc_Brandybuck "Saradoc Brandybuck") and [Merimac](/wiki/Merimac_Brandybuck "Merimac Brandybuck"). He became [Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland) after his father's death in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1363](/wiki/Third_Age_2963 "Third Age 2963").<sup id="cite_ref-Brandybuck_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brandybuck-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the deaths of his sister, [Primula Brandybuck](/wiki/Primula_Brandybuck) and her husband [Drogo Baggins](/wiki/Drogo_Baggins) in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1380](/wiki/Third_Age_2980 "Third Age 2980"), Rorimac took their son, [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) under his guardianship in [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall) for nine years. [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins), a kinsman of Drogo, often enjoyed Old Rory's hospitality, and eventually he proposed that Frodo could come with him to [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton).<sup id="cite_ref-party_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-party-2">[2]</a></sup>

Rorimac was present at [Bilbo's Farewell Party](/wiki/Bilbo%27s_Farewell_Party). After Bilbo's [disappearance](/wiki/Unseen "Unseen"), Rory was one of the first, if not the only, to suspect "Mad Baggins" was off on an adventure again, and told so to his [daughter-in-law](/wiki/Esmeralda_Took "Esmeralda Took") to quiet her shock; he expressed satisfaction that there was more food to feast with and promptly asked his nephew Frodo for more wine. As part of his departure, Bilbo left him a dozen of bottles of [Old Winyards](/wiki/Old_Winyards), and as soon as he tasted the first one, Rory voted Bilbo a capital fellow.<sup id="cite_ref-party_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-party-2">[2]</a></sup>

Rory died six years later, passing on the title of Master of Buckland to his eldest son [Saradoc](/wiki/Saradoc_Brandybuck "Saradoc Brandybuck").<sup id="cite_ref-Brandybuck_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brandybuck-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) noted that the names of the Bucklanders had a vaguely [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) style.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-3">[3]</a></sup>

*Rory* is a northern Irish form of the [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) name *Rhuadri* or *Rhuaidri*, which means "red" or "ruddy". An ancient Irish hero wasd called Rory O'More. Rory or as a full name *madadh ruadh* ("red dog") is the common name for the fox in many Higland tales where the fox is portrayed as shrewd. This is a fitting name for Rory Brandybuck, because we are told about him that "neither age nor an enormous dinner had clouded his wits".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
