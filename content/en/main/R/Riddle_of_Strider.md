The **Riddle of Strider**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was a poem that was written by [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) in honour of his friend, the [Dúnadan](../D/Dúnedain.md) when he first revealed his true identity as [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md).

## History {#History}

The poem is first given in [the letter](../G/Gandalf's_letter.md) left for [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) by [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) in [Bree](../B/Bree.md). In that letter, it appears as part of a postscript reminding Frodo to make sure that the "[Strider](<../S/Strider_(Aragorn).md>)" he meets is "the real Strider". The poem thus appears in that context as a means of identifying Aragorn. Aragorn indeed later quotes the first two lines, not knowing the poem is in the letter, and this does help to confirm his identity.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Bilbo himself recites the poem at the [Council of Elrond](../C/Council_of_Elrond.md) when [Boromir](../B/Boromir.md) expresses doubts about Aragorn's claim to be the [Heir of Isildur](../H/Heir_of_Isildur.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Poem {#Poem}

<dl><dd><i>All that is gold does not glitter,</i><br/><i>Not all those who wander are lost;</i><br/><i>The old that is strong does not wither,</i><br/><i>Deep roots are not reached by the frost.</i><br/><i>From the ashes a fire shall be woken,</i><br/><i>A light from the shadows shall spring;</i><br/><i>Renewed shall be <a href="../A/Andúril.md" title="Andúril">blade that was broken</a>,</i><br/><i>The crownless again shall be <a class="mw-redirect" href="../K/Kings_of_Gondor.md" title="Kings of Gondor">king</a>.</i></dd></dl>

## Analysis {#Analysis}

The first line is clearly a re-working of the traditional proverb "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_that_glitters_is_not_gold" title="wikipedia:All that glitters is not gold">All that glitters is not gold</a>", shifting its meaning. The second line, perhaps one of the lines from Tolkien's work most often quoted out of context,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> refers to Aragorn's travels. The third and fourth lines concern Aragorn's heritage, while the remaining lines describe his destiny. The "blade that was broken" refers to the [Shards of Narsil](../S/Shards_of_Narsil.md), which is indeed remade for Aragorn.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1981: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1981 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Gandalf reads out the Riddle when Frodo reads the letter in Bree.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game).md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Although the letter is read aloud, the Riddle is absent. Strider simply tells his name after Frodo reads the words &#34;His name is Aragorn&#34;. Later, in Rivendell, Frodo uses the line &#34;the old that is strong does not wither&#34; to refer to Bilbo, and Arwen says &#34;From the ashes a fire shall be woken&#34; when she gives Aragorn Andúril.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Return_of_the_King.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The last four lines are recited by <a href="../A/Arwen.md" title="Arwen">Arwen</a> upon her return to <a href="Rivendell.md" title="Rivendell">Rivendell</a> as she stands before the Shards of Narsil.  She later encourages <a href="../E/Elrond.md" title="Elrond">Elrond</a> to re-forge the sword, and presumably to deliver it to Aragorn.</dd></dl>

<b>2022: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Rings_of_Power.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><b>September 23: <i><a href="../P/Partings.md" title="Partings">Partings</a></i>:</b>
<dl><dd>The line &#34;<i>That not all who wonder or wander are lost</i>&#34; is a line within the <a href="../T/This_Wandering_Day.md" title="This Wandering Day">song</a> that was sung in the <a href="Rhovanion_Hills.md" title="Rhovanion Hills">Rhovanion Hills</a> by <a href="../P/Poppy_Proudfellow.md" title="Poppy Proudfellow">Poppy Proudfellow</a> during the <a href="../H/Harfoots.md" title="Harfoots">Harfoot</a> migration from the <a href="../N/Norfield_Glen.md" title="Norfield Glen">Norfield Glen</a> to the <a href="../G/Grove.md" title="Grove">Grove</a>.</dd></dl></dd></dl>
