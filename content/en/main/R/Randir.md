<i><b>randir</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-PE_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE-1">[1]</a></sup> (or <i><b>randír</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-VT_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-2">[2]</a></sup>) is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word meaning "wandering man, pilgrim"<sup id="cite_ref-PE_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE-1">[1]</a></sup> or "wanderer".<sup id="cite_ref-VT_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In the manuscript "[Word, Phrases and Passages](/wiki/Parma_Eldalamberon_17 "Parma Eldalamberon 17")", Sindarin *randir* consists of a derivative of the root [RĂNĂ](/wiki/RAN "RAN") ("wander") + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ndir&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ndir (page does not exist)">ndir</a></i> ("man").<sup id="cite_ref-PE_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the manuscript "[The Rivers and Beacon-hills of Gondor](/wiki/The_Rivers_and_Beacon-hills_of_Gondor)", Sindarin *randír* derives from the [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [RAN](/wiki/RAN).<sup id="cite_ref-VT_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Examples {#Examples}

- <i><a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Mith<b>randir</b></a></i>
- <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Aerandir" title="Aerandir">Ae<b>randir</b></a></i>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) may have been inspired by the old, and now obsolete French verb <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/randir" title="wiktionary:randir">randir</a></i>, meaning "to walk very fast".
