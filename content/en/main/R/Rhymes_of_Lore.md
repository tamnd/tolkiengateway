The **Rhymes of Lore** were small poems that refer to ancient events of history.

## History {#History}

On his way to [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) mumbled some rhymes in many languages. [Pippin](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) was able to pick up a few lines from one about them:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Tall ships and <a class="mw-redirect" href="../H/High_Men.md" title="High Men">tall kings</a><br/>Three times three,<br/>What brought they from the <a href="../N/Númenor.md" title="Númenor">foundered land</a> <br/>Over the <a href="../B/Belegaer.md" title="Belegaer">flowing sea</a>? <br/><a href="../S/Seven_Stars.md" title="Seven Stars">Seven stars</a> and <a href="../P/Palantíri.md" title="Palantíri">seven stones</a> <br/>And one <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../W/White_Tree.md" title="White Tree">white tree</a>.
</p>
</blockquote>

When Pippin inquired about it, Gandalf suggested that [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) may have forgotten them all. To this, Pippin told him that they have their own rhymes which Gandalf would presumably not be interested in hearing,<sup id="cite_ref-gandalf_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gandalf-1">[1]</a></sup> such as [Sam's](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) rhyme of <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../O/Oliphaunt.md" title="Oliphaunt">Oliphaunt</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> After this, Gandalf explained that this particular rhyme refers to the [Palantíri](../P/Palantíri.md) that were made long ago by the [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md), possibly by [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) himself, and "*they were remembered only in a rhyme of lore among the [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md)*" of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-gandalf_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gandalf-1">[1]</a></sup>

The [Long List of the Ents](../L/Long_List_of_the_Ents.md) may also be an example of a Rhyme of Lore.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The Rhyme that Gandalf muttered had been running through Tolkien's mind for some time, even before having thought of the *palantíri* or what the "seven stones" were. When finally he wrote about [Wormtongue](../W/Wormtongue.md) hurling the [Orthanc-stone](../O/Orthanc-stone.md), he made the connection.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Robert Foster](Robert_Foster.md) theorized that the Rhymes of Lore were designed as a medium to aid in the retention of various ancient facts.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-3">[3]</a></sup> They are comparable to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomic_poetry#Medieval_and_early_modern_gnomic_literature" title="wikipedia:Gnomic poetry">Gnomic Verses</a> that preserve important ideas of the Anglo-Saxon society. Their rhyme make them easy to remember.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

A musical version of this poem was recorded by the [Tolkien Ensemble](../T/Tolkien_Ensemble.md) on their album *[A Night in Rivendell](../A/A_Night_in_Rivendell.md)*.
