The **Rhymes of Lore** were small poems that refer to ancient events of history.

## History {#History}

On his way to [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) mumbled some rhymes in many languages. [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") was able to pick up a few lines from one about them:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Tall ships and <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/High_Men" title="High Men">tall kings</a><br/>Three times three,<br/>What brought they from the <a href="/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor" title="Númenor">foundered land</a> <br/>Over the <a href="/wiki/Belegaer" title="Belegaer">flowing sea</a>? <br/><a href="/wiki/Seven_Stars" title="Seven Stars">Seven stars</a> and <a href="/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri" title="Palantíri">seven stones</a> <br/>And one <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/White_Tree" title="White Tree">white tree</a>.
</p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

When Pippin inquired about it, Gandalf suggested that [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) may have forgotten them all. To this, Pippin told him that they have their own rhymes which Gandalf would presumably not be interested in hearing,<sup id="cite_ref-gandalf_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gandalf-1">[1]</a></sup> such as [Sam's](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") rhyme of <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Oliphaunt" title="Oliphaunt">Oliphaunt</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> After this, Gandalf explained that this particular rhyme refers to the [Palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri) that were made long ago by the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor), possibly by [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) himself, and "*they were remembered only in a rhyme of lore among the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain)*" of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor).<sup id="cite_ref-gandalf_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gandalf-1">[1]</a></sup>

The [Long List of the Ents](/wiki/Long_List_of_the_Ents) may also be an example of a Rhyme of Lore.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The Rhyme that Gandalf muttered had been running through Tolkien's mind for some time, even before having thought of the *palantíri* or what the "seven stones" were. When finally he wrote about [Wormtongue](/wiki/Wormtongue) hurling the [Orthanc-stone](/wiki/Orthanc-stone), he made the connection.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Robert Foster](/wiki/Robert_Foster) theorized that the Rhymes of Lore were designed as a medium to aid in the retention of various ancient facts.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-3">[3]</a></sup> They are comparable to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomic_poetry#Medieval_and_early_modern_gnomic_literature" title="wikipedia:Gnomic poetry">Gnomic Verses</a> that preserve important ideas of the Anglo-Saxon society. Their rhyme make them easy to remember.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

A musical version of this poem was recorded by the [Tolkien Ensemble](/wiki/Tolkien_Ensemble) on their album *[A Night in Rivendell](/wiki/A_Night_in_Rivendell)*.
