**Rog** was the [Gnome](/wiki/Gnome)-lord of the [people of the Hammer of Wrath](/wiki/House_of_the_Hammer_of_Wrath "House of the Hammer of Wrath") in Gondolin according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.

## History {#History}

Rog was a blacksmith, and chief of the [folk of the Hammer of Wrath](/wiki/House_of_the_Hammer_of_Wrath "House of the Hammer of Wrath"). He was considered the strongest of the [Noldoli](/wiki/Noldoli), and was like considered third greatest in valour.<sup id="cite_ref-LT2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT2-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:174</sup>

During the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin), he led his people against the attack of [Balrogs](/wiki/Balrogs) and [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) in the northern gate. When the gate was broken, he stirred his kindred against the Balrogs with words of passion, and though many were slain, they slew a number of the fiery creatures "that... was a marvel and dread to the hosts of [Melko](/wiki/Melko), for ere that day never had any Balrogs been slain by the hand of Elves or Men". Seeing that they had no way back, Rog rushed his men outside the city, where the folk of the Hammer were hemmed in, and every one of them perished, including him.<sup id="cite_ref-LT2_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT2-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:178–9</sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Rog*, in earlier writings *Rôg*, seems curious as a name for an elf-lord of Gondolin: no other one-syllable names appear in other writings.

The meaning or etymology of the name is unclear, and in the context of the later works it would mean "Demon" (cf. *[raug](/wiki/Raug "Raug")*), in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), as an element in *[Balrog](/wiki/Balrog)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> But the early *[Gnomish Lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon)* gives an adjective: *rôg*, *rog* ("doughty, strong"), related with *arog* ("swift, rushing") and *raug* of the same meaning.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) form of his name was *Rōka*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Rog's death "without the walls" is mentioned in the *[Quenta Noldorinwa](/wiki/Quenta_Noldorinwa)*,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> which was the main text used by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) for his edition of the chapter "[Of Tuor and the Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Of_Tuor_and_the_Fall_of_Gondolin)" within *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. However, Christopher removed the mention of this character, explaining that his father would likely not have used that out of phase name.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
