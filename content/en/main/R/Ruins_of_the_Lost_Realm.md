<i><b>Ruins of the Lost Realm</b></i> is the first expansion of the second edition of the [*The One Ring* Roleplaying Game](</wiki/The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game)> "The One Ring (role-playing game)"). A version for [The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying), titled <i><b>Ruins of Eriador</b></i>, was also released.

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Eriador, the lone-lands described in The Lord of the Rings™, comes alive as never before in this supplement for The One Ring™, the official tabletop roleplaying game based on the works of J.R.R. Tolkien. A region often described as deserted becomes a crucible for adventure, offering many hours of gameplay.
</p>
<ul><li><b>Chapter  1: Fog Over Eriador</b> offers a description of the regions that once were part of the kingdom of Arnor, with a focus on the city of Tharbad.</li>
<li><b>Chapter 2: A Gathering Storm</b> provides the Loremaster with a set of narrative elements aimed at building a possible future for the land of Eriador as a whole, including the description of many looming threats, Loremaster characters and their agendas.</li>
<li><b>Chapter 3: Landmarks</b> adds twelve sites of interest in the style of the one presented in the core volume. These locations depict both ‘historical’ sites mentioned in The Lord of the Rings™ or The Hobbit™ and others that have been created specifically for the game</li></ul>
</blockquote>

## Plot {#Plot}

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2965](/wiki/Third_Age_2965 "Third Age 2965"), [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) sent a group of [Black Númenórean](/wiki/Black_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Black Númenóreans") spies on a mission to Eriador. They were tasked with finding [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) and setting up local agents to report on the travels of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), especially concerning the [Grey Havens](/wiki/Grey_Havens). Should they succeed, then Sauron would grant them all of former [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) after his final victory. The spies departed from [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar) on the Kathu­phaz­gân, a ship of immense size with a crew of 500 sailors and slaves. Though required to return to Umbar every few months for provisions, the ship carried a massive treasury to bribe locals. The primary spies were Zoril, an impatient warlord who would rather become queen as soon as possible, and the sorceress Usapthon, who was fanatically loyal to her deity Sauron. A single orc, Lug, also travelled with them, and he was in charge of the enslaved crew.<sup id="cite_ref-black_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-black-1">[1]</a></sup>

The spies presented themselves as refugees in [Lond Daer](/wiki/Lond_Daer) later that year. In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_2967&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 2967 (page does not exist)">2967</a>, they recruited the local merchant Johan Fleet. Many more operatives arrived from Umbar the next year, posing as his caravan guards. [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman)'s agents became aware of the spies' efforts, capturing some for questioning, while Usapthon clashed with Saruman himself for control of the weather. As a result, in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2969](/wiki/Third_Age_2969 "Third Age 2969"), Usapthon departed on a pilgrimage to [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar), and tried to raise evil spirits there. Her journey was hindered by the efforts of Saruman's agents. Meanwhile, in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_2970&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 2970 (page does not exist)">2970</a>, Zoril arranged for [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) to be taken over by a new ally of hers. The Black Númenóreans attacked [Swanfleet](/wiki/Swanfleet) afterwards, in order to secure Tharbad. In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_2971&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 2971 (page does not exist)">2971</a>, Usapthon returned from Angmar and sought to rouse the [Barrow-wights](/wiki/Barrow-wights) into action with her new knowledge.<sup id="cite_ref-black_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-black-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The rebuilt city of [Lond Daer](/wiki/Lond_Daer) fell in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2972](/wiki/Third_Age_2972 "Third Age 2972"), either due to an attack from the Kathu­phaz­gân, or due to Dunlending raids.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup> Over the following year, the Kathu­phaz­gân was dragged up the Greyflood, close to Tharbad. By [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2975](/wiki/Third_Age_2975 "Third Age 2975"), the spies' scouts had finally located Rivendell, though Elrond commanded the river to drown most of them. A single survivor remained, and while Zoril attempted to find the last scout, the thieves of Tharbad revolted against the Black Númenóreans out of greed for imagined treasures.<sup id="cite_ref-black_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-black-1">[1]</a></sup> Dunlending raids grew bolder in the meantime, ultimately leading to the looting of the Dwarven fortress of [Harmelt](/wiki/Harmelt) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2975](/wiki/Third_Age_2975 "Third Age 2975").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Reception {#Reception}

In a review for [Other Minds 26](/wiki/Other_Minds_26), [Thomas Morwinsky](/wiki/Thomas_Morwinsky) praised the book for its production quality and the flexibility offered by its adventures, though he noted that the choice to present as Tharbad still inhabited in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2965](/wiki/Third_Age_2965 "Third Age 2965") for gameplay reasons marked a break from the first edition's stricter adherence to Tolkien's works. He found that to only be an actual issue if the game master wants to stick closely to both the timeline and canon of the *Lord of the Rings*, given that Tharbad's description works well for earlier years. The basic layout of the city reminded him of *[Thieves of Tharbad](/wiki/Thieves_of_Tharbad)*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>
