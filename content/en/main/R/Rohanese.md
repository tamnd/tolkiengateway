**Rohanese** was a [Mannish](/wiki/Mannish) [language](/wiki/Languages "Languages") that was spoken by the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan).

## History {#History}

The Rohan language is derived from the language of the [Éothéod](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od), who were among the [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen) and was related to other Northmen languages, such as those of [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion), [Esgaroth](/wiki/Lake-town "Lake-town"), and [Dale](/wiki/Dale).

The [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) before their [Wandering Days](/wiki/Wandering_Days) in the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin) had contact with that people and their languages had many words in common. For example the Rohirrim had retained the legend of the being known as *[kûd-dûkan](/wiki/K%C3%BBd-d%C3%BBkan "Kûd-dûkan")* (translated as *hol-bytla*), a term which became *[kuduk](/wiki/Kuduk "Kuduk")* by the Hobbits, the name they had for themselves.

Many archaic [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") names bear similarities to Rohan's, since the ancestors of [The Shire](/wiki/The_Shire) hobbits lived on the upper reaches of the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), close to the ancestors of the Rohirrim, and there was apparently a good deal of linguistic cross-fertilisation.

Despite its relation to [Westron](/wiki/Westron), the Rohan language was not intelligible to its speakers. [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) was unable to understand the songs, however he noted that the language is like the land itself: rich and rolling in part, and else hard and stern as the mountains.

## Structure {#Structure}

Words in the language of Rohan often include the element *lô-/loho*, which means "horse".<sup id="cite_ref-Languages_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Languages-2">[2]</a></sup> *[Lōgrad](/wiki/L%C5%8Dgrad)* means "Rohan" or "[Horse-mark](/wiki/Riddermark "Riddermark")".<sup id="cite_ref-Modernized_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Modernized-3">[3]</a></sup> <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Loht%C3%BBr&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lohtûr (page does not exist)">Lohtûr</a></i> means "[Horse-fols](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od "Éothéod")" or "Horse-land". The latter shows the element *[tûr](/wiki/T%C3%BBr "Tûr")* also seen in the name <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=T%C3%BBrac&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tûrac (page does not exist)">Tûrac</a></i> "[People-king](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden "Théoden")".<sup id="cite_ref-Languages_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Languages-2">[2]</a></sup>

The word *trahan* means "burrow".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Translation_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Name {#Name}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) consistently, with only one exception, used the name **Rohan** for the name of the language of the Rohirrim in his notes on the [Nomenclature of The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings") that he made to assist translators in translating the book into other languages, which he had almost completed on 2 January 1967.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> J.R.R. Tolkien also used the name *Rohan* for the name of the language of the Rohirrim in a manuscript for an earlier draft version of Appendix F of The Lord of the Rings.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Also [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), in one instance, refers to the language of Rohan as "Rohan".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

J.R.R. Tolkien used the adjective **Rohanese** once in an [essay about the names of rivers](/wiki/The_Rivers_and_Beacon-hills_of_Gondor "The Rivers and Beacon-hills of Gondor"), which he wrote after 23 June 1969<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> but it is not clear by the context if the word is the name of a language, or simply an adjective. He used the word Rohanese twice, in the essay about the Eldarin bases related to light and darkness, which was written on Allen &amp; Unwin scrap paper dating from the late 1960s.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> and once in his Nomenclature of The Lord of the Rings.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

J.R.R. tolkien used the term "language of Rohan" in Appendix F of *The Lord of the Rings*,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rohan_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rohan-13">[13]</a></sup> the term "language of the Rohirrim" in the Prologue<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> and in the chapter The King of the Golden Hall<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> and the term "language of the Mark in the chapter The Road to Isengard<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>.

[Robert Foster](/wiki/Robert_Foster) in *[The Complete Guide to Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Complete_Guide_to_Middle-earth)* uses the name **Rohirric**, which has stuck among the students of Tolkien's languages. Perhaps it was modelled on "Rohirrim" and the ending *-ic* of "Adûnaic". [Christopher Gilson](/wiki/Christopher_Gilson) uses "Rohirric" in the List of Abbreviations to "[Words, Phrases &amp; Passages in *The Lord of the Rings*](/wiki/Parma_Eldalamberon_17 "Parma Eldalamberon 17")",<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> as well as [Helge Fauskanger](/wiki/Helge_Fauskanger) in [Ardalambion](/wiki/Ardalambion).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

[Lisa Star](/wiki/Lisa_Star) in [Tyalië Tyelelliéva 17](/wiki/Tyali%C3%AB_Tyelelli%C3%A9va_17) claimed that she saw the name **Rohirian** written in a small note at the bottom of a draft of [The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings) appendix on languages in [Marquette University](/wiki/Marquette_University).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="/wiki/Old_English" title="Old English">Old English</a></b></div></div>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r398211">.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote{position:relative;margin:0.5rem 0;padding:0.75rem 1rem;box-shadow:var(--tg-border-color-base)0px 0px 0px 1px inset;border-radius:var(--tg-border-radius-medium);background:var(--tg-color-surface-2);font-size:var(--tg-font-size-small);line-height:var(--tg-line-height-xx-small);display:flex;gap:0.5rem;align-items:center}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote-icon{display:flex;align-items:center}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote-icon .mw-file-element{display:block}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote-icon .mw-file-element[src$=".svg"]{filter:var(--filter-invert);opacity:0.5}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote.tg-hatnote--red{background:var(--background-color-destructive-subtle);color:var(--color-emphasized)}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote.tg-hatnote--yellow{background:var(--background-color-warning-subtle);color:var(--color-emphasized)}.mw-parser-output .tg-hatnote.tg-hatnote--green{background:var(--background-color-success-subtle);color:var(--color-emphasized)}</style>

Tolkien turned names in the Rohan language into modernized Old English names.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> Even modernized names show a strong Anglo-Saxon influence. Old English was supposed to render an archaic form of [Westron](/wiki/Westron), which was supposedly rendered by Modern English. This solution occurred to Tolkien in 1942, when he was searching for an explanation of the Eddaic name of the dwarves already published in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

Some words show the plural ending "-as", as were Old English nouns of the strong-masculine declension.

The Rohirrim used the Germanic patronymic "-ing". They called themselves the **[Eorlingas](/wiki/Eorlingas)**, and [Beorn](/wiki/Beorn)'s people were the **[Beornings](/wiki/Beornings)**, Scyld's people were the Scyldingas in Norse and Anglo-Saxon mythology.

[Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden) was referred to as "Théoden King", rather than "King Théoden", just as Scandinavian and Anglo-Saxon kings had the word "konungr"/"cyning" ("king") added after their names, e.g. *Hervarðar konungr*, rather than before.

Some Old English names that render Rohirric words include:

- **[Éothéod](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od):** from "eoh" ("war-horse") and "þeod" ("folk", "people", "nation")
- **[Gríma](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma):** possibly from "grima" ("mask", "helmet", "ghost")
- **[Eorl](/wiki/Eorl):** from "eorl" ("nobleman")
- **[Théodred](/wiki/Th%C3%A9odred):** from "þeod" ("folk", "people", "nation") and "ræd" ("counsel")
- **[Elfwine](/wiki/Elfwine):** from [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) ("[Elf-friend](/wiki/Elf-friend)")

### Translation {#Translation}

As [Westron](/wiki/Westron) is rendered in the novels with English, the language of Rohan is translated into [Old English](/wiki/Old_English). This is because [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") tried to reproduce for English readers its archaic flavour in relationship to the Common Speech. Westron is an amalgamated language which, although deriving from [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), was formed from the languages of the [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men), much like the English language with many influences from [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_language" title="wikipedia:Norman language">Norman</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

However, the relationship between the two pairs of languages is not identical: Old English is the direct ancestor of modern English, but Rohan was not the direct ancestor of Westron, since the latter derives from [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic).

In some cases, Tolkien did not provide genuine Old English words, but rather modernized the forms and spellings of Old English words,<sup id="cite_ref-Modernized_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Modernized-3">[3]</a></sup> because the Hobbits modernized the names that they heard in the language of Rohan if the were made of elements that they recognized or if they were similar to place-names in the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-Rohan_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rohan-13">[13]</a></sup> Such names are:

- [Éothain](/wiki/%C3%89othain) instead of *eohþeg(e)n*<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup>
- Firien instead of *Firgen* in the names [Halifirien](/wiki/Halifirien), [Firienfeld](/wiki/Firienfeld) and [Firienholt](/wiki/Firienholt)<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>
- [Elfwine](/wiki/Elfwine) instead of *Ælfwine*<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>
- [Gamling](/wiki/Gamling) instead of *Gameling*<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup>
- [Greyhame](/wiki/Greyhame) instead of *greg-hama*<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup>
- [Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma")<sup id="cite_ref-Rohan_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rohan-13">[13]</a></sup> instead of *wyrm-tunge*<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>
- [Woses](/wiki/Woses) instead of *wasan*; also isolated from the English term *Woodwoses*.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>
- [Dunharrow](/wiki/Dunharrow)<sup id="cite_ref-Rohan_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rohan-13">[13]</a></sup> instead of *Dunhaerg*<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup>
- [Entwade](/wiki/Entwade), [Entwash](/wiki/Entwash), [Entwood](/wiki/Entwood) instead of *Entwaed, Entwaesc, Entwudu*<sup id="cite_ref-Entwade_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Entwade-33">[33]</a></sup>
- [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras) and instead of <i>Eodoras<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup></i>
- [Snowbourn](/wiki/Snowbourn)<sup id="cite_ref-Rohan_13-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rohan-13">[13]</a></sup> instead of *snawburna*<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup>
- [Shadowfax](/wiki/Shadowfax) instead of *Sceadu-fæx*<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup>
- [Stoningland](/wiki/Stoningland) instead of *Staning-(land)*<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup>
- [Upbourn](/wiki/Upbourn) instead of *Upburnan*<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup>

The reason for the use of modernized forms of those names by speakers of [Westron](/wiki/Westron) was that they were said to be intelligible for them; [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) were familiar with the place-names of Rohan (like *Entwade*),<sup id="cite_ref-Entwade_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Entwade-33">[33]</a></sup> while Hobbits recognized some common elements with [their dialect](/wiki/Hobbitish "Hobbitish").<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-5">[5]</a></sup>
