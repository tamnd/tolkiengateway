The **Rhimdath** or **Rushdown** was a short, early tributary of the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) River.

## Course {#Course}

It flowed from the its source on the eastern side of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) southeastward for about 25 miles until its confluence with an unnamed tributary that also flowed down from the Misty Mountains. The Rushdown then continued 25 miles southeastward until its confluence with the Anduin roughly halfway between the [Langwell](/wiki/Langwell)-[Greylin](/wiki/Greylin) confluence and the [Carrock](/wiki/Carrock).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Given the name "Rushdown" it probably had a steep gradient.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The river Rhimdath is unnamed on the [Map of Wilderland](/wiki/Map_of_Wilderland) in the *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, *[The Third Map of The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Third_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, the [General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth) and [The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age](/wiki/The_West_of_Middle-earth_at_the_End_of_the_Third_Age) map.

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) added the names "*Rhimdath*" and "Rushdown" in pencil on the [Map of Wilderland](/wiki/Map_of_Wilderland) in one of his copies of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*.<sup id="cite_ref-RS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RS-2">[2]</a></sup> He also added the names "R.Rushdown" and "(Rhimdad)" on [The First Map of The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The name "Rushdown" also appears on a late map of the northern part of Middle-earth drawn by J.R.R. Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Rhimdath* is a [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) name. The first element in *Rhimdath* is related to [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Rhib-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rhib- (page does not exist)">rhib-</a></i>, <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Rhimp&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rhimp (page does not exist)">rhimp</a></i>, *rhimmo* ("to flow like a \[?torrent\]"), derived from the [stem](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [RIP-](/wiki/RIP "RIP") ("rush, fly, fling").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> As the stem [DAT-](/wiki/DAT "DAT") is glossed as "fall down",<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> it is possible that the second element in *Rhimdath* is a derivative of this stem.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2021: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Originating in the <a href="/wiki/Misty_Mountains" title="Misty Mountains">Misty Mountains</a>, Rhimdath forms a large mountain lake before dropping below and continuing its flow east. The lake is home to &#34;Lyndelby&#34;, a village of <a href="/wiki/Fallohides" title="Fallohides">Fallohides</a> that remains isolated from the larger world and is protected by the <a href="/wiki/Great_Eagles" title="Great Eagles">Great Eagles</a> who maintain its secret.</dd></dl>
