The **Ringló** was a cold and swift river<sup id="cite_ref-NM_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rivers_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rivers-2">[2]</a></sup> in the central regions of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-FRmap_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRmap-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Course {#Course}

The Ringló arose in a valley in the eastern part of [Lamedon](/wiki/Lamedon)<sup id="cite_ref-RKmap_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKmap-4">[4]</a></sup> in a high snowfield , which fed an icy pool that spread into a shallow lake at the snowmelting season.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rivers_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rivers-2">[2]</a></sup> From this source it flowed south-west for about 50 miles to [Ethring](/wiki/Ethring) where the road from the [Hill of Erech](/wiki/Hill_of_Erech) to [Linhir](/wiki/Linhir) crossed the river.<sup id="cite_ref-RKmap_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKmap-4">[4]</a></sup> The Ringló continued south-west for another 50 miles to its confluence with the river [Ciril](/wiki/Ciril) and then flowed southwest for another 50 miles to its confluence with the river [Morthond](/wiki/Morthond) upstream of [Edhellond](/wiki/Edhellond) before emptying near [Cobas Haven](/wiki/Cobas_Haven)<sup id="cite_ref-Map2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map2-5">[5]</a></sup> into the [Bay of Belfalas](/wiki/Bay_of_Belfalas).<sup id="cite_ref-FRmap_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRmap-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Near the confluence of the Ringló and the Morthond was the ancient Elf-haven of Edhellond. This refuge had been founded at the beginning of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) by a remnant of [Elves](/wiki/Elves) from [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

After the founding of Gondor, the Ringló served as the boundary between [Anfalas](/wiki/Anfalas) to the west and [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas) to the east.<sup id="cite_ref-RKmap_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKmap-4">[4]</a></sup>

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) on [9 March](/wiki/9_March) [3019](/wiki/TA "TA")<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> [Dervorin](/wiki/Dervorin), the son of the lord of [Ringló Vale](/wiki/Ringl%C3%B3_Vale) arrived in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) on foot with a company of three hundred men to aid in the city's defence.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> On [10 March](/wiki/10_March) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019")<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli), [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas), the [Grey Company](/wiki/Grey_Company), and the [Army of the Dead](/wiki/Oathbreakers "Oathbreakers") crossed the Ringló on their way from [Erech](/wiki/Erech) to [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Ringló* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, which means "Chillflood".<sup id="cite_ref-NM_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rivers_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rivers-2">[2]</a></sup> It its composed of the elements *ring* ("cold", "chill")<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> and *ló* ("flood").<sup id="cite_ref-NM_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rivers_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rivers-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Its name referred to the river's source, a high snowfield, which fed an icy pool that spread into a shallow lake at the snowmelting season.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rivers_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rivers-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The course and placement of the Ringló varied greatly as [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. In the [First Map of The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The First Map of The Lord of the Rings") the Ringló flowed to the sea and the Morthond was set well west of it (and [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth) was far to the west of both).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> When he developed Outline VI for Book V in *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)* he drew a map that showed the Morthond east of the Ringló and the Ringló mostly flowed south to the Sea.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> When Tolkien made the detailed [Second Map](/wiki/The_Second_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Second Map of The Lord of the Rings") there were four rivers – [Calenhir](/wiki/Calenhir), Morthond, Kiril (later Ciril), and Ringló – that flowed independently until they all met just before exiting into Cobas Haven.<sup id="cite_ref-Map2_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map2-5">[5]</a></sup>

In two earlier versions of a note about the element *ló* in the name *Gwathló*, it is mentioned that the Ringló formed marshes or a fenland after its confluence with the river Morthond as the reason for the element ló in the name Ringló. The second revised version mentioned that the river was named Ringnen after its source and later renamed Ringló, because it formed a fenland near its confluence with the Morthond. J.R.R. Tolkien later struck out the fenland as the explanation for the lement *ló* and stated in the final version that there is no record of any swamps or marsh in the course of the Ringló, but retained that the river was named after its source and explained it as mentioned above.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rivers_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rivers-2">[2]</a></sup>
