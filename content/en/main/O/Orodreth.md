**Orodreth** was a lord of the [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md) and a dynast of the [House of Finarfin](../H/House_of_Finarfin.md). He served as the warden of [Minas Tirith](<../M/Minas_Tirith_(Beleriand).md>) under his uncle [Finrod](../F/Finrod.md) until the fortress was overrun by [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md).

After Finrod’s death, Orodreth was proclaimed [King of Nargothrond](../K/King_of_Nargothrond.md) but was killed during the [fall of the city](../F/Fall_of_Nargothrond.md). He was the son of the Noldorin lord [Angrod](../A/Angrod.md) and the father of [Finduilas](../F/Finduilas.md) and [Gil-galad](../G/Gil-galad.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Shibboleth_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shibboleth-1">[1]</a></sup>

Orodreth was noted as being among the [Lambengolmor](../L/Lambengolmor.md), "the greatest, kings, princes and warriors" among the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-Shibboleth_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shibboleth-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:note 23</sup>

## History {#History}

### Early History {#Early_History}

He was born in [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md) sometime during the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md). His name in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) was <i><b>Artaresto</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-5">[5]</a></sup>

During the [Flight of the Noldor](../F/Flight_of_the_Noldor.md), Orodreth spoke softly and sought to calm the Noldor to reflect before taking the decision to leave [Aman](../A/Aman.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-6">[6]</a></sup> After the return of the Noldor to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) and the completion of [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md), Orodreth's uncle [Finrod](../F/Finrod.md) gave the fortress of [Minas Tirith](<../M/Minas_Tirith_(Beleriand).md>) to Orodreth's keeping.<sup id="cite_ref-Beleriand_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beleriand-7">[7]</a></sup>

Orodreth held the fortress of Minas Tirith in the vale of [Sirion](../S/Sirion.md) until shortly after the [Dagor Bragollach](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md) [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md), lieutenant of [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md), overran the isle, took Minas Tirith by storm and turned it into [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](../T/Tol-in-Gaurhoth.md), the Isle of Werewolves, in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [457](../F/First_Age_457.md). Orodreth had to flee from Minas Tirith south to [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Fingolfin_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fingolfin-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Arrival of Beren {#Arrival_of_Beren}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/E/Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/e2/Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg/225px-Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="225" height="350" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/e2/Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg/338px-Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/e2/Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg/450px-Ebe_Kastein_-_King_Orodreth.jpg 2x" data-file-width="923" data-file-height="1435"/></a><figcaption><i>King Orodreth</i> by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Ebe_Kastein.md" title="Category:Images by Ebe Kastein">Ebe Kastein</a></figcaption></figure>

When [Beren](../B/Beren.md), son of [Barahir](../B/Barahir.md) came to Nargothrond in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [465](../F/First_Age_465.md), Finrod, the [King of Nargothrond](../K/King_of_Nargothrond.md), decided to accompany him on his [quest for the Silmaril](../Q/Quest_for_the_Silmaril.md), fulfilling his oath. However, [Celegorm](../C/Celegorm.md) and [Curufin](../C/Curufin.md), the [Sons of Fëanor](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md), were also at Nargothrond at the time, and persuaded almost all the people of Nargothrond to refuse to accompany Finrod on the quest for the Silmaril so that only [Edrahil](../E/Edrahil.md) and ten companions agreed to go with Finrod and Beren. Finrod gave his [crown](../C/Crown_of_Nargothrond.md) to Orodreth to rule Nargothrond as a regent in his absence. With Finrod gone, Celegorm and Curufin further swayed the hearts of the people of Nargothrond, and Orodreth had no power to withstand them. The ultimate goal of the brothers was to take control of all Elf-kingdoms before then seeking the Silmarils. When news came that Finrod had been killed and that Tol-in-Gaurhoth had been destroyed, the sons of Fëanor were shamed and fell from power, as the people cried out that the maiden [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md) had done what the sons of Fëanor dared not. Orodreth returned to power and expelled Celegorm and Curufin from Nargothrond.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-9">[9]</a></sup>

### Arrival of Túrin {#Arrival_of_Túrin}

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [487](../F/First_Age_487.md), word of the inspiring deeds of [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md) and [Beleg](../B/Beleg.md), the "Two Captains" of [Dor-Cúarthol](../D/Dor-Cúarthol.md) who had thrown the power of Angband back, became known in Nargothrond. Many there grew restless, believing that if an outlaw could do such hurt to the Enemy, what might not the Lord of Narog do. Orodreth, however, would permit none of his people to join Túrin, and sent messages that Túrin was not to set foot in Nargothrond or drive Orcs into it. He did, however, offer help other than arms, possibly being moved to do so by Thingol and Melian with whom exchanged messages with.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/A/Alarie_-_Turin_in_front_of_Orodreth.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/2/20/Alarie_-_Turin_in_front_of_Orodreth.jpg/300px-Alarie_-_Turin_in_front_of_Orodreth.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/2/20/Alarie_-_Turin_in_front_of_Orodreth.jpg/450px-Alarie_-_Turin_in_front_of_Orodreth.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/2/20/Alarie_-_Turin_in_front_of_Orodreth.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="453"/></a><figcaption><i>Túrin in front of Orodreth</i> by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Alarie.md" title="Category:Images by Alarie">Alarie</a></figcaption></figure>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [490](../F/First_Age_490.md),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> [Gwindor](../G/Gwindor.md), a prince of Nargothrond and an escaped thrall of Morgoth returning from his captivity in [Angband](../A/Angband.md), led [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md) son of [Húrin](../H/Húrin.md), a [Man](../M/Man.md) of the [House of Hador](../H/House_of_Hador.md) to Nargothrond. In Nargothrond, Túrin hid his name, calling himself *[Agarwaen](../A/Agarwaen.md)*, son of *[Úmarth](../_/Úmarth.md)* ("Bloodstained, son of Ill-fate"). [Finduilas](../F/Finduilas.md), daughter of Orodreth, fell in love with him, but he avoided her because she had previously been the beloved of his friend Gwindor. Túrin declined to tell her his name, so that she called him *Thurin* ("The Secret"). He was also called *Adanedhel* ("Man-Elf") because he was so alike to an Elf, though he was a Man.<sup id="cite_ref-Narn_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narn-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Turin_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH10_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH10-13">[13]</a></sup>

His identity did not remain hidden for long. Gwindor revealed to Finduilas that 'Agarwaen' was in fact Túrin, and 'Úmarth' his famous father Húrin. When the news of this reached Finduilas' father, Orodreth, King of Nargothrond, Túrin was given great honor and standing, but the revelation of Túrin's identity would also bring Morgoth's curse with him.<sup id="cite_ref-Narn_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narn-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Turin_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH10_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH10-13">[13]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/A/Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/bc/Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg/225px-Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="225" height="357" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/bc/Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg/338px-Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/bc/Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg/450px-Alan_Lee_-_Fall_of_Nargothrond.jpg 2x" data-file-width="984" data-file-height="1562"/></a><figcaption><i>Fall of Nargothrond</i> by <a href="../A/Alan_Lee.md" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

Túrin became a chief counselor of Orodreth, and was extremely influential in Nargothrond. He encouraged the people of Nargothrond to abandon their practice of secrecy, and they built a great bridge before the gates. Because of his prowess with [Gurthang](../G/Gurthang.md), his black sword, he himself became known as *Mormegil* ("Black Sword").<sup id="cite_ref-Narn_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narn-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Turin_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH10_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH10-13">[13]</a></sup>

However, sometime after, two messengers, named [Gelmir](<../G/Gelmir_(messenger_of_Círdan).md>) and [Arminas](../A/Arminas.md), sent there by [Círdan](../C/Círdan.md), arrived to Nargothrond, delivering the message from [Ulmo](../U/Ulmo.md), Lord of Waters himself, to the King Orodreth, advising him to shut the doors of Nargothrond and destroy the bridge before its gates. At this time Orodreth relied upon the counsel of Túrin, who scorned the words of the messengers, for it was upon his advice that the bridge had been built and it was his policy for the King's forces to go forth openly to war, and sent them away.<sup id="cite_ref-Narn_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narn-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Turin_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Fall_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-14">[14]</a></sup>

This would prove to be a fatal mistake, since in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [495](../F/First_Age_495.md),<sup id="cite_ref-GAS275_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GAS275-4">[4]</a></sup> Morgoth unleashed his forces at Nargothrond. Led by [Glaurung](../G/Glaurung.md) the dragon, they devastated the army of Orodreth at the [Battle of Tumhalad](../B/Battle_of_Tumhalad.md). In that battle Orodreth was slain, and after that the [Fall of Nargothrond](../F/Fall_of_Nargothrond.md) was inevitable, due to the bridge that allowed Glaurung and Morgoth's host to ravage the city, and most of its people were either enslaved or killed (including Orodreth's daughter Finduilas).<sup id="cite_ref-Narn_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narn-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Turin_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Fall_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Orodreth* translates to "mountaineer".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that it is a combination of *[orod](../E/Ered.md)* ("mountain") and *reth* ("climber").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

His Quenya name was <i><b>Artaresto</b></i> (*arta* ("noble"); *resto* may be derived from √RETH (“endurance”)<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[note 1]</a></sup>) which Sindarized to <i><b>Rodreth</b></i> and was further altered to *Orodreth* because of his love of the mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

In *Ælfwine’s translation of the Quenta into Old English; Old English equivalents of Elvish names*, he is called **Ordred**;<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> while [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) does not provide a translation, it could mean "spear council" ("ord" meaning "spear",<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup> and "rǣd", commonly shortened to "red", meaning council<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup>)

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Decline into weakness and insignificance {#Decline_into_weakness_and_insignificance}

As noted by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), in *[The Lays of Beleriand](../T/The_Lays_of_Beleriand.md)*, Orodreth goes through a steady decline as the legendarium evolved.

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>In the poem, on the other hand, Orodreth emerges as hostile and formidable, and his character is carefully outlined: he is quick to anger (1973) but his wrath is cold and long-enduring (2133–4), he is seldom moved to pity (1969, 2134), grim-hearted and deep-counselled (2132–3), but capable of deep love (1970) as also of fierce hate (2135). Afterwards, as the legends developed, Orodreth underwent a steady decline into weakness and insignificance, which is very curious. Many years later, when meditating the development of the Túrin saga, my father noted that Orodreth was ‘rather a weak character’; cf. the Narn, p. 160: ‘he turned as he ever did to Túrin for counsel’. Ultimately he was to be displaced as the second King of Nargothrond (Unfinished Tales p. 255, note 20). But all this is a far cry from the hard and grim king in his underground hall depicted in the poem; Felagund had not yet emerged, nor the rebellious power of Celegorm and Curufin in Nargothrond (see further p. 246).
</p>

### Early legendarium {#Early_legendarium}

In earlier versions of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), Orodreth was a more important character, and the original king of Nargothrond. However, his importance diminished over time.

In one of the early versions, he had two sons whose names were translated as [Ordhelm](Ordhelm.md) and [Ordláf](Ordláf.md) in [Old English](Old_English.md) by [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md), though the original [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) names were lost to time.<sup id="cite_ref-Ord_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ord-21">[20]</a></sup> In another version, his two sons were [Haldir](<../H/Haldir_(son_of_Orodreth).md>) \[&lt; Halmir\] who was slain after being trapped by Orcs and hung on a tree,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:112</sup> and a younger son [Orodlin](<Orodlin_(son_of_Orodreth).md>).<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:309</sup>

### Parentage and children {#Parentage_and_children}

In the published versions of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* and *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)* Orodreth is the son of [Finarfin](../F/Finarfin.md) and younger brother of [Finrod Felagund](../F/Finrod.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Beleriand_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beleriand-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Beren_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

In J.R.R. Tolkien's updates to the genealogical tables of the descendants of Finwë, dating between 1959-1968, Orodreth's lineage went through several revisions. First, while named as *Artanáro Rhodothir*, he was changed to be son of Finrod Felegund. When Tolkien apparently recalled that Finrod had no wife or son, Orodreth was then made the son of Finrod's brother, Angrod. Orodreth's name was then changed from *Artanáro* to *Artaresto*. Finally, in a note dated August 1965, Tolkien fully elaborated on both Orodreth (*Artaresto*) being the son of Angrod, and Gil-galad (*Artanáro*) in turn being the son of Orodreth.<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-5">[5]</a></sup>

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) did not incorporate these changes into the published materials, but he did make several comments on them. *"For a reason unknown to me, my father displaced the second King of Nargothrond and made him a member of the same family in the next generation; but this and associated genealogical changes were never incorporated in the narratives of The Silmarillion."*<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> *"There can be no doubt that this was my father’s last word on the subject; but nothing of this late and radically altered conception ever touched the existing narratives, and it was obviously impossible to introduce it into the published Silmarillion. It would nonetheless have been very much better to have left Gil-galad’s parentage obscure."*<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-5">[5]</a></sup>

### A note from *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)* {#A_note_from_The_Nature_of_Middle-earth}

However, in a very late note dated c. 1972-3, the name *Orodreth* is again mentioned in the list of some renowned heroes in the royal lines of old, so it seems that Tolkien abandoned the name *Arothir* (which was itself meant to replace *Orodreth* a few years earlier).<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup>
