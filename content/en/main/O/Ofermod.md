"**Ofermod**"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> is an essay by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien); it explores the meanings of the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) word *ofermod*, which appears in the original Old English fragment <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Maldon" title="wikipedia:The Battle of Maldon">The Battle of Maldon</a></i>. The word is used to describe Beorhtnoth's state of mind at one point in the fragment - Tolkien took the word to mean that Beorhtnoth was a victim of overweening pride (a character flaw that would appear in several of Tolkien's later characters, [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) and [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin) perhaps being the best examples).

## Publication history {#Publication_history}

The essay was originally published in [1953](/wiki/1953) along with Tolkien's alliterative poem *[The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm's Son](/wiki/The_Homecoming_of_Beorhtnoth_Beorhthelm%27s_Son)* and another essay titled "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Beorhtnoth%27s_Death&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Beorhtnoth&#39;s Death (page does not exist)">Beorhtnoth&#39;s Death</a>".

It has since been republished in the [2001](/wiki/2001) (and later) editions of *[Tree and Leaf](/wiki/Tree_and_Leaf)*.
