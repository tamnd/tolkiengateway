"**Ofermod**"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> is an essay by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md); it explores the meanings of the [Old English](Old_English.md) word *ofermod*, which appears in the original Old English fragment <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Maldon" title="wikipedia:The Battle of Maldon">The Battle of Maldon</a></i>. The word is used to describe Beorhtnoth's state of mind at one point in the fragment - Tolkien took the word to mean that Beorhtnoth was a victim of overweening pride (a character flaw that would appear in several of Tolkien's later characters, [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) and [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md) perhaps being the best examples).

## Publication history {#Publication_history}

The essay was originally published in [1953](../_/1953.md) along with Tolkien's alliterative poem *[The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm's Son](../T/The_Homecoming_of_Beorhtnoth_Beorhthelm's_Son.md)* and another essay titled "<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Beorhtnoth%27s_Death&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Beorhtnoth&#39;s Death (page does not exist)">Beorhtnoth&#39;s Death</a>".

It has since been republished in the [2001](../_/2001.md) (and later) editions of *[Tree and Leaf](../T/Tree_and_Leaf.md)*.
