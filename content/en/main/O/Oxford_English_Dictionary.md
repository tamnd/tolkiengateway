The <i><b>Oxford English Dictionary</b> (<b>OED</b>)</i>, or <i><b>New English Dictionary</b></i> as it was called at its initiation, is the standard English dictionary. [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) spent several years as an assistant, and several of his own words and works would be cited in it.

## Early History {#Early_History}

The dictionary project started in 1878. Though it was originally not a part of the [University of Oxford](../U/University_of_Oxford.md), the institute took over the project later. The sections covering A-H were published by [1900](../_/1900.md), but both the [war](../W/World_War_I.md) and the death of Sir <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Murray_(lexicographer)" title="wikipedia:James Murray (lexicographer)">James Murray</a>, the original editor, in [1915](../_/1915.md) meant that the section U-Z was not complete yet.<sup id="cite_ref-BioOxI_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioOxI-2">[2]</a></sup> After Murray's death, three editors remained: [Henry Bradley](../H/Henry_Bradley.md), William Craigie, and C.T. Onions. Most of their assistants had gone to war and not returned (either not to the Dictionary or not at all), so new assistants were necessary.<sup id="cite_ref-RoWTaL_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RoWTaL-3">[3]</a></sup>

## The OED in Tolkien {#The_OED_in_Tolkien}

The OED makes two appearances in Tolkien's work. The four original editors - Murray, Bradley, Craigie and Onions - were referenced in *[Farmer Giles of Ham](../F/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham.md)*:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Some may well ask what a blunderbuss was. Indeed, this very question, it is said, was put to the <a href="../F/Four_Wise_Clerks_of_Oxenford.md" title="Four Wise Clerks of Oxenford">Four Wise Clerks of Oxenford</a>, and after thought they replied: &#39;A <a href="../B/Blunderbuss.md" title="Blunderbuss">Blunderbuss</a> is a short gun with a large bore firing many balls or slugs, and capable of doing execution within a limited range without exact aim. (Now superseded in civilized countries by other firearms.)&#39; 
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i>Farmer Giles of Ham</i><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Later, in *[The Notion Club Papers](../T/The_Notion_Club_Papers.md)*, the N.E.D. appears:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;Yes, <i>doink</i> has come on a lot lately,&#39; said Lowdham. &#39;But it&#39;s not brand-new, of course. I think it&#39;s first recorded, in the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Third_Supplement_to_the_N.E.D.&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Supplement to the N.E.D. (page does not exist)">Third Supplement to the N.E.D.</a>, in the fifties, in the form <i>dŏing</i>: seems to have started in the Air Force in the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Six_Years%27_War&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Six Years&#39; War (page does not exist)">Six Years&#39; War</a>.&#39;
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../A/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham.md" title="Alwin Arundel Lowdham">Alwin Arundel Lowdham</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></cite></p>
