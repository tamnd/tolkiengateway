<i><b>ondo</b></i> means "stone" or "rock" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

From [OS](/wiki/OS) \**gondo*, [PQ](/wiki/PQ) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gondo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gondo (page does not exist)">gon(do)</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> from possible [root](/wiki/Root "Root") \*GON

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Ondoher](/wiki/Ondoher) = "Stone Lord"
- [Ondolindë](/wiki/Ondolind%C3%AB) = "Rock of the Music of Water"
- [Ondonórë](/wiki/Gondor "Gondor") = Stone land, [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor)

## Cognates {#Cognates}

- [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[gond](/wiki/Gond "Gond")*

## Real-world inspiration {#Real-world_inspiration}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) was inspired by the early Celtic word *ond*, also meaning "rock".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> <span></span>
