**Ogres** were monstrous and destructive creatures of legend and folklore of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).

During the [Riddle-game](/wiki/Riddle-game) with [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum), [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins), in his attempt to solve Gollum's fifth riddle, sat thinking of all the horrible names of all the giants and ogres he had ever heard told of in tales.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

No ogre is ever mentioned in the annals of the [Elder Days](/wiki/Elder_Days) and none are known to have played any role in the wars and battles. It is entirely possible that they were simply a mythical race.

It is also possible that Ogre was just another name for the [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls).<sup id="cite_ref-HMN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HMN-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ogre#English" title="wiktionary:ogre">Ogre</a></i> derives, through French, from the Greco-Latin name Orcus. It is interesting to note that Orcus is supposedly the origin also for the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) word *[Orc](/wiki/Orc)*, although [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) doubted that etymology.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

*Ogres* were used by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) in early drafts of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") as a name for one of the monsters bred by [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth") along with [Gongs](/wiki/Gongs) and the other [Úvanimor](/wiki/%C3%9Avanimo "Úvanimo").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

They are mentioned as cannibal [giants](/wiki/Giants "Giants"). Those of the north were known as *Hongwir* while the southern ones were named *Sarqindi*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> *Sarqindi* is glossed as "Cannibal-ogres",<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and [Patrick H. Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_H._Wynne) and [Christopher Gilson](/wiki/Christopher_Gilson) have suggested that *hongwir* might be related to the [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) word *ongwe* (meaning "pain").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In the *[The Lay of the Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)*, it was noted that Húrin's helm "was hewn in war when he battled with Beren his brother-in-arms against ogres and Orcs and evil foemen" in the first version;<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> and emended slightly to "hewn in wars when he battled with Beren as brother and comrade against ogres and Orcs and evil foes" in the second.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Tolkien mentioned again ogres out of context in analyses of the name *[Orc](/wiki/Orcs "Orcs")*: "...the gloss *Þyrs* 'ogre'..." , and of the name *[Ettendales](/wiki/Ettendales)*: "This is meant to be a Common Speech (not Elvish) name, though it contains an obsolete element *eten* 'troll, ogre'."<sup id="cite_ref-HMN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HMN-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In his childhood, Tolkien and his brother [Hilary](/wiki/Hilary_Tolkien "Hilary Tolkien") gave a miller and a farmer, who frightened the boys and lived near the [Sarehole Mill](/wiki/Sarehole_Mill) in the Moseley area of [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), the nicknames "White Ogre" and "Black Ogre", respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> These characters also appear in stories written by Hilary Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

Tolkien enjoyed reading E.A. Wyke-Smith's *[The Marvellous Land of Snergs](/wiki/The_Marvellous_Land_of_Snergs)* ([1927](/wiki/1927)) to his children. The book recounts the adventures of two children and a Snerg ("a race of people only slightly taller than the average table"), and their tale include meeting the vegetarian ogre Golithos.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
