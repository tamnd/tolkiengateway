The **Stone of Orthanc** was one of the seven *[palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri "Palantíri")* brought to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) by [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and his sons at the end of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It was set in the [Tower of Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc "Orthanc") and used during the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) by [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman), casually by [Peregrin Took](/wiki/Peregrin_Took), and strategically by [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn).

## History {#History}

The [Exiles of Númenor](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor) built the Tower of Orthanc on what were then the northern borders of the realm of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), and set the *palantír* in the unbreachable fortress.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> As the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) wore on, the line of Kings came to an end, and the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) came to dwell in the lands south of Orthanc. By the last years of the Age, the remote Stone of Orthanc was all but forgotten by the [Stewards of Gondor](/wiki/Stewards_of_Gondor). The [Wizard](/wiki/Wizard) [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) had not forgotten the Stone, however, and took over the stewardship of Orthanc with the hope that the Stone would still be there.

Saruman's hopes were realised when he unlocked the impregnable Tower he found the Stone waiting inside, untouched for thousands of years. He kept his discovery secret from the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council), intending to use it for his own designs. It seems, however, that Saruman failed to account for the other *palantíri* in Middle-earth. The lost [Ithil-stone](/wiki/Ithil-stone) had fallen into the hands of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), who used its power to bend the Stone of Orthanc to his own will, and with it, Saruman himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Palantir_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palantir-3">[3]</a></sup>

After suffering great setbacks during the War of the Ring, Saruman lost the Stone, to his great anger. While he parleyed with [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) from the Tower, his servant [Gríma Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma")—not realizing the nature of the Stone—cast it at their enemies below, unwittingly granting them a great gift. [Pippin](/wiki/Pippin) picked it up and Gandalf hastily took it from him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The Stone was used twice after being thrown from the Tower, each time leading the user to encounter the Dark Lord himself. Pippin was the first to use it, in a foolish mistake. Having felt drawn to it, he secretly took the Stone from Gandalf while he slept. He was revealed to Sauron, who falsely believed Pippin to be a captive in Orthanc, where he thought the Stone still resided. Having rescued Pippin from Sauron's inlfuence via the Stone, Gandalf entrusted the *palantír* to [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) for safekeeping.<sup id="cite_ref-Palantir_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palantir-3">[3]</a></sup>

Shortly after, [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) knowingly used the *palantír* to reveal his ancestry to Sauron and displayed his sword Andúril, reforged from the sword that had cut the [Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") from Sauron's finger some three thousand years before. This act drew Sauron into open war, distracting his attention from his own land of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), so giving [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") a chance to reach [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
