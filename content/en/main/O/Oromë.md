**Oromë** was a [Vala](../V/Valar.md) and one of the [Aratar](../A/Aratar.md),<sup id="cite_ref-vala_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vala-1">[1]</a></sup> also known as the **Huntsman of the Valar**<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and the **Great Rider**.<sup id="cite_ref-Captivity_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Captivity-3">[3]</a></sup> He is considered one of the more powerful of the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-vala_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vala-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Attributes {#Attributes}

Oromë was a hunter of monsters and evil creatures, riding on his steed [Nahar](../N/Nahar.md), and blowing his great horn [Valaróma](../V/Valaróma.md). He loved [horses](../H/Horses.md) and [hounds](../H/Hounds.md) as well as all trees and forests. In [Yavanna](../Y/Yavanna.md)'s woods in [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md), he trained his folk and beasts to hunt the evil creatures of Melkor.<sup id="cite_ref-vala_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vala-1">[1]</a></sup> He hunted all foul beasts of Morgoth, and when he blew his horn, all would know that the woods had been purged.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Oromë was known for his terrible wrath, in contrast to [Tulkas](../T/Tulkas.md), who laughed often.<sup id="cite_ref-vala_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vala-1">[1]</a></sup>

Oromë was the brother of [Nessa](../N/Nessa.md) and the husband of [Vána](../V/Vána.md).<sup id="cite_ref-vala_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vala-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/K/Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/49/Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg/300px-Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="480" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/49/Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg/450px-Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/49/Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg/600px-Kip_Rasmussen_-_Orome_Hunts_the_Monsters_of_Morgoth.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="1638"/></a><figcaption><i>Orome Hunts the Monsters of Morgoth</i> by Kip Rasmussen</figcaption></figure>

During the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md), after most of the Valar had withdrawn completely from Middle-earth and hidden themselves in [Aman](../A/Aman.md), Oromë was the last who came to Valinor, and even then he still hunted in the forests of Middle-earth on occasion.<sup id="cite_ref-vala_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vala-1">[1]</a></sup> He visited [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) during the [Sleep of Yavanna](../S/Sleep_of_Yavanna.md), hunting monsters and fell creatures and the shadows fled temporarily until he left.<sup id="cite_ref-s1_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-s1-5">[5]</a></sup>

Being a powerful huntsman, he was active in the struggles against [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md), who feared Oromë in his anger. In efforts against Oromë, Melkor raised the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) as an impediment to his riding,<sup id="cite_ref-Captivity_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Captivity-3">[3]</a></sup> and sowed fear among the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) of a [dark Rider](../H/Hunter_and_Rider.md) who, upon his wild horse, would devour Elves who wondered to far.

Oromë was the first among the Valar to find the Elves at [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md). When at length some dared to approach him, and asked his name, he answered: "Oromë". When they asked what it meant, he again answered: "Oromë. To me only is it given; for I am Oromë"; while Oromë had many glorious titles, he withheld them so that the Elves would not be afraid. When the Elves further asked the name of his horse, and if it bore any meaning, Oromë answered: "Nahar, and he is called from the sound of his voice, when he is eager to run”.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

It was Oromë who gave the Elves the name "[Eldar](../E/Eldar.md)". Seeking to ensure their safety, Oromë accompanied the Elves from Cuiviénen to [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md).

After the destruction of the Two Trees of Valinor, Oromë led his host in pursuit of Morgoth and [Ungoliant](../U/Ungoliant.md). However, they were thwarted by Ungoliant's unlight.<sup id="cite_ref-Darkening_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Darkening-7">[7]</a></sup>

Because Oromë was the only Vala who travelled in Middle-earth during the [Elder Days](../E/Elder_Days.md), it was believed, even during the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) that the wild [oxen](../C/Cows.md) found near the [Sea of Rhun](../S/Sea_of_Rhun.md) descended from his [Kine](../K/Kine_of_Araw.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In the aftermath of the [Dagor Bragollach](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md), [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md) rode to Angband alone to challenge Morgoth to single combat. Those who saw him thought Oromë himself had arrived; for a great madness of rage was upon him, so that his eyes shone like the eyes of the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruin_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruin-9">[9]</a></sup>

During the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../B/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md) in the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), King [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md) displayed a drive and ferocity comparable to that of Oromë.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Oromë* is said to be derived from his [Valarin](../V/Valarin.md) name <i><b>Arōmēz</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-11">[11]</a></sup>

His name is translated as "Horn-blowing" and "Sound of Horns".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

### Other names {#Other_names}

<i><b>Araw</b></i> was the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) form of the name of the Oromë,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> while its [North Sindarin](../N/North_Sindarin.md) form was <i><b>Arum</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-11">[11]</a></sup>

<i><b>Tauron</b></i> was an epithet used by the [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md) for Oromë.<sup id="cite_ref-Index_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Index-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PM_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-16">[16]</a></sup> In the *[Valaquenta](../V/Valaquenta.md)*, *Tauron* is translated as "Lord of Forests".<sup id="cite_ref-Index_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Index-15">[15]</a></sup> Another translation is "The Forester".<sup id="cite_ref-Index_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Index-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PM_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-16">[16]</a></sup> The language, to which the name pertains, remains non-explicit in [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md)'s texts:

1. [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word? The root of the word could be *[taure](../T/Taure.md)* ("great wood") plus the ending *[-on](../_/-on.md)*. The name would thus have the sense "One of forests".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>
2. [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) word? This would be suggested by its usage among the Sindar and by the [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) form it replaced (see below).

<i><b>Béma</b></i> was the name used by the [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md) for Oromë. As the great huntsman and horseman of the Valar, he and his steed Nahar were known to the horse-loving people of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md), who claimed that their great horses, the [Mearas](../M/Mearas.md), had ancestors brought out of [the West](../A/Aman.md) by Béma himself.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> The name *Béma* is also from the [tongue of Rohan](../R/Rohanese.md) ([Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) was inspired by the [Old English](Old_English.md) word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/beme" title="wiktionary:beme">béme</a></i> "trumpet").<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

Tolkien used at least two earlier forms of the by-name *Tauron*:

1. *Tavros* - the form *Tauros* replaced - used in such early texts as the *[Lay of Leithian](../L/Lay_of_Leithian.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> The name is [Gnomish](../N/Noldorin.md), defined as "Chief wood fay 'the Blue Spirit of the Woods'".<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[note 1]</a></sup>
2. *Tauros* - the form *Tauron* replaced - is a [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) word defined in the *[Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)* as meaning "Forest-Dread" ([roots](../S/Sundocarme.md) [TÁWAR](../T/TAW.md) + <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=GOS&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="GOS (page does not exist)">GOS</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup>

In [Eriol](../E/Eriol.md)'s [Old English](Old_English.md) translations, Oromë is referred to as *Wáðfréa* "Huntinglord", *Huntena fréa* "Hunting Lord and Lord of Hunters" and *Wealdafréa* "Lord of Forests". The name *Béaming* is a translation of Q. *Aldaron*.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earliest form of the legendarium Oromë was described as the son of [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) and [Yavanna](../Y/Yavanna.md) whilst having the daughter [Nielíqui](../N/Nielíqui.md) with [Vána](../V/Vána.md).<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> ([Nessa](../N/Nessa.md) was already his sister; see [Valarindi](../V/Valarindi.md), Children of the Valar)
