**Old English** (also called **Anglo-Saxon**, though [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) apparently avoided the term<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>) was the ancestor language of modern English. It was also the mother language of characters [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) and [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol).

Anglo-Saxon was typically written in a [runic script](/wiki/Runes "Runes") before the introduction of the Latin alphabet.

## History {#History}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I have to teach or talk about Old English to such a lot of young persons who simply are not equipped by talent or character to grasp it or profit by it.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Tolkien was a professor of Anglo-Saxon at Pembroke College from [1925](/wiki/1925) to [1945](/wiki/1945). He favored especially the Mercian dialect and was strongly influenced by Old English literature such as *[Beowulf](</wiki/Beowulf_(poem)> "Beowulf (poem)")*.

In earlier notes concerning *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two)*, Tolkien commented that Old English was the only language the Elves of [Eressëa](/wiki/Eress%C3%ABa) could talk to Men, and that was how they talked to Ælfwine. The Elves learned Old English while living with Men in [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany).

The *[Gnomish Lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon)*, which belongs to that phase of the [Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium), mentions the [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) phrase <i><a class="external text" href="https://eldamo.org/content/words/word-1427946497.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">“Goldogrin” di Sacthoðrin</a></i> \*"“Goldogrin” \[translated\] into English". <a class="external text" href="https://eldamo.org/content/words/word-411293273.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Sacthoðrin</a> or \*"language of the Sactha" is the Gnomish name for the English language (in its Anglo-Saxon phase). Sactha (apparently referring to the Saxons) seems to be derived from the Gnomish root of "fight".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Tolkien wrote in Old English several texts of his legendarium, which he (fictionally) attributed to [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) such as the <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Earliest_Annals_of_Valinor&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Earliest Annals of Valinor (page does not exist)">Earliest Annals of Valinor</a></i>; they were published more recently in *[The Shaping of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Shaping_of_Middle-earth)* and are commented on and edited by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien). These texts have been criticized because Christopher Tolkien did not provide a translation and they remain understandable only by Anglo-Saxon students.

In those works, Eriol gave several Old English names to several elements of the legendarium; in some of them, Tolkien attempted to imitate the Elvish sound and form, but with a new meaning. For example [Angband](/wiki/Angband) is rendered as *Engbend* "cruel-bond", [Balrog](/wiki/Balrog) as *Bealuwearg* "baleful-monster" or [Silmaril](/wiki/Silmaril) as *Sigelmaerels* (actually referring to the [Nauglamír](/wiki/Nauglam%C3%ADr)). At least one name, *[Mickleburg](/wiki/Mickleburg)*, survived in the published *[Silmarillion](/wiki/Silmarillion)*.

Anglo-Saxon runes were used on [Thrór's Map](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r%27s_Map) in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* to display the [Cirth](/wiki/Cirth) as used by the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves).

Tolkien devised a [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) mode for Old English, for texts written by [Edwin Lowdham](/wiki/Edwin_Lowdham) in <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Strange_Case_of_Arundel_Lowdham&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Strange Case of Arundel Lowdham (page does not exist)">The Strange Case of Arundel Lowdham</a></i>, writings associated with *[The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers)*. (<a class="external text" href="http://www.forodrim.org/daeron/mdtci.html#DTS50" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">DTS50</a> and <a class="external text" href="http://www.forodrim.org/daeron/mdtci.html#DTS51" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">DTS51</a>).

## Rohanese {#Rohanese}

For *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Tolkien used several Anglo-Saxon names and words, in [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese), the language of the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> This simulated the archaic sense the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) felt with the Rohirrim; Hobbits spoke [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) [Westron](/wiki/Westron), represented in the book by English.

However the relation of Old English and English is not the same as with Rohanese and Westron, since the latter descends from [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), not Rohanese.

He stated that Anglo-Saxon (i.e. Old English) is not the source of names in any other language than the language of the Eorlingas except for a few names in Hobbit-dialect derived from the [Vale of Anduin](/wiki/Vale_of_Anduin), the names [Déagol](/wiki/D%C3%A9agol) and [Sméagol](/wiki/Sm%C3%A9agol); and the names [Gladden River](/wiki/Gladden_River) and [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Tolkien noted that the name [Stoor](/wiki/Stoors "Stoors") was based on the early English word *stor*, *stoor* ("large", "strong"), which is now obsolete.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
