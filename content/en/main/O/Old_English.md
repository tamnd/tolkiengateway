<p>I cannot (of course) understand why Anglo-Saxon should seem difficult...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

**Old English** (also called **Anglo-Saxon**, though [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) apparently avoided the term<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>) was the ancestor language of modern English. It was also the mother language of characters [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md) and [Eriol](../E/Eriol.md).

Anglo-Saxon was typically written in a [runic script](../R/Runes.md) before the introduction of the Latin alphabet.

## History {#History}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I have to teach or talk about Old English to such a lot of young persons who simply are not equipped by talent or character to grasp it or profit by it.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Tolkien was a professor of Anglo-Saxon at Pembroke College from [1925](../_/1925.md) to [1945](../_/1945.md). He favored especially the Mercian dialect and was strongly influenced by Old English literature such as *[Beowulf](<../B/Beowulf_(poem).md>)*.

In earlier notes concerning *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two.md)*, Tolkien commented that Old English was the only language the Elves of [Eressëa](../E/Eressëa.md) could talk to Men, and that was how they talked to Ælfwine. The Elves learned Old English while living with Men in [Luthany](../L/Luthany.md).

The *[Gnomish Lexicon](../G/Gnomish_Lexicon.md)*, which belongs to that phase of the [Legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), mentions the [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) phrase <i><a class="external text" href="https://eldamo.org/content/words/word-1427946497.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">“Goldogrin” di Sacthoðrin</a></i> \*"“Goldogrin” \[translated\] into English". <a class="external text" href="https://eldamo.org/content/words/word-411293273.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Sacthoðrin</a> or \*"language of the Sactha" is the Gnomish name for the English language (in its Anglo-Saxon phase). Sactha (apparently referring to the Saxons) seems to be derived from the Gnomish root of "fight".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Tolkien wrote in Old English several texts of his legendarium, which he (fictionally) attributed to [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md) such as the <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Earliest_Annals_of_Valinor&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Earliest Annals of Valinor (page does not exist)">Earliest Annals of Valinor</a></i>; they were published more recently in *[The Shaping of Middle-earth](../T/The_Shaping_of_Middle-earth.md)* and are commented on and edited by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md). These texts have been criticized because Christopher Tolkien did not provide a translation and they remain understandable only by Anglo-Saxon students.

In those works, Eriol gave several Old English names to several elements of the legendarium; in some of them, Tolkien attempted to imitate the Elvish sound and form, but with a new meaning. For example [Angband](../A/Angband.md) is rendered as *Engbend* "cruel-bond", [Balrog](../B/Balrog.md) as *Bealuwearg* "baleful-monster" or [Silmaril](../S/Silmaril.md) as *Sigelmaerels* (actually referring to the [Nauglamír](../N/Nauglamír.md)). At least one name, *[Mickleburg](../M/Mickleburg.md)*, survived in the published *[Silmarillion](../S/Silmarillion.md)*.

Anglo-Saxon runes were used on [Thrór's Map](../T/Thrór's_Map.md) in *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* to display the [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md) as used by the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md).

Tolkien devised a [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) mode for Old English, for texts written by [Edwin Lowdham](../E/Edwin_Lowdham.md) in <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=The_Strange_Case_of_Arundel_Lowdham&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Strange Case of Arundel Lowdham (page does not exist)">The Strange Case of Arundel Lowdham</a></i>, writings associated with *[The Notion Club Papers](../T/The_Notion_Club_Papers.md)*. (<a class="external text" href="http://www.forodrim.org/daeron/mdtci.html#DTS50" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">DTS50</a> and <a class="external text" href="http://www.forodrim.org/daeron/mdtci.html#DTS51" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">DTS51</a>).

## Rohanese {#Rohanese}

For *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, Tolkien used several Anglo-Saxon names and words, in [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md), the language of the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> This simulated the archaic sense the [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) felt with the Rohirrim; Hobbits spoke [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) [Westron](../W/Westron.md), represented in the book by English.

However the relation of Old English and English is not the same as with Rohanese and Westron, since the latter descends from [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md), not Rohanese.

He stated that Anglo-Saxon (i.e. Old English) is not the source of names in any other language than the language of the Eorlingas except for a few names in Hobbit-dialect derived from the [Vale of Anduin](../V/Vale_of_Anduin.md), the names [Déagol](../D/Déagol.md) and [Sméagol](../S/Sméagol.md); and the names [Gladden River](../G/Gladden_River.md) and [Gladden Fields](../G/Gladden_Fields.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Tolkien noted that the name [Stoor](../S/Stoors.md) was based on the early English word *stor*, *stoor* ("large", "strong"), which is now obsolete.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
