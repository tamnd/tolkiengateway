**Old Sindarin** is the conventional name for the hypothetical earliest form of the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) language, gradually mutated from [Common Telerin](/wiki/Common_Telerin).

## History {#History}

The stage of "Old Sindarin" refers to a period where the Sindarin words had not undergone [lenitions](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") of their consonants, nor affections of vowels by *[i](/wiki/I-affection "I-affection")* or *[a](/wiki/A-affection "A-affection")*, nor <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Apocope&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Apocope (page does not exist)">apocope</a> of final vowels. The first changes that already occurred in Old Sindarin, which made it differentiate from [Common Telerin](/wiki/Common_Telerin), was the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_glottal_fricative" title="wikipedia:Voiceless glottal fricative">aspiration</a> in some sounds; this can be seen in [Telerin](/wiki/Telerin) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Alpa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Alpa (page does not exist)">alpa</a></i> but OS <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Alpha&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Alpha (page does not exist)">alpha</a></i> &gt; S. *[alph](/wiki/Alph "Alph")*. Further changes followed in the process of time.

The OS long vowels of *é*, *á*, *ó* had been changed to other sounds by the time of Sindarin.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

This language was spoken in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) during or after the [Great March](/wiki/Great_March), perhaps by the [Eglath](/wiki/Eglath). However when the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) returned to [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) at the rising of the [Moon](/wiki/Moon), the language had already fully evolved into Sindarin.

It is possible that Old Sindarin was the common ancestor of [Mithrimin](/wiki/Mithrimin), [Falathrin](/wiki/Falathrin) and [Doriathrin](/wiki/Doriathrin).

## Other versions {#Other_versions}

In published writing referring to the early legendarium, only the term **[Old Noldorin](/wiki/Old_Noldorin)** was used. In that early conception, it was the language spoken by the [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes) in [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r), (in contrast to the later legendarium of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, where the Noldor spoke [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) both in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) and later in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth)). Old Noldorin does show some similarities to Quenya and [Telerin](/wiki/Telerin).

Most of the corpus of ON is given in *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)* and their purpose is to illustrate the etymology of some [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) words. Even after [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") renamed "Noldorin" into "Sindarin", almost all rules of etymology and derivation have been retained the same (see "**[Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin)**" for the discussion of some minor differences).

More recent works like *[Quendi and Eldar](/wiki/Quendi_and_Eldar)* show ancient forms of Sindarin that seem to possess the same structure and to belong to the same evolution as the Old Noldorin of the *Etymologies*. It is therefore possible that an ancient form of Sindarin similar to what Tolkien imagined as "Old Noldorin" did exist in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) of his later concepts; but Tolkien did not refer to that stage as a stable, identifiable language and did not go to the process to name it; making "Old Sindarin" to seem a non-canon, but plausible name for it.

Note that in these recent sources, Tolkien provides these forms with an [asterisk](/wiki/Asterisk "Asterisk") as "unattested", suggesting that they belong to an unwritten stage of the language, before the invention of the [Cirth](/wiki/Cirth). This is not the case in the "Old Noldorin" forms of the *Etymologies* where they are unasterisked, supposedly written with [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) in Kôr.
