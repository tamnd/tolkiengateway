<i><b>otsola</b></i> is a [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) name for the "week". [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) envisioned this [Elvish](/wiki/Elves "Elves") week as having seven days, like our own, and provided the names of the weekdays in Qenya.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Otsola* contains *[otso](/wiki/Otso "Otso")* ("seven") and *galā* ("daytime") (cf. later [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *[cala](/wiki/Cala "Cala")*). Its [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) translation is <i><b>othlad</b></i>, from *odin* ("seven"), and Tolkien derives it from [Primitive Elvish](/wiki/Primitive_Elvish) <i>*ot·g&#39;lāta</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-gl62_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gl62-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

An early name for *otsola* is *otsan*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Earlier Gnomish name is <i><b>ochlad</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-gl62_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gl62-2">[2]</a></sup>

In *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)*, Tolkien wrote about the five-day week called <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Lemnar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lemnar (page does not exist)">lemnar</a></i>. In the background of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* however the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) use the six-day week called *[enquië](/wiki/Enqui%C3%AB "Enquië")* in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya). The [Men](/wiki/Men) use a seven-day week,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but the Elvish term is not given. <span></span>
