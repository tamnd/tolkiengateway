**Of Lembas** is the fifteenth chapter of *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](../T/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth.md)*, forming part of the *[Dangweth Pengoloð](../D/Dangweth_Pengoloð.md)* manuscript, written between [1951](../_/1951.md)-[1959](../_/1959.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Content {#Content}

Like the rest of the manuscript, it was written with a beautiful calligraphic style, although not with the same quality, just accompaning the main text on Elvish tongues. The title includes a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) question as part of the title: '*Mana i·coimas in·Eldaron?' maquente Elendil?*. Tolkien added the Quenya title to make the text a companion to the *Dangweth Pengoloð* in which [Pengolodh](../P/Pengolodh.md) answers [Ælfwine](../_/Ælfwine.md)'s (Elendil) questions.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

### The text {#The_text}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>This food the <a href="../E/Eldar.md" title="Eldar">Eldar</a> alone knew how to make. It was made for the comfort of those who had need to go upon a long journey in the wild, or of the hurt whose life was in peril. Only these were permitted to use it. The Eldar did not give it to <a href="../M/Men.md" title="Men">Men</a>, save only to a few whom they loved, if they were in great need.<br/><br/>The Eldar say that they first received this food from the <a href="../V/Valar.md" title="Valar">Valar</a> in the beginning of their days in the <a href="../G/Great_Journey.md" title="Great Journey">Great Journey</a>. For it was made of a kind of corn which <a href="../Y/Yavanna.md" title="Yavanna">Yavanna</a> brought forth in the fields of <a href="../A/Aman.md" title="Aman">Aman</a>, and some she sent to them by the hand of <a class="mw-redirect" href="Orome.md" title="Orome">Orome</a> for their succour upon the long march.<br/><br/>Since it came from Yavanna, the queen, or the highest among the elven-women of any people, great or small, had the keeping and gift of the lembas, for which reason she was called <i><a href="../M/Massánië.md" title="Massánië">massánië</a></i> or <i>besain</i>: the Lady, or breadgiver.<br/><br/>Now this corn had in it the strong life of Aman, which it could impart to those who had the need and right to use the bread. If it was sown at any season, save in frost, it soon sprouted and grew swiftly, though it did not thrive in the shadow of plants of <a href="../M/Middle-earth.md" title="Middle-earth">Middle-earth</a> and would not endure winds that came out of the <a href="../A/Angband.md" title="Angband">North</a> while <a href="../M/Morgoth.md" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a> dwelt there. Else it needed only a little sunlight to ripen; for it took swiftly and multiplied all the vigour of any light that fell on it.<br/><br/>The Eldar grew it in guarded lands and sunlit glades; and they gathered its great golden ears, each one, by hand, and set no blade of metal to it. The white haulm was drawn from the earth in like manner, and woven into corn-leep for the storing of the grain: no worm or gnawing beast would touch that gleaming straw, and rot and mould and other evils of Middle-earth did not assail it. From the ear to the wafer none were permitted to handle this grain, save those elven-women who were called <i><a href="../Y/Yavannildi.md" title="Yavannildi">Yavannildi</a></i> (or by the <a href="../S/Sindar.md" title="Sindar">Sindar</a> the <i>Ivonwin</i>), the maidens of Yavanna; and the art of the making of the lembas, which they learned of the Valar, was a secret among them, and so ever has remained.<br/><br/><i>Lembas</i> is the <a href="../S/Sindarin.md" title="Sindarin">Sindarin</a> name, and comes from the older form <i>lenn-mbass</i> &#39;journey-bread&#39;. In <a href="../Q/Quenya.md" title="Quenya">Quenya</a> it was most often named <i>coimas</i> which is &#39;life-bread&#39;.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Quente <a href="../P/Pengolodh.md" title="Pengolodh">Quengoldo</a>.</cite></p>
