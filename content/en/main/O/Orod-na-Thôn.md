**Orod-na-Thôn** ("Pine-mountain"<sup id="cite_ref-UnfInd_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UnfInd-1">[1]</a></sup> or "Mount of the Pine Tree(s)"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>) was a mountain in [Dorthonion](../D/Dorthonion.md).<sup id="cite_ref-UnfInd_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UnfInd-1">[1]</a></sup> It is mentioned by [Treebeard](../T/Treebeard.md) in his song *[In the Willow-meads of Tasarinan](../I/In_the_Willow-meads_of_Tasarinan.md)*:

<dl><dd><i>Ah! the wind and the whiteness and the black branches of Winter upon Orod-na-Thôn!</i><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup></dd></dl>

It has also been assumed that *Orod-na-Thôn* is a synonym for *Dorthonion* 'Land of Pines',<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but Tolkien states clearly in his unfinished index of place-names occurring in *The Lord of the Rings* that it is a separate mountain in this region.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Elements of the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name are:

- *[orod](Orod.md)* 'mountain'<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
- *-[na](../N/Na.md)-* 'with' in sense of possessing<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
- *[thôn](../T/Thôn.md)* 'pine'<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
