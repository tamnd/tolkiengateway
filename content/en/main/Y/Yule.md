**Yule** was the [Northern Mannish](/wiki/Northern_Mannish) name of the midwinter festival, observed around the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands). The days around Yule were the Yule-tide.

In the [Shire Calendar](/wiki/Shire_Calendar), Yule referred to two days (the last of the previous and the first of the new year). Due to a peculiarity of the Calendar, the 2 Yuledays were "between" and outside the formal months of [Foreyule](/wiki/Foreyule) and [Afteryule](/wiki/Afteryule). They always fell on the same days of the week: the last day of the year, [1 Yule](/wiki/1_Yule), was always a [Highday](/wiki/Highday) (Friday), while the first day of the following year, [2 Yule](/wiki/2_Yule), was always a [Sterday](/wiki/Sterday) (Saturday).<sup id="cite_ref-Shire_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shire-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup>

Around them, the six-day festival of [Yuletide](/wiki/Yuledays "Yuledays") was held, running from [29](/wiki/29_December "29 December") [Foreyule](/wiki/Foreyule) through [2](/wiki/2_January "2 January") [Afteryule](/wiki/Afteryule).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

Yule represents a "translation" of a [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) word, but the word itself doesn't occur in [Westron](/wiki/Westron).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-4">[3]</a></sup>

However it's possible that it derives from a form of [Northern Mannish](/wiki/Northern_Mannish) and later used in [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese). Since [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) were in part [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen), the word was recognisable as a 'northern name' for the midwinter festival.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-4">[3]</a></sup>

A [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word for "Yule and the beginning of [Sun](/wiki/Sun)'s return" was <i><b>Amanar</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>
