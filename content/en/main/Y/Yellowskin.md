<i><b>Yellowskin</b></i> or the <i><b>Year-book of <a href="/wiki/Tuckborough" title="Tuckborough">Tuckborough</a></b></i> was the oldest of the [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") chronicle books, said to contain entries dating back some 900 years before the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) (since about [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2000](/wiki/Third_Age_2000 "Third Age 2000")).<sup id="cite_ref-Calendar_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendar-1">[1]</a></sup>

It contained chronicles of the [Took family](/wiki/Took_Family "Took Family"), including marriages, births and deaths, but also legal details and references to important events in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") as a whole.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendar_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendar-1">[1]</a></sup>

*Yellowskin* became a source for much of the historical information, especially about the Tooks, to find its way into the *[Red Book of Westmarch](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendar_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendar-1">[1]</a></sup>

The book recorded an archaic form of the [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) language, and from this the archaic names of the weekdays (Sterrendei, Sunnendei etc.) were preserved in the *Red Book*.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendar_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendar-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name 'Yellowskin' presumably derives from the aged and decayed condition of the book, suggesting that the records of the Tooks were recorded on vellum, or some other form of parchment made from skin. The name may have been inspired by that of a thirteenth-century manuscript called *Fagrskinna*, meaning "fine vellum" in Old Norse. (*Fagrskinna* was destroyed by fire in the eighteenth century, but not before a copy was made.)

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the second version of [Appendix F](/wiki/Appendix_F), the official name of the book popularly called *Yellowskin* was *The Great Writ of Tuckborough* and it was supposed to be nearly a thousand years old.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> <span></span>
