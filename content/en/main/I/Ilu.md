**Ilu** is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word that means "all, everything, the world". As a cosmological term it includes everything that exists, the created being ([Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4)) but also [God](/wiki/God) and the [spirits](/wiki/Spirits "Spirits") who created it, and all [souls](/wiki/F%C3%ABa "Fëa").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the early concept of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), *Ilu* was the name of "the World", signifying *both* the inhabited world and also the whole universe.

Ilu is everything that exists, and comprises of the habitable world proper ([Ambar](/wiki/Ambar)), with its layers of air and skies, the [Sun](/wiki/Sun), the [Moon](/wiki/Moon) and the [stars](/wiki/Stars "Stars"). All these are enveloped in the outer layer of air, [Vaiya](/wiki/Vaiya).

The boundaries of Ilu are the [Ilurambar](/wiki/Ilurambar), the "shell" that encloses Ilu, separating Vaiya from [kúma](/wiki/K%C3%BAma "Kúma") and non-existence.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

This concept evolved in a different cosmology in *[The Later Quenta Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion)*, where new concepts are introduced: The habitable world is named *[Arda](/wiki/Arda)*, more or less equivalent to both [Ambar](/wiki/Ambar) and Ilu; the whole existence, or created universe, is named *[Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4)*, which, unlike the former Ilu, is vast and might include other worlds other than Arda.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *Ilu* is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and means "universe", and is an alternative form of *[ilúvë](/wiki/Il%C3%BAv%C3%AB "Ilúvë")*. They come from the [root](/wiki/Root "Root") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=IL&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="IL (page does not exist)">IL</a> meaning "all".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Ilu does not appear in the published [Silmarillion](/wiki/Silmarillion), but the alternate form *[ilúvë](/wiki/Il%C3%BAv%C3%AB "Ilúvë")* survives, as in the name *[Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar)*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Christopher Gilson](/wiki/Christopher_Gilson) has noted that the name *Ilu* is presumably inspired by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian" title="wikipedia:Akkadian">Akkadian</a> word *ilu* ("gods").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> <span></span>
