**I-affection** or <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_umlaut" title="wikipedia:Germanic umlaut">Umlaut</a></b> is a phonological phenomenon which is an integral part of the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) language.

The phenomenon refers to the vowels of a word to be 'slid' higher and fronter to another sound, usually *e*, drawn because of a following *i* (usually near the end of the word) in a sort of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/vowel_harmony" title="wikipedia:vowel harmony">vowel harmony</a>. This started to occur after the stage called "[Old Sindarin](/wiki/Old_Sindarin)".

Umlaut has a grammatical significance in Sindarin since it is how plural of nouns is formed.

## Examples {#Examples}

The following verbs show how the vowels of the word-stems slid into *e* because of the affection of *i*.

- <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gonod&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gonod (page does not exist)">gonod</a></i> "count" + *-ia* &gt; <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Genedia-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Genedia- (page does not exist)">g<i>e</i>n<i>e</i>d<b>i</b>a</a></i> "to reckon"
- *[aglar](/wiki/Aglar "Aglar")* "glory" + *-ia* &gt; <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Egleria-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Egleria- (page does not exist)"><i>e</i>gl<i>e</i>r<b>i</b>a</a></i> "to praise"
- <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Said&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Said (page does not exist)">said</a></i> "private" + *-ia* &gt; <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Seidia-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Seidia- (page does not exist)">s<i>ei</i>d<b>i</b>a-</a> &#34;to set aside&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></i>

The same effect is seen with other endings such as *[-ien](/wiki/-ien)* or *[-ian](/wiki/-ian)(d)* as seen in

- *[Balar](/wiki/Balar)* an island + *[-iand](/wiki/-iand)* &gt; <i><a href="/wiki/Beleriand" title="Beleriand">B<i>e</i>l<i>e</i>r<b>i</b>and</a></i> "Land of Balar"

Another ending which causes umlaut is *[-il](/wiki/-il)*

- <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=AM1&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="AM1 (page does not exist)">AM1</a> + *-il* &gt; <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Emil&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Emil (page does not exist)"><i>e</i>m<b>i</b>l</a></i> "mother"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
- *[brand](/wiki/Brand "Brand")* "high" + *-il* &gt; <i><a href="/wiki/Brennil" title="Brennil">br<i>e</i>nn<b>i</b>l</a></i> "lady"
- <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Arn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Arn (page does not exist)">arn</a></i> "king" + *-il* &gt; <i><a href="/wiki/Ernil" title="Ernil"><i>e</i>rn<b>i</b>l</a></i> "prince"

It also can be triggered by compounded words

- <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Mad-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mad- (page does not exist)">mad-</a> + <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gl%C3%AE&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Glî (page does not exist)">glî</a> &gt; <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Medli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Medli (page does not exist)">m<i>e</i>dl<b>i</b></a></i> "[bear](/wiki/Bear "Bear")" (honey-eater)
- <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ath-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ath- (page does not exist)">ath-</a> + ring &gt; <i><a href="/wiki/Ethring" title="Ethring"><i>E</i>thr<b>i</b>ng</a></i>
- *[or](/wiki/Or "Or")* "over" + *[i](/wiki/I "I")* "the" &gt; <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Erin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Erin (page does not exist)"><i>e</i>r<b>i</b>n</a></i> "on the"

The [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) plural ending <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=-%C4%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="-ī (page does not exist)">-ī</a></i> was retained in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and Old Sindarin. In the later stages the \**-i* affected the previous vowels, especially the preceding one<sup id="cite_ref-ITE_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ITE-3">[3]</a></sup>

Then, all final vowels were <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Apocope&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Apocope (page does not exist)">apocoped</a> in Sindarin, including the plural markers. All traced of plural were simply the affected vowels.

To summarize this, the envisioned history of word *[adar](/wiki/Adar "Adar")* pl. *edair* would be like this:

- [OS](/wiki/OS) \**atari* "fathers" &gt; \**edeiri* &gt; \**edeir* &gt; *edair*

In a different form it is seen in [Ilkorin](/wiki/Ilkorin). For example *adar* pl. *edrin*, *aman* pl. *emnin*, *[boron](/wiki/Boron "Boron")* pl. burnin, <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gangel&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gangel (page does not exist)">gangel</a></i> pl. *genglin* and *[thorn](/wiki/Thorn "Thorn")* &gt; *thurin*.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The phenomenon is common in Germanic and Welsh, on which Sindarin is based on. For example the plural of W. *bard* (&lt; ancient \**bardos*) is *beird* (&lt; \**bardoi*)<sup id="cite_ref-ITE_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ITE-3">[3]</a></sup> The same process is responsible for some plurals in English, such as *man &gt; men* or *foot &gt; feet*. <span></span>
