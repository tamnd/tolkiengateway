**Ingwë** was the King of the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar), and the High King of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-princes_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-princes-8">[8]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Ingwë was one of the [Minyar](/wiki/Minyar) born at [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen). He was the eldest son of [Ilion](/wiki/Ilion), and a descendant of [Imin](/wiki/Imin) and [Iminyë](/wiki/Iminy%C3%AB), from eldest son to eldest son. He was beloved by his people, and at some point before the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey) he wed [Ilwen](/wiki/Ilwen) and had several children at Cuiviénen by the time [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB) found the [Quendi](/wiki/Quendi).<sup id="cite_ref-Gol_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gol-9">[9]</a></sup>

When Oromë invited them to [Aman](/wiki/Aman), Ingwë, along with [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB) and [Elwë](/wiki/Elw%C3%AB_Singollo "Elwë Singollo"), were the most eager to visit, and so followed him as ambassadors and travelled to the Blessed Realm. Once there, Ingwë desired to "dwell in the presence of Varda", and was reluctant to return to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-Ard_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ard-10">[10]</a></sup>

When they returned, they held a [great debate](/wiki/Debate_of_the_Quendi "Debate of the Quendi"), and told their peoples about its beauty and bliss and became their leaders during the [Great March](/wiki/Great_March). Ingwë was the leader of the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar), the foremost of the clans to follow Oromë, who were the most eager to reach the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/West" title="West">West</a>, which they did quickly.<sup id="cite_ref-journey_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-11">[11]</a></sup>

After the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey), Ingwë never returned or set eyes upon [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) again.<sup id="cite_ref-journey_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-11">[11]</a></sup>

He lived in [Tirion](/wiki/Tirion) for a while, where he also built the great, white tower at the topmost part of the city, called [Mindon Eldaliéva](/wiki/Mindon_Eldali%C3%A9va) or the Tower of Ingwë. It was never used, except by those who tended to the lamp at its summit.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[note 1]</a></sup>

There he begot many children, as noted by Finwë when Finwë complained to the Valar about his widowhood.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>

At some point, however, he went to live on [Taniquetil](/wiki/Taniquetil) at the feet of [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB).<sup id="cite_ref-princes_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-princes-8">[8]</a></sup>

[Indis](/wiki/Indis), the second wife of [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB), was of his close kin.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

Ingwë is described as being tall and beautiful,<sup id="cite_ref-Gol_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gol-9">[9]</a></sup> and his hair as golden and curly.<sup id="cite_ref-Cur_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cur-7">[7]</a></sup> He is also said to have been "more given to thought than arts".<sup id="cite_ref-Gol_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gol-9">[9]</a></sup>

The name *Ingwë* is in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya). It means "chief", being derived from the root <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=ING&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="ING (page does not exist)">ING</a> ("highest", "top"), to which is attached the suffix *[-wë](/wiki/-w%C3%AB)* ("person").<sup id="cite_ref-Shib_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shib-16">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:340</sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>

The Vanyar called themselves the *Ingwer*, the chieftains, on account of being considered the leaders and principal kindred of the Eldar, and their king's proper title was <i><b>Ingwë Ingweron</b></i>: ‘chief of the chieftains’.<sup id="cite_ref-Shib_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shib-16">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:340</sup>

In the earlier [*Etymologies*](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies"), *Ingwë* is said to be a compound of *[ing](/wiki/Inga "Inga")* ("first") + the ending *[-we](/wiki/-w%C3%AB "-wë")* ("man").<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Early legendarium {#Early_legendarium}

In the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), according to *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, the character that would later become known as *Ingwë* was called **Inwë**.<sup id="cite_ref-Com_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Com-19">[18]</a></sup>

Inwë was one of the three Elves (the other two being [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB) and [Tinwë](/wiki/Tinw%C3%AB)) that volunteered to go with [Nornorë](/wiki/Nornor%C3%AB), the messenger of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor), after their discovery at [Koivië-néni](/wiki/Koivi%C3%AB-n%C3%A9ni) - for [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) wished to learn about their "coming" and their "desires".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup>

When Inwë arrived to Valinor, he was filled with wonder and longing for the light of [Laurelin](/wiki/Laurelin), one of the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees), and his words moved [Yavanna](/wiki/Yavanna) and [Vána](/wiki/V%C3%A1na) so that they convinced most of the other Valar to summon the Elves to Valinor to live amongst them. So Inwë and the other ambassadors were sent back to Koivië-néni, and managed to convince most of the Elves to come live with the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup>

Thereafter, Inwë became the leader of the [Teleri](/wiki/Vanyar "Vanyar"), the first clan of the Elves, and led them to Valinor. He was also regarded as the eldest<sup id="cite_ref-Dea_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dea-22">[21]</a></sup> and greatest<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup> of all the Elves, and afterwards was considered the King of all the Eldar,<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-24">[23]</a></sup> and his home was at the highest point in [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r), a great elven city in Valinor, which had a [silver tower](/wiki/Mindon_Eldali%C3%A9va#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium "Mindon Eldaliéva") "shooting skyward like a needle".<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup> The Valar also gave Inwë a seedling of one of the Two Trees, which "blossomed eternally without abating" in his court.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup>

Many years later, after the [fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin"), the Elves of [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r) heard of the plight of the [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes) who were enslaved by [Melko](/wiki/Melko), and against the wishes of the Valar, Inwë led them to the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands) to wage war against Melko.

And even though the Elves were victorious, and Melko defeated and bound by [Tulkas](/wiki/Tulkas), Inwë was, however, killed during the war.<sup id="cite_ref-Dea_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dea-22">[21]</a></sup>

After the war, with the Elves being forbidden to return to Valinor,<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup> Ingil his son led them from the Great Lands to [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa), the Lonely Isle, where he became their leader.<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-24">[23]</a></sup>

His kin and descendants were called the [Inwir](/wiki/Inwir), one of whom was [Meril-i-Turinqi](/wiki/Meril-i-Turinqi) his great-granddaughter,<sup id="cite_ref-Dea_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dea-22">[21]</a></sup> the Lady of Tol Eressëa at the time of the arrival of [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol) the Mariner, in c. <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century" title="wikipedia:5th century">5th century AD</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Com_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Com-19">[18]</a></sup>

In one outline, the island of Tol Eressëa was also called *[Inwinórë](/wiki/Inwin%C3%B3r%C3%AB)* after Inwë, but that was later changed to *[Ingilnórë](/wiki/Ingiln%C3%B3r%C3%AB)*, in honour of his son Ingil instead.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[27]</a></sup>

Inwë might have spoken <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Inwelin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Inwelin (page does not exist)">Inwelin</a>, a dialect of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Telellin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Telellin (page does not exist)">Telellin</a> spoken by the royal clan of Inwir.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup>

#### Ingwë of Luthany {#Ingwë_of_Luthany}

In later outlines of the *Book of Lost Tales*, in which Tol Eressëa ceased to be identified with England, the island of [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany) appears as a precursor to England, from which the Elves of the Great Lands sail *to* Tol Eressëa, now a separate island.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup>

In that conception of the legendarium, there appears a character called Ing or Ingwë, a [Mannish](/wiki/Men "Men") King of Luthany who helps and welcomes the Elves to his land, after their war with Melko and, afterwards, with unfriendly Men in the Great Lands.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup>

However, he is *not* to be identified with the character of Inwë, or that of Ingwë in the later legendarium, even though [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) feels there has to be some connection between the two characters.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup>

#### Early etymology {#Early_etymology}

The name *Inwë* is in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya). In the *[Qenya Lexicon](/wiki/Qenya_Lexicon)*, it is said to be derived from the root <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=INI&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="INI (page does not exist)">INI</a> ("small"),<sup id="cite_ref-Ing_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ing-33">[32]</a></sup> but the earlier, original gloss of the root was "[fairy](/wiki/Fairy "Fairy")".<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup>

#### Other names {#Other_names}

The [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) cognate of *Inwë* is <i><b>Inwithiel</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-24">[23]</a></sup> changed from <i><b>Gim Githil</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup>

His proper (Gnomish) name, according to the *[Gnomish Lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon)* was <i><b>Inweg</b></i> or <i><b>Im</b></i>, with <i><b>Githil</b></i> being the Gnomish cognate of <i><b>Isil</b></i>, another one of his names.<sup id="cite_ref-Ing_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ing-33">[32]</a></sup> <i><b>Githiel</b></i> was the poetic form of *Githil*.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[35]</a></sup>

The original Gnomish form of his name was <i><b>Ginweg</b></i>, later changed to <i><b>Gim</b></i> or *Githil*.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[36]</a></sup>

He was also called <i><b>Tur yan Eldaron</b></i>, meaning "King of the Elves" in Qenya, with <i><b>Tur nan Églathon</b></i> being its Gnomish equivalent.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[37]</a></sup>

In the *[Sketch of the Mythology](/wiki/Sketch_of_the_Mythology)*, from c. [1926](/wiki/1926), the name <i><b>Ing</b></i> is described as being the Gnomish form of *Ingwë*, as opposed to the earlier conception of *Ing* being a rejected Qenya form of *Inwë*.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[38]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

#### *Quenta Noldorinwa* and the *Lhammas* {#Quenta_Noldorinwa_and_the_Lhammas}

In the *[Lhammas](/wiki/Lhammas)*, a text from the 1930s, Ingwë is mentioned as one of the original, and the eldest, of the Elves to be [awakened](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves "Awakening of the Elves") at [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen), and who led his people, chief of whom were known as the *Ingwelindar* or *Ingwi*, to [Aman](/wiki/Aman).<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[39]</a></sup>

In the *[Quenta Noldorinwa](/wiki/Quenta_Noldorinwa)* from c. [1930](/wiki/1930), he was still supposed to be one of the leaders of the Eldar in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) (unlike in the later versions, where it was his son Ingwion who led them), and he was still supposed to die in that war, as in the early legendarium in the *Book of Lost Tales*.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[40]</a></sup>

#### The Three Ambassadors {#The_Three_Ambassadors}

Ingwe was mentioned in several texts which were eventually published in *[The Nature of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth)*. As in the published *Silmarillion* several of them describe him, as one of the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Three_Ambassadors&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Three Ambassadors (page does not exist)">Three Ambassadors</a>, going to Aman and witnessing its splendor.

According to one text, the ambassadors, after their return to Middle-earth, spoke to the Elves at Cuiviénen. There, Ingwë spoke with great reverence towards the three [Elf-fathers](/wiki/Elf-fathers), regretting the fact that they themselves were unwilling to go and visit Aman, but he nonetheless asked Oromë whether or not they might still go there, and judge for themselves whether or not the Elves should embark on the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey) - unfortunately, the three Elf-fathers were unwilling to go.

In another text, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) changed his mind about the nature of the three ambassadors, and instead proposed that [Imin](/wiki/Imin), [Tata](/wiki/Tata) and [Enel](/wiki/Enel), the three Elf-fathers, ought to become the three ambassadors to Aman, with Ingwë, Finwë and Elwë accompanying them as the representatives of the younger generations of the Elves.

There, the three (new) ambassadors were undecided, and therefore, the Elves of Cuiviénen asked their younger associates to speak. Here Ingwë, again, spoke with reverence towards Imin, Tata and Enel, and ultimately agreed with Imin in his desire for the beauty of Valinor.

Finally, Imin agreed to a vote, and about two thirds of the Elves gathered at the time agreed to go to Aman.<sup id="cite_ref-Ard_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ard-10">[10]</a></sup>
