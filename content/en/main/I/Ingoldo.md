**Ingoldo** was the *[amilessë](/wiki/Amiless%C3%AB "Amilessë")* of both [Finarfin](/wiki/Finarfin) and his son [Finrod Felagund](/wiki/Finrod "Finrod"). The name means simply "the [Ñoldo](/wiki/Noldor "Noldor"), one eminent in the kindred". It was usually used by Finrod's brothers and [sister](/wiki/Galadriel "Galadriel"), who esteemed him. As Finrod came to Middle-earth and had a good relations with the [Atani](/wiki/Atani), this name spread. Thus it was used as a given name in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), and later also in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), where it was changed to *[Ingold](/wiki/Ingold)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Finwe_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finwe-1">[1]</a></sup>

If [Sindarized](/wiki/Sindarized), the name would have been <i><b>Angoloð</b></i> (spelled *Angolodh*).<sup id="cite_ref-Finwe_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finwe-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In earlier texts, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) considered this name as the mother-name of [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin), meaning that he came from both the kin of the *Ing*ar ([Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar)) and the N*oldo*r, while the mother-name of Finarfin was *Ingalaurë*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien also considered to change *Fingolfin* to <i><b>Ingolfin</b></i>, so this would be the Sindarized form of his mother-name, but soon all this conception was discarded.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
