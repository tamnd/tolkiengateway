**Ingoldo** was the *[amilessë](../A/Amilessë.md)* of both [Finarfin](../F/Finarfin.md) and his son [Finrod Felagund](../F/Finrod.md). The name means simply "the [Ñoldo](../N/Noldor.md), one eminent in the kindred". It was usually used by Finrod's brothers and [sister](../G/Galadriel.md), who esteemed him. As Finrod came to Middle-earth and had a good relations with the [Atani](../A/Atani.md), this name spread. Thus it was used as a given name in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md), and later also in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), where it was changed to *[Ingold](Ingold.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Finwe_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finwe-1">[1]</a></sup>

If [Sindarized](../S/Sindarized.md), the name would have been <i><b>Angoloð</b></i> (spelled *Angolodh*).<sup id="cite_ref-Finwe_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finwe-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In earlier texts, [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md) considered this name as the mother-name of [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md), meaning that he came from both the kin of the *Ing*ar ([Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md)) and the N*oldo*r, while the mother-name of Finarfin was *Ingalaurë*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien also considered to change *Fingolfin* to <i><b>Ingolfin</b></i>, so this would be the Sindarized form of his mother-name, but soon all this conception was discarded.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
