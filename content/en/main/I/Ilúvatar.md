<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

**Eru Ilúvatar**, also known as the **One**, is the single omniscient and omnipotent creator. He has been existing eternally in the [Timeless Halls](/wiki/Timeless_Halls) and possesses the [Flame Imperishable](/wiki/Flame_Imperishable) in his spirit which kindles existence from nothingness.

## The Creator {#The_Creator}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_Melkor_Weaves_Opposing_Music.jpg"><img src="/w/images/9/93/Ted_Nasmith_-_Melkor_Weaves_Opposing_Music.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="296" height="450" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="296" data-file-height="450"/></a><figcaption>Eru during the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Great_Music" title="Great Music">Great Music</a>, art by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

Eru created the [Ainur](/wiki/Ainur) before anything else, whom he kindled with the Flame Imperishable. Each Ainu came from a part of his mind. To further their comprehension, he presented his thought in the form of music, and listened as the Ainur picked up his themes and elaborated on them, slowly learning to sing in harmony with each other. Eventually he showed them his greatest theme, and made them sing it in harmony and develop it with newly granted powers. This was the [Music of the Ainur](/wiki/Music_of_the_Ainur).

Out of this great music, Eru showed them the [Vision](/wiki/Vision_of_Il%C3%BAvatar "Vision of Ilúvatar") which showed vast halls of spaces and stories unfolded in the deeps of Time, and some Ainur were drawn to it. Eru said "*[eä](</wiki/Ea_(verb)> "Ea (verb)")*" ("let it be") and thus [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4), the universe, was created.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Upon creating Eä he also created and fixed the natural laws and physical rules (*[axani](/wiki/Axan "Axan")*) in which it will function, limited within impossibilities (*[únati](/wiki/%C3%9Anat "Únat")*) that can't be broken by any being, no matter how powerful or evil.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Ilúvatar also set and fixed the conditions of the World (*[Ambarmenie](/wiki/Ambarmenie)*) in which the creatures would live.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## The God {#The_God}

The Ainur entered Eä and shaped the world according to the Music. Eru delegated most direct action within [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4) to the Ainur, including the shaping of the Earth ([Arda](/wiki/Arda)) itself.<sup id="cite_ref-S1_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S1-9">[9]</a></sup>

The Ainur were not omniscient and there were some things beyond their comprehension; those were the creation of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and [Men](/wiki/Men), who are directly the [Children of Ilúvatar](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar) (*[Eruhíni](/wiki/Eruh%C3%ADni)*) created without the delegation of the Ainur. Other things known by Eru alone are their destiny, and [the End](/wiki/The_End "The End") itself.

The activities of Eru in regard to the life of Arda or Eä are not clear. [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) was the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/vicegerent" title="wikipedia:vicegerent">vicegerent</a><sup id="cite_ref-S1_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S1-9">[9]</a></sup> of Eru on Arda and it is known that he sought for his consent several times, like before the creation of the [Ents](/wiki/Ents)<sup id="cite_ref-aule_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aule-10">[10]</a></sup> or before the [Battle of the Powers](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Powers).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> Instances of Eru's direct intervention were:

- Sanctified the creation of the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) by [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB), giving them life and sapience, and were his adopted Children.
- At the behest of [Yavanna](/wiki/Yavanna), he allowed the [Ents](/wiki/Ents) into being.<sup id="cite_ref-aule_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aule-10">[10]</a></sup>
- The creation of the Eruhíni, the [Awakening of the Elves](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves) and the [Awakening of Men](/wiki/Awakening_of_Men).
- [Beren](/wiki/Beren) crossing the [Girdle of Melian](/wiki/Girdle_of_Melian) and meeting [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
- The [Númenórean Catastrophe](/wiki/Changing_of_the_World "Changing of the World"),<sup id="cite_ref-NM|P3xv_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM|P3xv-5">[5]</a></sup> something that the Valar themselves could not have done.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>
- Several coincidences and signs of a higher providence throughout history, such as [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) finding the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
- The resurrection of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) after he was killed by [Durin's Bane](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane), something that could only be done by an Authority higher than the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-Murray_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Murray-15">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:203</sup>
- The fall of [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) into the fires of [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom), thus indirectly causing the destruction of the One Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter-3">[3]</a></sup>

According to [some sages](/wiki/Wise "Wise") among the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), Eru would [someday enter Eä](/wiki/Old_Hope "Old Hope") itself to save his beloved Children.<sup id="cite_ref-MR|P4c_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR|P4c-2">[2]</a></sup> It is said that after the [End of Days](/wiki/Dagor_Dagorath "Dagor Dagorath"), Eru will unite the Ainur and the Secondborn to create a music even greater than the one of creation.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

### Worship of Eru {#Worship_of_Eru}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>For that name we do not utter ever in jest or without full intent
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Finrod_Felagund" title="Finrod Felagund">Finrod Felagund</a> in the <i><a href="/wiki/Athrabeth_Finrod_ah_Andreth" title="Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth">Athrabeth</a></i></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Eru was considered transcendental, removed and distant from the affairs of Arda and was seldom worshiped and his name was too holy to be invoked.

Tolkien explains that "*the High Elves had no 'religion' (or religious practices, rather) for those had been in the hands of the gods, praising and adoring* Eru *'the One',* Ilúvatar *the Father of All on the [Mt. of Aman](/wiki/Taniquetil "Taniquetil")*".<sup id="cite_ref-Murray_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Murray-15">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:204</sup> Thus it is said that Manwë made a [High feast](/wiki/High_feast) in praise of Eru to celebrate each gathering of fruits.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

In the centre of the island of Numenor the great mountain, [Meneltarma](/wiki/Meneltarma), was completely devoted to Eru. The [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) worshiped him at the very pinnacle which they named the [Hallow of Eru](/wiki/Hallow_of_Eru) and consecrated from all the people; allowing no visitors except at the ceremonial [Three Prayers](/wiki/Three_Prayers) held at their respective times in the year ([Coranar](/wiki/Coranar)).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

On the other hand, as both the Eldar and the Númenóreans knew that Eru was the only God, they held worship of any other person to be an abomination,<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> save those who mistook Eru as one of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

[Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) swore [his Oath](/wiki/Oath_of_F%C3%ABanor "Oath of Fëanor") in the name of Eru.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) bound the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) with an oath to Eru; the next known instance when a Man invoked Eru's name "*who is above all thrones for ever*", was by [Cirion](/wiki/Cirion) in his [Oath](/wiki/Oath_of_Eorl "Oath of Eorl") with [Eorl](/wiki/Eorl), millennia later.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

<i><b>Ilúvatar</b></i> (pron. [N](/wiki/Noldorin "Noldorin") <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[iˈluːvatar]</span>, [V](/wiki/Vanyarin "Vanyarin") <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[iˈluːβatar]</span>) is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) for "Father of All", more commonly referred to as Eru **Ilúvatar**.

The name *Ilúvatar* is a compound of two words, *[ilu](/wiki/Ilu "Ilu")* or *[ilúvë](/wiki/Il%C3%BAv%C3%AB "Ilúvë")* ("all, universe") and *[atar](/wiki/Atar "Atar")* ("father").

<p>&#39;Ilúvatar was the first beginning, and beyond that no wisdom of the <a href="/wiki/Valar" title="Valar">Valar</a> or of <a href="/wiki/Eldar" title="Eldar">Eldar</a> or of <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a> can go.&#39;<br/>&#39;Who was Ilúvatar?&#39; asked <a href="/wiki/Eriol" title="Eriol">Eriol</a>. &#39;Was he of the <a href="/wiki/Gods" title="Gods">Gods</a>?&#39;<br/>&#39;Nay,&#39; said <a href="/wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)" title="Rúmil (elf of Tirion)">Rúmil</a>, &#39;that he was not, for he made them. </p>

Ilúvatar appears since the earliest form of the Legendarium, in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*. It is to be noted that in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) the name *Ilúvatar* meant "Sky-father" since the element *il-* refers also to the sky (cf. [Ilmen](/wiki/Ilmen)), but this etymology was dropped in favour of the newer meaning in later revisions. Another name, less used, was <i><b>Ainatar</b></i> ("Holy Father").<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup>

The word *Eru* first appeared as an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) word: <i><b>Êru</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> It was first used as a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word in a [list of names](</wiki/Ainulindal%C3%AB_(Morgoth%27s_Ring)#Alteration_in_last_revision_1951> "Ainulindalë (Morgoth's Ring)") of 1951.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

Ilúvatar was also called <i><b>Sanavaldo</b></i>, which is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and means "*the Almighty*".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien understood Eru not as a "fictional deity" but as a name in a fictional language for the actual monotheistic God, although in a mythological or fictional context. In a draft of a letter of [1954](/wiki/1954) to Peter Hastings, manager of the Newman Bookshop (a Catholic bookshop in [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford)), Tolkien defended non-orthodox aspects as rightly within the scope of his mythology, as an exploration of the infinite "potential variety" of God. Regarding the possibility of reincarnation of [Elves](/wiki/Elves), Hastings had written:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>God has not used that device in any of the creations of which we have knowledge, and it seems to me to be stepping beyond the position of a sub-creator to produce it as an actual working thing, because a sub-creator, when dealing with the relations between creator and created, should use those channels which he knows the creator to have used already
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Peter Hastings</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Tolkien's reply contains an explanation of his view of the relation of (divine) Creation to (human) [sub-creation](/wiki/Sub-creation "Sub-creation"):

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>We differ entirely about the nature of the relation of sub-creation to Creation. I should have said that liberation &#34;from the channels the creator is known to have used already&#34; is the fundamental function of &#34;sub-creation&#34;, a tribute to the infinity of His potential variety [...] I am not a metaphysician; but I should have thought it a curious metaphysics — there is not one but many, indeed potentially innumerable ones — that declared the channels known (in such a finite corner as we have any inkling of) to have been used, are the only possible ones, or efficacious, or possibly acceptable to and by Him!
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien, <a href="/wiki/Letter_153" title="Letter 153">Letter 153</a>, pp. 188-189</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Hastings had also criticized the description of [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil) by [Goldberry](/wiki/Goldberry): *"He is"*, saying that this seemed to imply that Bombadil was God.

Tolkien replied to this:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>As for Tom Bombadil, I really do think you are being too serious, besides missing the point. [...] You rather remind me of a Protestant relation who to me objected to the (modern) Catholic habit of calling priests Father, because the name father belonged only to the <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father" title="wikipedia:God the Father">First Person</a></b>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien, <a href="/wiki/Letter_153" title="Letter 153">Letter 153</a>, p. 191</cite></p>
</blockquote>
