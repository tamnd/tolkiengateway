**Ing** or **Ingwë**<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Jut_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jut-1">[1]</a></sup> was the [Mannish](/wiki/Men "Men") King of [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany), and the legendary ancestor of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="wikipedia:Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Ang_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ang-4">[2]</a></sup> according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Following the march of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) of [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r) to the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands) to free the enslaved [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes), the remaining Elves waged a war with evil Men, and afterwards retreated to the land of [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany) ruled by Ing, who was friendly to them.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[4]</a></sup>

After some time, [Eärendel](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel), fleeing from [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB), came to Luthany and was given aid by Ing. In return for his help, Eärendel allowed Ing to drink *[limpë](/wiki/Limp%C3%AB "Limpë")*, which made him immortal.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 3]</a></sup> Eärendel also spoke to him of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa), where many of the Elves of Luthany moved to. This inspired Ing, "longing for the Elves", to try to sail there; however, due to the anger of Ossë for helping Eärendel, he and all his fleet were driven east, and only Ing managed to survive.<sup id="cite_ref-Ang_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ang-4">[2]</a></sup>

There, he became the king of the people known as the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ingwaiwar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ingwaiwar (page does not exist)">Ingwaiwar</a> (named after him). He instructed them in many things, including the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and the Elves, and set their hearts toward sailing west.<sup id="cite_ref-Ang_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ang-4">[2]</a></sup>

At some point, Ing left the Ingwaiwar, departing in a boat, and was never heard of again.<sup id="cite_ref-Ang_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ang-4">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[note 4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[6]</a></sup>

His people, the Ingwaiwar, eventually conquered Luthany and became known as the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people" title="wikipedia:English people">English</a>. However, unlike all other previous invaders, they were friendly to the Elves owing to the teachings of Ing.<sup id="cite_ref-Jut_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jut-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[7]</a></sup>

Among Ing's descendants was [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) the Mariner, who travelled to Tol Eressëa, and learned the histories of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[8]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Ing* is of unknown origin. Another name for the character was *Ingwë*.

*Ing* was also an earlier, rejected ([Qenya](/wiki/Qenya)) name for the character of [Inwë](/wiki/Inw%C3%AB), the leader of the [Teleri](/wiki/Vanyar "Vanyar") in *The Book of Lost Tales*.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[9]</a></sup>

In the *[Sketch of the Mythology](/wiki/Sketch_of_the_Mythology)*, from c. [1926](/wiki/1926), the name *Ing* is described as being the Gnomish form of *[Ingwë](/wiki/Ingw%C3%AB)*, as opposed to the earlier conception of *Ing* being a rejected Qenya form of *Inwë*.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[10]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The main inspiration behind the character was the figure of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yngvi" title="wikipedia:Yngvi">Ing</a></i> in the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English_rune_poem" title="wikipedia:Old English rune poem">Runic Poem</a></i>, of whom it is said: "Ing was first seen by men among the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geats" title="wikipedia:Geats">East Danes</a>, until he departed eastwards over the waves; his car sped after him."<sup id="cite_ref-Jut_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jut-1">[1]</a></sup>

The <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ingwaiwar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ingwaiwar (page does not exist)">Ingwaiwar</a>, his followers, were probably based on the people called the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingaevones" title="wikipedia:Ingaevones">Inguaeones</a></i>, the ancestors of the English.<sup id="cite_ref-Jut_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jut-1">[1]</a></sup>
