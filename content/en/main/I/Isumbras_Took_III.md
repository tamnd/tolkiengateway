**Isumbras Took III** was the twenty-third [Thain](/wiki/Thain) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), the eleventh of the [Took Family](/wiki/Took_Family).

## History {#History}

Isumbras was the son of [Thain Isengrim II](/wiki/Isengrim_Took_II "Isengrim Took II"), and inherited the title at his father's death in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1122](/wiki/Third_Age_2722 "Third Age 2722"). He had two sons, [Ferumbras II](/wiki/Ferumbras_Took_II "Ferumbras Took II") and [Bandobras](/wiki/Bandobras_Took "Bandobras Took"), who moved North to [Long Cleeve](/wiki/Long_Cleeve).<sup id="cite_ref-took_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-took-1">[1]</a></sup>

Isumbras died in [1159](/wiki/Third_Age_2759 "Third Age 2759"), the year when the [Long Winter](/wiki/Long_Winter) was followed by the [Days of Dearth](/wiki/Days_of_Dearth).<sup id="cite_ref-took_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-took-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> His son Ferumbras succeeded him to the Thainship.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

"Isumbras" is of French and [Anglo-Saxon](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") origin and means "iron-arm". It comes from the French *bras* meaning "arm", and the Anglo-Saxon and Middle High German word *[isen](/wiki/Isen "Isen")* which means "iron".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The name appears in <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Isumbras" title="wikipedia:Sir Isumbras">Sir Isumbras</a></i>, an English medieval romance about a man who suffers a lifetime of hardship but who finds happiness in his latter years.
