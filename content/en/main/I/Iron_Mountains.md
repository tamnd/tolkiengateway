The **Iron Mountains** or **Ered Engrin** were an immense mountain range in the far north of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), stretching almost from one end of the continent to the other, though falling short of the sea on either side.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Of old the Iron Mountains came close to touching the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) (*Ered Luin*) of the West and the Red Mountains (*[Orocarni](/wiki/Orocarni)*) of the East,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> but in the wars between the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth") the mountain range was distorted. Melkor himself created the mountains as a fence for his citadel of [Utumno](/wiki/Utumno).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Melkor's great fortresses of [Angband](/wiki/Angband) and [Utumno](/wiki/Utumno) were built in the mountains. North of the range lay the regions of ever-lasting cold.

After the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) the Iron Mountains were broken and disappeared, at least the part of their length that lay directly north of Beleriand.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

[Karen Wynn Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad), in her book *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth)*, imagined that remnants of the Iron Mountains after the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) could perhaps be all the northern mountain ranges of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) and [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion): the [Mountains of Angmar](/wiki/Mountains_of_Angmar), the [Ered Mithrin](/wiki/Grey_Mountains "Grey Mountains"), and the [Iron Hills](/wiki/Iron_Hills), still occupied by [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) and [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons) until the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> However, this depiction predates publication of *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*, where it is revealed that the Grey Mountains and Iron Hills existed in the First Age independently of the Iron Mountains. It was during this age that the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) of [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Moria "Moria") first colonized the Iron Hills. They also considered the Grey Mountains, which lay between Khazad-dûm and the Iron Hills, to be part of their territory.<sup id="cite_ref-relations_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relations-5">[5]</a></sup>
