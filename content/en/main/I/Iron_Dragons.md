**Monsters**<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:169</sup> were "serpents of bronze and iron" and fire that were forged by the smiths of [Melko](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth")<sup id="cite_ref-melko_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-melko-2">[2]</a></sup> within the pits of [Angband](/wiki/Angband) to be used against the peoples of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin) during its [fall](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin").<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:176</sup>

## History {#History}

It was [Meglin](/wiki/Meglin) who first conceived of the creation of Monsters of fire and iron, and he brought this idea to Melko during his betrayal.<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:169</sup> When the "serpents of bronze and iron" came against Gondolin, they piled against the northern gate where the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) within would swarm into the breach.<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:176</sup>

Based on their descriptions, it can be said that the these Monsters<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:169</sup> were inanimate in nature.<sup id="cite_ref-Commentary_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Commentary-3">[3]</a></sup> They were made by the most cunning of Melko's smiths and sorcerers. Their iron was expertly linked, so that they could coil and crawl like slow rivers over difficult terrain. Within these devices were Orcs armed with scimitars and spears, ready to emerge at need. It is said that after their appearance at Gondolin, such devices would not be seen again until the [Great End](/wiki/Dagor_Dagorath "Dagor Dagorath").<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:170</sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

While many of Tolkien's works echo the conflicts of [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I), *The Fall of Gondolin* is particularly striking in the descriptions of the Monsters<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:169</sup> that Melko has built in his preparation for the battle. According to [John Garth](/wiki/John_Garth), the narrative of the battle "appears to echo Tolkien's creative development and slow acceptance of duty in the first year of [the war](/wiki/World_War_I "World War I")" whereas the latter part echoes his personal experience of battle. The Monsters<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:169</sup> of iron and bronze which are capable of crossing difficult terrain and which contain within them companies of orcs, could resemble tanks that made their debut in the Battle of the Somme.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In the commentary to the text, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) seems to support this view saying that "the language employed suggests that some at least of the 'Monsters' were inanimate 'devices'," although he also points out they are also referred as "beasts".<sup id="cite_ref-Commentary_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Commentary-3">[3]</a></sup>
