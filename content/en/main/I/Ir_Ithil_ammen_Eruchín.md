"<i><b>Ir Ithil ammen Eruchín</b></i>" is the first line of a poem written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) that was posthumously published in *[The Lays of Beleriand](/wiki/The_Lays_of_Beleriand)* in [1985](/wiki/1985).<sup id="cite_ref-song_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-song-1">[1]</a></sup>

Though Tolkien never gave the poem a name, it is referred to by some people as **[Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien)'s Song**<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-2">[2]</a></sup>, a term first used by [Patrick H. Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_H._Wynne), who analyzed the poem in [1990](/wiki/1990).<sup id="cite_ref-first_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-3">[3]</a></sup> The poem was also analyzed by [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) in his [2004](/wiki/2004) book, *[A Gateway to Sindarin](/wiki/A_Gateway_to_Sindarin)*.<sup id="cite_ref-gateway_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gateway-4">[4]</a></sup>

<td><div class="poem">
<p>Ir Ithil ammen Eruchín<br/>
      <a href="/wiki/Menel" title="Menel">menel</a>-vîr síla díriel<br/>
si loth a galadh lasto dîn!<br/>
      A Hîr Annûn gilthoniel,<br/>
le linnon im Tinúviel!<sup id="cite_ref-song_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-song-1">[1]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>
</td>
<td><div class="poem">
<p>When the Moon, for us, the Children of Eru<br/>
a heavenly jewel shines silver, having watched<br/>
Here/now let flower and tree listen in silence/below!<br/>
O Lord of the West, star-kindling,<br/>
to thee I sing, I, the Nightingale!<sup id="cite_ref-first_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-3">[3]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>
</td></tr></tbody></table>
