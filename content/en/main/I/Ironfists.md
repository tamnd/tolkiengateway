The **Ironfists** were one of the seven houses of the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) that dwelt in [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn). They were originally paired with the [Stiffbeards](/wiki/Stiffbeards).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

The locations of the four [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") clans, including the Ironfists, who lived in the [East](/wiki/East) are unknown. The distance between their mansions in the East and the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains), specifically [Gundabad](/wiki/Gundabad), was said to be as great or greater than that of Gundabad's distance from the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) in the West.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

It is possible that the Ironfists were among the "*Houses of other Fathers*" who sent troops to help [Durin's Folk](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk) in the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](/wiki/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Late in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), when war and terror grew in the [East](/wiki/East) itself, considerable numbers of Ironfists and dwarves of the Eastern clans left their ancient homelands. They sought refuge in Middle-earth's western lands, where some of them may have met [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The unnamed Dwarven tribes were given invented names in an early supplement.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> While these were published before the publication of Tolkien&#39;s late note on the name of the Dwarven houses of the East, it has been suggested that the Ironfists could correspond to <b>Thelór&#39;s tribe</b> (as portrayed in <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">MERP</a></i>).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2002-5: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The House corresponding to the Ironfists is called <b>Sindri&#39;s Folk</b>, said to inhabit a mountain chain of central Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2012: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Dwalin" title="Dwalin">Dwalin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thorin" title="Thorin">Thorin</a> mentioned Thorin, the heir of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin" title="Durin">Durin</a> having a meeting with his <a href="/wiki/Dwarves" title="Dwarves">kin</a>, with &#34;envoys from the Seven Kingdoms&#34;, the same number as the Houses of the Dwarves, and also <a href="/wiki/Dwarves_of_the_Iron_Hills" title="Dwarves of the Iron Hills">Dwarves of the Iron Hills</a>. It&#39;s unspecified on why Thorin had a meeting with all houses of the Dwarves, however it&#39;s likely for the purpose of declaring war on <a href="/wiki/Smaug" title="Smaug">Smaug</a> and to reclaim his homeland.</dd></dl>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/ce/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg/300px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="169" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/ce/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg/450px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/ce/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg/600px-The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online_-_Ironfists.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="1080"/></a><figcaption>The &#34;Zhélruka&#34; in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i></figcaption></figure>

<b>2018: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The House corresponding to the Ironfists is called the <b>Zhélruka</b>, meaning &#34;iron hand&#34; in old Slavonic on which their &#34;outer&#34; names are based.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup> The Zhélruka were the first of major Houses (other than Longbeards) to be introduced to the game, which greatly fleshed out their history, culture, language and even the unique writing system. They style themselves with elaborate moustaches and mutton chops instead of beards and fight with curved swords instead of axes. Their banners depict an iron gauntlet wielding a war-gammer.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The Zhélruka awoke in the east and fought with <a href="/wiki/Stiffbeards" title="Stiffbeards">Kámbrada</a> over the mountains of <a href="/wiki/Orocarni" title="Orocarni">Ered Cernin</a>. Their homes destroyed at the end of the <a href="/wiki/First_Age" title="First Age">First Age</a>, they were granted leave to dwell in <a href="/wiki/Gundabad" title="Gundabad">Gundabad</a> by <a href="/wiki/Durin_II" title="Durin II">Durin II</a>, but the refugees never arrived, becoming lost in the <a href="/wiki/Grey_Mountains" title="Grey Mountains">Grey Mountains</a>. A widespread legend among the Zhélruka was the existence of their lost city in the <a href="/wiki/Grey_Mountains" title="Grey Mountains">Grey Mountains</a> that rivaled <a href="/wiki/Moria" title="Moria">Khazad-dûm</a>: &#34;Thafar-gathol, the Grey Citadel&#34;. A proud culture with a distrust of outsiders, the Zhélruka had a long history with the Longbeards that varied between alliance and enmity. Their kings wielded the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Rings" title="Seven Rings">Ring</a> &#34;Tínya&#34; until it was devoured by the <a href="/wiki/Cold-drakes" title="Cold-drakes">Cold-dragon</a> &#34;Hrímil Frost-heart&#34;.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>When <a href="/wiki/Thr%C3%A1in" title="Thráin">Thráin II</a> called the dwarves to war, &#34;King Vélat&#34; led the Zhélruka from their home in &#34;<a href="/wiki/Orocarni" title="Orocarni">Ered Cernin</a>&#34;. Vélat fell in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Azanulbizar" title="Battle of Azanulbizar">Battle of Azanulbizar</a> and was succeeded by his son &#34;Vékun II&#34;. Shortly after <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>&#39;s downfall, Vékun&#39;s son &#34;Prince Ingór&#34;, brother of &#34;King Ótek VII&#34;, led an expedition into the Grey Mountains and formed an uneasy partnership with the Longbeard expedition he encountered there. Ingór&#39;s dwarves helped the Longbeards secure an abandoned stronghold in Ered Mithrin and joined Prince <a href="/wiki/Durin_VII" title="Durin VII">Durin</a>&#39;s campaign to reclaim Gundabad, although Ingór&#39;s host answered only to him. The dwarves were eventually successful and <a href="/wiki/Durin_VII" title="Durin VII">Durin VII</a> reinforced <a href="/wiki/Durin_II" title="Durin II">his own decree</a> by allowing Zhélruka to dwell within Gundabad.</dd></dl>
