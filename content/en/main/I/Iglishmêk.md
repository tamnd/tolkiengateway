**Iglishmêk** was a [Dwarvish](../D/Dwarves.md) language of silent gesture-signs.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT39_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT39-2">[2]</a></sup>

Considered far more elaborate and organized than the Elvish systems ([mátengwië](../M/Mátengwië.md)), iglishmêk was a secondary language which Dwarves started learning almost as soon as their spoken one. This was used for secret communication amongst themselves when dealing with strangers, and unlike the uniformity of their spoken language ([Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md)), iglishmêk varied greatly between Dwarvish communities. The gestures were very swift and subtle and could hardly be detected, let alone understood, by uninitiated onlookers. They were no more willing to teach this than their spoken language, but some Noldorin loremasters were allowed to learn enough to understand.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Examples {#Examples}

- "I am listening" = slight raising of right-hand forefinger, followed by a similar raising of the left-hand forefinger.<sup id="cite_ref-VT39_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT39-2">[2]</a></sup>
- "Listen!" = slight raising of both forefingers simultaneously.<sup id="cite_ref-VT39_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT39-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Magnus Åberg](../M/Magnus_Åberg.md) has theorized that the word *iglishmêk* is composed of \**'igal* (\*"language", as in *aglâb*) and \**shimak* (\*"gesture").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The concept of communication by signs used by non-deaf users is very similar to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_sign_languages" title="wikipedia:Monastic sign languages">monastic sign languages</a> created by [Christian](../C/Christianity.md) monks in order to keep mandatory or voluntary silence.
