**Klett-Cotta Verlag**, till [1977](../_/1977.md) **Ernst Klett**, full name **Verlagsgemeinschaft Ernst Klett-J.G. Cotta’sche Buchhandlung Nachf. GmbH**, is a German publishing company.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The domicile of the book publisher is in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart" title="wikipedia:Stuttgart">Stuttgart</a>, Germany. Klett-Cotta publishes books of diferent genres, from Fantasy, Nonfiction, History to books about Psychology, Educational science and Philosophy.

During the 1960s Michael Klett (\*[1938](../_/1938.md)), son of the former head of the publishing company Ernst Klett ([1911](../_/1911.md)- [1998](../_/1998.md)), effected, that [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* was incorporated in Klett's publishing program. The book was declined by every greater German publishing house before. In [1966](../_/1966.md) Ernst Klett made [Allen &amp; Unwin](../A/Allen_and_Unwin.md) an official offer for the German publishing rights of *The Lord of the Rings*. The first German *The Lord of the Rings*-edition, translated by [Margaret Carroux](../M/Margaret_Carroux.md) and [Ebba-Margareta von Freymann](../E/Ebba-Margareta_von_Freymann.md), was published [1969](../_/1969.md)/[70](../_/1970.md) under the title *Der Herr der Ringe*. With the second edition of [1972](../_/1972.md), Paperback and slipcase, *Der Herr der Ringe* became a success for Klett.

The publishing company was renamed after the merger of Ernst Klett and the publisher Cotta’sche Verlagsbuchhandlung in 1977. In [1987](../_/1987.md), *[The Complaint of Mîm the Dwarf](../T/The_Complaint_of_Mîm_the_Dwarf.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-Guide_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guide-2">[2]</a></sup> was translated by [Hans J. Schütz](../H/Hans_J._Schütz.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Guide_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guide-2">[2]</a></sup> and published in *[Klett-Cotta: Das erste Jahrzehnt 1977-1987: Ein Almanach](Klett-Cotta%253A_Das_erste_Jahrzehnt_1977-1987%253A_Ein_Almanach.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Most of Tolkien's works in German were and are published first or only by Klett-Cotta.

## Editions {#Editions}

### The Lord of the Rings (2003) {#The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(2003)}

- <a class="internal mw-magiclink-isbn" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Special:BookSources/3608930000">ISBN 3608930000</a> - Four-volume (The Lord of the Rings and Appendices) limited edition of 3,333 copies.<sup id="cite_ref-mytolkiencollection_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mytolkiencollection-4">[4]</a></sup>
- <a class="internal mw-magiclink-isbn" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Special:BookSources/3608911111">ISBN 3608911111</a> - Single-volume limited edition of 1,111 copies was released, each hand-signed and numbered by the calligrapher and book illuminator, Alexandra Remmes. This single volume, 1,416 in length, features full leather binding (smooth parchment-colored leather), blind embossing on the front cover, raised spine bands, two-color colored edges all around, two ribbon bookmarks, four-color text printing with high-quality gold embossing, and thread stitching.<sup id="cite_ref-mytolkiencollection_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mytolkiencollection-4">[4]</a></sup>
- A second limited edition of only 111 copies was also produced, identified with roman numerals I to CXI. This edition was not sold to the general public.<sup id="cite_ref-mytolkiencollection_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mytolkiencollection-4">[4]</a></sup>
