The name given to the calendar system used in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) throughout the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), and in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) and [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) throughout much of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). It divided the year into twelve months (properly called [astar](/wiki/Astar "Astar")), and included three days that did not belong to any month: [yestarë](/wiki/Yestar%C3%AB "Yestarë"), [loëndë](/wiki/Lo%C3%ABnd%C3%AB "Loëndë") and [mettarë](/wiki/Mettar%C3%AB "Mettarë").

## History {#History}

The King's Reckoning was of [Eldarin](/wiki/Eldar "Eldar") origin and calculation started from [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1](/wiki/Second_Age_1 "Second Age 1"). The [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) made some alterations more convenient to them, like dividing the *[loa](/wiki/Loa "Loa")* into shorter and more regular *[astar](/wiki/Astar "Astar")*, and, according to the custom of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) or the [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men), the year began in mid-winter instead of the spring. Later they added one more day to the [enquië](/wiki/Enqui%C3%AB "Enquië"), and reckoned the day from sunrise instead of sunset.<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

Every thousand years, a *millennial deficit* accumulated and three times in the history of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), this was adjusted in the end of each millennium.

The calendar was later used in [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor); with the defeat of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and the "resetting" of the reckoning to [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1](/wiki/Third_Age_1 "Third Age 1") ([S.A. 3442](/wiki/Third_Age_1 "Third Age 1")), and making T.A. 4 a leap year, the system was dislocated and the deficit enlarged in the following centuries.

In the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) came to [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), and adapted the calendar into the [Shire Calendar](/wiki/Shire_Calendar). [Bree](/wiki/Bree) also adopted the Shire Calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

The King's Reckoning in Gondor lasted until the loss of [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur) the last [King of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor "Kings of Gondor"). As the millennial deficit and errors accumulated, [Mardil Voronwë](/wiki/Mardil "Mardil"), the first [Ruling Steward](/wiki/Ruling_Steward), revised the system, and replaced it with the system known as the [Stewards' Reckoning](/wiki/Stewards%27_Reckoning).<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

In leap years, [loëndë](/wiki/Lo%C3%ABnd%C3%AB "Loëndë") was replaced with two [enderi](/wiki/Enderi "Enderi"); this occurred in years divisible by 4 but not by 100 (*[haranyë](/wiki/Harany%C3%AB "Haranyë")*), resulting in a 366-day year, similar to modern <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="wikipedia:Gregorian calendar">Gregorian calendar</a> leap years. In addition, in years divisible by 1000 loëndë was replaced by three enderi<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>, resulting in a 367-day year. These leap year rules held throughout the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) and continued based on the restarted numbering of [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) years, and the 2-day "millennial additions" continued through [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2000](/wiki/Third_Age_2000 "Third Age 2000"). This calendar was replaced by the [Stewards' Reckoning](/wiki/Stewards%27_Reckoning) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2060](/wiki/Third_Age_2060 "Third Age 2060").<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Days of the week {#Days_of_the_week}

When the Kings' Reckoning was first implemented the Númenóreans used the [Elven](/wiki/Elven) week of six days. At unspecified points of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) two changes were made; the first being to rename the fourth day Aldúya to Aldëa (Orgaladh in Sindarin) in order to change the dedication to the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/White_Tree" title="White Tree">White Tree</a>, and the second being to insert an extra day, Eärenya (Oraearon), after the fifth day Menelya.<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup> Thus, the eventual Númenórean week was as follows:

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

According to [Jim Allan](/wiki/Jim_Allan) in *[An Introduction to Elvish](/wiki/An_Introduction_to_Elvish)*, each of the elvish month names has a correspondence to the months of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Republican_Calendar" title="wikipedia:French Republican Calendar">French Republican Calendar</a>; for example, Nénimë/Pluviôse mean "Rainy", Súlìmë/Ventôse mean "Windy". Jim Allan suggests that Tolkien used this similarity because the French Republican Calendar was based on earlier unrecorded Germanic month names.<sup id="cite_ref-allan_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allan-9">[8]</a></sup>
