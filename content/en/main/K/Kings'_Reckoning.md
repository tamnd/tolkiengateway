The name given to the calendar system used in [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) throughout the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md), and in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) throughout much of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md). It divided the year into twelve months (properly called [astar](../A/Astar.md)), and included three days that did not belong to any month: [yestarë](../Y/Yestarë.md), [loëndë](../L/Loëndë.md) and [mettarë](../M/Mettarë.md).

## History {#History}

The King's Reckoning was of [Eldarin](../E/Eldar.md) origin and calculation started from [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [1](../S/Second_Age_1.md). The [Númenóreans](../N/Númenóreans.md) made some alterations more convenient to them, like dividing the *[loa](../L/Loa.md)* into shorter and more regular *[astar](../A/Astar.md)*, and, according to the custom of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) or the [Middle Men](../M/Middle_Men.md), the year began in mid-winter instead of the spring. Later they added one more day to the [enquië](../E/Enquië.md), and reckoned the day from sunrise instead of sunset.<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

Every thousand years, a *millennial deficit* accumulated and three times in the history of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md), this was adjusted in the end of each millennium.

The calendar was later used in [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) and [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md); with the defeat of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) and the "resetting" of the reckoning to [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1](../T/Third_Age_1.md) ([S.A. 3442](../T/Third_Age_1.md)), and making T.A. 4 a leap year, the system was dislocated and the deficit enlarged in the following centuries.

In the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) the [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) came to [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md), and adapted the calendar into the [Shire Calendar](../S/Shire_Calendar.md). [Bree](../B/Bree.md) also adopted the Shire Calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

The King's Reckoning in Gondor lasted until the loss of [Eärnur](../E/Eärnur.md) the last [King of Gondor](Kings_of_Gondor.md). As the millennial deficit and errors accumulated, [Mardil Voronwë](../M/Mardil.md), the first [Ruling Steward](../R/Ruling_Steward.md), revised the system, and replaced it with the system known as the [Stewards' Reckoning](../S/Stewards'_Reckoning.md).<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

In leap years, [loëndë](../L/Loëndë.md) was replaced with two [enderi](../E/Enderi.md); this occurred in years divisible by 4 but not by 100 (*[haranyë](../H/Haranyë.md)*), resulting in a 366-day year, similar to modern <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="wikipedia:Gregorian calendar">Gregorian calendar</a> leap years. In addition, in years divisible by 1000 loëndë was replaced by three enderi<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="../../help/R/References.md" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>, resulting in a 367-day year. These leap year rules held throughout the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) and continued based on the restarted numbering of [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) years, and the 2-day "millennial additions" continued through [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2000](../T/Third_Age_2000.md). This calendar was replaced by the [Stewards' Reckoning](../S/Stewards'_Reckoning.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2060](../T/Third_Age_2060.md).<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Days of the week {#Days_of_the_week}

When the Kings' Reckoning was first implemented the Númenóreans used the [Elven](../E/Elven.md) week of six days. At unspecified points of the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) two changes were made; the first being to rename the fourth day Aldúya to Aldëa (Orgaladh in Sindarin) in order to change the dedication to the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../W/White_Tree.md" title="White Tree">White Tree</a>, and the second being to insert an extra day, Eärenya (Oraearon), after the fifth day Menelya.<sup id="cite_ref-D2_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-D2-1">[1]</a></sup> Thus, the eventual Númenórean week was as follows:

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

According to [Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) in *[An Introduction to Elvish](../A/An_Introduction_to_Elvish.md)*, each of the elvish month names has a correspondence to the months of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Republican_Calendar" title="wikipedia:French Republican Calendar">French Republican Calendar</a>; for example, Nénimë/Pluviôse mean "Rainy", Súlìmë/Ventôse mean "Windy". Jim Allan suggests that Tolkien used this similarity because the French Republican Calendar was based on earlier unrecorded Germanic month names.<sup id="cite_ref-allan_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allan-9">[8]</a></sup>
