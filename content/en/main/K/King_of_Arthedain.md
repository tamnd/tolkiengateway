The **King of Arthedain** was the hereditary title of the rulers of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), one of the three successor kingdoms of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) after its division in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The first king of Arthedain was [Amlaith](/wiki/Amlaith), the eldest son of [Eärendur](</wiki/E%C3%A4rendur_(King_of_Arnor)> "Eärendur (King of Arnor)") the last [King of Arnor](/wiki/King_of_Arnor).<sup id="cite_ref-North_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-North-2">[2]</a></sup> The kingdom of Arnor split into three pieces after the death of Eärendur. The other two kingdoms were named [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur).

Unlike his forefathers, the first king of Arthedain, Amlaith, did not take a name in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> the "High-elven Tongue",<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but instead took a name in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), as did all the Kings of Arthedain and [Chieftains of the Dúnedain](/wiki/Chieftains_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain) that descended from him.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Beginning with [Argeleb I](/wiki/Argeleb_I) onwards to the Chieftains of the Dúnedain, their names included the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) prefix *Ar(an)-* indicating loyal claim to the whole Arnor, after the lines of [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur) failed<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and the kings of Arthedain claimed the title King of Arnor.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Practically speaking, however, their power was limited to the land of Arthedain which gradually diminished over the course of their seven hundred-year struggle with [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar).

Angmar finally triumphed over Arthedain in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1974](/wiki/Third_Age_1974 "Third Age 1974") with the destruction of [Fornost](/wiki/Fornost). Arvedui, the last king, fled to the north, where he perished in a winter storm while trying to escape the frozen wastes of [Forochel](/wiki/Forochel). Arvedui's son, Aranath, chose not to claim the kingship and instead became the first [Chieftain of the Dúnedain](/wiki/Chieftain_of_the_D%C3%BAnedain) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1976](/wiki/Third_Age_1976 "Third Age 1976").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-1">[1]</a></sup> This marked the end of the kingship, though not of the dynasty of Isildur.

## Duties and office {#Duties_and_office}

The duties would have been highly similar to those of the [Kings of Arnor](/wiki/Kings_of_Arnor), though much reduced in scale. Several kings were involved in the wars against Angmar, where they would have led the forces of Arthedain against their foes.

The kings would also share the same badges of office that the Arnorian kings once used: the [Shards of Narsil](/wiki/Shards_of_Narsil), the [Ring of Barahir](/wiki/Ring_of_Barahir), and the [Sceptre of Annuminas](/wiki/Sceptre_of_Annuminas). After the dissolution of the kingship, these would pass to the keeping of [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond), with the brief exception of the Ring of Barahir, which had to be retrieved from the [Lossoth](/wiki/Lossoth).<sup id="cite_ref-North_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-North-2">[2]</a></sup>

The kings controlled two of the three northern [Palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri): those in [Elostirion](/wiki/Elostirion) and [Annuminas](/wiki/Annuminas). After the fall of [Amon Sûl](/wiki/Amon_S%C3%BBl), they would gain the palantìr formerly housed there as well. However, the latter two palantíri would be lost forever in the shipwreck that killed Arevdui.
