**Klaus Ensikat** (born [January 16](/wiki/16_January "16 January"), [1937](/wiki/1937)) is a German illustrator, graphic artist and caricaturist.

After his apprenticeship as a window dresser, Ensikat studied at the College of Applied Arts in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obersch%C3%B6neweide" title="wikipedia:Oberschöneweide">Berlin-Oberschöneweide</a> ([1954](/wiki/1954)- [1958](/wiki/1958)).

Until [1960](/wiki/1960) he worked as a self-employed graphic artist, during the middle-sixties he worked as a teacher at an art college. From [1995](/wiki/1995) to [2002](/wiki/2002) he worked as a teacher for drawing at the college of higher education in Hamburg.

Klaus Ensikat's filigree illustrations received many prizes; in 1995 Ensikat was awarded the *Children's Literature Award* and in [1996](/wiki/1996) he received the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen_Award" title="wikipedia:Hans Christian Andersen Award">Hans Christian Andersen Award</a> of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Board_on_Books_for_Young_People_(IBBY)" title="wikipedia:International Board on Books for Young People (IBBY)">International Board on Books for Young People (IBBY)</a> for his lifework.

Klaus Ensikat illustrated the second German [The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)-edition of [1971](/wiki/1971) based on the first translation made by [Walter Scherf](/wiki/Walter_Scherf).
