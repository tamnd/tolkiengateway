The <i><b>Kalevala</b></i> is a book and epic poem which [Elias Lönnrot](../E/Elias_Lönnrot.md) compiled from [Finnish](../F/Finnish.md) and Karelian folklore in the 19th century. It is held to be the national epic of Finland and is traditionally thought of as one of the most significant works of Finnish literature.

"Kalevala" is the name of the homeland of the heroes in the book *Kalevala*.

## Tolkien and the *Kalevala* {#Tolkien_and_the_Kalevala}

The Finnish mythology, as presented in the *Kalevala*, had a profound impact on the young [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md), and became an inspiration for the creation of his [Legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md). In an early letter to [Edith Bratt](../E/Edith_Bratt.md), Tolkien mentions that he is doing a reworking of one of the stories from the *Kalevala* (resulting in the manuscript "[The Story of Kullervo](../T/The_Story_of_Kullervo.md)").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> In [1955](../_/1955.md), writing to his friend [W.H. Auden](../W/W.H._Auden.md), he says that *"the beginning of the legendarium \[...\] was in an attempt to reorganize some of the Kalevala, especially the tale of Kullervo the hapless, into a form of my own"*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Túrin Turambar and Kullervo {#Túrin_Turambar_and_Kullervo}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/G/Gallen_Kallela_Kullervos_Curse.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/b4/Gallen_Kallela_Kullervos_Curse.jpg/300px-Gallen_Kallela_Kullervos_Curse.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="537" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/b4/Gallen_Kallela_Kullervos_Curse.jpg/450px-Gallen_Kallela_Kullervos_Curse.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/b/b4/Gallen_Kallela_Kullervos_Curse.jpg 2x" data-file-width="473" data-file-height="847"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Kullervo&#39;s Curse&#34; by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akseli_Gallen-Kallela" title="wikipedia:Akseli Gallen-Kallela">Akseli Gallen-Kallela</a></figcaption></figure>

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The germ of my attempt to write legends of my own to fit my private languages was the tragic tale of the hapless Kullervo in the Finnish <i>Kalevala</i>. It remains a major matter in the legends of the First Age (which I hope to publish as <i>The Silmarillion</i>)
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien, <a href="../L/Letter_257.md" title="Letter 257">Letter 257</a></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

A considerable part of the tale of [Túrin Turambar](../T/Túrin.md) is derived from the *Kalevala*, where the counterpart of Túrin is [Kullervo](Kullervo.md), son of Kalervo.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Kalervo comes into conflict with the kin of Untamo, and soon a gang of men attacks Kalervo killing his family and men, and only one maid is spared her life to serve as a slave for Untamo. But soon the maid gives birth to Kullervo. Later Kullervo is sold to Ilmarinen, from who he soon escapes. Kullervo finds out that her parents are alive as well, and hears from them that his sister has disappeared. Kalervo has to go to pay the families taxes, and on his way back Kullervo meets a fair maiden and seduces her. Afterwards she realizes that Kullervo is her brother, and out of shame she jumps into rapids and drowns. Desperate and self destructive Kullervo returns home and attacks Untamo and slays him. After the killing he talks to his sword, and says that it has bled a lot of innocent blood, and tells it that now the sword can bleed some guilty blood as well. The sword replies:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Thus his trusty sword makes answer,<br/>
Well divining his intentions:<br/>
&#39;Why should I not drink thy life-blood,<br/>
Blood of guilty Kullerwoinen,<br/>
Since I feast upon the worthy,<br/>
Drink the life-blood of the righteous?&#39;
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Canto XXXVI, vv.302-309</cite></p>
</blockquote>

After the sword replies, Kullervo thrusts himself to the blade and dies.
