**Kelvar** was a name used by the [Valie](../V/Valar.md) [Yavanna](../Y/Yavanna.md) to refer to that part of her natural realm capable of moving or escaping, as opposed to the [olvar](../O/Olvar.md) which were rooted in place.<sup id="cite_ref-S2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S2-1">[1]</a></sup>

The kelvar, as all creatures, live within *[Ambarmenië](../A/Ambarmenië.md)* the ways created and fixed by [Ilúvatar](../I/Ilúvatar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

It is said that during the [Spring of Arda](../S/Spring_of_Arda.md), when Yavanna planted her seeds, "beasts came forth and dwelt in the grassy plains, or in the rivers and the lakes, or walked in the shadows of the woods." However singing birds didn't appear yet "for these things waited still their time in the bosom of Yavanna; but wealth there was of her imagining."

However when the [Two Lamps](../T/Two_Lamps.md) where destroyed and there was no light in the [Outer Lands](../O/Outer_Lands.md), the Valie put them to sleep and they stopped growing during the [Sleep of Yavanna](../S/Sleep_of_Yavanna.md), until there would be light again.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Only [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md)'s beasts roamed the earth in the shadows, that were kept at bay by [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md) the hunter.<sup id="cite_ref-S2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S2-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the awakening of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), some of the kelvar were said to have been inhabited by powerful spirits. Most famous among these were the great [Eagles](../E/Eagles.md) that overlooked events in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) from their mountainous eyries.<sup id="cite_ref-S2_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S2-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Kelvar* is an [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) word meaning "animals, living things that move",<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> probably derived from the stem <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=KEL&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="KEL (page does not exist)">KEL-</a>, meaning "go" or "run".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> It has been suggested that *kelvar* is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word, and that the (unattested) singular form would likely be *kelva*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> The terms kelvar and olvar, then, refer respectively to animal and plant life, and are essentially equivalent in meaning to fauna and flora.

Other words for "animal" (names which in general only referred to "four-footed beasts, and never to reptiles or birds") were [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *laman* (pl. *lamni* or *lamani*), and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) *lavan* (pl. *levain*).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md), one of [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s early conceptions of an [Elven](../E/Elvish.md) language, the word for "living creature, animal" is *cuid*. The word *meg* is glossed as "any small animal, especially mole".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
