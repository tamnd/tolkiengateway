**Kortirion** was the chief city of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa), according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Cot_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cot-1">[1]</a></sup> It later became known as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick" title="wikipedia:Warwick">Warwick</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ala_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ala-2">[2]</a></sup>

The city was located on the [Gliding Water](/wiki/Gliding_Water)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> at the heart of the island, in the realm of [Alalminórë](/wiki/Alalmin%C3%B3r%C3%AB), the Land of [Elms](/wiki/Elms).<sup id="cite_ref-Cot_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cot-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Foundation {#Foundation}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png"><img src="/w/images/thumb/5/50/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png/200px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="164" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/5/50/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/5/50/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png/400px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Cortirion.png 2x" data-file-width="742" data-file-height="607"/></a><figcaption><i>Cortirion</i> by <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a></figcaption></figure>

Kortirion was founded by [Ingil](/wiki/Ingil) son of [Inwë](/wiki/Inw%C3%AB), and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) of [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r) that came with him to [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) following the [March of Liberation](/wiki/March_of_Liberation). He called the town *Koromas*, meaning "the Resting of the Exiles of Kôr", and built a [great tower](/wiki/Tirin_na_Gilweth "Tirin na Gilweth") there. The city was afterwards often called *Kortirion*, in reference to the tower.<sup id="cite_ref-Cot_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cot-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Arrival of Eriol {#Arrival_of_Eriol}

Located in the city was the [Cottage of Lost Play](/wiki/Cottage_of_Lost_Play), where [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol), the [Mannish](/wiki/Men "Men") mariner who found Tol Eressëa, stayed for a while.<sup id="cite_ref-Cot_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cot-1">[1]</a></sup> There, he heard many tales of the history of the Elves and the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), told to him by the inhabitants of Kortirion, including [Lindo](/wiki/Lindo), the lord of the Cottage of Lost Play, his wife [Vairë](</wiki/Vair%C3%AB_(wife_of_Lindo)> "Vairë (wife of Lindo)"),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Rúmil](</wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium> "Rúmil (elf of Tirion)"), the old door-ward of the Cottage, and [Littleheart](/wiki/Littleheart), son of [Voronwë](/wiki/Voronw%C3%AB).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

After a while, he also went to the home of [Meril-i-Turinqi](/wiki/Meril-i-Turinqi), the Lady of Tol Eressëa, who lived at the foot of the tower of Ingil (her grandfather<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>), amidst a *[korin](/wiki/Korin "Korin")* of [elms](/wiki/Elms "Elms").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> There, she told him the story of *[the Chaining of Melko](/wiki/The_Chaining_of_Melko "The Chaining of Melko")*,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> and *[the Coming of the Elves and the Making of Kôr](/wiki/The_Coming_of_the_Elves_and_the_Making_of_K%C3%B4r "The Coming of the Elves and the Making of Kôr")*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Before winter came to Kortirion that year, Eriol also visited many of the homes of the [Teleri](/wiki/Vanyar#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium "Vanyar") and the [Inwir](/wiki/Inwir)<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> (the royal clan of the Teleri), who lived there, and learned more of the language, history and customs of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

### The Faring Forth {#The_Faring_Forth}

Sometime during the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century" title="wikipedia:5th century">5th century AD</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup> when the disastrous [Faring Forth](/wiki/Faring_Forth) took place, and the island of Tol Eressëa was dragged across the Ocean and anchored off the coast of the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands), Men invaded the island, along with [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs), [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls) and other evil beings.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup> In the ensuing destruction, Kortirion was one of the places that were ravaged by Men from the Great Lands, with the Elves of Kortirion fleeing from them, and eventually fading away.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup>

However, not all Men were hostile to the Elves, and these were led by [Heorrenda](/wiki/Heorrenda), [Hengest and Horsa](/wiki/Hengest_and_Horsa), the sons of Eriol.<sup id="cite_ref-War_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-War-17">[16]</a></sup> Kortirion itself was later conquered by Hengest, and became known as *Warwíc* (in the [language](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") of his people).<sup id="cite_ref-Guo_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guo-18">[17]</a></sup>

The city would eventually become known as *Warwick*.<sup id="cite_ref-War_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-War-17">[16]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Kortirion* is in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya). It is comprised of *[Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r)* (the name of the ancient elven city in Valinor) + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Tirion_(Qenya_word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tirion (Qenya word) (page does not exist)">tirion</a></i> ("(great or mighty) tower; city on a hill").<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>

In the etymology of Tolkien's earliest languages, the name of the hill of [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r) refers to its roundness. It is derived from the primitive Elvish name <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Guord&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Guord (page does not exist)">Guord</a></i>, from which came Qenya <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Qora&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Qora (page does not exist)">Qora</a></i>, and Gnomish <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gwar_(Gnomish_name)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gwar (Gnomish name) (page does not exist)">Gwar</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Guo_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guo-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) cognates of *Kortirion* are, depending on the text, either <i><b>Corthirion</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-Cor_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cor-20">[19]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> or <i><b>Mindon Gwar</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> (with <i><b>Gwarmindon</b></i> being the hypothetical true cognate of *Kortirion*). Both Qenya and Gnomish names mean "Tower of Kôr". It was also called the <i><b>Citadel of the Island</b></i> or the <i><b>Citadel of the World</b></i>, by "those that speak of it with love".<sup id="cite_ref-Cot_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cot-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes that his father intended to relate the Gnomish form *gwar-* with the first element of *War*wick ([Welsh](/wiki/Welsh): Caer*gwar*), suggesting it to be Elvish in origin.<sup id="cite_ref-Guo_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guo-18">[17]</a></sup>

The city was also called <i><b>Kormas</b></i> or <i><b>Koromas</b></i>, meaning "the Resting of the Exiles of [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r)" in Qenya, being a combination of *Kôr* + the suffix <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=-mas&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="-mas (page does not exist)">-mas</a></i> ("-ton, -by"; i.e. town).<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup> Its Gnomish cognate is <i><b>Corangos</b></i> (changed from an earlier form *Cormath*<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup>).<sup id="cite_ref-Cor_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cor-20">[19]</a></sup>

It was also called <i><b>New Kôr</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

In [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), the city was called <i><b>Warwíc</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Guo_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guo-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Early legendarium {#Early_legendarium}

In some of the outlines for the continuation of *The Book of Lost Tales*, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) decided to completely change the nature of the framework of the tales.

In that reimagining of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Tol Eressëa ceased to be identified with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a>/<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles" title="wikipedia:British Isles">British Isles</a> - instead, it became a completely different island, and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain" title="wikipedia:Great Britain">Great Britain</a> became the island of [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany), while the mariner that came to Tol Eressëa was called [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), an <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people" title="wikipedia:English people">Englishman</a> sailing *from* England/Luthany.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup>

So, as opposed to the previous conception, where the Elves of the Great Lands sailed directly to Tol Eressëa following the defeat of [Melko](/wiki/Melko) in the [March of Liberation](/wiki/March_of_Liberation), the Elves now sailed to Luthany instead.

There, they built many cities and settlements - and, after their *eventual* return to Tol Eressëa, named many places there *after* the ones in Luthany. For example, the city of Kortirion (the New) in Tol Eressëa was named after **Kortirion the Old** (i.e. Warwick) in Luthany.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup>

In one note, the capital city of Tol Eressëa where Ælfwine was brought to is called **[Rôs](/wiki/R%C3%B4s#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium)**.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[27]</a></sup> However, other notes continue to have Ælfwine come to Kortirion (the New) instead.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup>

#### *Ælfwine of England* {#Ælfwine_of_England}

In the text called *[Ælfwine of England](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine_of_England)* (c. [1920](/wiki/1920)) from *The Book of Lost Tales*, Kortirion (the Old) appears as a city in [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany) (here called <i><b>Lúthien</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[note 2]</a></sup>).

It was ruled by the [Prince of Gwar](/wiki/Prince_of_Gwar) named [Óswine](</wiki/%C3%93swine_(prince_of_Gwar)> "Óswine (prince of Gwar)"), who was friendly to the Elves that still lived on the island. In that city also dwelt the [minstrel](/wiki/Minstrels "Minstrels") [Déor](</wiki/D%C3%A9or_(father_of_%C3%86lfwine)> "Déor (father of Ælfwine)"), along with his wife [Éadgifu](/wiki/%C3%89adgifu) of [Lionesse](/wiki/Lionesse) and their son Ælfwine.

While Ælfwine was still a boy, a fierce people called the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Forodwaith_(The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Forodwaith (The Book of Lost Tales) (page does not exist)">Forodwaith</a> (i.e. <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings" title="wikipedia:Vikings">Vikings</a>) laid siege to the city, during which Óswine, the Prince of Gwar died, as well as Déor and Éadgifu, Ælfwine's parents.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[29]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

In the later version of the legendarium from the [1930](/wiki/1930), in the preamble to *[Quenta Noldorinwa](/wiki/Quenta_Noldorinwa)*, **Kortirion** was mentioned as a city in Tol Eressëa where [Eriol](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine "Ælfwine") saw and read the *[Golden Book](/wiki/Golden_Book)*, which formed the basis for his writing of the *Book of Lost Tales*.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[30]</a></sup>

The city of **Cortirion** in Tol Eressëa also appears in the conclusion to the [1937](/wiki/1937) *[Quenta Silmarillion](</wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road)> "Quenta Silmarillion (Lost Road)")*, where it is stated that [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) alone of all [Men](/wiki/Men) "brought tidings of Cortirion to the [Hither Lands](/wiki/Middle-earth "Middle-earth")".<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[31]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The city of Kortirion was inspired by the town of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick" title="wikipedia:Warwick">Warwick</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a>, where [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Bratt) lived from [1913](/wiki/1913) until her marriage with [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) in [1916](/wiki/1916), and where Tolkien visited her from [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford).<sup id="cite_ref-Ala_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ala-2">[2]</a></sup>

The Gliding Water which flows through Kortirion was probably inspired by the river <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Avon,_Warwickshire" title="wikipedia:River Avon, Warwickshire">Avon</a>, which flows through Warwick.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[32]</a></sup>

Tirin na Gilweth, the tower of Ingil, might have been inspired by a tower which, according to legend, once stood on Ethelfleda's Mound in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_Castle" title="wikipedia:Warwick Castle">Warwick Castle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[33]</a></sup>
