**Kingfishers** were bright blue birds.

## History {#History}

Kingfishers lived on the waterside, feeding on [fish](/wiki/Fish "Fish"). One notable kingfisher lived, according to [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") folklore, in the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest). It jestfully harassed [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil), who in return compared it to a weathercock, and commented that, despite its lordy appearance, it lives in a dirty home.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup> Upon flying away it lost a bright blue feather which Tom took replacing the old battered [swan](/wiki/Swans "Swans")-feather in his hat.<sup id="cite_ref-Boat_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boat-1">[1]</a></sup> Tom still bore a noticeable large blue feather on his hat when he met [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and his companions.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

[Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) was also familiar with the Kingfisher; when [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir) saw what he thought was a black squirrel or kingfisher, he asked [Anborn](/wiki/Anborn) whether there were any black kingfishers in [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood),<sup id="cite_ref-Pool_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pool-4">[3]</a></sup> a forest known for its black animals.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup> Anborn saw the mysterious creature had four limbs, and quickly dismissed both ideas. It turned out to be [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum), hiding in the [Forbidden Pool](/wiki/Forbidden_Pool).<sup id="cite_ref-Pool_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pool-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Kingfishers* were originally called *the King's fisher*, as they, like [swans](/wiki/Swans "Swans"), were royal animals<sup id="cite_ref-L240_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L240-6">[5]</a></sup> although the historical association is unknown.

## Other names {#Other_names}

Several [Eldarin](/wiki/Eldarin) words for the bird are known as well: in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), it was called <i><b>halatir</b></i> (or *halatir(no)*), from the [roots](/wiki/Sundocarm%C3%AB "Sundocarmë") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=SKAL&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="SKAL (page does not exist)">SKAL</a> and [TIR](/wiki/TIR), literally meaning "fishwatcher".<sup id="cite_ref-Etym_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Etym-7">[6]</a></sup> [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) had a similar word, <i><b>heledirn</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Etym_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Etym-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The Kingfisher, and especially the rivalry between the kingfisher and the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Old_Swan&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Old Swan (page does not exist)">Old Swan</a> in *[Bombadil Goes Boating](/wiki/Bombadil_Goes_Boating)*, was inspired by its use in European royal houses. Originally, the swan was the animal owned by kings, but later, it became the kingfisher. In [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), both yearned for the return of the [rightful King](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn").<sup id="cite_ref-L240_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L240-6">[5]</a></sup>
