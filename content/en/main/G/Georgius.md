**Georgius** was the son of [Farmer Giles](/wiki/Farmer_Giles) who became the king of the [Little Kingdom](/wiki/Little_Kingdom). In the fragmentary legend of Georgius and his page [Suovetaurilius](/wiki/Suovetaurilius) (also known as Suet) there is mention of an outpost called [Farthingho](/wiki/Farthingho) that was established to keep an eye on the neighboring [Middle Kingdom](/wiki/Middle_Kingdom).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## The Non-Sequel to the Story {#The_Non-Sequel_to_the_Story}

After *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* was published the company of [Allen and Unwin](/wiki/Allen_and_Unwin) wanted a sequel. Before and during his writing of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) asked if they would publish *[Farmer Giles of Ham](/wiki/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham)*. The problem was that *Farmer Giles* was too short for a stand-alone book. Tolkien offered to write another story set in the [Little Kingdom](/wiki/Little_Kingdom), about "the adventures of Prince George (the farmer's son) and the fat boy [Suovetaurilius](/wiki/Suovetaurilius) (vulgarly Suet) and the Battle of Otmoor"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> which would add enough material for a book. Eventually *Farmer Giles* would be published but Tolkien never wrote the sequel, which remained only a sketch.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <span></span>
