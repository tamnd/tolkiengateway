**Gurthang** was the sword of [Túrin Turambar](../T/Túrin.md), reforged from [Anglachel](../A/Anglachel.md) the sword wielded by [Beleg Cúthalion](../B/Beleg.md) when Túrin slew him in a tragic turn of events.

It was black and shone with a pale fire. With it Túrin performed great deeds but also senseless acts, and it broke when Túrin killed himself upon the sword.

## History {#History}

Túrin was led to [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md) by [Gwindor](Gwindor.md) who had aided Beleg in the rescue. Once in Nargothrond Túrin had Anglachel forged anew. He renamed the blade *Gurthang*, "Iron of Death". The sword was wielded by Túrin throughout his time at Nargothrond as he became a captain and led the [Elves of Nargothrond](../E/Elves_of_Nargothrond.md) into open warfare, a tactic they were not well versed in. His increasing fame with Gurthang gave Túrin a new name: *[Mormegil](../M/Mormegil.md)* "The Black Sword" of Nargothrond.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Túrin's exploits with the Elves of Nargothrond culminated with the [Battle of Tumhalad](../B/Battle_of_Tumhalad.md) which led to the [ruin of Nargothrond](../F/Fall_of_Nargothrond.md). There Túrin faced [Glaurung](Glaurung.md) and rose his sword against the dragon, but then he stood still by an enchantment from the dragon's look. Briefly freed, Túrin tried to give stroke against Glaurung's eyes, but the dragon imposed upon him a new enchantment that led him towards [Dor-lómin](../D/Dor-lómin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> While running in despair with his black sword drawn, he was spotted by [Tuor](../T/Tuor.md) and [Voronwë](../V/Voronwë.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Soon later, Gurthang was used by Túrin to slay the [Easterling](../E/Easterlings.md) chief [Brodda](../B/Brodda.md) in his own [hall](../B/Brodda's_hall.md)<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and it remained with him when he came to [Brethil](../B/Brethil.md). There he was recognized by [Dorlas](../D/Dorlas.md) as the Black Sword, but wishing to be rid of his past, Túrin put Gurthang away, using bows and spears instead.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Only when orcs started to threaten Brethil in numbers did Túrin once again take up Gurthang to repulse them. When [Glaurung](Glaurung.md) came to Brethil pursuing Túrin, a desperate council was held in [Ephel Brandir](../E/Ephel_Brandir.md) as to how to deal with the threat. Túrin told the [Haladin](../H/Haladin.md) of the tale of [Azaghâl](../A/Azaghâl.md) Lord of [Belegost](../B/Belegost.md) and how his knife forced Glaurung to flee, Túrin then declared "*But here is a thorn sharper and longer than the knife of Azaghâl*", and showed them Gurthang. Then the Haladin cried out "*The Black Thorn of Brethil!*"<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

Túrin succeeded in slaying Glaurung with Gurthang at [Cabed-en-Aras](../C/Cabed-en-Aras.md) but fell into a swoon when he wrested it from Glaurung's belly.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> He was then told by [Brandir](../B/Brandir.md) that his wife was in fact his sister [Nienor](../N/Nienor.md) as recounted by Glaurung, and in a rage, Túrin slew Brandir. Discovering from [Mablung](../M/Mablung.md) that this was in fact true, Túrin returned to Cabed-en-Aras to commit suicide, addressing Gurthang with courteous words:

Túrin cast himself upon Gurthang and the blade broke asunder. It was buried with Túrin beneath the [Stone of the Hapless](../S/Stone_of_the_Hapless.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>

According to a prophecy, Túrin will use his black sword in the [Final Battle](../F/Final_Battle.md) to kill Morgoth definitely.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[note 2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Gurthang* is [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) for "Iron of Death",<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup> from *[gurth](Gurth.md)* ("death") + *[ang](../A/Ang.md)* ("iron").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/L/Līga_Kļaviņa_-_Turin.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/47/L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Turin.jpg/250px-L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Turin.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="356" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/47/L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Turin.jpg/375px-L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Turin.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/4/47/L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Turin.jpg/500px-L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Turin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="854"/></a><figcaption><i>Túrin</i> by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Līga_Kļaviņa.md" title="Category:Images by Līga Kļaviņa">Līga Kļaviņa</a></figcaption></figure>

In the earliest version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*, the black sword appears for first time when Túrin is already among the [Rodothlim](../R/Rodothlim.md). He had not used any sword after killing Beleg, simply using a club, so he asked for a sword to Orodreth. A new one was made for him, "and it was made by [magic](../M/Magic.md) to be utterly black save at its edges, and those were shining bright and sharp as but [Gnome](Gnome.md)-steel may be." It was heavy and had a black sheath. Túrin named it <i><b>Gurtholfin</b></i> ("Wand of Death"). It is said that sometimes the sword jumped to Túrin's hands by itself and whispered him dark words. Now Túrin began cleansing the lands of the Rodothlim from any enemy, and became known as the Blacksword, *Mormagli* or *Mormakil* among the Gnomes.<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:83–84</sup>

Thereafter, the sword has the same function in the story as in later versions: the failed confrontation with [Glorund](Glorund.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:85</sup> killing Brodda,<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:90</sup> saving [Nienóri](../N/Nienóri.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:99</sup> and the slaying of Glorund.<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:107</sup>

The sword had become Turambar's most precious possession, for all things died when touched by it, so he tried to recover it from the dragon's body and passed out because of the poison.<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:108</sup> Later, when Turambar knew the bitter truth, he asked death to the sword, claiming it was the only thing left to him, and Gurtholfin answered it would take any blood gladly. Thus Turambar cast himself upon the sword's point and died. In this version, there is no mention of the sword being broken.<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:112</sup>

Gurtholfin will also appear in the end of the world, as it is said that "Turambar indeed shall stand beside Fionwë in the [Great Wrack](Great_Wrack.md), and [Melko](../M/Melko.md) and his drakes shall curse the sword of Mormakil".<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:116</sup>

### Early etymology {#Early_etymology}

The name *Gurtholfin* ("Wand of Death") is in [Gnomish](Gnomish.md), from *gurth(u)* ("death") + *olf(in)* ("branch, wand, stick").<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[15]</a></sup> The first form of the name was *Gortholfin*, but it was changed during the writing of the narrative.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[16]</a></sup> Its [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) cognate was <i><b>Urdolwen</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[17]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The scene of Túrin's suicide is directly inspired by the death of Kullervo in the *[Kalevala](../K/Kalevala.md)*, although Tolkien gave more importance to the sword in his story.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[18]</a></sup> After knowing he had committed incest, Kullervo asks his sword to take his guilty flesh:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The sword [...]<br/>answered with this word: &#34;Why<br/>should I not eat what I like<br/>not eat guilty flesh<br/>not drink blood that is to blame?<br/>I&#39;ll eat even guiltless flesh<br/>I&#39;ll drink blameless blood&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[19]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

In the first version of the story in *The Book of Lost Tales* (see above), the words are even closer to the source of inspiration, as the black sword answers that it will drink any blood without distinction.<sup id="cite_ref-Foaloke_16-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foaloke-16">[14]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:112</sup>
