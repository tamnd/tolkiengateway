**Grór** was a [Dwarf](../D/Dwarves.md) and a significant leader among the [people of Durin](../D/Durin's_Folk~15256.md).

## Life {#Life}

### Birth and early Life {#Birth_and_early_Life}

He was born into the [House of Durin](../H/House_of_Durin.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2563](../T/Third_Age_2563.md) during its last years in the [Grey Mountains](Grey_Mountains.md). He was the youngest of the three sons of King [Dáin I](../D/Dáin_I.md), who ruled [Durin's folk](../D/Durin's_folk.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Line_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Line-1">[1]</a></sup>

When Grór was seven years old, in [2570](../T/Third_Age_2570.md) dragons began [afflicting the dwarves in the north](../W/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Dragons.md). Nineteen years later, in [2589](../T/Third_Age_2589.md) his father King [Dáin I](../D/Dáin_I.md) was slain by a [cold-drake](../C/Cold-drakes.md) outside [his hall](../D/Dáin's_Halls~64102.md) in the Grey Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> A short time afterwards most of Durin's Folk left the Grey Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-Grór_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grór-3">[3]</a></sup> In [2590](../T/Third_Age_2590.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA2590_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA2590-4">[4]</a></sup> Grór left the Grey Mountains with many followers and established a colony in the Iron Hills<sup id="cite_ref-Grór_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grór-3">[3]</a></sup> becoming the Lord of the Iron Hills. In the same year,<sup id="cite_ref-TA2590_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA2590-4">[4]</a></sup> his brother [Thrór](../T/Thrór.md), the oldest son<sup id="cite_ref-Line_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Line-1">[1]</a></sup> and heir of Dáin, and the rest of the Dwarves of the Grey Mountains moved back to the Lonely Mountain<sup id="cite_ref-Thrór_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thrór-5">[5]</a></sup>.

### Lord of the Iron Hills, and later life {#Lord_of_the_Iron_Hills,_and_later_life}

Over the next few centuries a large quantity of ore was traded between the Dwarves of the Lonely Mountain and the Dwarves of the Iron Hills,<sup id="cite_ref-Thrór_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thrór-5">[5]</a></sup> until the Dragon [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md) [destroyed](../S/Sack_of_Erebor.md) the Kingdom under the Mountain and scattered the Longbeards who dwelt there<sup id="cite_ref-Thrór_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thrór-5">[5]</a></sup> in [2770](../T/Third_Age_2770.md)<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>. Most of the survivors subsequently went to their kinsmen in the Iron Hills.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [2799](../T/Third_Age_2799.md)<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> the final battle of the [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](../W/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs.md) [occurred in the Dimrill Dale](../B/Battle_of_Azanulbizar.md). Grór's son [Náin](<../N/Náin_(son_of_Grór).md>), accompanied by his son [Dáin](../D/Dáin_Ironfoot.md) led reinforcements from the Iron Hills to the battle, but was killed by the master of the [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) of [Moria](../M/Moria.md), [Azog](../A/Azog.md), before the [East Gate](Great_Gates.md) of Moria. The then still quite young Dáin killed Azog and the dwarves won the battle. With the war over, Dáin led his people home to the Iron Hills.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Six years later, in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2805](../T/Third_Age_2805.md), Grór died at 241 years of age and his grandson Dáin became Lord of the Iron Hills.

Many years later, in [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md) when Thrór's last descendant, [Thorin](../T/Thorin.md), was killed in the [Battle of Five Armies](../B/Battle_of_Five_Armies.md), Dáin II Ironfoot became the new [King under the Mountain](../K/King_under_the_Mountain.md) and [King of the Longbeards](../K/Kings_of_Durin's_Folk.md),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> a title that remained with the descendants of Grór<sup id="cite_ref-Line_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Line-1">[1]</a></sup>.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) has suggested that *Grór* (like *[Gróin](Gróin.md#Etymology)*) is derived from [Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md) *gróa* ("to grow"), thus meaning "Growing-one".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
