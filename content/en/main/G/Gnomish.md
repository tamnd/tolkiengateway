**Gnomish**, or **I·Lam na·Ngoldathon** ("The Tongue of the [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes)"),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> the name of which is **Goldogrin**, was the language of the Gnomes spoken in the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands).

This language was the first incarnation of the [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic)-like [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) language invented by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") during the first drafts of his [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") during [1917](/wiki/1917) and later. The language would evolve into the language known as [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) around [1930](/wiki/1930) and finally envisioned as [the language of the Sindar](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin") during the writing of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.

Unlike [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), and like [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), Goldogrin was an inflected language with a full case system; For example, the genitive plural "of the Gnomes" is *Ngoldathon* as seen above. The ending *-on* is also known from later Quenya. It is also seen in later names such as [Caras Galadhon](/wiki/Caras_Galadhon) (said to be [Silvan Elvish](/wiki/Silvan_Elvish)) and perhaps [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion).

Gnomish is notable for being one of the very few languages by Tolkien about which he proceeded into assembling a definite [lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon "Gnomish Lexicon") and grammar notes (published in [*Parma Eldalamberon*, issue 11](/wiki/Parma_Eldalamberon_11 "Parma Eldalamberon 11")).

Since Sindarin is a continuous revision of Gnomish, it kept its Celtic phonology, some common grammar elements (like the <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=-in&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="-in (page does not exist)">-in</a></i> plural ending and Welsh style mutations) and much of the vocabulary (eg. <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Lam&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lam (page does not exist)">lam</a></i> "language", <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Mab&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mab (page does not exist)">mab</a></i> "hand", *[gloss](/wiki/Gloss "Gloss")* "white").

Several Gnomish words seem like they would derive from [roots](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") known from *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)* or that they would belong to a dialect of Sindarin; for example the word *gweg* "man" could have been a Sindarin word derived from <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=WEG&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="WEG (page does not exist)">WEG</a> (see [Denweg](/wiki/Denweg)).
