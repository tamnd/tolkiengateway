**Golfimbul** was a king of the [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md#Orcs_and_Goblins) of [Mount Gram](../M/Mount_Gram.md).<sup id="cite_ref-HParty_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HParty-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Golfimbul led a band of Orcs in an invasion of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2747](../T/Third_Age_2747.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [Bandobras Took](../B/Bandobras_Took.md) met Golfimbul in the [Northfarthing](../N/Northfarthing.md) of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) and defeated him in the [Battle of Greenfields](../B/Battle_of_Greenfields.md). During the battle Bandobras, who was large enough to ride a horse, charged at Golfimbul and knocked off his head with a club. Golfimbul's head flew through the air for 100 yards and went down a rabbit hole. It is said that this is how the game of [golf](Golf.md) was invented.<sup id="cite_ref-HParty_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HParty-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Jim Allan suggests that *Golfimbul* has a Scandinavian origin, means "Wind-terrible" and contains the elements *Gol-* ("wind") and *Fimbul* ("might" or "great").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

"Fimbul" is Old Norse for "great".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Robert Foster](../R/Robert_Foster.md) notes that the connection with the English word "golf" suggests that the name is either fictitious or translated [Westron](../W/Westron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

At an early stage in writing *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) used the name **[Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md)** for the Goblin-leader, which also contained the word "golf" for the joke about the origin of the game (although not as prominently as in "Golfimbul").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Many years later, in the never-finished 1960 rewrite of the story, [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) intended to change the name to **Gulfimbul**.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) speculated that this was either a further evolution of Tolkien's languages or because he was dropping the golf-joke reference.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> The 1960 revision was an effort to "upgrade" *The Hobbit* to the same epic level as *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.

### Games {#Games}

<dl><dd>In <a class="mw-redirect" href="../B/Brockenborings.md" title="Brockenborings">Brockenborings</a> there is a wooden statue depicting Bandobras standing in a golf pose over Golfimbul&#39;s head. Every Spring, <a href="../T/The_Shire.md" title="The Shire">the Shire</a> celebrates Bullroarer Took Day when the Hobbits place tankards of the drink under his statue in his honor.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The epic book prologue in the Shire involves the player trying to thwart <b>Lubach</b>, who believes that he is the descendant of Golfimbul, from invading the Shire with a group of Goblins. He dwells in a landmark in the Greenfields referred to as <b>Golfimbul&#39;s Hole</b>.</dd></dl>
