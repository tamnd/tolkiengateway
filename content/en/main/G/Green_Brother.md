The **Green Brother** is a mysterious character in the *[Father Christmas Letters](/wiki/Letters_from_Father_Christmas "Letters from Father Christmas")*. He is the son of [Grandfather Yule](/wiki/Grandfather_Yule) and the brother of [Father Christmas](/wiki/Father_Christmas).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Like Father Christmas, the Green Brother collects food, clothes, and toys for poor children.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Father's Christmas's green trousers were a present from his green brother.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

While Deborah and Ivor Rogers argued in *[J.R.R. Tolkien: A Critical Biography](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_A_Critical_Biography)* (1982) that the Green Brother represented "the embodiment of ancient springtime fertility myths" (suggestive of the <a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight_(disambiguation)" title="Sir Gawain and the Green Knight (disambiguation)">Green Knight</a>), Kris Swank has suggested that the Green Brother:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>might have been Tolkien’s explanation to his children why Victorian pictures of Father Christmas showed him wearing green robes. Grandfather Yule, whose name is also Nicholas ‚after the Saint‛ (FCL 39), might, then, refer to the pre-Victorian image of the mid-winter spirit
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Kris Swank<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

The Green Brother would thus in the latter's interpretation rather be a Christmas figure than a summer figure.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
