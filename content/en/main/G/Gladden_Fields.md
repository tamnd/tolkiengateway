The **Gladden Fields** ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) **Loeg Ningloron**) was, at the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), a large marsh on the east side of the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) that extended along either side of its tributary, the [Gladden River](/wiki/Gladden_River) (Sîr Ninglor), south of the [Carrock](/wiki/Carrock).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In ancient times a lake had formed at their meeting-place, but by the beginning of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) the lake had been replaced by a wilderness of marshes and islets filled with reeds, rushes, and vast clumps of yellow irises grown taller than a man that grew there in hosts.<sup id="cite_ref-Note_13_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note_13-2">[2]</a></sup> In the 25th century it was still characterised by great beds of iris and flowering reeds.<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) when the [Silvan Elves](/wiki/Silvan_Elves) first entered the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin), they found a lake in the deep depression where the Anduin and Gladden met. Although wider to the west, the lake probably did reach the sloping edge of [Greenwood the Great](/wiki/Mirkwood "Mirkwood") . After many years the lake filled with sediment and became a marsh.<sup id="cite_ref-Note_13_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note_13-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2](/wiki/Third_Age_2 "Third Age 2")<sup id="cite_ref-TA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-4">[4]</a></sup> near this place, on a slope east of the Anduin, [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) and his three oldest sons were ambushed by [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) when they marched back towards [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor). Isildur's sons were killed in that battle, while he attempted to escape using the power of invisibility of [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). He ran to the Anduin and tried to cross it. Due to the current he was swept downstream until he reached the western bank near the inflow of the river Gladden. But the Ring had slipped from Isildur's finger and when he stood up he was seen by Orcs, who laid there to kill any survivors of the ambush, and was slain by their arrows.<sup id="cite_ref-shadow_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadow-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

During the reign of King [Calimehtar](</wiki/Calimehtar_(King_of_Gondor)> "Calimehtar (King of Gondor)") of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) (who died in [1936](/wiki/Third_Age_1936 "Third Age 1936")) the [Éothéod](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od) were a small people living in the Vale of Anduin; their southern boundary was the Gladden Fields.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

During the height of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar), after [1356](/wiki/Third_Age_1356 "Third Age 1356"), some [Stoors](/wiki/Stoors) from the [Angle of Eriador](/wiki/Angle "Angle") passed again the Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-TA_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-4">[4]</a></sup> and probably about 1410<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> a band resettled in the Vales.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> It was here, about [2463](/wiki/Third_Age_2463 "Third Age 2463"), that the [hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") [Déagol](/wiki/D%C3%A9agol) boated and while fishing retrieved the One Ring; only to be killed by his friend [Sméagol](/wiki/Sm%C3%A9agol).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Borondir](/wiki/Borondir), the messenger of Gondor sent by the Steward [Cirion](/wiki/Cirion) in [2510](/wiki/Third_Age_2510 "Third Age 2510")<sup id="cite_ref-TA_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-4">[4]</a></sup> to seek aid from the Éothéod (who by then had migrated farther up the Anduin valley), was pursued as he rode north as far as the Gladden Fields.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2851](/wiki/Third_Age_2851 "Third Age 2851"), after a meeting of the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council), [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) began searching near the Gladden Fields for the One Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-4">[4]</a></sup> He became alarmed to discover that [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)'s servants were also searching the region.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

From [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *glaedene*, "gladden" is another name for the "flag" or "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_(plant)" title="wikipedia:Iris (plant)">iris</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-11">[11]</a></sup> [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) identified the "gladden" as the yellow-flowered species <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_pseudacorus" title="wikipedia:Iris pseudacorus">Iris pseudacorus</a></i> growing in streams and marshes, and not the species now usually spelt <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/gladdon" title="wikipedia:gladdon">gladdon</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Tolkien suggested to translators to avoid if possible the 'learned' name iris.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-11">[11]</a></sup>
