**Gimilkhâd** was the younger son of Queen [Inzilbêth](/wiki/Inzilb%C3%AAth) and King [Ar-Gimilzôr](/wiki/Ar-Gimilz%C3%B4r) of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor). His older brother [Inziladûn](/wiki/Inzilad%C3%BBn) followed his mother's beliefs (she was secretly one of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful)). Inziladûn took the royal title of [Tar-Palantir](/wiki/Tar-Palantir) and attempted to lead the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) back to reverence for the [Valar](/wiki/Valar). However, Gimilkhâd believed in the ways of his father, became the leader of the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men), and opposed the policies of his brother. His son usurped the [Sceptre](/wiki/Sceptre_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Sceptre of Númenor") of Tar-Palantir's daughter [Míriel](/wiki/Tar-M%C3%ADriel "Tar-Míriel"), took the title of [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n),<sup id="cite_ref-Tar-Palantir_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tar-Palantir-2">[2]</a></sup> and brought destruction upon the island realm.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Gimilkhâd* is an [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) name, but its meaning is not glossed. Paul Strack notices the first element must be *[gimil](/wiki/Gimil "Gimil")* ("stars"), but it is not clear what the last element *khâd* means.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Line of Elros](/wiki/The_Line_of_Elros:_Kings_of_N%C3%BAmenor "The Line of Elros: Kings of Númenor")*, Gimilkhâd's birth occurred in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3044](/wiki/Second_Age_3044 "Second Age 3044") and his death in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3243](/wiki/Second_Age_3243 "Second Age 3243") (indicating an age of 199).<sup id="cite_ref-Ar-Gimilzor_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ar-Gimilzor-1">[1]</a></sup> In *The Silmarillion* it is stated that "Gimilkhâd died two years before his two hundredth year".<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[3]</a></sup>
