**Gilraen the Fair** ([T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2907](../T/Third_Age_2907.md)-[3007](../T/Third_Age_3007.md)) was a [Dúnadan of the North](../D/Dúnadan_of_the_North.md), the daughter of [Dírhael](../D/Dírhael.md) and [Ivorwen](../I/Ivorwen.md), and a descendant of the first chieftain, [Aranarth](../A/Aranarth.md).

## History {#History}

Gilraen was born in the year [2907](../T/Third_Age_2907.md) of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> [Arathorn II](../A/Arathorn_II.md) (heir to [Arador](../A/Arador.md), the [Chieftain of the Dúnedain](../C/Chieftains_of_the_Dúnedain.md)) sought to marry her, but her father Dírhael opposed the match because Gilraen was younger than was customary for marriage and Arathorn was a "stern man of full age".<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup> Dírhael also opposed the match because he had foreseen that Arathorn would live a short life. However, his wife Ivorwen was also foresighted, and she persuaded Dírhael that a hasty marriage between Arathorn and Gilraen was necessary if Arathorn's life was to be short, saying that

> \[...\] The days are darkening before the storm, and great things are to come. If these two wed now, hope may be born for our people; but if they delay, it will not come while this age lasts.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup>

Gilraen and Arathorn were married in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2929](../T/Third_Age_2929.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Arathorn became Chieftain of the Dúnedain upon his father Arador's death a year later, and Gilraen bore a son, [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md), in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2931](../T/Third_Age_2931.md). Arathorn was killed while riding out against Orcs with [Elladan and Elrohir](../E/Elladan_and_Elrohir.md) in [T.A. 2933](../T/Third_Age_2933.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup>, only four years after his marriage to Gilraen.

With Arathorn's death, Gilraen's young son Aragorn became the [Heir of Isildur](../H/Heir_of_Isildur.md). To protect Isildur's Heir from Sauron, Gilraen and Aragorn were brought to [Imladris](../R/Rivendell.md) to live in [Elrond's](../E/Elrond.md) household, where Aragorn was known as [Estel](../E/Estel.md) and his real name and lineage were kept secret even from him until his twentieth year.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup>

When Aragorn revealed his love for [Arwen](../A/Arwen.md), Gilraen counseled caution. She stated that Arwen's lineage was more noble than Aragorn's, that it was not fitting for Elves and humans to marry, and that the Elvish ancestry of the Númenoreans, which both she and Aragorn shared, had come about in "another age of the world, before our race was diminished."<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup> She thought that Elrond would disapprove of the match, and that his support of the Heirs of Isildur -- without which they "would soon come to an end"-- might therefore be endangered by Aragorn's love for Arwen.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup>

Sometime after [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2980](../T/Third_Age_2980.md), Gilraen returned to her people in [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md), after which she seldom saw her son. Her final words to Aragorn, the [linnod](../L/Linnod.md) *Ónen i-Estel Edain, ú-chebin estel anim*, referenced her son's childhood name *Estel,* or Hope: "I gave [Hope](../E/Estel.md) to the Dúnedain, I have kept no hope for myself."<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-2">[2]</a></sup>

She died less than a year later, in [3007](../T/Third_Age_3007.md),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> aged one-hundred years.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Gilraen* is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name. It means "one adorned with a tressure set with small gems in its network" and is a combination of *[gil](Gil.md)* ("star") and *raen* ("netted", "enlaced").<sup id="cite_ref-VT42_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT42-5">[5]</a></sup> More loosely translated, it means "Netted Star".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It was a common name given to women of high rank among the [Rangers of the North](../R/Rangers_of_the_North.md), because they used to wear such tressures.<sup id="cite_ref-VT42_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT42-5">[5]</a></sup>
