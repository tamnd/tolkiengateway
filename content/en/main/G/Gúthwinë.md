**Gúthwinë** was the sword of [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer).<sup id="cite_ref-Sortie_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sortie-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Guthwine_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guthwine-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On 29 February [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Éomer engaged and killed the Uruk leader [Uglúk](/wiki/Ugl%C3%BAk) in a duel of swords, presumably wielding Gúthwinë<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>.

When [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) and his companions arrived at [Meduseld](/wiki/Meduseld) on 2 March, Éomer had been imprisoned by [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden) on the advice of [Gríma](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma). Ordered by Théoden to set Éomer free, [Háma](/wiki/H%C3%A1ma) returned Éomer's sword to Éomer at Éomer's request. Éomer knelt before Théoden and offered him his sword. At Gandalf's suggestion, Théoden took the sword and brandished it, and it seemed to those who watched that strength and firmness returned to his arm.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Éomer carried Gúthwinë at the [Battle of the Hornburg](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Hornburg). When he and [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) led a sortie against the party battering the gate of the [Hornburg](/wiki/Hornburg), he drew the sword<sup id="cite_ref-Sortie_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sortie-1">[1]</a></sup> and gave the battle cry "Gúthwinë for the Mark!"<sup id="cite_ref-Guthwine_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guthwine-2">[2]</a></sup>. This is the only place in the text where the name of the sword appears.

At the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields), upon seeing the Standard of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) appear on the lead ship approaching the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Harlond" title="Harlond">Harlond</a>, Éomer cast his sword into the air and sang as he caught it.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It can be assumed that the sword was Gúthwinë.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Gúthwinë* is [Old English](/wiki/Old_English),<sup id="cite_ref-RC_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-7">[7]</a></sup> representing a [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) word meaning "battle-friend".<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-8">[8]</a></sup> [Christopher Gilson](/wiki/Christopher_Gilson) and [Wayne Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) suggest that it is a compound of *guÞ* ("war", "battle", "fight") and *wine* ("friend").<sup id="cite_ref-RC_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-8">[8]</a></sup> The word guþwine is translated as "comrade" in the online Bosworth-Toller Dictionary of Old English.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
