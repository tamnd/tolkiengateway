<i><b>gar</b></i> is a [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) word of uncertain meaning.

The word appears in two places:

1. in a phrase on a draft of [Thrór's Map](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r%27s_Map): "<i><a href="/wiki/Lheben" title="Lheben">Lheben</a> <a href="/wiki/T%C3%A2l" title="Tâl">teil</a> <a href="/wiki/Brand_(word)#Noldorin" title="Brand (word)">brann</a> <a href="/wiki/I" title="I">i</a> <a href="/wiki/Annon" title="Annon">annon</a> <a href="/wiki/Ar" title="Ar">ar</a> <a href="/wiki/N%C3%AAl" title="Nêl">neledh</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Neledhi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Neledhi (page does not exist)">neledhi</a> <b>gar</b> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Godrebh&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Godrebh (page does not exist)">godrebh</a></i>"<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>
2. in an Elvish poem published in *[The Monsters and the Critics](/wiki/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics)*: "\[...\] <i>Damrod dir hanach dalath benn <b>gar</b> meilien</i> \[...\]"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

David Kiltz has suggested that *gar* likely means "to go", or something similar.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond "Wayne G. Hammond") and [Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull "Christina Scull") also appear to translate *gar* as "go" in their translation of the phrase on the draft of Thrór's Map.<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earlier [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish), *gar* means "place, district".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
