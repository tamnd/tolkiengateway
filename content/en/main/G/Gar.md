<i><b>gar</b></i> is a [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) word of uncertain meaning.

The word appears in two places:

1. in a phrase on a draft of [Thrór's Map](../T/Thrór's_Map.md): "<i><a href="../L/Lheben.md" title="Lheben">Lheben</a> <a href="../T/Tâl.md" title="Tâl">teil</a> <a href="../B/Brand_(word).md#Noldorin" title="Brand (word)">brann</a> <a href="../I/I.md" title="I">i</a> <a href="../A/Annon.md" title="Annon">annon</a> <a href="../A/Ar.md" title="Ar">ar</a> <a href="../N/Nêl.md" title="Nêl">neledh</a> <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Neledhi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Neledhi (page does not exist)">neledhi</a> <b>gar</b> <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Godrebh&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Godrebh (page does not exist)">godrebh</a></i>"<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>
2. in an Elvish poem published in *[The Monsters and the Critics](../T/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics.md)*: "\[...\] <i>Damrod dir hanach dalath benn <b>gar</b> meilien</i> \[...\]"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

David Kiltz has suggested that *gar* likely means "to go", or something similar.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) also appear to translate *gar* as "go" in their translation of the phrase on the draft of Thrór's Map.<sup id="cite_ref-AI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earlier [Gnomish](Gnomish.md), *gar* means "place, district".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
