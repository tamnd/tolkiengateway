**Ereinion Gil-galad** was the fourth and last [High King of the Noldor](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

Gil-galad was *either*<sup><a href="#Parentage">NB</a></sup> the son of [Orodreth](/wiki/Orodreth) and grandson of [Angrod](/wiki/Angrod) and [Eldalótë](/wiki/Eldal%C3%B3t%C3%AB),<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-3">[3]</a></sup> or the son of [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon) and thus grandson of [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin),<sup id="cite_ref-Fingon_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fingon-4">[4]</a></sup> since there were different accounts about his actual parentage, while the precise year and place of his birth invariably remained murky (see *[below](#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium)*).

In any case, he was still an [elven-child](/wiki/Elven_life_cycle "Elven life cycle") at the time of the [Dagor Bragollach](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach) when [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) broke the [Siege of Angband](/wiki/Siege_of_Angband) in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [455](/wiki/First_Age_455 "First Age 455"). As a result, shortly after the [fall of Fingolfin](/wiki/Fall_of_Fingolfin "Fall of Fingolfin") a [year later](/wiki/First_Age_456 "First Age 456"), his father sent his wife and son to [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan) at the [Havens of the Falas](/wiki/Havens_of_the_Falas) for safekeeping. After the capture of [Minas Tirith](</wiki/Minas_Tirith_(Beleriand)> "Minas Tirith (Beleriand)") by the forces of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), the [Pass of Sirion](/wiki/Pass_of_Sirion) was open to Morgoth's hosts, although they were still kept at bay by the still mighty realm of [Hithlum](/wiki/Hithlum) and also the power of [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond).<sup id="cite_ref-Fingon_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fingon-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

But at the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad) in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [472](/wiki/First_Age_472 "First Age 472"), the [Union of Maedhros](/wiki/Union_of_Maedhros) was utterly defeated and [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon), High King of the Noldor, slain by [Gothmog](</wiki/Gothmog_(balrog)> "Gothmog (balrog)") the Lord of [Balrogs](/wiki/Balrogs), so the crown passed to Fingon's brother [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) in [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin). After Hithlum was destroyed and occupied by the [Easterlings](</wiki/Easterlings_(First_Age)> "Easterlings (First Age)"), there was no power left that could withstand the enemies, and the ports at the Falas were [besieged and captured](/wiki/Fall_of_the_Falas "Fall of the Falas") by the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) in [473](/wiki/First_Age_473 "First Age 473"). Yet Círdan, Gil-galad, and many other Elves could flee from death on ship and established a refuge upon the [Isle of Balar](/wiki/Isle_of_Balar).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Gil-galad finally received the [Kingship of the Noldor](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor "High King of the Noldor") in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [510](/wiki/First_Age_510 "First Age 510"), when Gondolin [was lost](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin") and Turgon died.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Beside the refuge upon Balar, he and Círdan also maintained the small, hidden port at the [Mouths of Sirion](/wiki/Mouths_of_Sirion), where many refugees from Nargothrond, [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), and Gondolin were sheltered, until the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) ravaged between [545](/wiki/First_Age_545 "First Age 545") and [587](/wiki/First_Age_587 "First Age 587") at the very end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

After the destruction of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) during the War of Wrath, which marked the beginning of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), Gil-galad founded a kingdom in [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) in the far north-west of Middle-earth, roughly between the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) and the [Great Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer") around the [Gulf of Lhûn](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lh%C3%BBn) and the havens of [Forlond](/wiki/Forlond), [Harlond](</wiki/Harlond_(Lindon)> "Harlond (Lindon)"), and [Mithlond](/wiki/Mithlond) were founded in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1](/wiki/Second_Age_1 "Second Age 1"). Gil-galad was the last heir of the kings of the Noldor in [exile](/wiki/Exile_of_the_Noldor "Exile of the Noldor"), and acknowledged as High King of the Elves of the West. Many Elves, both the [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar) and the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor), joined him. But soon, there was again unrest among the Noldor, and many of them left Lindon. Led by [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor), grandson of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor), they founded the realm of [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion), probably also stirred up by the finding of *[mithril](/wiki/Mithril "Mithril")* in [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Moria "Moria"), in c. [750](/wiki/Second_Age_750 "Second Age 750"). Also, some of the Sindar and many of the [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor) did not wish to live alongside the Noldor, who had done them a [great evil](/wiki/Second_Kinslaying "Second Kinslaying"), and migrated eastwards to [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) or [Greenwood the Great](/wiki/Mirkwood "Mirkwood").<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-12">[12]</a></sup>

When Prince [Aldarion](/wiki/Tar-Aldarion "Tar-Aldarion") of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) came to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), they established friendship with the [Elves](/wiki/Elves). In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [882](/wiki/Second_Age_882 "Second Age 882") Gil-galad gave him [a letter for his father](/wiki/Gil-galad%27s_letter "Gil-galad's letter"), the [King of Númenor](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor), [Tar-Meneldur](/wiki/Tar-Meneldur). He warned him that a new shadow had arisen in the [East](/wiki/East) and besought him for aid.<sup id="cite_ref-Aldarion_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aldarion-13">[13]</a></sup>

Around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1000](/wiki/Second_Age_1000 "Second Age 1000"), [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) tried to make contact with the Elves under the name of [Annatar](/wiki/Sauron#Other_names "Sauron"), the "*Lord of Gifts*", but Gil-galad and Círdan did not trust him and rejected his proposals. However, Sauron was welcomed in Eregion and the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power) were forged. Around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1600](/wiki/Second_Age_1600 "Second Age 1600"), Sauron forged [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") in secret, and then the Elves of Eregion found out that they were betrayed. Sauron demanded the Rings, and when they refused, in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1693](/wiki/Second_Age_1693 "Second Age 1693") the [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron) began. Celebrimbor had rescued the [Three Rings](/wiki/Three_Rings) of the Elves in time, sending [Vilya](/wiki/Vilya) and [Narya](/wiki/Narya) to Gil-galad and [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan) in Lindon, respectively, while the third ring [Nenya](/wiki/Nenya) was given to [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel).

As Sauron invaded [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), Gil-galad sent [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) to aid Eregion and also called on Númenor for aid. Elrond was unable to avert Sauron and retreated to [Imladris](/wiki/Imladris) with some survivors. Eriador was being overrun until a great fleet of the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) sent by [Tar-Minastir](/wiki/Tar-Minastir) arrived at Lindon. With united forces, Sauron's army was driven back and defeated in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1701](/wiki/Second_Age_1701 "Second Age 1701"). After this war, the Elves were not further troubled by Sauron for a long time, and Gil-galad appointed Elrond as his vice-regent, passing the ring Vilya to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Vilya_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vilya-14">[14]</a></sup>

When [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n) captured Sauron and took him to Númenor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3262](/wiki/Second_Age_3262 "Second Age 3262"), the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands) at last found peace, and Gil-galad was free to extend his dominion to the north, south, and east beyond the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin).

After the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) in [3319](/wiki/Second_Age_3319 "Second Age 3319"), [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and his sons came to Middle-earth and founded the realms of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in the south and [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in the north. To honour Elendil, Gil-galad built the [White Towers](/wiki/White_Towers), which housed [one of](/wiki/Elostirion-stone "Elostirion-stone") the *[Palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri)*.

But Sauron also escaped, and Gondor was soon attacked; Elendil's son [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) had to flee, and sailed north to his father. Elendil and Gil-galad then formed the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) against Sauron in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3431](/wiki/Second_Age_3431 "Second Age 3431").<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-12">[12]</a></sup> It was said that the hosts of Gil-galad joined Elendil at [Amon Sûl](/wiki/Weathertop "Weathertop").<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-15">[15]</a></sup>

In the following years, the Allies marched to Imladris where they camped for three years. Then they crossed the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and marched south, where they [defeated](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad "Battle of Dagorlad") a great army outside [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). They broke through [Cirith Gorgor](/wiki/Cirith_Gorgor) and besieged [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)'s [Dark Tower](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr "Barad-dûr").<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-12">[12]</a></sup> Gil-galad fought with his long sword and his spear [Aeglos](</wiki/Aeglos_(spear)> "Aeglos (spear)") that was much feared by the enemy.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Council_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-16">[16]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:243</sup>

By his power, the siege was broken, and his army advanced to the slopes of [Orodruin](/wiki/Orodruin) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441"). There he was engaged by Elendil and Gil-galad in a single combat. In the end of the [Siege of Barad-dûr](/wiki/Siege_of_Barad-d%C3%BBr), Sauron was defeated but both Gil-galad and Elendil were killed in the act.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SA_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-12">[12]</a></sup> In the [Scroll](/wiki/Scroll_of_Isildur "Scroll of Isildur") he wrote in [Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Tirith "Minas Tirith") before riding north, Isildur wrote that Gil-galad was killed by the heat of [Enemy's](/wiki/Dark_Lord "Dark Lord") fiery hand.<sup id="cite_ref-Council_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-16">[16]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:253</sup>

*Gil-galad* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, meaning "Star of Radiance",<sup id="cite_ref-Finwain_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finwain-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Galad_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-3">[3]</a></sup> "Starlight"<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-15">[15]</a></sup> or "Star of bright light". The name consists of the elements *[gil](/wiki/Gil "Gil")* ("star") + *[galad](/wiki/Galad "Galad")* ("radiance").<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

According to early version, 'Gil-galad' was in fact his [mother-name](/wiki/Amiless%C3%AB "Amilessë").<sup id="cite_ref-Parentage_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parentage-19">[19]</a></sup> However, later version explains it was an [epessë](/wiki/Epess%C3%AB "Epessë") given to him because his armour and shield were overlaid with silver and set with a device of white stars, shone from afar like a star in sunlight or moonlight and could be seen by Elvish eyes at a great distance if he stood upon a height; by this name he was chiefly remembered in legend.<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-3">[3]</a></sup>

*Gil-galad* already appears in *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)* as [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) for "Starlight".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

As a descendant of kings, the Sindarin [birth name](/wiki/Father-name "Father-name") of Gil-galad was <i><b>Ereinion</b></i> ("Scion of Kings"),<sup id="cite_ref-Galad_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galad-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> from plural *[aran](/wiki/Aran "Aran")* ("king") + *[-ion](/wiki/-ion)* (patronymic suffix).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

According to early version, Gil-galad's [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) father-name was <i><b>Artanáro</b></i>, or in Sindarin <i><b>Rodnor</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Parentage_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parentage-19">[19]</a></sup> [Paul Strack](/wiki/Paul_Strack) explains it can be translated as "Noble Fire".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup>

An unclear name is <i><b>Finellach</b></i>, which was included in [Gil-galad's letter](/wiki/Gil-galad%27s_letter) to [Tar-Meneldur](/wiki/Tar-Meneldur) (but omitted by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien)).<sup id="cite_ref-Aldarion_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aldarion-13">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Parentage_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parentage-19">[19]</a></sup> [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) explains it can mean "Flame of Hair and Eye".<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> Alternatively, it can mean something like "Flame-eyed Fin(wë)".

Another isolated name was <i><b>Finwain</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Finwain_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finwain-2">[2]</a></sup> clearly Sindarin, a combination of *fin* (Sindarin for *[Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB)*) + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gwain&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gwain (page does not exist)">(g)wain</a></i> ("young").<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>

[1937](/wiki/1937), along with Elendil (in the previous conception, Elrond is the leader of the Elves who remained behind). Both characters create a great league of Elves and Men and fight [Thû](/wiki/Th%C3%BB) in Mordor, but both are slain by him. In that text he is mentioned as a descendant of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) and an Elven king of Beleriand.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

In a marginal note of §28 of the conclusion of *[Quenta Silmarillion](</wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road)> "Quenta Silmarillion (Lost Road)")*, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) pencilled the names *Gilgalad* and *Lindon*.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> This note was probably used by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) to introduce Gil-galad at the end of [chapter 24](/wiki/Of_the_Voyage_of_E%C3%A4rendil_and_the_War_of_Wrath "Of the Voyage of Eärendil and the War of Wrath") of the published version of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, beside Círdan, Celeborn, and Galadriel.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup>

### Parentage {#Parentage}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_(colored).jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/9/95/Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_%28colored%29.jpg/225px-Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_%28colored%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="225" height="300" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/9/95/Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_%28colored%29.jpg/338px-Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_%28colored%29.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/9/95/Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_%28colored%29.jpg/450px-Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Orodreth_%28colored%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1068"/></a><figcaption><i>Scion of Kings son of Orodreth</i> by <a href="/wiki/User:Sirielle" title="User:Sirielle">Karolina Węgrzyn</a></figcaption></figure>

In the initial drafts of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Tolkien still considered Gil-galad to be a descendant of Fëanor.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup>

Later, through the further writing of *The Lord of the Rings*, Tolkien reconsidered Gil-galad to be a son of [Finrod Felagund](/wiki/Finrod "Finrod"). In additions of uncertain date made to the [later *Quenta Silmarillion*](/wiki/Later_Quenta_Silmarillion "Later Quenta Silmarillion") it was told that Felagund sent away both his [wife](</wiki/Meril_(mother_of_Gil-galad)> "Meril (mother of Gil-galad)") and son from Nargothrond to the Havens of the Falas for their safety.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup> In the late 1950s, he decided that Felagund was unmarried and childless, since [Amarië](/wiki/Amari%C3%AB) the [Vanya](/wiki/Vanyar "Vanyar") whom he loved remained in the [West](/wiki/Aman "Aman"), and suggested in a marginal note to the *[Grey Annals](/wiki/The_Grey_Annals "The Grey Annals")* that Gil-galad *might be* the son of [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon) (under the name *Findor*).<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>

However, in the early 60s, Tolkien returned to an idea of Finrod having a wife<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> and child, and thus he moved Orodreth down a generation, making him Finrod's son instead of his brother. Apparently recalling the story in the *Grey Annals*,<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> Tolkien spotted the contradiction and moved Orodreth to become in the same generation the son of Finrod’s brother Angrod (who with [Aegnor](/wiki/Aegnor) held the heights of [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion) and was slain in the [Battle of Sudden Flame](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach "Dagor Bragollach")), changing his Quenya name from *Artanáro* to *Artaresto*. Finally in [August](/wiki/August) [1965](/wiki/1965), Tolkien concluded that the best solution to the problem of Gil-galad's parentage was to shift him into the son of Orodreth who escaped in Nargothrond after Angrod was slain and dwelt there with his uncle Finrod, later succeeding him. Orodreth married a Sindarin lady of the [North](/wiki/Northlands "Northlands"), and their children were [Finduilas](/wiki/Finduilas) and Gil-galad.<sup id="cite_ref-Parentage_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parentage-19">[19]</a></sup>

Tolkien's son and literary executor Christopher Tolkien had no doubt that this was his father's last word on the subject, but since nothing of this late and radically altered conception ever touched the existing narratives, he made the editorial decision not to incorporate this revision into the published version of *The Silmarillion* and chose instead to present Gil-galad as a son of Fingon; otherwise, all aspects of the move would have required considerable reworking of the story. For a consistency with the published *Silmarillion*, Christopher also edited a line in *[Aldarion and Erendis: The Mariner's Wife](/wiki/Aldarion_and_Erendis:_The_Mariner%27s_Wife)* that originally referred to Gil-galad as a dynast of the [House of Finarfin](/wiki/House_of_Finarfin).<sup id="cite_ref-Aldarion_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aldarion-13">[13]</a></sup>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_(colored).jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/c2/Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_%28colored%29.jpg/225px-Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_%28colored%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="225" height="300" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/c2/Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_%28colored%29.jpg/338px-Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_%28colored%29.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/c2/Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_%28colored%29.jpg/450px-Karolina_W%C4%99grzyn_-_Scion_of_Kings_son_of_Fingon_%28colored%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1068"/></a><figcaption><i>Scion of Kings son of Fingon</i> by Karolina Węgrzyn</figcaption></figure>

Many years later in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)* he regretted about his past decision and stated it was a mistake on his part and did not in fact represent his father's conception of the character, suggesting that it would have been better to have left Gil-galad's parentage **obscure** in *The Silmarillion*.<sup id="cite_ref-Parentage_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parentage-19">[19]</a></sup>

[Renée Vink](/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e_Vink) of the [Dutch Tolkien Society](/wiki/Unquendor "Unquendor") suggests that the only good reason for making Gil-galad son of Fingon is the correspondence of the colours, blue and silver, of Gil-galad's heraldic device and Fingolfin's banner, noting that the publication of *The Silmarillion*, based on a limited "grasp of the material", created a "virtually unshakeable" tradition for this parentage. She argues that Orodreth has a better claim to paternity, for several reasons: the crown of the Noldor in exile (in Middle-earth) then comes to a descendant of [Finarfin](/wiki/Finarfin), king of the Noldor in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor); a descendant of Finarfin would fight Sauron to avenge Finarfin's son Finrod; and as a brother to Finduilas, he (alone of the Noldorin Kings) would fight with a spear, the weapon that killed his sister.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup>

### Date of birth {#Date_of_birth}

In a [1959](/wiki/1959) manuscript on chronology published in *[The Nature of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth)*, Tolkien contemplated various revisions to the years of birth of various characters in the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in accord with a new conception of how Elves aged over time. In a rough note appended to this manuscript, he suggested that "Gilgalad" became King in Lindon "under" Galadriel c. S.A. 10-20, and that in order to be of suitable age to rule he must have been born in YT 1481/V.Y. 4981 in Aman.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup> This would be incompatible with the later revision of Gil-galad's genealogy made in August 1965, since his mother is explicitly mentioned to be a Sindarin lady, as described above in the previous section.<sup id="cite_ref-Parentage_19-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parentage-19">[19]</a></sup>
