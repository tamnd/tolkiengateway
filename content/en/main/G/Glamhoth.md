<i><b>Glamhoth</b></i> is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) word for [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md). Literally, it means "the dinhorde, the yelling horde", and is a descriptive term referring to the loud and barbarous speech of the Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-UT_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The first part, *glam*, means "barbaric speech", and is an expansion of <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Lam&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lam (page does not exist)">lam</a></i> ("tongue, language"). An individual within the group is referred to as a *glamog*<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>. The second element, *[hoth](../H/Hoth.md)* is a common suffix to denote a collection of people, usually used negatively.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Examples {#Examples}

- <i><a href="Gurth_an_Glamhoth!.md" title="Gurth an Glamhoth!">Gurth an <b>Glamhoth</b>!</a></i> = "Death to the Orcs!"
- <i><a href="Glamdring.md" title="Glamdring"><b>Glam</b>dring</a></i> = "Foe-hammer"

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

This word has a long history in Tolkien's mind, but always had evil connotations. In its earliest appearances, it meant "fierce hate",<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but later, its meaning became "shouting, confused noise", and became (poetically) associated with [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md).

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Throughout history, populations have always considered the languages of other peoples inferior, or uncouth to the ears. The term <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian" title="wikipedia:Barbarian">&#34;Barbarian&#34;</a> originally meant "brabbler". Similarly, Germany is called <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_for_Germany" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">&#34;Land of the Mute&#34;</a>, "land of those who do not speak our tongue" in some Slavic countries. A similar pattern is found in [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md), where the word for "shout" (*rûkh*) is related to the words for "Orc", *[uruk](../U/Uruk.md)*.
