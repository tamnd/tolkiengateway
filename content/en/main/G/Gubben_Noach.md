**Gubben Noach** is a traditional Swedish drinking song ("Old Man Noah"), written by 18th century composer <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Michael_Bellman" title="wikipedia:Carl Michael Bellman">Carl Michael Bellman</a>. It is published as the 21st song in *[Songs for the Philologists](/wiki/Songs_for_the_Philologists)* in [1936](/wiki/1936).

Two verses of the Swedish original are included here, together with 3 verses of an Icelandic translation, by Eiríkur Björnsson.

## Swedish original {#Swedish_original}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Gubben Noach, gubben Noach var en hedersman:<br/>
       När han gick ur arken,<br/>
       plantera ban på marken<br/>
Mycket vin, ja, mycket vin, ja: detta gjorde han.<br/>
<br/>
Han väl viste, han väl viste, att en mänska var<br/>
       torstig av naturen<br/>
       som de andra djuren —<br/>
därför han ock, därför han ock vin planterat har. <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

## Icelandic translation {#Icelandic_translation}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Gamli Noi, gamli guðhræddur og vis;<br/>
       mikilshattar maður,<br/>
       mörgum velviljaður.<br/>
þott hann drykki, þott hann drykki, þa samt bar hann pris.<br/>
<br/>
Aldrei drakk hann, aldrei drakk hann of mikið i senn,<br/>
       utan einu sinni<br/>
       a hann trui eg rynni.<br/>
Glappaskotin, glappaskotin, ganga svo til enn.<br/>
<br/>
Viltu vinur, viltu vinur vita hvar hann sat?<br/>
       Bustaður einn batur,<br/>
       Borginn var rjett latur,<br/>
Gamli Noi, gamli Noi hæst a Ararat.
</p>
</div>
