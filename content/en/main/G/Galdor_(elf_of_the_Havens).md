**Galdor** of the Havens was an [Elf](/wiki/Elf) who in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) lived in [Mithlond](/wiki/Mithlond) under [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan).

## History {#History}

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) he was sent in an errand to [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell). He was present in the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond) representing Círdan. He asked about proofs that the ring of [the halfling](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") was indeed the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring), and also inquired why [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) was not with them. It was [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) who answered his questions, narrating his story on how he discovered the One Ring, and Saruman's treachery.

In a later point Galdor mentioned that he knew about the figure of [Iarwain](/wiki/Iarwain), and he agreed with [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel) that he had not the power to defy [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and keep the Ring from him.

Then, he agreed with Gandalf that not even the [Sea](/wiki/Sea) could hide the Ring for ever. Galdor expected that the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Ringwraiths) (who were recently defeated in [Bruinen](/wiki/Bruinen)) would soon return and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) could not stop Sauron from coming all the way to [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) and reach the Sea. These were the last comments before Elrond decided that the only option is to destroy the Ring in [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In late writings, Tolkien speculated that this Galdor and the [Galdor](/wiki/Galdor_of_the_Tree "Galdor of the Tree") of *[The Fall of Gondolin](</wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter)> "The Fall of Gondolin (chapter)")* may have been the same,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> but he ultimately rejected the notion: if Galdor had remained behind, he would either have completely rejected the call of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), and thus have Fallen, or he would have had to leave and then return like [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel) did, which would make him a far more powerful person than his appearance in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* suggests.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> For this reason Tolkien speculated that "Galdor" was simply a common [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name.
