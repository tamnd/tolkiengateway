**Gamil Zirak**, also called **Zirak**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> or the **Old**, was a [Dwarf](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") smith and the master of [Telchar](/wiki/Telchar) of [Nogrod](/wiki/Nogrod).<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Gamil Zirak was a Dwarf smith, master of Telchar of Nogrod, maker of many renowned works. Gamil Zirak was a great craftsman himself, and the treasuries of [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) were known to hold examples of his work.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Even though it isn't explicitly stated in the text, the fact that Gamil Zirak was the master of Telchar of Nogrod makes it probable that he also lived in that city.

Whether he lived in Nogrod or its neighboring city [Belegost](/wiki/Belegost), it's probable that Gamil Zirak was one of the [Firebeards](/wiki/Firebeards) or [Broadbeams](/wiki/Broadbeams), the two dwarven houses that settled in the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> It's also probable that, besides [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul), he spoke [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), for that was the custom among the Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*[Gamil](/wiki/Gamil)* is probably a [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) word, of unknown meaning. Some people find worthy of noting its striking similarity to [Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse) *gamall* ("old"),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> which quite possibly was the inspiration for the former, since Gamil Zirak was also called "the Old", for it woudn't be the first time a Dwarvish name was taken from Old Norse.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 1]</a></sup>

*[Zirak](/wiki/Zirak)* is a Khuzdul word meaning "tine"<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> or "spike".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>
