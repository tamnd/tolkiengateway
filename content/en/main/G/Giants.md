The **Giants** were a mysterious and seldom-mentioned [race](../../category/R/Races.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) whose origins remain obscure.

## History {#History}

Giants are mainly beings of legend. [Gandalf the Grey](Gandalf.md) was known for telling stories about them,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and [Bilbo](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) had heard of Giants in tales.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

A local legend among the [indigenous people](../P/Pre-Númenóreans.md) of [Gondor](Gondor.md) told of Giants living in the [lands](../O/Outlands.md) by the [Sea](../B/Belegaer.md), north of the [Bay of Belfalas](../B/Bay_of_Belfalas.md). The tale says that Giants made the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md) to keep [Men](../M/Men.md) out of their lands. One of them, [Tarlang](../T/Tarlang.md), tripped, and broke his neck. The other Giants did not clean up his body, which became incorporated in the land instead. The Giant's neck became [Tarlang's Neck](../T/Tarlang's_Neck.md), his head [Dol Tarlang](../D/Dol_Tarlang.md), and the stones he was carrying [Cûl Veleg](../C/Cûl_Veleg.md) and [Cûl Bîn](../C/Cûl_Bîn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Tarlang_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tarlang-4">[4]</a></sup> The city of [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md), as stood on [Amon Tirith](../A/Amon_Tirith.md), seemed to have been "carved" by Giants out of the rock.<sup id="cite_ref-rk_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rk-7">[7]</a></sup>

The [Ettenmoors](../E/Ettenmoors.md) owed their name to some [gigantic creatures](../E/Etten.md) of that land, although these were perhaps merely some kind of [trolls](../T/Trolls.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Some say that [Beorn](../B/Beorn.md) is a bear descended from the great and ancient bears of the mountains that lived there before the Giants came, though Gandalf found this story less likely to be true.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-1">[1]</a></sup>

The "stone-giants" lived in the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) during the late [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) where great [bears](../B/Bears.md) lived there before their arrival.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-1">[1]</a></sup> They found a sport in throwing rocks at each other, and then into the depths below to hear them shatter among the trees.<sup id="cite_ref-Hill_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hill-2">[2]</a></sup>

Presumably, not all Giants were savage or evil, as Gandalf hoped to convince a decent Giant to block the [Front Porch](../F/Front_Porch.md) to [Goblin-town](Goblin-town.md) to make the [High Pass](../H/High_Pass.md) in the Misty Mountains safe again.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

Some [Tolkienists](../T/Tolkienists.md) assume that giants are a fleeting idea of Tolkien's while writing *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* that was never considered again for incorporation into the developing legendarium. [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) has argued that they might have become "free agents"; not wicked, but simply not aware of their surroundings.<sup id="cite_ref-Giants_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Giants-11">[11]</a></sup> [Robert Foster](../R/Robert_Foster.md) comments that the stone-giants "may be no more serious than [Golfimbul](Golfimbul.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Other theories attempt to include the Giants among the known races of Arda. For example, they might be a large race of [Men](../M/Men.md) (like [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) are a small race of Men), [Trolls](../T/Trolls.md), or simply "nature <a class="mw-disambig" href="../S/Spirit_(disambiguation).md" title="Spirit (disambiguation)">spirits</a>" more or less like [Tom Bombadil](../T/Tom_Bombadil.md).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Furthermore, [Douglas A. Anderson](../D/Douglas_A._Anderson.md)'s annotations in *[The Annotated Hobbit: Revised and Expanded Edition](../T/The_Annotated_Hobbit%253A_Revised_and_Expanded_Edition.md)* express the opinion that stone-giants are a variety of Troll.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)* and associated writings {#The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_and_associated_writings}

Giants originally had a larger part in the legendarium. In one early manuscript, the Giants are counted among the [úvanimor](../_/Úvanimo.md), servants of [Melko](../M/Morgoth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Lost_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lost-15">[15]</a></sup> In another manuscript, the Giants are counted among the Earthlings, and are divided between the "wood-giants" ([Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) *ulbandi*) and "mountainous-giants" (Qenya *taulir*).<sup id="cite_ref-Parma_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parma-16">[16]</a></sup> And in other early writings, two Giants are named: [Nan](<../N/Nan_(Giant).md>) (said to be like an [Elm](../E/Elms.md)-tree) and [Gilim](Gilim.md) (whose name is [Gnomish](Gnomish.md) for "winter").<sup id="cite_ref-Lays_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lays-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

### Giants and Ents {#Giants_and_Ents}

Giants were the wicked precursors of [Ents](../E/Ents.md). The elm-like features of the Giant Nan, and that in early versions of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* it was the Giant [Treebeard](../T/Treebeard.md) who held [Gandalf](Gandalf.md) captive, not [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md),<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> makes the connection between Giants and Ents within [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s imagination clear.<sup id="cite_ref-Giants_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Giants-11">[11]</a></sup>

An early name for the [Ettenmoors](../E/Ettenmoors.md) was called "Entish Lands". As [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) notes, "Ent" comes from an [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) word for "giant",<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> and was used before Tolkien conceived the later benevolent Ents of *[The Two Towers](../T/The_Two_Towers.md)*. The word is seen at various points in *[Beowulf](<../B/Beowulf_(poem).md>)*, for example line 2717, *enta geweorc*, "the work of Giants".<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

### Giants as a subgroup of Men {#Giants_as_a_subgroup_of_Men}

In a draft for what would become the chapter *[The Shadow of the Past](../T/The_Shadow_of_the_Past.md)* from *[The Fellowship of the Ring](../T/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)*, it is mentioned that the [Necromancer](../S/Sauron.md) was rallying his forces and causing wars and devastation from his stronghold in [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md), and that Giants were said to be among those forces. There, the Giants were described as "a [Big Folk](../M/Men.md) only far bigger and stronger than Men the \[?ordinary\] Big Folk, and no stupider, indeed often full of cunning and wizardry".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

### Other names and etymologies {#Other_names_and_etymologies}

An early [root](../S/Sundocarmë.md) for "giant" is given as <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=NOROTH&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="NOROTH (page does not exist)">NOROTH</a>. This yields [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Norsa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Norsa (page does not exist)">norsa</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-NOROTH_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOROTH-23">[23]</a></sup> and *Noroth* (likely supposed to be a [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) word<sup id="cite_ref-Web_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Web-24">[24]</a></sup>). A discarded Quenya word was *hanako*, from a root KHAN-AK.<sup id="cite_ref-Vin_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vin-25">[25]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In Germanic mythology names for the Giants were *jotun* ([Norse](../N/Norse.md)) and *eoten* ([Old English](../O/Old_English.md)). From the latter word Tolkien derived the name "[etten](../E/Etten.md)".

In the *[Return of the King](../T/The_Return_of_the_King.md)* it is noted that [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) "seemed to have been not builded but carven by giants out of the bones of the earth".<sup id="cite_ref-rk_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rk-7">[7]</a></sup> [Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) have suggested that this notion derives from [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) mythology, in which Giants were often portrayed as builders of ancient structures.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

## Other fiction {#Other_fiction}

In *[Farmer Giles of Ham](../F/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham.md)*, [a giant](../T/The_Giant.md) troubles [Farmer Giles](../F/Farmer_Giles.md)' land, who chases him off with his blunderbuss.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>

In a rejected portion of the Foreword to *[The End of Bovadium](../T/The_End_of_Bovadium.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup>, [Dr. Gums](../D/Dr._Gums.md) mentions that "a race of giants" that once inhabited "the prehistoric remains in this island" were often employed to excavate "vast pits and tunnels for no ascertainable purpose" and to transport "large quantities of stone to inaccessible regions", according to "popular tradition".<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup>

### Films {#Films}

<b>2012: <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_An_Unexpected_Journey.md" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><i>Stone-giants</i> are seen throwing rocks and crashing into each other as <a href="../T/Thorin_and_Company.md" title="Thorin and Company">Thorin and Company</a> travel over the <a href="../M/Misty_Mountains.md" title="Misty Mountains">Misty Mountains</a>. In the film, they are interpreted as literally being colossal stone entities.</dd></dl>

### Games {#Games}

<b>1982: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Several divisions and races of giants are described and given statistics, such as <i>Stone Giants</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup> Ice Giants,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> Giants of the Southern Misty Mountains,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> and Red Giants.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> Where the <a href="../C/Coldfells.md" title="Coldfells">Coldfells</a> met the Misty Mountains, the Stone-giants maintained shrines known as the Crugiau Cerrig (<a href="../L/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md#Blarm" title="Languages in Middle-earth Role Playing">Bl.</a> “Stone Piles”), at which they and Trolls worshipped creation. Dwarves who disturbed those piles died in mysterious circumstances. The energy of the stones, in some way tied to Sauron, increased the fertility of local Trolls.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1995: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game.md" title="Middle-earth Collectible Card Game">Middle-earth Collectible Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Giants, called <i>Thunder&#39;s Companions</i>, are one of the Hazard Creatures.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="../T/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game).md" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (2003 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Stone-giants are golem-like creatures that appear in the fourth level, hurling rocks at Bilbo as he tries to travel along a mountain path. They are completely made of stone, and their stones are dangerous.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>&#34;Mountain Giants&#34; are large and powerful beings in the Goblin faction, with brown scaly skin. They can hurl stones at great distance, and serve as the faction&#39;s artillery units.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Several tribes of giants appear in the game, including <i>Stone-giants</i>, Ogres (also called Jorthkyn or Earth-kin), and Ice-giants. While some are enemy creatures, others remain neutral and can be befriended by the player.</dd></dl>

<b>2011: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_War_in_the_North.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>A renegade <i>Stone-Giant</i> named Bargrisar appears in the game, serving as a boss character. Swayed by <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">Sauron</a> to serve him, he becomes the leader of a large party of Orcs and Trolls. Backed by his army, Bargrisar seeks to destroy the nests of the <a href="Great_Eagles.md" title="Great Eagles">Great Eagles</a> and plunder the North for riches. Similar to the portrayal in <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_An_Unexpected_Journey.md" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>, he is a being composed entirely of stone.</dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="../L/Lego_The_Hobbit%253A_The_Video_Game.md" title="Lego The Hobbit: The Video Game">Lego The Hobbit: The Video Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Multiple stone-giants appear as the main conflict in the level <i>Over Hill and Under Hill</i>, in which their fighting poses a threat to the party.</dd></dl>

<b>1989: <a href="../T/The_Hobbit_(comic_book).md" title="The Hobbit (comic book)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (comic book)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Giants are displayed as bearded gigantic men with regular leather attire.</dd></dl>
