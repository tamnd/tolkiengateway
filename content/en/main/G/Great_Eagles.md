The **Great Eagles**, also simply referred to as the **Eagles**, were immense, sentient bird-like creatures that served as messengers of [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB).

They were capable of speech and often helped [Men](/wiki/Men), [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and [Wizards](/wiki/Wizards) in their efforts to defeat foes such as [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) and [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron).

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/06/Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="432" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/06/Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/06/Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien_are_Flown_to_Safety.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1112" data-file-height="1600"/></a><figcaption><i>Beren and Lúthien are Flown to Safety</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

At a command of Manwë, the Lord of the Eagles, [Thorondor](/wiki/Thorondor), kept his eyries at the top of the [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim), the volcanoes above [Angband](/wiki/Angband), for a time.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> While he lived there, Thorondor helped [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon) rescue [Maedhros](/wiki/Maedhros).

Thorondor wounded Morgoth in the face after Morgoth's battle with [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin), and he carried Fingolfin's body to the [Echoriath](/wiki/Echoriath), where he was buried by Fingon. Years later, three of the Great Eagles came to the aid of [Beren](/wiki/Beren) and [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien), bearing them away from Thangorodrim after both had drained their strength in the [Quest for the Silmaril](/wiki/Quest_for_the_Silmaril).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

Thorondor's folk later removed their eyries to the [Crissaegrim](/wiki/Crissaegrim), part of the [Echoriad](/wiki/Echoriad) near [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin). There they became friends of [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon), bringing him news and keeping spies off their borders. Because of their guardianship, [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) were unable to approach either the nearby mountains<sup id="cite_ref-Silm-TFG_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silm-TFG-8">[7]</a></sup> or the important ford of [Brithiach](/wiki/Brithiach) to the south.<sup id="cite_ref-Tuor_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tuor-9">[8]</a></sup>

They redoubled their watch after the coming of [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> enabling Gondolin to remain undiscovered for the longest of all [Elven](/wiki/Elves "Elves") realms. When the city [fell](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin") at last, the Eagles of Thorondor protected the [survivors](/wiki/Exiles_of_Gondolin "Exiles of Gondolin"), driving away the orcs that ambushed them at [Cirith Thoronath](/wiki/Cirith_Thoronath), the Eagles' Cleft north of Gondolin.<sup id="cite_ref-Silm-TFG_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silm-TFG-8">[7]</a></sup>

The Eagles fought alongside the [army](/wiki/Host_of_the_Valar "Host of the Valar") of the Valar, the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), and the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) during the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) at the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age). After the appearance of winged [dragons](/wiki/Dragons "Dragons"), all the great birds gathered under Thorondor and aided [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil), destroying the majority of the dragons.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>

### Second Age (Númenor) {#Second_Age_(Númenor)}

In the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), a pair of Eagles had an eyrie in the King's House in [Armenelos](/wiki/Armenelos), the capital of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), until the reign of [Tar-Ancalimon](/wiki/Tar-Ancalimon), when the [Kings of Númenor](/wiki/Kings_of_N%C3%BAmenor) became hostile to the Valar.

The [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) believed that three Great Eagles, the **[Witnesses of Manwë](/wiki/Witnesses_of_Manw%C3%AB)**, were sent by Manwë to guard the summit of [Meneltarma](/wiki/Meneltarma). These appeared whenever one approached the hallow and stayed in the sky during the [Three Prayers](/wiki/Three_Prayers).

Many Eagles lived upon the hills around [Sorontil](/wiki/Sorontil) in the north of the island.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

Eagle-shaped storm clouds, called the "Eagles of the Lords of the West", were sent by Manwë when he tried to reason with or threaten the Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup>

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

By the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), a colony of Eagles under the [Great Eagle](/wiki/Great_Eagle) lived in the northern parts of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains). They mostly nested upon the eastward slopes not far from the [High Pass](/wiki/High_Pass) leading from [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), and thus in the direct vicinity of the [Goblin-town](/wiki/Goblin-town) beneath; often afflicting the goblins and disrupting their plans.

These Eagles helped [Radagast](/wiki/Radagast) and the Elves of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) in watching the land and in gathering news about the Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> However, as a result of feeding on the sheep of the local [Woodmen](/wiki/Woodmen) of [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood), their relationship with Men was strained, causing them to fear men's bows.

The Eagles rescued [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) from a band of [Goblins](/wiki/Orcs#Orcs_and_Goblins "Orcs") and [Wargs](/wiki/Wargs) and carried them to the [Carrock](/wiki/Carrock).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> Some days later, they spied the mustering of goblins all over the Mountains to be gathered under the Great Eagle in the [Battle of Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies) near [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"). It was only with their help that the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves), [Men](/wiki/Men) and Elves managed to defeat the goblins.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> The Great Eagle became known as the [King of All Birds](/wiki/King_of_All_Birds).

The Eagles appeared in great numbers at the [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon), helping to fight against the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl). It was [Gwaihir](/wiki/Gwaihir), his brother [Landroval](/wiki/Landroval), and [Meneldor](/wiki/Meneldor) who rescued [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) from [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) after [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") had been destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

While *[thoron](/wiki/Thoron "Thoron")* means "eagle" in both [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) (*soron* in a Quenya without the shibboleth of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor))<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>, the Quenya name of the Great Eagles in particular is possibly <i><b><a href="/wiki/Anaxarta" title="Anaxarta">Anaxartar</a></b></i>, with the individual being <i><b>Anaxarta</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-anaxarta_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-anaxarta-1">[1]</a></sup>

### Early legendarium names {#Early_legendarium_names}

In [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish), one of [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s early conceptions of an [Elven](/wiki/Elvish "Elvish") language, a word for "eagle" is *ioroth* (poetic form *ior*). A cognate of the same meaning in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) is the poetic *ea(r)* or *earen*. Another Gnomish word for "an eagle" is *thorn*.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> The <i><b>Thornhoth</b></i> was the name for the eagle-folk in this early version.<sup id="cite_ref-Fall_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fall-21">[20]</a></sup>

## Origin and nature {#Origin_and_nature}

Tolkien's view of the Great Eagles' nature alternated over time. In early stages, he considered the Eagles as bird-shaped [Maiar](/wiki/Maiar)<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup>, while at other points he speculated that they may be beasts granted the ability to speak by the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-MR409-11_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR409-11-23">[22]</a></sup>

In [The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion), Eagles are described as:<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Spirits in the shape of hawks and eagles flew ever to and from his halls; and their eyes could see to the depths of the seas, and pierce the hidden caverns beneath the world.
</p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

In a text from c. 1959, Tolkien speculated that the Eagles and [Huan](/wiki/Huan) could be Maiar, but that they could also be beasts elevated by the Valar to possess the ability to speak.<sup id="cite_ref-MR409-11_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR409-11-23">[22]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>But true &#39;rational&#39; creatures, &#39;speaking peoples&#39;, are all of human / &#39;humanoid&#39; form. Only the Valar and Maiar are intelligences that can assume forms of Arda at will. Huan and <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Sorontar" title="Sorontar">Sorontar</a> could be Maiar - emissaries of Manwë. But unfortunately in &#39;The Lord of the Rings&#39; Gwaehir and Landroval are said to be descendants of Sorontar.
(...)
In summary: I think it must be assumed that &#39;talking&#39; is not necessarily the sign of the possession of a &#39;rational soul&#39; or <a href="/wiki/F%C3%ABa" title="Fëa">fëa</a>. (...)
The same sort of thing may be said of Huan and the Eagles: they were taught language by the Valar, and raised to a higher level - but they still had no fëar.
</p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

In a later text, the Great Eagles were first envisioned by Manwë during the Music of the Ainur, and appeared before the awakening of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ents_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ents-28">[27]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>When the Children awake, then the thought of Yavanna will awake also, and it will summon spirits from afar, and they will go among the kelvar and the olvar, and some will dwell therein, and be held in reverence, and their just anger shall be feared (...) Did not thy thought and mine meet also, so that we took wing together like great birds that soar above the clouds? That also shall come to be by the heed of Ilúvatar, and before the Children awake there shall go forth with wings like the wind the Eagles of the Lords of the West.
</p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

In a later c. [1970](/wiki/1970) text, the "mighty speaking eagles" are definitively referred to as Maiar:<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The most notable were those Maiar who took the form of the mighty speaking eagles that we hear of in the legends of the war of the Ñoldor against Melkor, and who remained in the West of Middle-earth until the fall of Sauron and the Dominion of Men, after which they are not heard of again.
</p>
</blockquote>

<h2><span id=".22Flying_the_Ring_to_Mount_Doom.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&#34;Flying_the_Ring_to_Mount_Doom&#34;">&#34;Flying the Ring to Mount Doom&#34;</span></h2>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_At_the_Foot_of_Mount_Doom.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0f/Ted_Nasmith_-_At_the_Foot_of_Mount_Doom.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_At_the_Foot_of_Mount_Doom.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="256" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/0f/Ted_Nasmith_-_At_the_Foot_of_Mount_Doom.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_At_the_Foot_of_Mount_Doom.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/0/0f/Ted_Nasmith_-_At_the_Foot_of_Mount_Doom.jpg 2x" data-file-width="527" data-file-height="450"/></a><figcaption>Eagles flying to Mount Doom. Art by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a>.</figcaption></figure>

Some readers have questioned why the Eagles simply didn't carry Frodo into Mordor and drop the [One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") in Mount Doom, or at least aid the Fellowship at some part of the journey, such as helping them avoiding the [Redhorn Gate](/wiki/Redhorn_Gate) and [Moria](/wiki/Moria).

In [Letter 210](/wiki/Letter_210), Tolkien said that the Eagles were "not taxis," and reiterated that the Fellowship's mission depended upon secrecy, so depicting a long arduous journey on foot was required to maintain their stealthy approach. Tolkien further states that Eagles should be used carefully as a plot device, and described them as a "dangerous machine" that he used sparingly.<sup id="cite_ref-letter210_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter210-30">[29]</a></sup>

Tolkien wrote of the Eagles in explicit terms of [eucatastrophe](/wiki/Eucatastrophe "Eucatastrophe") in [Letter 89](/wiki/Letter_89), where he describes their coming to save the day in *The Hobbit*.<sup id="cite_ref-letter89_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter89-31">[30]</a></sup> Since eucatastrophe is by nature unexpected, the Eagles by design would not and could not have been considered by the other characters as available or feasible options to take.

Furthermore, the Great Eagles would have been fairly obvious and defenseless to Sauron, who would have seen them coming from a distance and caused to quickly deduce their plan. Ringwraiths and their [Fell beasts](/wiki/Fell_beasts) and/or Sauron's legions of archers and his siege machines would most likely have stopped the attempt upon spotting the Eagles in the vicinity of Mordor. Thus, a small party was needed to go on foot to minimize the risk of attracting notice.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien's painting of an eagle on a crag appears in some editions of *The Hobbit*. According to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), the author based this picture on a painting by Archibald Thorburn of an immature Golden Eagle, which Christopher found for him in *The Birds of the British Isles* by T.A. Coward. However, Tolkien's use of this model does not necessarily mean that his birds were ordinary Golden Eagles.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earliest version of the fall of Gondolin, the king of the Eagles, Thorndor (later Thorondor), had no love for Melko (later Melkor) because he had caught many Eagles and tortured them for the magic words that would enable him to fly (in order to challenge Manwë for command of the air). When the Eagles refused to reveal the magic words Melko cut off their wings in order to fashion a pair for himself, "but it availed not".<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup>
