**George Sydney Benedict Sayer** ([1 June](/wiki/1_June), [1914](/wiki/1914) – [20 October](/wiki/20_October), [2005](/wiki/2005)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>) was head of the English Department at Malvern College, Worcestershire, from [1949](/wiki/1949) until [1974](/wiki/1974).

He was a pupil, and later close personal friend, of [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis) during his undergraduate years. Sayer wrote the memoir and biography <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Jack:_C.S._Lewis_and_His_Times&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Jack: C.S. Lewis and His Times (page does not exist)">Jack: C.S. Lewis and His Times</a></i>. In August [1947](/wiki/1947) Sayer also befriended [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

In late August [1952](/wiki/1952) Tolkien visited Sayer and his wife and lent them the typewritten manuscript of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. During this visit, Sayer was to capture Tolkien singing and reading excerpts from *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and the manuscripts of *The Lord of the Rings* (see [1952 tape recording](/wiki/1952_tape_recording)).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

While preparing publication of *The Lord of the Ring*, Tolkien was unable to write a brief (less than 100 words) sketch description of his work as a publicity blurb. Sayer helped him and sent a text to [Allen and Unwin](/wiki/Allen_and_Unwin), who in turn drafted a shorter one, which was first printed in the [1953](/wiki/1953) catalogue.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
