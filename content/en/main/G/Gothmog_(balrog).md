**Gothmog** was the [Lord of Balrogs](/wiki/Lord_of_Balrogs) and the High-captain of Angband, one of the foremost captains of the Dark Lord [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) during the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).

## History {#History}

He is first mentioned at the [Dagor-nuin-Giliath](/wiki/Dagor-nuin-Giliath) when his Balrogs surrounded and attacked [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor), whom Gothmog himself mortally wounded, before they were forced to depart to Angband when the [Sons of Fëanor](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor) came to their father's aid.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

He next appeared at the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad) where he was named high-captain of [Angband](/wiki/Angband), signifying his status as one of Morgoth's most important servants.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup> There at the Nirnaeth Arnoediad he slew [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon), thus allowing him to boast of having slain two of the five [High Kings of the Noldor](/wiki/High_Kings_of_the_Noldor).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> During this same battle he captured [Húrin Thalion](/wiki/H%C3%BArin "Húrin") alive by the command of Morgoth, although this order allowed Húrin to slaughter many of Gothmog's troll-guards he was eventually captured, bound and dragged to Angband by Gothmog.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:59</sup>

His final appearance was as the marshal of the hosts at the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin), where he slew, and was slain by, [Ecthelion](/wiki/Ecthelion_of_the_Fountain "Ecthelion of the Fountain") in the square of the King.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

The name *Gothmog* is derived from the roots GOS-/GOTH- ("dread"), and MBAW- ("compel, force, subject, oppress"); found also in the title for Morgoth *Bauglir*: the tyrant or oppressor.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:359, 372</sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

In [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish), the name Gothmog is given alternative translations of "Strife-and-hatred"<sup id="cite_ref-List_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-List-7">[6]</a></sup> and "Voice of Goth (Morgoth), an Orc-name."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

In [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) his names were given as **Kalimbo**,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup> **Kosomot**,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> or **Kosmoko**.<sup id="cite_ref-List_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-List-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### *The Book of Lost Tales* {#The_Book_of_Lost_Tales}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Abe_Papakhian_-_Ecthelion_and_Gothmog_(colour).jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/a0/Abe_Papakhian_-_Ecthelion_and_Gothmog_%28colour%29.jpg/300px-Abe_Papakhian_-_Ecthelion_and_Gothmog_%28colour%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="385" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/a0/Abe_Papakhian_-_Ecthelion_and_Gothmog_%28colour%29.jpg/450px-Abe_Papakhian_-_Ecthelion_and_Gothmog_%28colour%29.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/a/a0/Abe_Papakhian_-_Ecthelion_and_Gothmog_%28colour%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="467" data-file-height="600"/></a><figcaption><i>Ecthelion and Gothmog</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Abe_Papakhian" title="Category:Images by Abe Papakhian">Abe Papakhian</a></figcaption></figure>

Gothmog already appears in the earliest stage of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") of *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*. In the character list appended to *[The Fall of Gondolin](</wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter)> "The Fall of Gondolin (chapter)")* Gothmog was described as "a son of Melko and the ogress [Fluithuin](/wiki/Fluithuin),"<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:216</sup> but eventually, the idea that the Valar had [children](/wiki/Valarindi "Valarindi") was discarded altogether.

When the armies of Melko attacked [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin) and surrounded it, they could not climb the slippery stone of [Amon Gwareth](/wiki/Amon_Gwareth). Then Gothmog led them to the northern gate of the city and piled his iron siege equipment against it until it broke from sheer pressure.<sup id="cite_ref-III_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-III-12">[11]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:176</sup> He also took a front-line position against [Rog](/wiki/Rog), and with treacherous strategy he cut off the rearguard of the [House of the Hammer of Wrath](/wiki/House_of_the_Hammer_of_Wrath), destroying them completely.<sup id="cite_ref-III_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-III-12">[11]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:179</sup> Later he beat down [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor) in single combat in the [Square of the Palace](/wiki/Square_of_the_Palace), but the elf-lord [Ecthelion of the Fountain](/wiki/Ecthelion "Ecthelion"), who was badly wounded, rose and stood over him. Ecthelion stood no chance against the Lord of Balrogs, and lost his sword in the brief struggle. But then Ecthelion leaped forward, and stabbed Gothmog in the breast with the spike atop his helm. They both fell into the [Fountain of the King](/wiki/Fountain_of_the_King), where Gothmog, if not already killed by the spike, drowned with his opponent.<sup id="cite_ref-III_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-III-12">[11]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:183–4</sup>

### Later concepts {#Later_concepts}

In one of Tolkien's early Middle-earth writings, *[The Lay of the Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)*, "[Lungorthin](/wiki/Lungorthin), Lord of Balrogs" is mentioned.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup> It is not, however, certain if it was another name for Gothmog, or it simply meant "a Balrog lord". According to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), the latter is more probable, as the name *Gothmog* was mentioned in the earliest writings, as well as the final version of Tolkien's mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>
