**Glamdring** was a sword, said to have belonged to [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon), but best known as the weapon of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) during the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring). It was known in Westron as the **Foe-hammer**, and Orcs knew it as **Beater**.

## History {#History}

### Beater {#Beater}

Glamdring was originally borne by Turgon, the King of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin).<sup id="cite_ref-shortrest_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shortrest-2">[2]</a></sup> He must have wielded it with strength during the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad) or the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin), for the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) named it "Beater", and fled before it. Even up until the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), [orcs of the Misty Mountains](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains "Orcs of the Misty Mountains") knew of this legendary sword,<sup id="cite_ref-overhill_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-overhill-4">[4]</a></sup> so it must have done some damage.

The name most likely originated in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad), where it is said that Turgon "hewed his way to the side of [his brother](/wiki/Fingon "Fingon")".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> No other tales mention the actions of the sword, and it is unknown what happened to it during the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin). Turgon perished as the [Tower of the King](/wiki/Tower_of_the_King) fell on top of him,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> but of the fate of Glamdring nothing is told.

### Survival {#Survival}

The sword survived roughly 6500 years from the Fall of Gondolin in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [510](/wiki/First_Age_510 "First Age 510") to its eventual rediscovery in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"). In that period, it travelled from Gondolin far to the east. Much mystery surrounds this feat, but [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) reasoned that Glamdring, along with [Orcrist](/wiki/Orcrist) and [Sting](/wiki/Sting), was plundered again and again, or carried off to the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) soon after the Fall of Gondolin.<sup id="cite_ref-shortrest_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shortrest-2">[2]</a></sup> Thus, after surviving the deluge of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath), it ended up in a [troll-hole](/wiki/Trolls%27_lair "Trolls' lair") in the [Trollshaws](/wiki/Trollshaws) of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador).

<h3><span id="At_Gandalf.27s_side"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="At_Gandalf&#39;s_side">At Gandalf&#39;s side</span></h3>

In May [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) and [a group of dwarves](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company "Thorin and Company") encountered three trolls in the Trollshaws - [William](/wiki/William), [Bert](/wiki/Bert) and <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Tom" title="Tom">Tom</a>. The Trolls captured Bilbo and the Dwarves, but Gandalf destroyed them by exposing them to sunlight. Glamdring, along with [Orcrist](/wiki/Orcrist) and [Sting](/wiki/Sting), were found in their cave nearby.<sup id="cite_ref-mutton_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mutton-1">[1]</a></sup> Gandalf claimed the weapon as his own, amazed by its appearance and inscription. The inscription was set in a script of [runes](/wiki/Runes "Runes") Gandalf [did not know](/wiki/Gondolinic_Runes "Gondolinic Runes"); he needed the knowledge of Elrond for that. Elrond translated the runes and called it by its Mannish name: "Foe-hammer".<sup id="cite_ref-shortrest_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shortrest-2">[2]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/df/Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg/200px-Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="255" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/df/Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg/300px-Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/df/Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg/400px-Donato_Giancola_-_You_Cannot_Pass.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2355" data-file-height="3000"/></a><figcaption>Gandalf wielding Glamdring, by <a href="/wiki/Donato_Giancola" title="Donato Giancola">Donato Giancola</a>.</figcaption></figure>

Gandalf would use the sword well; its first victim was the [Great Goblin](/wiki/Great_Goblin). Other Orcs fled as they recognized the sword as "Beater". This meant that they had legends about two glowing swords - Beater and [Biter](/wiki/Orcrist "Orcrist").<sup id="cite_ref-overhill_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-overhill-4">[4]</a></sup> Whether Gandalf used the sword again during the [Quest for Erebor](/wiki/The_Hobbit "The Hobbit") is uncertain, though it is likely that he wielded it in the [Battle of Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies).

Gandalf bore Glamdring at his side when the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) left [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> He used the blade during the [Battle of the Chamber of Mazarbul](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Chamber_of_Mazarbul), and a short time thereafter in the standoff with [the Balrog](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane "Durin's Bane"), during which battle his staff was destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> After the collapse of the [bridge](/wiki/Bridge_of_Khazad-d%C3%BBm "Bridge of Khazad-dûm"), Gandalf and the Balrog fought on to the [Endless Stair](/wiki/Endless_Stair) and [Durin's Tower](/wiki/Durin%27s_Tower), with Glamdring being the only weapon known to be in Gandalf's possession.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Gandalf's body perished there but he was sent back because his task was not yet complete. He had Glamdring with him when he met the [Three Hunters](/wiki/Three_Hunters); he gave it to [Háma](/wiki/H%C3%A1ma) at [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras) when asked to surrender it.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Gandalf would continue to bear it throughout the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), and carried it at his side when he, [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"), [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee"), [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") arrived at [the Prancing Pony](/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony "The Prancing Pony") in [Bree](/wiki/Bree). Gandalf and the Hobbits had seen so much war at the time that wearing a weapon did not seem odd.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

### Fate {#Fate}

On [29 September](/wiki/29_September), [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3021](/wiki/Third_Age_3021 "Third Age 3021"),<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> Gandalf left [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and sailed into the West.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Whether he took Glamdring with him is never mentioned, and is open to debate. It can be argued that he brought it over the Sea, as he did with [Shadowfax](/wiki/Shadowfax),<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> or that it was left behind in Middle-earth, weapons not being needed in the Undying Lands.

## Appearance {#Appearance}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/c2/John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg/85px-John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="85" height="175" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/c2/John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg/128px-John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/c2/John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg/170px-John_Howe_-_Glamdring.jpg 2x" data-file-width="197" data-file-height="406"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Glamdring&#34; by <a href="/wiki/John_Howe" title="John Howe">John Howe</a></figcaption></figure>

Glamdring and Orcrist are described as having "beautiful scabbards and jewelled hilts".<sup id="cite_ref-mutton_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mutton-1">[1]</a></sup> They would glow blue in the presence of Orcs. The "sword of Turgon" was "a white and gold sword in a ruel-bone (ivory) sheath".<sup id="cite_ref-ut1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ut1-3">[3]</a></sup>

When he took the sword, Gandalf said he could not read [the runes](/wiki/Gondolinic_Runes "Gondolinic Runes"). Elrond did, however, as he was a savant of all kinds of [runes](/wiki/Cirth "Cirth").

*Glamdring* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "Foe-hammer".<sup id="cite_ref-shortrest_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shortrest-2">[2]</a></sup> It is formed from the elements: *[glam](/wiki/Glamhoth "Glamhoth")*, literally "shouting, confused noise", but used for any body of [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs);<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> and *dring* ("beat, strike").<sup id="cite_ref-etym_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-etym-16">[16]</a></sup>

The sword was also given the crude name **Beater** by the Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-overhill_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-overhill-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In 1960, Tolkien began a complete rewrite of the Hobbit adding the fact that Glamdring was covered in dark blood when Gandalf found it, explaining why the runes were unreadable until cleaned by Elrond.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

It is not explained in the published *Hobbit* why Gandalf could not read the runes. A fitting explanation would be the "[Gondolinic Runes](/wiki/Gondolinic_Runes)", devised by Tolkien in either 1924 or 1930. This set of Runes was first published in 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

### Films {#Films}

<b>1977: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1977_film)" title="The Hobbit (1977 film)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1977 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Gandalf acquires Glamdring in the trolls&#39; cave. Elrond recognizes the sword at first glance; perhaps he simply deduces the fact that it was Glamdring because he had identified its mate Orcrist just before. After leaving <a href="/wiki/Rivendell" title="Rivendell">Rivendell</a>, Gandalf notably uses the sword only when rescuing Bilbo and the Dwarves from Goblin-town.</dd></dl>

<b>1978: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>On promotional art, Glamdring is portrayed as an ordinary longsword, with no inscription visible. In the movie itself, it goes unnamed, and is considerably shorter, only a one-handed sword. Oddly it is broken along with the Balrog&#39;s sword, but reappears intact in later scenes.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1980: <a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)"><i>The Return of the King</i> (1980 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Gandalf does not use a sword, only his staff.</dd></dl>

<b>2001-03: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Glamdring is a name given to the sword of Gandalf in the film&#39;s promotional materials, but it is not named in the films. The sword is, according to most replicas, 47 inches (approximately 120 centimeters) long,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> and does not glow blue. When asked, <a href="/wiki/Peter_Jackson" title="Peter Jackson">Peter Jackson</a> and <a href="/wiki/Philippa_Boyens" title="Philippa Boyens">Philippa Boyens</a> reacted jokingly that is was due to &#34;budgetary cuts&#34;, and they had &#34;not enough blue left&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In the films, Glamdring is depicted as a longsword-type weapon. It has a straight, double-edged blade with a very short ricasso right above the crossguard. The crossguard&#39;s prongs flare upwards, and the center of the guard houses a decorative blue gem. The sword&#39;s grip is wrapped in blue, and the pommel is elongated, broadening at the bottom. The rune inscription is engraved in the cross-guard, and adds power to the sword. With this extra power Gandalf was able to defeat <a href="/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane" title="Durin&#39;s Bane">Durin&#39;s Bane</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-lotrwaw_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotrwaw-22">[22]</a></sup> The actual inscription reads as thus:
<dl><dd>&#34;<i>Turgon <a href="/wiki/Aran" title="Aran">aran</a> Gondolin tortha gar a matha i vegil Glamdring gûd daedheloth, dam an <a href="/wiki/Glamhoth" title="Glamhoth">Glamhoth</a></i>&#34;.</dd></dl></dd>
<dd>This is <a href="/wiki/Sindarin" title="Sindarin">Sindarin</a>, and translates to &#34;Turgon, king of Gondolin, wields, has, and holds the sword Glamdring, Foe of Morgoth&#39;s realm, Hammer of the Orcs&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-lotrwaw_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotrwaw-22">[22]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2012: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Thorin finds several swords in the troll hoards and gives one to Gandalf. In Rivendell, Elrond identifies it as Glamdring, and in Goblin-town, the Great Goblin recognizes it as Beater. It has the same design as Glamdring in <i>The Lord of the Rings</i> films.</dd></dl>

### Radio series {#Radio_series}

<b>1968: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1968_radio_series)" title="The Hobbit (1968 radio series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1968 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Glamdring is found in the cave of the trolls, and later named by Elrond. Gandalf himself recounts how it was once borne by the King of Gondolin.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1981: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1981 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Glamdring is not named, and the presence of a sword is mentioned only once: when Gandalf has to surrender it to <a href="/wiki/H%C3%A1ma" title="Háma">Háma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup></dd></dl>

### Games {#Games}

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Glamdring is an Elven longsword. It glows with pale light when enemies are near.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game)" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (2003 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>As this game focuses on Bilbo rather than the entire group, Glamdring is never named, and only appears once when Gandalf and the player (as Bilbo) have to wield off several goblins in the Misty Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> An alternative survival theory is also given: the caves in the Trollshaws were - according to <a href="/wiki/Gl%C3%B3in" title="Glóin">Glóin</a> - dug by the <a href="/wiki/Witch-king" title="Witch-king">Witch-king</a> of <a href="/wiki/Angmar" title="Angmar">Angmar</a> during his reign in the region.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup></dd></dl>
