**Mount Gundabad** was a mountain adjacent to the northern reaches of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md). It was revered by the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) but usually occupied by [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md).

## Description {#Description}

From the northern part of the [Vales of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md), Mount Gundabad appeared to be the northern endpoint of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) and the western endpoint of the [Grey Mountains](Grey_Mountains.md), although slightly separated from both ranges.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> In actuality, the Misty Mountains continued past Mount Gundabad in a north-westerly direction (this extension was known as the [Mountains of Angmar](../M/Mountains_of_Angmar.md)). In all maps the mountain was shown as having three conjoined peaks. Bordering [Forodwaith](<../F/Forodwaith_(lands).md>) to the north, the mountain undoubtedly had a cold climate.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

According to the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../D/Durin.md" title="Durin">Durin the Deathless</a>, eldest of the [Fathers of the Dwarves](../F/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves.md), awoke at Mount Gundabad shortly after the [Awakening of the Elves](../A/Awakening_of_the_Elves.md). From that time forward, the mountain was revered by the Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-Relations_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Relations-3">[3]</a></sup> However, since Durin awoke alone he did not stay at the mountain; he walked southward until he founded [Khazad-dûm](../M/Moria.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In the early ages, Mount Gundabad did serve as a place of assembly for delegations of Dwarves, yet there is no mention of any making permanent residence there.<sup id="cite_ref-Relations_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Relations-3">[3]</a></sup>

In [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [1695](../S/Second_Age_1695.md), [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) invaded [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md). In [1697](../S/Second_Age_1697.md), he [conquered](../S/Sack_of_Eregion.md) [Eregion](../E/Eregion.md)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and would have overwhelmed [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md), leading refugees northward, but he was attacked in the rear by forces sent from Khazad-dûm. Sauron drove the Dwarves back but could not breach the [Doors of Durin](../D/Doors_of_Durin.md). Frustrated, he commanded the Orcs to harry the Dwarves wherever they could be found.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Soon thereafter, came the [First Sacking of Gundabad](../F/First_Sacking_of_Gundabad.md), followed by a long occupation by Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-Relations_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Relations-3">[3]</a></sup>

Around the year [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1300](../T/Third_Age_1300.md), the realm of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) arose.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> Its lands lay on both sides of the Misty Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> so Mount Gundabad was part of its domains. Although Angmar was destroyed in [1975](../T/Third_Age_1975.md),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> the last remnants of its people east of the mountains were driven away<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> in [1977](../T/Third_Age_1977.md).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> Gundabad itself remained populated with Orcs.

After the death of King [Thrór](../T/Thrór.md) in [2790](../T/Third_Age_2790.md), the Dwarves gathered for vengeance. The [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](../W/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs.md) began in [2793](../T/Third_Age_2793.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> and the [Second Sacking of Gundabad](../S/Second_Sacking_of_Gundabad.md) occurred.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Although it is likely that the Dwarves cleared the mountain of all Orcs, the Orcs returned and Mount Gundabad again served as their capital in the North.<sup id="cite_ref-Burst_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burst-11">[11]</a></sup>

In [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> [Thorin](../T/Thorin.md) [Oakenshield](../O/Oakenshield.md), [Gandalf](Gandalf.md), several Dwarves, and [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md), entered the Misty Mountains. While in the mountains, the [Great Goblin](Great_Goblin.md) was killed and the party escaped. Furious, the Orcs gathered at Mount Gundabad under the command of [Bolg](../B/Bolg.md) to seek revenge. Hearing of the death of [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md), they marched on the [Lonely Mountain](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Burst_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burst-11">[11]</a></sup> However, the Orcs lost the [Battle of Five Armies](../B/Battle_of_Five_Armies.md) and three parts of their numbers.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> Orcs still lived at Mount Gundabad by the end of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) but in a very reduced state.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Gundabad* is said to be a "in origin a [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md) name". Its meaning, however, is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

A recogniseable element is Khuzdul *gundu*, meaning "underground hall", but it is not known if this word is indeed part of the name *Gundabad*.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>Gundabad is translated as &#34;Mountain-home&#34;. Months after the fall of <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>, Elderslade is the site of the &#34;War of Three Peaks&#34;, in which <a href="../D/Durin_VII.md" title="Durin VII">Prince Durin</a>, son of <a href="../T/Thorin_Stonehelm.md" title="Thorin Stonehelm">Thorin Stonehelm</a>, leads an army of <a href="../D/Dwarves.md" title="Dwarves">Dwarves</a> to reclaim Gundabad from the forces of &#34;Gorgar the Ruthless&#34;, son of <a href="../B/Bolg.md" title="Bolg">Bolg</a>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Within the mountain, the &#34;Delvings of Gundabad&#34; are divided into five areas. &#34;Máttugard&#34; is the main citadel and site of Gundabad&#39;s throne. &#34;Deepscrave&#34; is a tall and deep cavern that once housed the common folk of Gundabad, now occupied by Orcs. The gem-studded &#34;Pit of Stone-jaws&#34; is home to Gundabad&#39;s tombs. In a basalt cavern at the heart of the mountain lies &#34;Gloomingtarn&#34;, called in Khuzdul &#34;Durthuzâram&#34;, the lake upon which Durin the Deathless awoke. The overgrown gardens of &#34;Clovengap&#34; are exposed to the sky through a rift in the mountainside.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>To the north of Gundabad lies &#34;Câr Bronach&#34;, the easternmost extent of Angmar. The western slopes of the mountain are called the &#34;Welkin-lofts&#34;, and are the site of several dwarven structures, including a meeting place for the Seven Houses.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>According to Longbeard legend, when he relocated his kingdom to Khazad-dûm, Durin I named his best friend, &#34;Mótsog&#34;, the first Lord of Gundabad. He came to regret that decision, for Mótsog betrayed Durin and led his &#34;Greymaul&#34; followers in an uprising against their fellow Longbeards.</dd></dl>
