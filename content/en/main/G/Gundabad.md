**Mount Gundabad** was a mountain adjacent to the northern reaches of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains). It was revered by the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) but usually occupied by [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs).

## Description {#Description}

From the northern part of the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin), Mount Gundabad appeared to be the northern endpoint of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and the western endpoint of the [Grey Mountains](/wiki/Grey_Mountains), although slightly separated from both ranges.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> In actuality, the Misty Mountains continued past Mount Gundabad in a north-westerly direction (this extension was known as the [Mountains of Angmar](/wiki/Mountains_of_Angmar)). In all maps the mountain was shown as having three conjoined peaks. Bordering [Forodwaith](</wiki/Forodwaith_(lands)> "Forodwaith (lands)") to the north, the mountain undoubtedly had a cold climate.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

According to the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin" title="Durin">Durin the Deathless</a>, eldest of the [Fathers of the Dwarves](/wiki/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves), awoke at Mount Gundabad shortly after the [Awakening of the Elves](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves). From that time forward, the mountain was revered by the Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-Relations_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Relations-3">[3]</a></sup> However, since Durin awoke alone he did not stay at the mountain; he walked southward until he founded [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Moria "Moria").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In the early ages, Mount Gundabad did serve as a place of assembly for delegations of Dwarves, yet there is no mention of any making permanent residence there.<sup id="cite_ref-Relations_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Relations-3">[3]</a></sup>

In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1695](/wiki/Second_Age_1695 "Second Age 1695"), [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) invaded [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador). In [1697](/wiki/Second_Age_1697 "Second Age 1697"), he [conquered](/wiki/Sack_of_Eregion "Sack of Eregion") [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and would have overwhelmed [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond), leading refugees northward, but he was attacked in the rear by forces sent from Khazad-dûm. Sauron drove the Dwarves back but could not breach the [Doors of Durin](/wiki/Doors_of_Durin). Frustrated, he commanded the Orcs to harry the Dwarves wherever they could be found.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Soon thereafter, came the [First Sacking of Gundabad](/wiki/First_Sacking_of_Gundabad), followed by a long occupation by Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-Relations_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Relations-3">[3]</a></sup>

Around the year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1300](/wiki/Third_Age_1300 "Third Age 1300"), the realm of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) arose.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> Its lands lay on both sides of the Misty Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> so Mount Gundabad was part of its domains. Although Angmar was destroyed in [1975](/wiki/Third_Age_1975 "Third Age 1975"),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> the last remnants of its people east of the mountains were driven away<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> in [1977](/wiki/Third_Age_1977 "Third Age 1977").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> Gundabad itself remained populated with Orcs.

After the death of King [Thrór](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r) in [2790](/wiki/Third_Age_2790 "Third Age 2790"), the Dwarves gathered for vengeance. The [War of the Dwarves and Orcs](/wiki/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Orcs) began in [2793](/wiki/Third_Age_2793 "Third Age 2793")<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> and the [Second Sacking of Gundabad](/wiki/Second_Sacking_of_Gundabad) occurred.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Although it is likely that the Dwarves cleared the mountain of all Orcs, the Orcs returned and Mount Gundabad again served as their capital in the North.<sup id="cite_ref-Burst_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burst-11">[11]</a></sup>

In [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-7">[7]</a></sup> [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin) [Oakenshield](/wiki/Oakenshield), [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), several Dwarves, and [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins), entered the Misty Mountains. While in the mountains, the [Great Goblin](/wiki/Great_Goblin) was killed and the party escaped. Furious, the Orcs gathered at Mount Gundabad under the command of [Bolg](/wiki/Bolg) to seek revenge. Hearing of the death of [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug), they marched on the [Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain).<sup id="cite_ref-Burst_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burst-11">[11]</a></sup> However, the Orcs lost the [Battle of Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies) and three parts of their numbers.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> Orcs still lived at Mount Gundabad by the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) but in a very reduced state.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Gundabad* is said to be a "in origin a [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) name". Its meaning, however, is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

A recogniseable element is Khuzdul *gundu*, meaning "underground hall", but it is not known if this word is indeed part of the name *Gundabad*.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>Gundabad is translated as &#34;Mountain-home&#34;. The region lying before the Gates of Gundabad, between <a href="/wiki/Langwell" title="Langwell">Langwell</a> and <a href="/wiki/Greylin" title="Greylin">Greylin</a>, is known as &#34;Elderslade&#34;. Months after the fall of <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>, Elderslade is the site of the &#34;War of Three Peaks&#34;, in which <a href="/wiki/Durin_VII" title="Durin VII">Prince Durin</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Thorin_Stonehelm" title="Thorin Stonehelm">Thorin Stonehelm</a>, leads an army of <a href="/wiki/Dwarves" title="Dwarves">Dwarves</a> to reclaim Gundabad from the forces of &#34;Gorgar the Ruthless&#34;, son of <a href="/wiki/Bolg" title="Bolg">Bolg</a>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Within the mountain, the &#34;Delvings of Gundabad&#34; are divided into five areas. &#34;Máttugard&#34; is the main citadel and site of Gundabad&#39;s throne. &#34;Deepscrave&#34; is a tall and deep cavern that once housed the common folk of Gundabad, now occupied by Orcs. The gem-studded &#34;Pit of Stone-jaws&#34; is home to Gundabad&#39;s tombs. In a basalt cavern at the heart of the mountain lies &#34;Gloomingtarn&#34;, called in Khuzdul &#34;Durthuzâram&#34;, the lake upon which Durin the Deathless awoke. The overgrown gardens of &#34;Clovengap&#34; are exposed to the sky through a rift in the mountainside.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>To the north of Gundabad lies &#34;Câr Bronach&#34;, the easternmost extent of Angmar. The western slopes of the mountain are called the &#34;Welkin-lofts&#34;, and are the site of several dwarven structures, including a meeting place for the Seven Houses.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>According to Longbeard legend, when he relocated his kingdom to Khazad-dûm, Durin I named his best friend, &#34;Mótsog&#34;, the first Lord of Gundabad. He came to regret that decision, for Mótsog betrayed Durin and led his &#34;Greymaul&#34; followers in an uprising against their fellow Longbeards.</dd></dl>
