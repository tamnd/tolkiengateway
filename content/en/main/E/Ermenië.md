**Ermenië** is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) term meaning **prime proceeding**. Among other renderings, it is termed the "one beginning",<sup id="cite_ref-reincarnation_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reincarnation-1">[1]</a></sup> or the "Prime Impulse".<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup>

A view held by some was that Ermenië referred to the start of life *within* the Tale of [Arda](../A/Arda.md).<sup id="cite_ref-reincarnation_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reincarnation-1">[1]</a></sup> In this view, the matters of [Eä](Eä.md) proceeded from a single <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Erma&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Erma (page does not exist)">Erma</a> ("Prime matter") which was shaped into <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Nassi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nassi (page does not exist)">nassi</a> ("forms", in this case, as "materials").<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup> Ermenië, the start of life, was then introduced, and it the unfolding of it was <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ercanti%C3%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ercantië (page does not exist)">Ercantië</a> (the "Prime Pattern"). Eventually, by catastrophe or simply the cessation of the impulse in living things to generate, Ercantië would complete and thus end. Following this, nassi would be reduced to Erma, and the Tale would end as it began.<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup>

However, another view, held to be more true to lore received from the [Valar](../V/Valar.md) and experienced history, was that Ermenië referred to the *uttermost* beginning, before Eä was even realized. In this view Ermenië referred to the first Theme which [Eru](Eru.md) taught to the [Ainur](../A/Ainur.md), which they then cooperatively elaborated on during the [Ainulindalë](../A/Ainulindalë.md). In this view, there was no single Prime Pattern of life (Ercantië), but a great number of early *Major* Patterns (<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Arcanti%C3%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Arcantië (page does not exist)">Arcantiër</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup>

The potential catastrophe in which all living things perished was thought to be a possible outcome achievable by [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md). This might be brought about in the event of him making a last attempt to achieve mastery or revenge of destruction.<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:note 8</sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Ermenië* is a compound of **[Er](Er.md)** ("one", "single", "prime"; when capitalized is a reference to Eru<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>) and <i>*<a href="../M/Mēnie.md" title="Mēnie">mēnie</a></i> ("determination", "impulse", "device")

It is variously rendered as the **Prime Impulse**,<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup> the **Device of Eru**,<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup> the **Theme of Eru**,<sup id="cite_ref-impulse_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-impulse-2">[2]</a></sup> and the **one beginning**.<sup id="cite_ref-reincarnation_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reincarnation-1">[1]</a></sup>
