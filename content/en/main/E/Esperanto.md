**Esperanto** is a widely spoken constructed language, the words of which were derived from European roots.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

As a teenager, Tolkien learned the language. He wrote notes about it in the [Book of the Foxrook](/wiki/Book_of_the_Foxrook) in [1909](/wiki/1909), and it inspired his first script, [Privata Kodo Skauta](/wiki/Privata_Kodo_Skauta).<sup id="cite_ref-Seven_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seven-2">[2]</a></sup> In [1932](/wiki/1932), Tolkien wrote a [letter](/wiki/A_Philologist_on_Esperanto "A Philologist on Esperanto") which was published in [The British Esperantist](/wiki/The_British_Esperantist,_Vol.28,_May_1932 "The British Esperantist, Vol.28, May 1932"), detailing his opinions on the language.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> By [1956](/wiki/1956), his opinion on the language had soured, concluding that "Volapük, Esperanto, Ido, Novial, &amp;c &amp;c are dead, far deader than ancient unused languages, because their authors never invented any Esperanto legends".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[William Auld](/wiki/William_Auld), who primarily wrote in the language, translated [The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings) as [La mastro de l' ringoj](/wiki/La_mastro_de_l%27_ringoj), and contributed to [La hobito](/wiki/La_hobito). In [2015](/wiki/2015), the book [J.R.R. Tolkien the Esperantist: Before the Arrival of Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien_the_Esperantist:_Before_the_Arrival_of_Bilbo_Baggins) was published.
