**Esperanto** is a widely spoken constructed language, the words of which were derived from European roots.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

As a teenager, Tolkien learned the language. He wrote notes about it in the [Book of the Foxrook](../B/Book_of_the_Foxrook.md) in [1909](../_/1909.md), and it inspired his first script, [Privata Kodo Skauta](../P/Privata_Kodo_Skauta.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Seven_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seven-2">[2]</a></sup> In [1932](../_/1932.md), Tolkien wrote a [letter](../A/A_Philologist_on_Esperanto.md) which was published in [The British Esperantist](../T/The_British_Esperantist,_Vol.28,_May_1932.md), detailing his opinions on the language.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> By [1956](../_/1956.md), his opinion on the language had soured, concluding that "Volapük, Esperanto, Ido, Novial, &amp;c &amp;c are dead, far deader than ancient unused languages, because their authors never invented any Esperanto legends".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[William Auld](../W/William_Auld.md), who primarily wrote in the language, translated [The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) as [La mastro de l' ringoj](../L/La_mastro_de_l'_ringoj.md), and contributed to [La hobito](../L/La_hobito.md). In [2015](../_/2015.md), the book [J.R.R. Tolkien the Esperantist: Before the Arrival of Bilbo Baggins](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien_the_Esperantist%253A_Before_the_Arrival_of_Bilbo_Baggins.md) was published.
