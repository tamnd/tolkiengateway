**Enedwaith** ([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"), pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[eˈnedwa͡ɪθ]</span>), also known as the **Central Wilderness**,<sup id="cite_ref-n76_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-n76-1">[1]</a></sup> was a region in the west of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) between the river [Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3) and the river [Isen](/wiki/Isen).

## Geography {#Geography}

Enedwaith was a region between the river Gwathló and the coast of the [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer) sea from the mouth of the Gwathló to the mouth of the river Isen in the west, the river [Glanduin](/wiki/Glanduin) in the north, the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) in the east and the river Isen in the south.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Enedwaith consisted primarily of open plains without mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-GwathloFirst_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GwathloFirst-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Earliest inhabitants {#Earliest_inhabitants}

Many of the inhabitants of the forests in Enedwaith were of the same kind as the [Folk of Haleth](/wiki/Haladin "Haladin"), the second folk of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain). They moved westwards later than the Folk of Haleth and had stayed in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), either in settlements or wandering around, and had not moved on to [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand). The language of the Folk of Haleth was not related to the language of the [Folk of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador "House of Hador") and the [Folk of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor").<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

At the time of the explorations of [Aldarion](/wiki/Aldarion), heir to the throne of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), Enedwaith was covered by an almost continuous forest with the exception of the great fens of the [Swanfleet](/wiki/Swanfleet). The native inhabitants were quite numerous and warlike and lived in scattered communities in the forests without a central leadership. In the beginning, the native inhabitants of Enedwaith were in awe of the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans).<sup id="cite_ref-ThirdParagraph_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ThirdParagraph-6">[6]</a></sup> Enedwaith was still covered with a dense forest at the time of the earlier settlements of the Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

The native inhabitants started to ambush the Númenóreans when the felling of trees by the Númenóreans became devastating to the forests. As a consequence, the Númenóreans treated the native inhabitants as enemies. The natives of Enedwaith that survived the conflicts with the Númenóreans fled into the eastern mountains to a region that would later be known as [Dunland](/wiki/Dunland).<sup id="cite_ref-ThirdParagraph_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ThirdParagraph-6">[6]</a></sup>

In the time of the Númenórean King [Ar-Adûnakhôr](/wiki/Ar-Ad%C3%BBnakh%C3%B4r), the [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") settlers between [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir) and the [Gulf of Lune](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lune) classified the inhabitants of the forests of [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath) as [Men of Shadow](/wiki/Men_of_Darkness "Men of Darkness") instead of as [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men). This was due to their hostility to the Númenóreans. It was also because the Númenóreans did not recognize them as kinsmen of the Edain. This was due to the language of the inhabitants of the forests of Minhiriath not being related to the languages of the Folk of Hador and the Folk of Beor, which were the languages from which the [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) language of Númenor was descended.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

After the establishment of the kingdoms of Arnor and Gondor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320"), Enedwaith belonged to neither of those two kingdoms.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

In the earlier centuries of Arnor and Gondor<sup id="cite_ref-NM_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-10">[10]</a></sup> Enedwaith belonged to neither of the two kingdoms.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup> In those ancient days, the [Gwathló](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3) was the eastern border of Arnor and the Isen was the western border of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-11">[11]</a></sup> Until the decay of Arnor, both kingdoms shared an interest in this region, but were mainly concerned with the building and maintenance of the [North-South Road](/wiki/North-South_Road), which was the main route of transport between the two kingdoms except by sea, and of the bridge at the fortified town and haven of [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) and of the long causeways on which the road ran to Tharbad on either side of the Gwathló across the fens in the plains of Enedwaith.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup> There were no permanent settlements of people of Númenórean origin in Enedwaith,<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup> except at Tharbad,<sup id="cite_ref-NM_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-10">[10]</a></sup> where a large garrison of soldiers, mariners, engineers<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup> and river-wardens was stationed.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-10">[10]</a></sup> In addition, the drainage works were built and maintained and the banks of the rivers [Mitheithel](/wiki/Mitheithel) and Gwathló were strengthened.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-10">[10]</a></sup>

After the decay and receding of the kingdom of Arnor, which had originally included Minhiriath,<sup id="cite_ref-NM_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT_10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-10">[10]</a></sup> Enedwaith became a part of Gondor during the days of the Kings of Gondor. Like before, Gondor was mainly concerned with the maintenance and patrolling of the North-South-Road.<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-11">[11]</a></sup>

After the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1636](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636") the region fell quickly into decay and turned back into wild fenlands long before the War of the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-Glanduin_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Glanduin-2">[2]</a></sup> A fairly numerous, barbarous folk<sup id="cite_ref-GwathloSecond_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GwathloSecond-12">[12]</a></sup> of [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) continued to live as fishers and fowlers in the marshlands of the mouths of the Gwathló and the Isen<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-11">[11]</a></sup> and between the mouths of those rivers<sup id="cite_ref-GwathloSecond_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GwathloSecond-12">[12]</a></sup> and the remnants of the people that the Rohirrim later called Dunlendings continued to live in the foothills of the western side of the Misty Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-11">[11]</a></sup> in the east of Enedwaith.<sup id="cite_ref-GwathloSecond_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GwathloSecond-12">[12]</a></sup>

After the death of the last king of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), [Arvedui](/wiki/Arvedui), and the end of the kingdom of Arthedain, in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1975](/wiki/Third_Age_1975 "Third Age 1975"),<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> and the death of the last king of Gondor, [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur), in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2050](/wiki/Third_Age_2050 "Third Age 2050"),<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> the weakened realm of Gondor retreated from Enedwaith and the Isen and the Gap of [Calenardhon](/wiki/Calenardhon) became the western border of Gondor. As a consequence, the Dunlendings of Enedwaith ceased to be subjects of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Isen_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Isen-11">[11]</a></sup>

After the [Fell Winter](</wiki/Fell_Winter_(Third_Age)> "Fell Winter (Third Age)") of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2911](/wiki/Third_Age_2911 "Third Age 2911"),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Enedwaith was devasted by great floods and the city of [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) was ruined and deserted in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2912](/wiki/Third_Age_2912 "Third Age 2912").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

At the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) the south-east of Enedwaith was still in places well-wooded, but most of the plains were grasslands.<sup id="cite_ref-GwathloSecond_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GwathloSecond-12">[12]</a></sup>

After the War of the Ring at the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), the North-kingdom of Arnor was reestablished by [Aragorn II](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn").<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> It is probable that Enedwaith was resettled, because [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) announced that the [Greenway](/wiki/Greenway) would be opened again, and that there would be people and fields where there was wilderness before and that there would be room enough for people between the Isen and the [Greyflood](/wiki/Gwathl%C3%B3 "Gwathló").<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Enedwaith* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name.<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-19">[19]</a></sup> It means "middle-region" or "middle-people".<sup id="cite_ref-Letter168_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter168-20">[20]</a></sup> It contains the element *ened(h)* ("middle, centre").<sup id="cite_ref-Letter168_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter168-20">[20]</a></sup> Its second element is probably the [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") form *waith*<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-19">[19]</a></sup> of *[gwaith](/wiki/Gwaith "Gwaith")* ("people").<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

The form *Enedwaith* appears in the [General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth), but [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) suggested *Enedhwaith* in his annotations to [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

While writing *[Of Dwarves and Men](/wiki/Of_Dwarves_and_Men),* Tolkien typed *Enedwaith* and subsequently added *h* manually on the typed text, until he started typing *Enedhwaith,* which he maintained in *[The Rivers and Beacon-hills of Gondor](/wiki/The_Rivers_and_Beacon-hills_of_Gondor)*. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) preferred the spelling *Enedwaith* for the *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)* for agreement with the published *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> It exists a letter from J.R.R. Tolkien to Pauline Baynes where "he has decided that the spelling Enedwaith should not be changed"<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup>, however Pauline Baynes ignored it for some reason, since the modified name does appear on [the map](/wiki/A_Map_of_Middle-earth "A Map of Middle-earth").
