**Eryn Vorn** ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for **Blackwood**<sup id="cite_ref-RClxv_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RClxv-1">[1]</a></sup>) was a wooded cape in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador),<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup> and a region of dark pine trees.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Located in western [Minhiriath](/wiki/Minhiriath), Eryn Vorn (likely named so by the Númenóreans) was originally part of the vast ancient treescape that covered most of north-western [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). During the Second Age, however, these forests were decimated by the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) in their greed for ship-building timber, before being almost completely burnt down by the forces of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) during the ensuing war in Eriador.<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup>

By the latter half of the Second Age, the surviving natives of Minhiriath had retreated either north to [Bree](/wiki/Bree), or hidden themselves in Eryn Vorn which was probably all that remained of the vast forests in Minhiriath for long years thereafter.<sup id="cite_ref-RClxv_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RClxv-1">[1]</a></sup>

From [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861"), Eryn Vorn nominally formed a part of [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and by the end of the Third Age, scattered woodlands had reappeared in much of the rest of Minhiriath.

It is probable that people remained hidden in Eryn Vorn by the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). Although Bree was the westernmost permanent settlement of [Men](/wiki/Men) by the late Third Age,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> the woods of Minhiriath were home of a few, secretive hunter-folk at the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring). These wild people were afraid to cross the river [Baranduin](/wiki/Baranduin) because of the Elves who dwelt beyond.<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Eryn Vorn* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and has been given the translation "Blackwood". The name consists of *[eryn](/wiki/Eryn "Eryn")* ("wood") and *vorn* ("dark"),<sup id="cite_ref-RClxv_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RClxv-1">[1]</a></sup> the latter being the [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") form of *[morn](/wiki/Morn "Morn")*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

The name *Eryn Vorn* first appeared on [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes)'s *[A Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/A_Map_of_Middle-earth)* ([1970](/wiki/1970)), for which [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") had supplied <a class="external text" href="https://www.tolkiensociety.org/2015/11/tolkiens-annotated-map-of-middle-earth-transcribed/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">notes</a> on additional names. The first printing of the map wrongly has *Erin Voru*.<sup id="cite_ref-RClxv_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RClxv-1">[1]</a></sup>

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Eryn Vorn, the Black Wood (called <i>Choil Borba</i> in the local tongue), covers the promontory of Rast Vorn (the Dark Cape). The large wood is a wild land, inhabited by <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Woses" title="Woses">Woses</a> and evil creatures.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1995-8: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game" title="Middle-earth Collectible Card Game">Middle-earth Collectible Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The card game features the &#34;<a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Woses" title="Woses">Woses</a> of the Eryn Vorn&#34; in the expansion <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_Against_the_Shadow" title="Middle-earth: Against the Shadow">Against the Shadow</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game)" title="The One Ring (role-playing game)">The One Ring</a> — <a href="/wiki/Ruins_of_the_Lost_Realm" title="Ruins of the Lost Realm">Ruins of the Lost Realm</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In their long isolation, the Tree-folk of Eryn Vorn had forgotten their old tongues. They not only hunted intruders into their forest, but even raided outside it, which was a particular pain to the people of <a href="/wiki/Lond_Daer" title="Lond Daer">Lond Daer</a>. Most captives were brought to the Tree of Sorrows and hung from it, drained of their blood.<sup id="cite_ref-erynvorn_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erynvorn-10">[9]</a></sup> Their original religion, nature-worship based on shreds of knowledge from dying <a href="/wiki/Ents" title="Ents">Ents</a>, was co-opted by allies of Sauron, who afterwards was worshipped as the King of Death.<sup id="cite_ref-tree_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tree-11">[10]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>There were many caves underneath the Tree of Sorrows, some of which were used by the Tree-folk. One of the unused caves was the Shunned Cave, which reached down to the foundations of the earth, to a trapped or sleeping <a href="/wiki/Nameless_things" title="Nameless things">Nameless Thing</a>. This was the source of the Tree&#39;s evil power.<sup id="cite_ref-tree_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tree-11">[10]</a></sup> Another cave held the monster Thunir. He was once a Tree Man who had joined with Sauron in revenge against Númenor. Fueled and transformed by vile concoctions over the centuries, he still lived by <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2965" title="Third Age 2965">2965</a> though was barely recognizable as a Man. Thunir was tended to by priests.<sup id="cite_ref-tree_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tree-11">[10]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Near the tree were old standing stones raised by Elves, though by <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2965" title="Third Age 2965">2965</a> their writings were no longer legible.<sup id="cite_ref-tree_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tree-11">[10]</a></sup> Behind the forest, at the far end of the cape, was a barrow mound topped by black stone pillar, which predated the Númenórean arrival. It is unknown whether this belonged to an Elf or Dwarf.<sup id="cite_ref-erynvorn_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erynvorn-10">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>
