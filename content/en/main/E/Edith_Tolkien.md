**Edith Mary Tolkien** (née **Bratt**) ([January 21](../_/21_January.md), [1889](../_/1889.md) – [November 29](../_/29_November.md), [1971](../_/1971.md)) was the daughter of Frances Bratt and Alfred Frederick Warrilow. She would become [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s wife.

## Biography {#Biography}

Edith never knew her father and was brought up in [Birmingham](../B/Birmingham.md) by her mother, and cousin Jenny Grove. Francis Bratt died when Edith was 14 and she was sent to the Dresden House, a boarding school where she developed a love for piano and soon moved into a boarding house.<sup id="cite_ref-ple_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ple-1">[1]</a></sup>

When Tolkien was living at [37 Duchess Road](../_/37_Duchess_Road.md), [Edgbaston](../B/Birmingham.md), Edith was a young female lodger who lived in the bedroom beneath Tolkien and his brother Hilary. At the time Tolkien was 16 and Edith was 19. They quickly became close but when Tolkien's guardian [Francis Xavier Morgan](../F/Francis_Xavier_Morgan.md) found out he forbade contact between them until Tolkien turned 21. During their years apart she got engaged to George Field but when Tolkien sent her a letter on the evening of his twenty-first birthday she broke off her engagement to Field and announced her engagement to Tolkien. Following her engagement she converted to Roman Catholicism.<sup id="cite_ref-ple_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ple-1">[1]</a></sup>

They were married on Wednesday, [22 March](../_/22_March.md), [1916](../_/1916.md) in the Church of St Mary Immaculate, in Warwickshire, England. Only a few months later Tolkien was shipped to Étaples, France.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Her Husband being in the military during [WWI](../W/WWI.md) was a very stressful time in her life. She kept a map of France on the wall to help her know where he was. After his return their first child [John Tolkien](../J/John_Tolkien.md) was born on [16 November](../_/16_November.md) [1917](../_/1917.md)

While stationed at Kingston upon Hull, Ronald and Edith went on a walk through the woods and she danced for him. He later tells an interviewer [Bill Cater](../B/Bill_Cater.md), "We walked in a wood where hemlock was growing, a sea of white flowers." This inspired the meeting of [Beren](../B/Beren.md) and [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> After the war the Tolkiens had 3 more children, [Michael](../M/Michael_Tolkien.md), [Christopher](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) and [Priscilla](../P/Priscilla_Tolkien.md).

In the 1960s they moved near [Bournemouth](../B/Bournemouth.md). Her grandson, [Simon Tolkien](../S/Simon_Tolkien.md) writes:

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Tolkiengrave.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/ec/Tolkiengrave.jpg/200px-Tolkiengrave.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/ec/Tolkiengrave.jpg/300px-Tolkiengrave.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/e/ec/Tolkiengrave.jpg/400px-Tolkiengrave.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536"/></a><figcaption>The grave of J.R.R. and Edith Tolkien</figcaption></figure>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Bournemouth was certainly not My Grandfather&#39;s first choice of place to live, and I&#39;m sure he missed Oxford. However, my grandmother loved the Miramar, and My Grandfather wanted to make her happy in the last years of their lives.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

Edith Tolkien died on [29 November](../_/29_November.md) [1971](../_/1971.md) at the age of 82 and was buried in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolvercote_Cemetery" title="wikipedia:Wolvercote Cemetery">Wolvercote Cemetery</a>. J.R.R. Tolkien never referred to Edith as *Lúthien*, but he considered her as such, so he asked that the name Lúthien be inscribed on her grave.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> When he died 21 months later he was buried with her and had *Beren* inscribed on the gravestone.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

In the biographical drama film, named [Tolkien](<../T/Tolkien_(film).md>), she was portrayed by actresses [Lily Collins](../L/Lily_Collins.md) and Mimi Keene.
