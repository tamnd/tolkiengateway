The **Elves** (*[Quendi](/wiki/Quendi)*) were the first of the races of the [Children of Ilúvatar](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar), known also as the [Firstborn](/wiki/Firstborn) for that reason. The Elves are distinguished from the other two races, the [Men](/wiki/Men) and the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves), especially by the fact of their near [immortality](/wiki/Immortality "Immortality").

About the same time that [Varda](/wiki/Varda), Queen of the [Valier](/wiki/Valier), ended her labours in creating the [Stars](/wiki/Stars), the Elves awoke beside the lake [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen). The first things they saw were the stars, and henceforth they adored them. The first sound they heard was the flowing of water, the noise and splash of water on the stones. And henceforth they loved water as well.

They made speech then, and called themselves the *[Quendi](/wiki/Quendi)*. [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth") was the first to be aware of them, and he caused evil spirits to go about among them. When one or a small group wandered abroad, they would often vanish. It is believed that Melkor may have created [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) with the elves he captured.

[Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB), the Huntsman of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), happened upon them when he heard their singing far off. He was amazed to see them, and called them the *Eldar*, "People of the Stars".

Although at first the Quendi were afraid of Oromë, the noblest among them saw that he was no dark horseman, as the lies of Melkor claimed. He had the light of [Aman](/wiki/Aman) in his eyes and face, and they were drawn to him.

After spending a while among the Quendi, Oromë returned to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) and took council with the other Valar and Valier. At the counsel of [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar), [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB), King of the Valar, decided that they must go to war against Melkor to protect the Quendi from him, beginning the [Battle of the Powers](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Powers). After a great battle and a siege against [Utumno](/wiki/Utumno), which reshaped the earth itself, Melkor was bound and cast into the prison of [Mandos](/wiki/Mandos). Then the Valar, pleased with the outcome, summoned the Elves to Valinor, seeking fellowship with them; and they were captivated by the beauty of the Elves.

At Oromë's urging, many of the Elves (especially the kindreds of [Ingwë](/wiki/Ingw%C3%AB), [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB), and [Elwë](/wiki/Elw%C3%AB)) agreed. But others, henceforth called the [Avari](/wiki/Avari), declared that they preferred starlight and the wide spaces of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). So the Elves were first sundered. During the journey to [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer), gradually the number of the Elves began to lessen as various groups dropped away. Some of the [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri) (kindred of Elwë) refused to cross the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and settled in [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) under the leadership of [Lenwë](/wiki/Lenw%C3%AB), to be called later the [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor). Elwë then went missing, and in dismay the rest of the Teleri remained behind, while the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) (kindred of Finwë) and [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar) (kindred of Ingwë) used an island as a ship, and found at last Aman and Valinor.

After several years, Oromë returned to search for the Teleri. Some, under [Olwë](/wiki/Olw%C3%AB), relented and followed. Others remained to continue to search for Elwë. Still others, under [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan), remained because in that time they had become devoted to [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB) and the Sea. Those Teleri that chose to remain were called the [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar). Elwë, who had fallen asleep due to his enchantment with [Melian](/wiki/Melian), returned to claim lordship and establish them in [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath). The Noldor and some of the Teleri, however, built the great cities of [Tirion](/wiki/Tirion) and [Alqualondë](/wiki/Alqualond%C3%AB), respectively, in Aman. The Vanyar dwelt in [Valimar](/wiki/Valimar), for they were closest to the Valar of the kindreds.

### Exile of the Noldor {#Exile_of_the_Noldor}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="/wiki/Exile_of_the_Noldor" title="Exile of the Noldor">Exile of the Noldor</a></b></div></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r398211"/>

<br>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg/200px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg/300px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/0d/Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg/400px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_The_Coming_of_Fingolfin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="650" data-file-height="401"/></a><figcaption><i>The Coming of Fingolfin</i> by <a href="/wiki/Jenny_Dolfen" title="Jenny Dolfen">Jenny Dolfen</a></figcaption></figure>

Melkor, having been released on the promise of good behaviour, spread lies about the Valar among the Noldor. [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor), the eldest son of Finwë and one of the greatest of all the Elves, hated Melkor more than all the other Noldor, but was paradoxically one of the most influenced by his lies. He forged weapons, and his greatest works, the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils), captured the light of the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees) – and his own heart. After Melkor stole the Silmarils and killed Finwë, Fëanor stirred the Noldor to open disobedience to the Valar. In an journey filled with treachery, death, and deceit, the Noldor entered in to exile, crossing over into [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand).

### Battles of Beleriand {#Battles_of_Beleriand}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="/wiki/Battles_of_Beleriand" title="Battles of Beleriand">Battles of Beleriand</a></b></div></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r398211"/>

<br>

There were five great battles fought in Beleriand. The [First Battle](/wiki/First_Battle) was the result of an attack by Melkor on Círdan and Elwë (now known as [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol)). Though the Elves managed to resist the attack successfully, this left Melkor essentially with full reign of Beleriand. Upon the sudden and unanticipated [Return of the Noldor](/wiki/Return_of_the_Noldor), the tables were reversed in the [Dagor-nuin-Giliath](/wiki/Dagor-nuin-Giliath). The third battle (“[Dagor Aglareb](/wiki/Dagor_Aglareb)”) occurred when Melkor tried unsuccessfully to destroy the Elves, breaking forth from [Angband](/wiki/Angband). This only resulted in the vigilant [Siege of Angband](/wiki/Siege_of_Angband). Morgoth was more successful in the next battle, [Dagor Bragollach](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach), which ended in the deaths of many Elven princes, among them [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin), [High King of the Noldor](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor). The siege was broken. Several decades later, [Maedhros](/wiki/Maedhros), eldest son of Fëanor, counterattacked in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad). Although at first very successful, the tide turned against the Elves, and ended in the destruction of [Hithlum](/wiki/Hithlum). It was not half a century later that [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), the last real stronghold of the Noldor, was [destroyed](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin"). [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), the centre of the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindar "Sindar") realm, was sacked by [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves).

### Salvation {#Salvation}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="/wiki/War_of_Wrath" title="War of Wrath">War of Wrath</a></b></div></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r398211"/>

<br> With the near destruction of the Elves, the last survivors were at the [Mouths of Sirion](/wiki/Mouths_of_Sirion) and [Balar](/wiki/Isle_of_Balar "Isle of Balar"), and were led by Gil-galad and Círdan. Among them was [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil), the son of [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor) and [Idril](/wiki/Idril). Eärendil made a miraculous voyage to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) to beg the pardon of the Valar. His request was granted. The Valar came across the Sea to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), and in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) thrust Morgoth into the [Void](/wiki/Void) and purged Beleriand. They offered to let the Elves return with them to Valinor; some accepted, but many others, under [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad), chose to remain.

### Decline {#Decline}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/4f/Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif/150px-Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="150" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/4f/Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif/225px-Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/4f/Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif/300px-Angus_McBride_-_Celebrimbor.gif 2x" data-file-width="493" data-file-height="529"/></a><figcaption><i>Celebrimbor</i> by <a href="/wiki/Angus_McBride" title="Angus McBride">Angus McBride</a></figcaption></figure>

Although Morgoth was gone to trouble the world no longer, [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), his greatest servant, was still there, and he made war on the remaining Elves who chose not to depart Middle-earth throughout the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and [Third Ages](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age").

During this time the Elves realized how [Men](/wiki/Men) were rising to take their place, and Sauron exploited their longing for the Undying Lands. [Annatar](/wiki/Annatar) corrupted [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor), the grandson of Fëanor, to make the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power), with the [Three Rings](/wiki/Three_Rings) being created specifically to preserve the Elves against the ravages of time. Annatar was a guise of Sauron who also forged a ring – [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). The Elves, however, realised the deception and defied Sauron, who then [waged War against them](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron "War of the Elves and Sauron"). In the following centuries Elves continued to heed the invitation of the Valar, desire the Sea and depart for the Undying Lands. Realms such as [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth) were deserted and gave their place to Men.

It was not until the end of the Third Age that the One Ring was destroyed, marring the Three Rings at the same time. In the years that followed, the last of the Elves departed across the Sea to Valinor, their mission against Sauron complete, and never to return.

Well into the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age) and the [Dominion of Men](/wiki/Dominion_of_Men), most Elves had left the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands), with most populations remaining in [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood) and [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon). [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) and [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) appeared abandoned around the time of [King Elessar](/wiki/King_Elessar)'s and [Arwen](/wiki/Arwen)'s death.<sup id="cite_ref-AppTale_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppTale-1">[1]</a></sup> The [Last Ship](/wiki/Last_Ship) of the Elves, carrying [Cirdan](/wiki/Cirdan) and [Celeborn](/wiki/Celeborn),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> apparently sailed early in the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-AppTale_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppTale-1">[1]</a></sup>

The remaining Elves in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) eventually faded, as their spirits overwhelmed and consumed their bodies. By the end of the world, all Elves will have become invisible to mortal eyes, known as *Lingerers*, except to those to whom they wish to manifest themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) had not revealed the role of the Elves after [the End](/wiki/The_End "The End").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Life and customs {#Life_and_customs}

<dl><dd><i>Main articles: </i><a href="/wiki/Elven_characteristics" title="Elven characteristics">Elven characteristics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Elven_life_cycle" title="Elven life cycle">Elven life cycle</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Elven_customs" title="Elven customs">Elven customs</a></dd></dl>

Besides being considered more beautiful than men, Elves were also generally taller. Their hair colour varied; but the basic rules were that the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) generally had dark hair (brown or black), the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar) golden, and the [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri) silver or dark. Their eyes are usually described as grey or blue.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/2/23/Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg/300px-Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="198" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/2/23/Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg/450px-Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/2/23/Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg/600px-Elena_Kukanova_-_A_child_of_the_Elder_Race.jpg 2x" data-file-width="850" data-file-height="562"/></a><figcaption><i>A child of the Elder Race</i> by <a href="/wiki/Elena_Kukanova" title="Elena Kukanova">Elena Kukanova</a></figcaption></figure>

Their lives were counted to begin at conception rather than birth, and though their minds sharpened much earlier in life than in the race of Men, their bodies grew more slowly. They were considered fully-grown at about a century. They married usually only once in their lives, and their children were often few and far-between.

Their most distinguishing characteristic from the [Mortal](/wiki/Mortals "Mortals") races was the fact that they were invulnerable to age or disease; unless they were killed by sword or sorrow, they would live to the end of the world. Unlike Men whose [fëar](/wiki/F%C3%ABar "Fëar") (spirits) left Arda when their bodies died, Elves' fëar were bound to Arda until its ending. If an Elf's hröa (body) died, its fëa would be summoned to the [Halls of Mandos](/wiki/Halls_of_Mandos), where the Valar could re-embody the Elf in a hröa that was identical to the Elf's previous hröa. However, if an Elf committed evil acts during their lifetime and refused to repent, the Valar could delay the Elf's re-embodiment, impose conditions on it, or refuse it altogether.<sup id="cite_ref-P4i_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-P4i-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> An Elf could refuse the summons to Mandos or choose to remain disembodied,<sup id="cite_ref-P4i_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-P4i-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> but the Elf's houseless fëa would still be unable to leave Arda.

## Arts, crafts, powers and magic {#Arts,_crafts,_powers_and_magic}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="/wiki/Magic" title="Magic">Magic</a></b></div></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r398211"/>

Other races often spoke of 'Elf magic', or of objects made by Elves as if they contained enchantments. It is unclear how accurate it is to call Elvish arts and crafts 'magic' or 'enchanted'. Elves themselves only used these words when attempting to simplify or clarify how elvish-made things seemed to have a special quality that no other races were able to achieve. Powerful Elves seemed to have control over nature and the elements, their clothes seemed to shine with their own light, their blades seemed to never lose their sharpness. Less educated folks could not explain these effects, so they simply called them 'magic'.

Elf-minstrels had the gift to make visions of the things they sung before their audiences.<sup id="cite_ref-AppTale_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppTale-1">[1]</a></sup>

When Oromë invited the Elves to Valinor, those who followed him on the Great Journey were called the Eldar,<sup id="cite_ref-B_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B-8">[8]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:374</sup> while those who refused were called the Avari.<sup id="cite_ref-S3_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S3-9">[9]</a></sup> The Eldar were divided into three clans−the Vanyar, the Noldor, and the Teleri.<sup id="cite_ref-S3_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S3-9">[9]</a></sup> All of the Vanyar and Noldor reached Aman. Two groups of Teleri abandoned the Great Journey: the Nandor, who came to live in the [Vale of Anduin](/wiki/Vale_of_Anduin),<sup id="cite_ref-S3_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S3-9">[9]</a></sup> and the Sindar, who remained in Beleriand.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> The Nandor eventually split into the Laiquendi, who migrated into Beleriand and settled in [Ossiriand](/wiki/Ossiriand), and the Silvan Elves, who established realms in [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood) and [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien). Those Teleri who completed the Great Journey and settled in Aman were called the Falmari.<sup id="cite_ref-S3_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S3-9">[9]</a></sup> Those Noldor who later returned to Middle-earth in exile were called the *Etyañgoldi*.<sup id="cite_ref-B_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B-8">[8]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:374</sup>

Since they came into the world long before [Men, the *Aftercomers*](/wiki/Men "Men"), it should be noted that the name **Firstborn** properly applied as a demonym to all Elves *as a whole*,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> while the very first 144 Elves, who were awakened by Ilúvatar himself at Cuiviénen, were called the **[Eru-begotten](/wiki/Eru-begotten)**.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien - Fangorn Forest.jpg">drawing</a> with <a href="/wiki/Beleg" title="Beleg">Beleg</a>, a rare depiction of an Elf made by Tolkien</figcaption></figure>

### Languages {#Languages_2}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") developed the Elves (and his whole [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium")) to serve as a setting for his languages that he constructed according to his [personal sense of beauty](/wiki/L%C3%A1maty%C3%A1v%C3%AB "Lámatyávë"). His Elven languages are of special interest to many Tolkien scholars. His most developed are [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), along with more obscure dialects for which he invented (at best) a small vocabulary, usually in earlier stages of his creation.

### Germanic influence {#Germanic_influence}

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, a diminutive fairy-like race of elves had once been a great and mighty people who had "diminished" as Men took over the world.<sup id="cite_ref-bolt1_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bolt1-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Fimi_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fimi-16">[16]</a></sup> They were influenced by the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elf" title="wikipedia:Elf">Elves</a> of Northern European mythologies, especially the god-like and human-sized <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_elf" title="wikipedia:Light elf">Ljósálfar</a></i> of Norse mythology,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> also appearing in medieval works such as <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Sir_Orfeo" title="Sir Orfeo">Sir Orfeo</a></i>, the Welsh [Mabinogion](/wiki/Pwyll_Prince_of_Dyved "Pwyll Prince of Dyved"), [Arthurian romances](/wiki/The_Fall_of_Arthur "The Fall of Arthur") and the legends of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann" title="wikipedia:Tuatha Dé Danann">Tuatha Dé Danann</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson1_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson1-18">[18]</a></sup>

Terry Gunnell also claims that the relationship between beautiful ships and the Elves is reminiscent of Njörðr and Skíðblaðni, Freyr's ship.<sup id="cite_ref-Articles_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Articles-19">[19]</a></sup>

### Celtic influence {#Celtic_influence}

Tolkien expressed a dislike of Celtic legends and denied that his legendarium is "Celtic",<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> however it is believed that Celtic Mythology had a great influence on Tolkien's writings on Elves,<sup id="cite_ref-Articles_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Articles-19">[19]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> and some of the stories Tolkien wrote as their 'legends' are directly influenced by it.<sup id="cite_ref-Fimi_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fimi-16">[16]</a></sup> For example, the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) are based on the Tuatha Dé Danann in the <i><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebor_Gab%C3%A1la_%C3%89renn" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Lebor Gabála Érenn</a></i>, and their migratory nature comes from early Irish/Celtic history.<sup id="cite_ref-Fimi_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fimi-16">[16]</a></sup> John Garth has also referenced the Tuatha Dé Danann in suggesting Tolkien was essentially rewriting Irish fairy traditions.

Tolkien also retains the usage of the Celtic and popular term 'fairy' for the same creatures.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> The Elves are also called fair folk based on [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) *Tylwyth teg* 'the beautiful kindred' = fairies,<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> although they are unrelated to fairies.

### Eschatology {#Eschatology}

The larger Elves are also inspired by Tolkien's [Christian theology](/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") — as representing the state of Men in Eden who have not yet "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Man" title="wikipedia:Fall of Man">fallen</a>" — similar to humans but fairer and wiser, with greater spiritual powers, keener senses, and a closer empathy with nature, freed from human limitations, immortal, with wills directly effective for the achievement of imagination and desire.<sup id="cite_ref-Carpenter_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Carpenter-24">[24]</a></sup>

### Fairies {#Fairies}

<p>By writing about elves as tall as men I am restoring tradition, trying to rescue the word from the nursery.

<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien, quoted by <a href="/wiki/William_Cater" title="William Cater">William Cater</a> in &#34;Lord of the Hobbits&#34; (Daily Express, <a href="/wiki/22_November" title="22 November">22 November</a> <a href="/wiki/1966" title="1966">1966</a>, p. 10</cite></p>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Traditional "Victorian" dancing [fairies](/wiki/Fairies "Fairies") and elves appear in Tolkien's early poetry,<sup id="cite_ref-bolt1_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bolt1-14">[14]</a></sup> and have influence upon his later works<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> in part due to the influence of a production of J.M. Barrie's *Peter Pan* in [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) in 1910<sup id="cite_ref-Carpenter_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Carpenter-24">[24]</a></sup> and his familiarity with the work of Catholic mystic poet, Francis Thompson<sup id="cite_ref-Carpenter_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Carpenter-24">[24]</a></sup> whose work Tolkien had acquired in 1914.<sup id="cite_ref-bolt1_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bolt1-14">[14]</a></sup>

In *The Book of Lost Tales* Tolkien includes both the more serious 'medieval' type of elves such as [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) and [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) alongside the frivolous, "Jacobean-era" type of elves such as the [Solosimpi](/wiki/Solosimpi) and [Tinúviel](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien "Lúthien").<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson1_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson1-18">[18]</a></sup>

Tolkien also developed the idea of children visiting [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) in their sleep. Elves would also visit and comfort chided or upset children at night. This theme was largely abandoned.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

However after the publication of *The Lord of the Rings*, Tolkien repeatedly expressed his misgivings concerning the undesirable associations of the name "elf" like those of *A Midsummer Night's Dream*, the Victorian notions of fairies or mischievous imps, the imaginations of Michael Drayton or the fanciful beings with butterfly wings.

He proposed that in <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Translations_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Translations of The Lord of the Rings (page does not exist)">translations</a> the "oldest available form of the name" be used for more elevated notions of beings "supposed to possess formidable magical powers in early Teutonic mythology" ([OED](/wiki/OED) viz. the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *ælf*, from Proto-Germanic <i>*albo-z</i>). Tolkien warned against associations to the debased English notion of *elfin* and suggested that Germans would not translate his *Elves* as *Elfen*, which might retain the undesirable images. He rather suggested words such as *Alp* or *Alb*, historically the more normal form and true cognate of English *elf*.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> [Margaret Carroux](/wiki/Margaret_Carroux) chose the word *Elben* (singular *Elb*) in her translation.
