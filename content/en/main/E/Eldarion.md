**Eldarion** was the second [High King](<../H/High_King_(Dúnedain).md>) of the [Reunited Kingdom](../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md). He was the only son of King [Elessar](../A/Aragorn.md) and Queen [Arwen Undómiel](../A/Arwen.md).

## History {#History}

Eldarion was born in the 1st century of the [Fourth Age](../F/Fourth_Age.md). He did not inherit the choice of becoming an Elf, but had a long youth like one, having an appeareance of 20 years old until 60.<sup id="cite_ref-NOME_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOME-6">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78</sup>

Eldarion became the second King of the [Reunited Kingdom](../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md) after the death of his father [Elessar](../A/Aragorn.md) in [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [120](../F/Fourth_Age_120.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-4">[2]</a></sup> At the time of his ascending to the throne, Eldarion was "full ripe and ready for kingship". It is known that Eldarion was given the tokens of kingship ([Andúril](../A/Andúril.md), etc.) by his father, who died soon after the crowning of his son. He was also recorded to have several sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-Tale_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tale-4">[2]</a></sup>

During Eldarion's reign, [Findegil](../F/Findegil.md) served as the royal scribe (or King's Writer). In [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [172](../F/Fourth_Age_172.md), Findegil wrote a copy of the [Thain's Book](../T/Thain's_Book.md) for the [Thain](../T/Thain.md) of the [Shire](../S/Shire.md), probably at the request of [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md)'s great-grandson. This copy was the only one to include [Bilbo's](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) complete [Translations from the Elvish](../T/Translations_from_the_Elvish.md), which Bilbo had composed while at [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) during his 'retirement' there.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup>

It was foretold that he should rule a great realm, and that it should endure for a hundred generations of [Men](../M/Men.md) after him, that is until a new age brought in again new things; from him should come the kings of many realms in long days after.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup>

Eldarion's reign as High King of the Reunited Kingdom came to an end in the year [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [220](../F/Fourth_Age_220.md) of the Fourth Age. Around this time, a [dark cult](../D/Dark_Tree.md) rose to power, but was discovered and defeated that same year. Whether the two events are connected with each other remains unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-L338_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L338-2">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

His name is [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md). Paul Strack explains it means "Son of the [Eldar](Eldar.md)", being a compound of *Eldar* and patronymic *[-ion](../_/-ion.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup> In [Letter 338](../L/Letter_338.md), [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) spelled this name <i><b>Eldaron</b></i>: this is probably a typographic error.<sup id="cite_ref-L338_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L338-2">[1]</a></sup>

## Genealogy {#Genealogy}

Eldarion was descended from several royal Elven houses, including the Three High Kings of the Elves, and from the [Edain](Edain.md). He was a grandson of [Elrond](Elrond.md) and, through his grandmother [Celebrían](../C/Celebrían.md), a great-grandson of [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md). He was the great-grandson of [Eärendil](Eärendil.md) the Mariner (Eldarion is descended from Eärendil on both sides of his family and in him the two genealogical lines of the [Half-elven](../H/Half-elven.md) are reunited), through his Half-elven mother Arwen; he was also the nephew of the Half-elf lords [Elladan](Elladan.md) and [Elrohir](Elrohir.md). He was descended also from the great [Kings of Westernesse](../K/Kings_of_Númenor.md) on his father's side.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-5">[3]</a></sup> Tolkien mentioned the existence of descendants, whose only known fact is that they possessed a normal life span of around 80–90 years.<sup id="cite_ref-NOME_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOME-6">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:79</sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the twelfth note to a text written around [1959](../_/1959.md) and published in *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)*, it is said that either the slow maturity of the [Half-elven](../H/Half-elven.md), or the "choice of kindred"<sup id="cite_ref-NOME_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOME-6">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78</sup> extended to the third generation, suggesting that Eldarion, and [Tar-Amandil](../T/Tar-Amandil.md), were among the Half-elven.<sup id="cite_ref-NOME_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOME-6">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:82</sup> However, this was changed to the second generation in the actual text.<sup id="cite_ref-NOME_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOME-6">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78</sup> In the same text, Tolkien wrote that Eldarion had lived until the age of 125, most likely having passed away in [Fo.A. 126](../F/Fourth_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-NOME_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NOME-6">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:79</sup> However, it predates the second edition of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, where the date of Aragorn's death was ultimately changed by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) from [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [100](../F/Fourth_Age_100.md) (as in the first edition) to [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [120](../F/Fourth_Age_120.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:420</sup>
