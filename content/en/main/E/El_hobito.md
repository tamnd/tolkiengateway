<i><b>El hobito</b></i> was the first translation into Spanish of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*. It was published in [1964](../_/1964.md) in Argentina.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On [3 July](../_/3_July.md) [1962](../_/1962.md), [Alina Dadlez](../A/Alina_Dadlez.md) sent Tolkien a copy of letter from the Argentinian publisher Compañia General Fabril Editora, concerning a Spanish translation of *The Hobbit*. Tolkien [replied](<../L/Letter_to_Alina_Dadlez_(20_July_1962).md>) on 20 July to Dadlez, sending her comments about how to translate specific words — suggesting the form *hobitos* for *[hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md)*. Dadlez forwarded Tolkien's comments to the publisher in Argentina, and wrote to Tolkien again on 17 September: the publishers point out that since *h* is mute in Spanish, perhaps the form *jobitos* could be used. Tolkien [replied](<../L/Letter_to_Alina_Dadlez_(19_September_1962).md>) on 19 September, saying that he still prefers *hobitos*: "I am sure many hobbits drop their *h*s like most rural folk in England".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

*El hobito* was apparently not very successful since only one edition was published. A second Spanish translation did not appear until 1982 (*El Hobbit*, published by Minotauro).<sup id="cite_ref-TL_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-5">[5]</a></sup>

Tolkien received a few copies of *El hobito* from the publisher in Argentina, and apparently gave one to his wife [Edith](Edith_Tolkien.md). A copy is known to have been signed by Tolkien on the title page, including a dedication in Spanish: "para Edita querida R" ("to \[my\] dear Edith\[,\] R\[onald\]"). After Tolkien's death, his eldest son Father [John Tolkien](../J/John_Tolkien.md) gave the book to Father Antonio Quevedo in September 1973.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-5">[5]</a></sup>

## From the back {#From_the_back}

Las páginas de este libro tienen la magia y el particular sabor de las antiguas sagas escandinavas. Hermanado con las obras de Swift, Bunyan y Lewis Caroll ... *El hobito* señala la plenitud creadora de J.R.R. Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-UAN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UAN-1">[1]</a></sup>

\[Translation: "The pages of this book contain the magic and the peculiar flavour of the ancient Norse sagas. Related to the works of Swift, Bunyan, and Lewis Carroll ... *El hobito* shows the full creativity of J.R.R. Tolkien."\]
