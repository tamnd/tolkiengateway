<i><b>El hobito</b></i> was the first translation into Spanish of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*. It was published in [1964](/wiki/1964) in Argentina.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On [3 July](/wiki/3_July) [1962](/wiki/1962), [Alina Dadlez](/wiki/Alina_Dadlez) sent Tolkien a copy of letter from the Argentinian publisher Compañia General Fabril Editora, concerning a Spanish translation of *The Hobbit*. Tolkien [replied](</wiki/Letter_to_Alina_Dadlez_(20_July_1962)> "Letter to Alina Dadlez (20 July 1962)") on 20 July to Dadlez, sending her comments about how to translate specific words — suggesting the form *hobitos* for *[hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits")*. Dadlez forwarded Tolkien's comments to the publisher in Argentina, and wrote to Tolkien again on 17 September: the publishers point out that since *h* is mute in Spanish, perhaps the form *jobitos* could be used. Tolkien [replied](</wiki/Letter_to_Alina_Dadlez_(19_September_1962)> "Letter to Alina Dadlez (19 September 1962)") on 19 September, saying that he still prefers *hobitos*: "I am sure many hobbits drop their *h*s like most rural folk in England".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

*El hobito* was apparently not very successful since only one edition was published. A second Spanish translation did not appear until 1982 (*El Hobbit*, published by Minotauro).<sup id="cite_ref-TL_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-5">[5]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/9/9f/El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg/300px-El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/9/9f/El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg/450px-El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/9/9f/El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg/600px-El_hobito_-_Inscribed_by_Tolkien.jpg 2x" data-file-width="900" data-file-height="675"/></a><figcaption>Tolkien&#39;s inscription in <i>El hobito</i>.</figcaption></figure>

Tolkien received a few copies of *El hobito* from the publisher in Argentina, and apparently gave one to his wife [Edith](/wiki/Edith_Tolkien "Edith Tolkien"). A copy is known to have been signed by Tolkien on the title page, including a dedication in Spanish: "para Edita querida R" ("to \[my\] dear Edith\[,\] R\[onald\]"). After Tolkien's death, his eldest son Father [John Tolkien](/wiki/John_Tolkien) gave the book to Father Antonio Quevedo in September 1973.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-5">[5]</a></sup>

## From the back {#From_the_back}

Las páginas de este libro tienen la magia y el particular sabor de las antiguas sagas escandinavas. Hermanado con las obras de Swift, Bunyan y Lewis Caroll ... *El hobito* señala la plenitud creadora de J.R.R. Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-UAN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UAN-1">[1]</a></sup>

\[Translation: "The pages of this book contain the magic and the peculiar flavour of the ancient Norse sagas. Related to the works of Swift, Bunyan, and Lewis Carroll ... *El hobito* shows the full creativity of J.R.R. Tolkien."\]
