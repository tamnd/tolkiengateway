<i><b>ethir</b></i> is the form of two unrelated [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) words (homophones) with different meanings:

## Ethir.1 {#Ethir.1}

"mouth of a river, estuary"

### Etymology {#Etymology}

It is derived from [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Etsiri&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Etsiri (page does not exist)">etsiri</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

It can be analyzed as <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ed&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ed (page does not exist)">ed</a></i> + *[sîr](../S/Sîr.md)* (*s* reduced to *h* because of [lenition](../L/Lenition.md)).

### Pronunciation {#Pronunciation}

There is some controversy on the pronunciation of the word regarding the digraph *th*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

One view says that it can be viewed as a compound: *ed/et* + *sîr* would trigger a lenition *ed/et* + *hîr*. If preposition and stem are understood as different entities, t/h should be pronounced separately (<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈethir]</span>) (like English "a*t-h*ome").

Another view would be that PQ *etsiri* evolved as [Old Sindarin](../O/Old_Sindarin.md) \**ett<sup>h</sup>ire*<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> before simplified as *ethir*. In that case the PQ cluster *ts* was directly derived as *th* in Sindarin. The word would be pronounced as <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈeθir]</span>.

## Ethir.2 {#Ethir.2}

"spy"

### Etymology {#Etymology_2}

Derived from <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ed&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ed (page does not exist)">ed</a></i> + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tirn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tirn (page does not exist)">tirn</a></i>. Examples include *[Amon Ethir](../A/Amon_Ethir.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
