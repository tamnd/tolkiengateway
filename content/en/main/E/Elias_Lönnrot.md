**Elias Lönnrot** ([9 April](/wiki/9_April) 1802 – [19 March](/wiki/19_March) [1884](/wiki/1884)) was a Finnish philologist and collector of traditional Finnish oral poetry. He is best known for composing the *[Kalevala](/wiki/Kalevala)*, the Finnish national epic compiled from national folklore, which had a great impact on [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In a 2007 article,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [Jason Fisher](/wiki/Jason_Fisher) makes a comparison between [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) and Lönnrot, from the aspect that both *"smoothed out complex, irregular source material into a coherent text"*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <span></span>
