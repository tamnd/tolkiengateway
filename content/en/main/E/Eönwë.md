**Eönwë** was the banner-bearer and herald of [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB), and chief of the [Maiar](/wiki/Maiar) along with [Ilmarë](/wiki/Ilmar%C3%AB).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg/200px-Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg/300px-Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/c0/Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg/400px-Mysilvergreen_-_Eonwe.jpg 2x" data-file-width="900" data-file-height="900"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Eonwe&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Mysilvergreen" title="Category:Images by Mysilvergreen">Mysilvergreen</a></figcaption></figure>

When [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) reached the shores of [Aman](/wiki/Aman), it was Eönwë who first greeted him and summoned him to come before the Powers of [Arda](/wiki/Arda).

When [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) decided to heed the appeal, he gave [his sword](/wiki/Sword_of_Manw%C3%AB "Sword of Manwë") to Eönwë<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> and sent him to Middle-earth to fight the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath), leading the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar).<sup id="cite_ref-Earendil_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earendil-4">[3]</a></sup> It is said that it was Eönwë who overthrew Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-PM_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-5">[4]</a></sup>

When [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) was defeated Eönwë played a key role in the aftermath. He took the two remaining [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils) and held them for safekeeping, but when the two remaining [Sons of Fëanor](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor) stole them and fled, Eönwë did not allow them to be slain.<sup id="cite_ref-Earendil_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earendil-4">[3]</a></sup>

Afterwards, [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) paid obeisance to Eönwë and abjured all of his evil deeds. But because Eönwë had not the power to pardon Sauron, he commanded him to return to Aman to receive Manwë's judgement. Unwilling to receive humiliation and sentencing, when Eönwë left Sauron hid in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and fell back into evil.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

At the dawn of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), Eönwë came among the three faithful houses of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) and taught them many things; and they were blessed with wisdom and power and longer [life-spans](/wiki/Mortality "Mortality").<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-7">[6]</a></sup>

These Edain eventually became the [High Men](/wiki/High_Men) of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-7">[6]</a></sup>

The name *Eönwë* is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) in form.<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-8">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> The meaning of the name is not glossed. The ancient [Loremasters](/wiki/Loremasters) knew no [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) etymology for the name, suggesting that it is probably of [Valarin](/wiki/Valarin) origin.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

The final element is the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) suffix *[-wë](/wiki/-w%C3%AB)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-8">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earlier conceptions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Eönwë was envisioned as the son of Manwë, but as the concept of the [Valarindi](/wiki/Valarindi) (i.e. the Children of the Valar) was abandoned, he was turned into Manwë's herald instead.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>

In some versions Eönwë was the one who would kill Morgoth for his love for [Arien](/wiki/Arien) (previously named *Urwendi*), instead of [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

In *[The Earliest 'Silmarillion'](/wiki/The_Earliest_%27Silmarillion%27)*, he was the son of [Tulcas](/wiki/Tulcas).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§17</sup>

In both early and later versions of the *[Akallabêth](/wiki/Akallab%C3%AAth)* it is revealed that it was Fionwë/Eönwë who overthrew Morgoth at the end of the War of Wrath, but [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) removed this reference in the published *[Silmarillion](/wiki/Silmarillion)*, believing that his father intended to diminish his role; however, Christopher Tolkien later felt the omission may have been an error on his part.<sup id="cite_ref-PM_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-5">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:143</sup>

### Earlier names {#Earlier_names}

In earlier works, such as *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, his name was *Fionwë* which translates to "Son", from [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Fion&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Fion (page does not exist)">fion</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-LT_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT-17">[16]</a></sup> This possibly alludes to his previous conception as the son of Manwë.

His other early name, *Urion*, means "He of the [Sun](/wiki/Sun)", from *[ur](/wiki/Ur "Ur")* ("the Sun"), *uru* ("fire") or *urin* ("blazing"),<sup id="cite_ref-LT_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT-17">[16]</a></sup> + the masculine suffix *[-ion](/wiki/-ion)*.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup>
