**Eöl**, known as the **Dark Elf**, was the great smith who dwelled within [Nan Elmoth](/wiki/Nan_Elmoth). He was akin to [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) of the [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 2]</a></sup>

His face was noble yet grim, "and his eyes could see deep into shadows and dark places" because he was night-sighted<sup id="cite_ref-journeys_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journeys-3">[2]</a></sup>. Eöl built up a friendship with the [Dwarves of the Blue Mountains](/wiki/Dwarves_of_the_Blue_Mountains). That friendship gave him an insight into their craft, and he came to forge the swords [Anguirel](/wiki/Anguirel) and [Anglachel](/wiki/Anglachel), the latter of which became [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin)'s sword [Gurthang](/wiki/Gurthang).

In [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [316](/wiki/First_Age_316 "First Age 316"), [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon)'s sister, [Aredhel](/wiki/Aredhel), strayed into his kingdom whom he took for his wife. Later, in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [400](/wiki/First_Age_400 "First Age 400"), Aredhel and their son [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin) fled Nan Elmoth for [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin) but were pursued by Eöl. In Gondolin, he tried to kill Maeglin with a poisoned javelin, but instead killed Aredhel; for this crime he was executed by being thrown from the cliff of [Caragdûr](/wiki/Caragd%C3%BBr). The darkness in Eöl's heart was inherited by his son Maeglin and sowed the seeds for the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin).

## History {#History}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Eöl was of the kin of King [Elu Thingol](/wiki/Thingol "Thingol").<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WJ|Editor_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ|Editor-4">[3]</a></sup> He was "restless and ill at ease in [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath)",<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> and when the [Girdle of Melian](/wiki/Girdle_of_Melian) was raised around the kingdom, he left his dwelling in the [Forest of Region](/wiki/Forest_of_Region) to dwell in the dark forest of [Nan Elmoth](/wiki/Nan_Elmoth), east of Doriath, where he had a smithy, dim halls, and servants similar to himself.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> Eöl's house was located in the middle of Nan Elmoth around fifteen miles from the northernmost edge where the [Celon](/wiki/Celon) River ran.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup> He had a preference for the land as it had been before the Sun, and "he loved the night and twilight under the stars". He had little love for the Noldor whom he blamed for the return of Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

He was unique among the Elves of old in that he had befriended the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves). Their travels into [East Beleriand](/wiki/East_Beleriand) followed two roads, and the northern way would bring them close to Nan Elmoth. There he would meet with them. Eöl was interested in learning from them, and he shared a rare friendship with the [Dwarves of the Blue Mountains](/wiki/Dwarves_of_the_Blue_Mountains). As a guest in the many mansions of [Nogrod](/wiki/Nogrod) and [Belegost](/wiki/Belegost), he developed his great skill in metalwork while they learned "much of what passed in the lands of the Eldar" from him.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> From this great skill, he devised [Galvorn](/wiki/Galvorn), a jet-black metal that was "as hard as the steel of the Dwarves", yet extremely malleable to the point that it was thin and supple. It was from this metal that he forged armor of his own making which he wore whenever he went abroad from Nan Elmoth,<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> "and so escaped many wounds"<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup> as the armor proved to be resistant to all darts and blades.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

Though Eöl was a skilled craftsman and a master sword-smith,<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-7">[5]</a></sup> among his greatest works were the two swords made from the iron of a meteorite, [Anglachel](/wiki/Anglachel) and [Anguirel](/wiki/Anguirel). Anglachel he gave to Thingol as a begrudged payment for dwelling in Nan Elmoth,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup> and it would later become the sword borne by [Beleg](/wiki/Beleg), and after him, [Túrin Turambar](/wiki/T%C3%BArin "Túrin").

### Of Eöl, Aredhel and their son {#Of_Eöl,_Aredhel_and_their_son}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d4/Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg/295px-Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="295" height="207" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d4/Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg/443px-Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d4/Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg/590px-Elena_Kukanova_-_Nan-Elmoth.jpg 2x" data-file-width="760" data-file-height="532"/></a><figcaption><i>Nan Elmoth</i> by <a href="/wiki/Elena_Kukanova" title="Elena Kukanova">Elena Kukanova</a></figcaption></figure>

During the year [316](/wiki/First_Age_316 "First Age 316") of the First Age, Eöl saw [Aredhel](/wiki/Aredhel), the sister of [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) the King of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), near the dim borders of Nan Elmoth. She was very fair and he desired her. Aredhel had become separated from her companions, and Eöl used his enchantments to draw her deeper into the woods and unable to find a way out, until she came, weary with wandering, to his home. He showed himself and welcomed her. She entered his home willingly and stayed. It was only then that he showed himself, to lead her into his home, taking her to wife, though Aredhel was not wholly unwilling. While Eöl forbade her to seek her [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) kin and commanded that she shunned sunlight, "they wandered far together under the stars or by the light of the sickle moon", and he allowed her to even wander alone within the forest.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

During the year [320](/wiki/First_Age_320 "First Age 320") of the First Age, Aredhel bore him a son whom in her heart she named *Lómion* in the [forbidden tongue](/wiki/Quenya "Quenya") of the Noldor. Twelve years later, Eöl gave to his son the name of [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin), perceiving that his son's eyes were more piercing than his own;<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> until that time, he only referred to Maeglin as "Iôn" ("son").<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup>

When Maeglin reached adulthood, Eöl often took him along whenever he visited "the cities of the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) in the east of [Ered Lindon](/wiki/Blue_Mountains "Blue Mountains")". Yet, unknown to Eöl, he loved his mother better as she told him tales of her kin, yet omitting how to get to them. When Maeglin told Eöl that he desired to see his mother's kin with his own eyes and speak to the [sons](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor "Sons of Fëanor") of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor), Eöl forbade his request and became wrathful at the mere mention of the kinslayers, drilling into Maeglin that he was of the "house of Eöl" and threatening to set him in bonds should he disobey him. Though Maeglin obeyed the commands, the damage to their relationship was done, for now Eöl mistrusted him enough so much so that he no longer took him with him beyond Ered Lindon.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

### The escape of Aredhel and Maeglin {#The_escape_of_Aredhel_and_Maeglin}

During the summer of the year [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [400](/wiki/First_Age_400 "First Age 400"), the Dwarves, as per their custom, invited Eöl to a midsummer <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Feast&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Feast (page does not exist)">Feast</a><sup id="cite_ref-journeys_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journeys-3">[2]</a></sup> at [Nogrod](/wiki/Nogrod), and he rode straight away to [Ered Lindon](/wiki/Blue_Mountains "Blue Mountains") in order to be on time to attend.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> However, he suspected that in his absence, his wife and son may seek to visit the sons of Fëanor. As such, "he secretly ordered his servants to keep a close watch on his wife and son" and he posted a watchman by the stream of [Celon](/wiki/Celon) at the north-eaves of Nan Elmoth.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/8/8d/Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg/300px-Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="247" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/8/8d/Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg/450px-Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/8/8d/Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg/600px-Catherine_Chmiel_-_Tales_of_Gondolin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="750" data-file-height="617"/></a><figcaption><i>Tales of Gondolin</i> by <a href="/wiki/Catherine_Karina_Chmiel" title="Catherine Karina Chmiel">Catherine Chmiel</a></figcaption></figure>

During the first night of the three day feast, "a dark shadow of ill foreboding" came to Eöl in his sleep. As a consequence, he left Nogrod the next morning without ceremony, riding homeward with all speed. He returned home at nightfall of the next day, learning from his watchman that his wife and son had fled north less than two days earlier, passing into the [Himlad](/wiki/Himlad) towards the [Pass of Aglon](/wiki/Pass_of_Aglon). Staying at his home only to mount a fresh horse, his swiftest steed, his wrath overcame him and he chased after them immediately.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup>

Eöl regained control of his wrath as he entered into the Himlad, remembering the danger of [Celegorm](/wiki/Celegorm) and [Curufin](/wiki/Curufin).<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> However, he was waylaid by the well-armed riders of Curufin before making it even halfway across the Himlad. The riders took Eöl by force with them to see their lord Curufin. It was around noon that day when the riders arrived at Curufin's camp where, with only a little courtesy, Eöl was greeted by Curufin.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup> When the son of Fëanor questioned him, Eöl told him that Aredhel and Maeglin left Nan Elmoth to visit him and that he wished to join them in that errand. While Curufin told Eöl that they would have been welcomed coldly if he did accompany them, Curufin revealed that they never intended to visit him, and that they were seen passing the [Arossiach](/wiki/Arossiach) riding swiftly westward almost two days before. Curufin suggested then that either Eöl was trying to deceive him or that he himself had been deceived.

After asking Curufin for his leave to depart the Himlad and to seek for the truth. Eöl thanked him, regarding him as kin by marriage. Curufin took offense to this since Aredhel's marriage was forced and he wished to have Eöl out of his sight ad he could not slay him according to the laws of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar).<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup> Yet despite this anger, Curufin instead counseled Eöl to return home, foreseeing that if Eöl continued his pursuit after those who do not love him anymore, he would never return to Nan Elmoth again. Eöl did not heed him as the hatred of all the Noldor drove him on pass the [Arossiach](/wiki/Arossiach), and he perceived then that his wife and son were fleeing to [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin),<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> realizing that Curufin's purpose was to specifically delay him.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup>

Upon reaching the [Ford of Brithiach](/wiki/Ford_of_Brithiach), Eöl spotted his wife and son due to the betrayal of their horses. Now seeing Aredhel Eöl followed them closely, marking their paths. Eventually, Eöl found his way to the [Dry River](/wiki/Dry_River), and the secret way led him by stealth to the [Orfalch Echor](/wiki/Orfalch_Echor) and the [Seven Gates of Gondolin](/wiki/Seven_Gates_of_Gondolin). There, Eöl revealed himself to the [Dark Guard](/wiki/Dark_Guard) at the [Dark Gate](/wiki/Gate_of_Wood "Gate of Wood") and claimed Aredhel as his wife, demanding to be brought before the [King](/wiki/Turgon "Turgon").<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

<h3><span id="Gondolin_and_E.C3.B6l.27s_death"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Gondolin_and_Eöl&#39;s_death">Gondolin and Eöl&#39;s death</span></h3>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/2/2c/Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg/290px-Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="290" height="452" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/2/2c/Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg/435px-Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/2/2c/Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg/580px-Ted_Nasmith_-_E%C3%B6l_is_Led_to_the_Walls.jpg 2x" data-file-width="616" data-file-height="960"/></a><figcaption><i>Eöl is led to the Walls</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

At Aredhel's request, [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) at first welcomed Eöl as a kinsman, but under the King's law, one who had found the way to the [Hidden City](/wiki/Gondolin "Gondolin") was not permitted to leave. Eöl refused to acknowledge the law or the right of the Noldor to "seize realms or to set bounds" and claimed the land as Teleri. He blamed the Noldor for bringing war to a peaceful land. He then stated that he was only in Gondolin to claim his son, though he did not claim his wife since Aredhel was just as much Turgon's sister as she was Eöl's wife.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> However, it is possible that because he mentioned he referred to Gondolin as a cage that Aredhel sickened in, that he was hoping that Aredhel may leave Gondolin again a second time eventually.

Turgon pointed out that the borders of Eöl's own "sunless woods", Nan Elmoth, were defended by Noldor swords, and if it were not for their presence, he would be a thrall in the pits of Angband. Eöl was left with one choice only: abide in Gondolin or die in Gondolin. The same choice was left for his son.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

Enraged at the humiliation and the loss of his freedom, Eöl chose death, for himself and for his son, and after staring down Turgon, he cast a javelin, which he had hidden beneath his cloak, at Maeglin. Aredhel stepped in front of her son, and she was struck in the shoulder. Eöl was taken by many, cast in bonds, and led away. The wound was treated and seemed minor, and Aredhel and Idril moved Turgon to mercy. Yet no one knew the point of the javelin had been poisoned until it was too late, and after Aredhel's wound sickened in the evening, she died that night.

Thus when Eöl came before Turgon he found no mercy, and was cast over the cliff of [Caragdûr](/wiki/Caragd%C3%BBr) to his death. Before he was hurled over, he accused his son of forsaking his father and his kin, cursing him to fail at all his hopes and to share the same death as himself.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup>

The meaning of the name *Eöl* is unknown, and also to which language it pertains. The word is neither [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) nor [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), but "Another name from prim\[itive\] [FG](</wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter)> "The Fall of Gondolin (chapter)") - meaningless then and now" since Tolkien concluded that not every name needed to have significance.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup>

Usually the reference to a 'Dark Elf' (*Moredhel* in Sindarin)<sup id="cite_ref-L297_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-11">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-App_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-12">[9]</a></sup> simply refers to an Elf who has not seen the light of Aman, but the concept of a darkened Elf would be one that may have been corrupted by Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien liked this concept as an explanation for his superb and insidious smith-craft, which was written in a margin note of Eöl's story,<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:320</sup> but he chose instead to consider him more likely acquainted with the Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-7">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 9</sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

In the early versions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") according to the linguistic materials related to *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) considered the name *Eöl* (with variant spellings of *Eol* and *Iol*) to be a [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) name, but without providing any etymology.<sup id="cite_ref-Iol_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iol-13">[10]</a></sup>

The [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) cognate of *Eol* was *Aiwala*,<sup id="cite_ref-Iol_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iol-13">[10]</a></sup> or alternatively *Aiwl*.<sup id="cite_ref-Iol_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iol-13">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Aivala_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aivala-14">[11]</a></sup>

However, in an another text, the Qenya cognate of *Eol* was given as *Aival* instead - but the reading of the second letter in *Aival* was unclear,<sup id="cite_ref-Aivala_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aivala-14">[11]</a></sup> which led [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) to conclude that it was actually <i>A<b>r</b>val</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[12]</a></sup>

In *Ælfwine’s translation of the Quenta into Old English; Old English equivalents of Elvish names*, he is called **Éor**;<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[13]</a></sup> Christopher Tolkien does not provide a translation.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### *The Book of Lost Tales* {#The_Book_of_Lost_Tales}

In the earliest version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, Eöl was said to have been an ill-favored leader of the Minors "of the [Mole-kin](/wiki/House_of_the_Mole "House of the Mole") of the [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes)" who had the favor of [Fingolma](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB "Finwë"). [Isfin](/wiki/Isfin) hated him, though he loved her from afar. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes several parallels with [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[14]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

Tolkien does have other renditions of this character that include references to him as an [Avar](/wiki/Avar) and even as a "darkened Elf".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:62</sup> At one point it is hinted that he could be one of the [Laegil](/wiki/Laegil).<sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:322</sup> In note 9 of Tolkien's essay *[Quendi and Eldar](/wiki/Quendi_and_Eldar)*, another draft of Eöl's story appears, dated as written between 1959-1960. In this version, he is an Avar who had once been of the second clan of Elves, the [Tatyar](/wiki/Tatyar). This was later contradicted by Tolkien in a very late essay, where he is called an Elda. It is unknown if Tolkien changed this or simply forgot, but it is possible to reconcile them by noting that, according to *Quendi and Eldar*, any rare Avar that joins the Sindar becomes an Elda. Eöl's marriage is a little different in this story as well. He found "the sister of King Turgon, astray in the wild near his dwelling, and he took her to wife by force: a very wicked deed in the eyes of the Eldar".<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-7">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:409</sup>

This incident is consistent with his late 1950s essay *[Laws and Customs Among the Eldar](/wiki/Laws_and_Customs_Among_the_Eldar)*, in which a Draft A footnote states that the rejection of bodily life applies strictly to the forced violation of another's spouse.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-19">[16]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 5</sup> Aredhel was unwed, and Tolkien's writing in *Quendi and Eldar* confirms Eöl took her "by force"—a surviving act of rape.<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-7">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:409</sup> *The Silmarillion'*s phrasing that she was "not wholly unwilling"<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> and that her life in Nan Elmoth was not "hateful to her for many years"<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_5-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-5">[4]</a></sup> reflects the narrative tradition ascribed to Loremasters such as [Pengoloð](/wiki/Pengolo%C3%B0), whereas this later note provides an explicit authorial clarification.<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-7">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:409</sup><sup id="cite_ref-Maeglin_1-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maeglin-1">[1]</a></sup>

This essay on the Eldar reflects a Draft A concept suggesting that Elven marriage is so spiritually absolute that a third party could not physically "force" a spouse.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-19">[16]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 5</sup> While this would theoretically mean Maeglin's desire for Idril was a futile pursuit of the impossible, it represents a narrow metaphysical ideal from the [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) framework. This is contrasted by another brief tale circa 1958 in the actual history of Arda Marred, where the [Ainu](/wiki/Ainu) maiden [Árië](/wiki/%C3%81ri%C3%AB) chose to release her spirit from [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4) to escape [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor)'s attempt to ravish her,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:381</sup> and the "very wicked deed" Eöl committed against Aredhel.<sup id="cite_ref-Quendi_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quendi-7">[5]</a></sup>

In a [1959](/wiki/1959) essay, Tolkien explored altering the metaphysics of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium").<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-21">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:76, 81</sup> One of these changes involved changing Eöl from a Telerin Elf to a Noldorin Elf who was born in [Aman](/wiki/Aman).<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-21">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:76, 81</sup> Though he was a Noldo, he was among a select few who were "[Avari](/wiki/Avari) at heart", yet joined the exiles because his people did.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[note 4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Nature_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-21">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 4</sup> Tolkien could not decide if Eöl already knew Isfin before the Noldor went to Aman and persuaded her to remain with him in [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand), or if she also decided not to go to Aman at the last minute and wandered alone in Beleriand.<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_21-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-21">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:76, 81</sup> Tolkien, however, quickly decides that this was impossible since Isfin was born in Aman.<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_21-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-21">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:76, 81</sup> Tolkien also decided in that moment that Maeglin also needed to be born in Aman and that the three of them were extremely attracted to Melkor's lies, growing to dislike their kin and Aman, joining Fëanor's host, and becoming estranged from all of their immediate kin.<sup id="cite_ref-Nature_21-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nature-21">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:76, 81</sup> However, in most developed versions, Eöl remains an Elf of Middle-earth whose "wicked deed" remains a unique transgression in Elven history.
