The **Easterlings** also known as **Swarthy Men**, or **Incomers** by the [People of Hador](../P/People_of_Hador.md), were tribes of [Men](../M/Men.md) that migrated west across the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md), after the [Edain](Edain.md), into [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) in the late [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).

## Origins {#Origins}

The ancestors of the Easterlings were Men that awoke in [Hildórien](../H/Hildórien.md), like the ancestors of the [Edain](Edain.md) some of them eventually left that land and journeyed west.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Their descendants would later settle around [Eriador](Eriador.md) and further East of it.<sup id="cite_ref-GA173_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA173-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [463](../F/First_Age_463.md) they made their way into [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) after the [Dagor Bragollach](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md) passing north about the [Ered Luin](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) and into [Lothlann](../L/Lothlann.md),<sup id="cite_ref-GA173_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA173-4">[4]</a></sup> having heard the rumour of its land and riches while others were instigated by the [Enemy](../M/Morgoth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-s18_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-s18-5">[5]</a></sup> Some folk remained in Eriador.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

There were two chieftains that were the greatest amongst the houses, [Bór](../B/Bór.md) and [Ulfang](../U/Ulfang.md), and they entered the service of the [House of Fëanor](../H/House_of_Fëanor.md) along with their folk and were given lands north and south of the [March](../M/March_of_Maedhros.md) to dwell in.<sup id="cite_ref-GA174_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA174-7">[7]</a></sup> However Ulfang and his sons were secretly in league with Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-s18_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-s18-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [472](../F/First_Age_472.md) the Easterlings were part of the [Union of Maedhros](../U/Union_of_Maedhros.md) and participated in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](../N/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad.md). In the battle the House of Ulfang betrayed the sons of Fëanor and attacked their former comrades, though the House of Bór remained faithful to the Fëanorians and fought back. Unfortunately for the Union the treachery of the House of Ulfang resulted to their utter defeat.<sup id="cite_ref-s18_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-s18-5">[5]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/L/Liz_Danforth_-_Ulfang.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/d/df/Liz_Danforth_-_Ulfang.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="289" height="405" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="289" data-file-height="405"/></a><figcaption><i>Ulfang</i> by Liz Danforth</figcaption></figure>

After the battle Morgoth sent the Easterlings that served him into Hithlum and shut them in, denying them the fertile lands of Beleriand. In Hithlum they enslaved the remaining people of Hador and their women were forced into marriage<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> such as [Aerin](../A/Aerin.md) who was wife to [Brodda](../B/Brodda.md) an Easterling chief.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [488](../F/First_Age_488.md)<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> the Easterlings and Orcs attacked a group of Elf refugees escaping Hithlum and captured an adolescent [Tuor](../T/Tuor.md), [Lorgan](../L/Lorgan.md) an Easterling chieftain made him his slave until he escaped three years later.<sup id="cite_ref-UT1_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT1-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [495](../F/First_Age_495.md)<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> Brodda was slain by [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md) who returned to [Dor-lómin](../D/Dor-lómin.md) looking for his [mother](../M/Morwen.md) and [sister](../N/Nienor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

The Easterlings fought for Morgoth in the [War of Wrath](../W/War_of_Wrath.md) but were defeated by the [Host of the Valar](../H/Host_of_the_Valar.md). After the war, those that survived fled back over the Ered Luin to [Eriador](Eriador.md) and beyond.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[note 1]</a></sup> Some of them became kings of the primitive [Men](../M/Middle_Men.md) and they fell under their shadow, for this these Men were neglected by the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> Some Easterlings related to the faithful folk of Bór still remained in Eriador, and from those people came the most ancient Men that dwelt in the north in the later [Ages](../A/Ages.md).<sup id="cite_ref-GA174_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA174-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Traits and culture {#Traits_and_culture}

The Easterlings are described as short and broad, with swart or sallow skin and having dark eyes and hair. Some had greater liking for the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) of the mountains than for the [Elves](Elves.md).<sup id="cite_ref-s18_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-s18-5">[5]</a></sup> Some of their women were proud and barbaric.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>

There were many houses of the Easterlings and they were not always friendly to each other or to outsiders,<sup id="cite_ref-GA173_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA173-4">[4]</a></sup> there were two chieftains that had 'the greatest followings and authority' Bór and Ulfang which suggest that they ruled other lesser houses of the Easterlings.<sup id="cite_ref-GA174_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA174-7">[7]</a></sup>

The folk of Bór were described as 'worthy folk and tillers of the earth' which indicate that they were early farmers.<sup id="cite_ref-GA174_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA174-7">[7]</a></sup>

It is seen that they were used to alliterate the names of father and sons/brothers, a custom also seen among the [House of Beor](../H/House_of_Beor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[note 2]</a></sup>
