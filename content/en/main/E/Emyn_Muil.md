The **Emyn Muil** was a range of hills around the lake [Nen Hithoel](/wiki/Nen_Hithoel) north of the falls of [Rauros](/wiki/Rauros).<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup>

The Emyn Muil were located south of the [Brown Lands](/wiki/Brown_Lands) and north of the [Nindalf](/wiki/Nindalf). The river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) flowed through the hills and then pooled in [Nen Hithoel](/wiki/Nen_Hithoel).<sup id="cite_ref-Map_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-2">[2]</a></sup> The [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) rode boats through Emyn Muil<sup id="cite_ref-River_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-River-3">[3]</a></sup> before the group broke up near the southern boundary of the hills.<sup id="cite_ref-Break_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Break-4">[4]</a></sup> Afterwards [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") hiked through the difficult terrain east of the river and met [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) just below the eastern edge of the Emyn Muil.<sup id="cite_ref-Taming_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Taming-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

The hills rose gradually in the north with great brakes thorn and sloe, brambles and creepers. Near the river ivy clung to grey stone chimneys and further ridges were topped with firs twisted by wind.<sup id="cite_ref-River_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-River-3">[3]</a></sup> On three sides Emyn Muil ended in cliffs: In the west the uplands ended abruptly in the [East Wall of Rohan](/wiki/East_Wall_of_Rohan).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> In the south the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) cascaded over [Rauros](/wiki/Rauros), a great waterfall.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In the east, although of lower elevation, the barren hills still ended in a steep edge.<sup id="cite_ref-Taming_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Taming-5">[5]</a></sup>

The [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) cut through the hills on its southward course. In the north the river narrowed and passed through [Sarn Gebir](/wiki/Sarn_Gebir). No boats could survive these rapids and travelers had to use a portage way on the west bank. South of Sarn Gebir the river plunged between towering cliffs until it passed between the two great statues of the [Argonath](/wiki/Argonath). A little further and the pent waters opened into a long oval lake called [Nen Hithoel](/wiki/Nen_Hithoel). On the southern end of the lake were three peaks: [Amon Lhaw](/wiki/Amon_Lhaw) on the east bank, [Tol Brandir](/wiki/Tol_Brandir) amid two arms of the river, and [Amon Hen](/wiki/Amon_Hen) on the western side. At the foot of Amon Hen was a green clearing, [Parth Galen](/wiki/Parth_Galen), and south of it was the [North Stair](/wiki/North_Stair) that allowed for passage to the bottom of Rauros and Emyn Muil.<sup id="cite_ref-River_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-River-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After Prince [Minalcar](/wiki/R%C3%B3mendacil_II "Rómendacil II") of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) had defeated a large army of [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1248](/wiki/Third_Age_1248 "Third Age 1248") (and took the name Rómendacil) he fortified the west bank of the Anduin and built the pillars of the Argonath in the Emyn Muil.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [2510](/wiki/Third_Age_2510 "Third Age 2510"), the Steward [Cirion](/wiki/Cirion) bequeathed the land of [Calenardhon](/wiki/Calenardhon) to [Eorl the Young](/wiki/Eorl "Eorl"). After pledging the [Oath of Eorl](/wiki/Oath_of_Eorl) the bounds of the new realm of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) were determined; in the east these were the Anduin and the [west-cliff](/wiki/East_Wall_of_Rohan "East Wall of Rohan") of the Emyn Muil.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

In [3002](/wiki/Third_Age_3002 "Third Age 3002") [Orc](/wiki/Orc) raiders battled with some [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim), and [Éomund](/wiki/%C3%89omund) was slain there.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) captured [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) on [1 February](/wiki/1_February),<sup id="cite_ref-Hunt_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hunt-11">[11]</a></sup> [3017](/wiki/Third_Age_3017 "Third Age 3017") in the [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> To avoid detection by the spies of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), Aragorn drove his captive through the northern Emyn Muil before crossing the Anduin.<sup id="cite_ref-Hunt_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hunt-11">[11]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019") the Fellowship of the Ring encountered the Emyn Muil. The following list of dates show what events took place in the hilly region:<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

- [23 February](/wiki/23_February): The Fellowship was attacked near Sarn Gebir
- [25 February](/wiki/25_February): They passed the Argonath and camped at Parth Galen.
- [26 February](/wiki/26_February): The [Fellowship broke](/wiki/Breaking_of_the_Fellowship "Breaking of the Fellowship") and [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir) was slain. Frodo and Sam struck east across the Emyn Muil while Aragorn, [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli), and [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas) pursued the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) carrying [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") west through the hills.
- [27 February](/wiki/27_February): Aragorn and his companions left the Emyn Muil at dawn, entering the [Eastemnet](/wiki/Eastemnet).
- [29 February](/wiki/29_February): Frodo and Sam descended from the Emyn Muil and captured Gollum.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Emyn Muil* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-14">[14]</a></sup> meaning "drear hills".<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that it is a compound of *emyn*, the plural form of *[amon](/wiki/Amon "Amon")* ("hill") and *muil* ("dreariness" or "drear").<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-14">[14]</a></sup>
