## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Though not often recognized as environmental or agrarian literature, the writings of <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien" title="J. R. R. Tolkien">J. R. R. Tolkien</a> demonstrate a complex and comprehensive ecological philosophy. The ecology of <a href="/wiki/Middle-earth" title="Middle-earth">Middle-earth</a> portrayed in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></i>, and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Silmarillion" title="The Silmarillion">The Silmarillion</a></i> brings together three potent and convincing elements of preservation and conservation--sustainable agriculture and agrarianism, horticulture independent of utilitarianism, and protection of unspoiled wilderness. Throughout his work, Tolkien reveals his vision of the natural world and environmental responsibility.
</p><p><i>Ents, Elves, and Eriador</i> examines the underlying environmental philosophy in Tolkien&#39;s major works as well as his lesser-known stories and essays. Matthew Dickerson and Jonathan Evans evaluate Tolkien&#39;s writing, especially his Middle-earth <a href="/wiki/Legendarium" title="Legendarium">legendarium</a>, in the context of modern environmental literature. The authors compare Tolkien&#39;s work with that of some of the most important environmental scholars and nature writers of the past century, including Wendell Berry, John Elder, Aldo Leopold, and Scott Sanders, highlighting Tolkien&#39;s intellectual depth.
</p><p>A vital contribution to environmental literature and an important addition to Tolkien scholarship, <i>Ents, Elves, and Eriador</i> offers all fans of Tolkien a new way to understand his writings. 
</p><p><br/>
</p>
</blockquote>
