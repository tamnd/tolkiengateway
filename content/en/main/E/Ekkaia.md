**Ekkaia** (also called the **Encircling Sea**, the **Outer Sea**, or the **Outer Ocean**) was the mighty sea that surrounded all the lands of [Arda](/wiki/Arda), [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and [Aman](/wiki/Aman) alike. [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo)'s halls were within the Outer Sea.

At its distant unknown edge stood the [Walls of the Night](/wiki/Walls_of_the_Night).<sup id="cite_ref-days_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-days-1">[1]</a></sup> Between its edge and the shores of the [world](/wiki/Ambar "Ambar") was also a chasm,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MR|P2r_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR|P2r-3">[3]</a></sup> into which [Tilion](/wiki/Tilion) plunged and carried the [Moon](/wiki/Moon) beneath the roots of [Arda](/wiki/Arda).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Its dark waters lapped the western shores of [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor),<sup id="cite_ref-days_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-days-1">[1]</a></sup> and during their time in Valinor [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) and [his sons](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor "Sons of Fëanor") visited the shores of these waters.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the North, the [Helcaraxë](/wiki/Helcarax%C3%AB) was the narrow strait between the Outer Sea and [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer), where in the chilled waters clashed hills of ice amidst vast fogs of deathly cold.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Ekkaia*, commonly understood to be [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya),<sup id="cite_ref-Helge_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helge-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-quick_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quick-8">[8]</a></sup> was not explained by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien").

[Helge Fauskanger](/wiki/Helge_Fauskanger) has tried to explain *Ekkaia* by deriving it from the unattested form *et-gaya* ("out-sea"; cf. *[et](/wiki/Et "Et")* "out").<sup id="cite_ref-Helge_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helge-7">[7]</a></sup>

Robert Ireland links *Ekkaia* to the [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") KHAYA ("far, distant"), which appears to include among its derivatives an adverb similar in form: *ekkaira* ("far off, far away").<sup id="cite_ref-quick_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quick-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Ekkaia appears first time in [The Later Quenta Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion), replacing the earlier concept of [Vaiya](/wiki/Vaiya). In earlier works such as the *[Ambarkanta](/wiki/Ambarkanta)*, Vaiya is not a sea, but an exotic material that encloses the flat [Ambar](/wiki/Ambar), becoming air above it, and water below it. In the published *[Silmarillion](/wiki/Silmarillion)* Ekkaia is clearly mentioned only to be a sea. However in the cosmological models of *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth)* (and fan-made maps), Ekkaia is conflated with the earlier concept of Vaiya, shown as an atmospheric layer that encloses the world.

The published *Silmarillion* mentions an unnamed chasm beyond Ekkaia into which the Moon plunges. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) later wrote that this reference comes from a note from his father,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> but that he actually settled with the Chasm to lie *before* the Outer Sea, rather than beyond it.<sup id="cite_ref-MR|P2r_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR|P2r-3">[3]</a></sup> This seems to be derived from the earlier concept of the [Chasm of Ilmen](/wiki/Chasm_of_Ilmen) that lies between Ambar and Vaia, but it is hard to understand it with the concept of Ekkaia as a sea.
