**Ekkaia** (also called the **Encircling Sea**, the **Outer Sea**, or the **Outer Ocean**) was the mighty sea that surrounded all the lands of [Arda](../A/Arda.md), [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) and [Aman](../A/Aman.md) alike. [Ulmo](../U/Ulmo.md)'s halls were within the Outer Sea.

At its distant unknown edge stood the [Walls of the Night](../W/Walls_of_the_Night.md).<sup id="cite_ref-days_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-days-1">[1]</a></sup> Between its edge and the shores of the [world](../A/Ambar.md) was also a chasm,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MR|P2r_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR|P2r-3">[3]</a></sup> into which [Tilion](../T/Tilion.md) plunged and carried the [Moon](../M/Moon.md) beneath the roots of [Arda](../A/Arda.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Its dark waters lapped the western shores of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md),<sup id="cite_ref-days_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-days-1">[1]</a></sup> and during their time in Valinor [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) and [his sons](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md) visited the shores of these waters.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the North, the [Helcaraxë](../H/Helcaraxë.md) was the narrow strait between the Outer Sea and [Belegaer](../B/Belegaer.md), where in the chilled waters clashed hills of ice amidst vast fogs of deathly cold.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Ekkaia*, commonly understood to be [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md),<sup id="cite_ref-Helge_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helge-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-quick_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quick-8">[8]</a></sup> was not explained by [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).

[Helge Fauskanger](../H/Helge_Fauskanger.md) has tried to explain *Ekkaia* by deriving it from the unattested form *et-gaya* ("out-sea"; cf. *[et](Et.md)* "out").<sup id="cite_ref-Helge_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helge-7">[7]</a></sup>

Robert Ireland links *Ekkaia* to the [root](../S/Sundocarme.md) KHAYA ("far, distant"), which appears to include among its derivatives an adverb similar in form: *ekkaira* ("far off, far away").<sup id="cite_ref-quick_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quick-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Ekkaia appears first time in [The Later Quenta Silmarillion](../T/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion.md), replacing the earlier concept of [Vaiya](../V/Vaiya.md). In earlier works such as the *[Ambarkanta](../A/Ambarkanta.md)*, Vaiya is not a sea, but an exotic material that encloses the flat [Ambar](../A/Ambar.md), becoming air above it, and water below it. In the published *[Silmarillion](../S/Silmarillion.md)* Ekkaia is clearly mentioned only to be a sea. However in the cosmological models of *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](../T/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth.md)* (and fan-made maps), Ekkaia is conflated with the earlier concept of Vaiya, shown as an atmospheric layer that encloses the world.

The published *Silmarillion* mentions an unnamed chasm beyond Ekkaia into which the Moon plunges. [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) later wrote that this reference comes from a note from his father,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> but that he actually settled with the Chasm to lie *before* the Outer Sea, rather than beyond it.<sup id="cite_ref-MR|P2r_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR|P2r-3">[3]</a></sup> This seems to be derived from the earlier concept of the [Chasm of Ilmen](../C/Chasm_of_Ilmen.md) that lies between Ambar and Vaia, but it is hard to understand it with the concept of Ekkaia as a sea.
