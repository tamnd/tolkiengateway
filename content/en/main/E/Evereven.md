**Evereven** or **Ever-eve** was a poetic name for the region of [Aman](../A/Aman.md) from which [Eärendil](Eärendil.md) set out on his epic journeys beyond the World. It appears only in [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md)'s *[Eärendillinwë](Eärendillinwë.md)* at [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> and so the term may have been meant as Bilbo's own invention. In the poem, it's described as being a land of hills and fountains.

[Galadriel's Song](../G/Galadriel's_Song.md) mentions a similar term: *Beneath the stars of Ever-eve in [Eldamar](Eldamar.md) it shone.*

The name seems to be a reference to the land of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> though its precise identity is difficult to pin down.

## Meaning {#Meaning}

In the original conception, Eärendil undertook an epic journey carrying him on a cycle from Evereven through [Evernight](Evernight.md), <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Evernoon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Evernoon (page does not exist)">Evernoon</a> and <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Evermorn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Evermorn (page does not exist)">Evermorn</a>. Much of this was lost from the final form of the song, so that while the names Evereven and Evernight both survived into [The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md), their context and meaning have become difficult to identify.
