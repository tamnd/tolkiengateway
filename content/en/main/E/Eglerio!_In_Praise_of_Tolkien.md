<i><b>Eglerio! In Praise of Tolkien</b></i>, written and edited by Anne Etkin, is a collection of articles about [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).

The book includes a photograph of Tolkien with Willian Scott's wife with the article "Tolkien — Hobbit and Wizard" by Nan C. Scott. Also included are some quotes attributed to Tolkien by Mrs Scott.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Contents {#Contents}

- Preface: Come to Middle-earth
- I. Eglerio! In Praise of Tolkien
    - Anne Etkin
- II. Letters from C.S. Lewis
    - Anne Etkin with Lucy Matthews
    - Letters by C.S. Lewis
- III. Tolkien's *Homecoming*, a Clue into his Thought and Character
    - Anne Etkin
- IV. Professor J.R.R. Tolkien: Creator of Hobbits and Inventor of a New Myhtology
    - [C.S. Lewis](../C/C.S._Lewis.md)
- V. J.R.R. Tolkien: A Chronological Biography
    - Ellen Lodwick and Anne Etkin
- VI. Tolkien and his readers
    - Anne Etkin with others
- VII. Tolkien — Hobbit and Wizard
    - Nan C.L. Scott
- VIII. Of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*
    - Anne Etkin
- IX. Middle-earth
    - Anne Etkin
- Acknowledgements

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>This book is a rich collection of exactly those things where Professor Tolkien&#39;s multitude of admirers, likes to find. Featured is a multi-part appreciation of Tolkien and his immortal The Lord of the Rings by one of his most avid and long-time admirers, writer Anne Etkin (known among Shirefolk as &#34;Anne of Briar Ditch&#34;). Adding to the bill are such treasures as Two never-before-published letters about Tolkien by another great literary giant, his close friend C.S. Lewis. C.S. Lewis&#39; famous London Times biographical obituary for Tolkien, written before either author had passed away. Nan C.L. Scott&#39;s recounting and photographs of her England visit with Tolkien. An examination of the now-published The Silmarillion, as analyzed by Anne Etkin. A photo section featuring Professor Tolkien, the homes he lived in, and places and things along the English country-side which readers will recognize from his stories.
</p>
</blockquote>
