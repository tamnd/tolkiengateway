**Electronic Arts** or **EA** for short is an American video games developing, publishing and distributing company, founded in [1982](/wiki/1982). Among its successes are *The Sims*, James Bond and various sports games, published by daughter company EA Sports.

## History {#History}

### Early efforts {#Early_efforts}

Electronic Arts had it first fantasy game with <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard%27s_Tale_(1985)" title="wikipedia:Bard&#39;s Tale (1985)">The Bard&#39;s Tale</a></i> in [1985](/wiki/1985), and was introduced to the world of [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") in [1990](/wiki/1990), when they held the UK distribution of [Interplay](/wiki/Interplay_Productions "Interplay Productions")'s *[The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I](</wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_The_Lord_of_the_Rings,_Vol._I_(1990_video_game)> "J.R.R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I (1990 video game)")*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> In 1994 they published [The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I](</wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_The_Lord_of_the_Rings,_Vol._I_(SNES)> "J.R.R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I (SNES)") for the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System" title="wikipedia:Super Nintendo Entertainment System">SNES</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Movie license {#Movie_license}

Electronic Arts purchased the interactive entertainment rights of [*The Lord of the Rings* (film series)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)") in [2002](/wiki/2002). That was after the video game *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)> "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)")* by [Vivendi](/wiki/Vivendi) came out, leading several people to believe that was an Electronic Arts game too. EA had to compress both the [first](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring") and the [second](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers "The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers") film into one game, which became *[The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers](/wiki/EA%27s_The_Two_Towers "EA's The Two Towers")* in [2002](/wiki/2002). A year later, a game based on the [third](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King "The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King") film was also made, *[The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King_(video_game)> "The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King (video game)")*. When all movie-based games were published, an "alternative [fellowship](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring "Fellowship of the Ring")"- game, *[The Lord of the Rings: The Third Age](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Third_Age)*, was made. Because it did not have rights to the book, it had to fill in the gameplay with movie-like aspects.

### Book license {#Book_license}

In [2005](/wiki/2005), a license to the books could be purchased from [Saul Zaentz](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz), which led the way to *[The Battle for Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth "The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth")*, *[The Battle for Middle-earth II](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II "The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II")* and an expansion, *[The Rise of the Witch-king](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II:_The_Rise_of_the_Witch-king "The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king")*. The popularity of these games made Electronic Arts to extend their license until [2008](/wiki/2008).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> One more title was planned; at first, this would have been *[The Lord of the Rings: The White Council](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_White_Council)*, but that has been cancelled.Its last game which was based on Tolkien's Middle-earth, *[The Lord of the Rings: Conquest](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Conquest)* was published in January [2009](/wiki/2009).

### Recent years {#Recent_years}

In [2022](/wiki/2022), it was announced that Electronic Arts will co-operate with [Middle-earth Enterprises](/wiki/Middle-earth_Enterprises) in order to release a new mobile RPG mobile game named *[The Lord of the Rings: Heroes of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Heroes_of_Middle-earth)* at an unspecified time.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The game was eventually released in [2023](/wiki/2023).

## Peter Jackson {#Peter_Jackson}

Despite borrowing music, character design and character voices from [*The Lord of the Rings* (film series)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)"), Electronic Arts did not leave room for [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson)'s input. This did not sit well with Jackson, who turned to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft" title="wikipedia:Ubisoft">Ubisoft</a> for the film tie-in game of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Kong_(2005_film)" title="wikipedia:King Kong (2005 film)">King Kong</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
