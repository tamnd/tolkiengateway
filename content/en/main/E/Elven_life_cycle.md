Owing to their longevity, the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) had a very different **Life cycle** than [Men](/wiki/Men). Most of the following information strictly refers only to the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar)—but much could probably be applied to the [Avari](/wiki/Avari) as well.

## Early life {#Early_life}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Jenny_Dolfen_-_Feanoreans_-_Family_picture.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/2/22/Jenny_Dolfen_-_Feanoreans_-_Family_picture.jpg/300px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_Feanoreans_-_Family_picture.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="440" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/2/22/Jenny_Dolfen_-_Feanoreans_-_Family_picture.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="587"/></a><figcaption><i>Family picture</i> by <a href="/wiki/Jenny_Dolfen" title="Jenny Dolfen">Jenny Dolfen</a></figcaption></figure>

Elves are born about one year after their begetting.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:212</sup> The day of their begetting is remembered, not the actual birthday itself, because bringing forth children is an act of will, and it required a "greater share and strength of their being, in mind and in body" than takes place "in the making of mortal children."<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:212</sup> By their first year, Elf children can speak, walk, and dance, and their quicker onset of mental maturity makes young Elves seem older than they actually are.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:209–10</sup> Elves' bodies developed slower than those of Men, but their minds developed more swiftly.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:209–10</sup> In their twenties, they might still appear physically seven years old, though the Elf-child would have mature language and skill,<sup id="cite_ref-NotesB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NotesB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:228</sup> whereas Men at the same age are already physically mature.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:210</sup>

Physical puberty is generally complete by their fiftieth year (by age fifty they reach their adult height), but they are not considered full-grown until a hundred years have passed.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:210</sup>

## Sexuality, marriage, and parenthood {#Sexuality,_marriage,_and_parenthood}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/a8/L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg/300px-L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="224" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/a8/L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg/450px-L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/a/a8/L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg/600px-L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a_-_Love_at_First_Sight.jpg 2x" data-file-width="900" data-file-height="673"/></a><figcaption><i>Love at First Sight</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_L%C4%ABga_K%C4%BCavi%C5%86a" title="Category:Images by Līga Kļaviņa">Līga Kļaviņa</a></figcaption></figure>

Elves marry for love, or at least with free will from both parties, typically early in life. Monogamy is practised and adultery is unthinkable.<sup id="cite_ref-NotesB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NotesB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:229</sup> By their very nature, they are "seldom swayed by the desires of the body" or influenced by lust.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:211</sup> They marry only once for it was ruled by Manwë that, "'since the Elves are by nature permanent in life within Arda, so also is their unmarred marriage.'"<sup id="cite_ref-marriage_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marriage-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:225</sup> [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB), the [King of the Noldor](/wiki/King_of_the_Noldor), was an exception. After his [first wife](/wiki/M%C3%ADriel "Míriel") died, from passing the majority of her life into [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor),<sup id="cite_ref-LawsA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LawsA-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:237</sup> and refused to be re-embodied, Finwë was permitted to marry again. This was pronounced by [Námo](/wiki/Mandos "Mandos") as the '[Statute of Finwë and Míriel](/wiki/Statute_of_Finw%C3%AB_and_M%C3%ADriel)'.<sup id="cite_ref-marriage_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marriage-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:226</sup>

Spouses may choose each other, in their youth, and be betrothed long before they are married. The betrothal is subject to parental approval from both houses unless the parties are of age and intend to marry soon. At which point, the betrothal is announced at a meeting of the two houses, during which the couple exchange silver rings. The betrothal lasts at least a year. A betrothal is revocable by a public return of the rings, which will then be molten, but revocation was rarely needed because "the Eldar do not err lightly" in the choice of their partner.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:211</sup> After their formal betrothal, the couple appoints a time for the wedding when at least a year has passed.

Marriage is celebrated at a feast of the two houses. The spouses return their betrothal rings, which they keep, and receive "slender rings of gold" which are worn upon "the index of the right hand."<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:211</sup> In Noldor tradition, the bride’s mother gives the groom a jewel to be worn and the bridegroom's father gives a similar gift to the bride. These ceremonies and traditions were only a way for the parents to show their love and to mark a respectful recognition of the two houses which would be joined. While it was considered "ungracious and contemptuous of kin", in days of peace, "to forgo the ceremonies," it was completely lawful for a couple to be married without them.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:212</sup> The indissoluble union was completed solely by the "act of bodily union" which achieved marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:212</sup> Technically, without ceremony or witnesses, only blessings exchanged between the bride and groom, including speaking of the name of [Eru](/wiki/Eru), and consummation are required for marriage.

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Tuuliky_-_Lumen_Melma.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d7/Tuuliky_-_Lumen_Melma.jpg/300px-Tuuliky_-_Lumen_Melma.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="510" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d7/Tuuliky_-_Lumen_Melma.jpg/450px-Tuuliky_-_Lumen_Melma.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/d/d7/Tuuliky_-_Lumen_Melma.jpg 2x" data-file-width="588" data-file-height="1000"/></a><figcaption><i>Lumen Melma</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Tuuliky" title="Category:Images by Tuuliky">Tuuliky</a></figcaption></figure>

The Elves view the sexual act as a "great delight and joy."<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:213</sup> While extra-marital sex is described as contrary to their nature, earlier drafts (Version A) explain that "marriage resides ultimately in the will of the fëa."<sup id="cite_ref-LawsA_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LawsA-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:233</sup> This implies that a union without the consent of the spirit is not a true marriage. In that same draft layer, it is noted that a married Elf would reject bodily life if taken by force by someone other than their spouse.<sup id="cite_ref-NotesB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NotesB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 5</sup> However, the revised Version B of the text moves away from these specific mechanics, stating more generally that "seldom is any tale told of deeds of lust among them."<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:210</sup> While "there is no record of any among the Elves that took another's spouse by force,"<sup id="cite_ref-NotesB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NotesB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 5</sup> the attempt was made by [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin) toward [Idril](/wiki/Idril).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:169</sup>

Spouses can sometimes live separately for extended periods of time. Though united in body and spirit, they remain individuals with different gifts of mind and body to pursue.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:213</sup> However, a sundering during pregnancy or during the early years of parenthood, such as by war, would be so grievous to the couple, and hurtful to the child, that they prefer to have children in peaceful times.<sup id="cite_ref-Rebirth_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rebirth-6">[6]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:221</sup>

Elves typically have four children or fewer. [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) and [Nerdanel](/wiki/Nerdanel), who had [seven sons](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor "Sons of Fëanor"), were a notable exception.<sup id="cite_ref-NotesB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NotesB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:Note 4</sup> Whenever the Eldar married, whether in youth or in later life, their children were produced within a relatively short time after their wedding. However, in mortal count, a century or two may pass before the begetting of the first child and even longer between child and child.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:212</sup> After their time of children, the desire to procreate soon ceases. They turn their powers of body and mind to other tasks and arts. Nonetheless, they cherish the days of bearing and raising children as the happiest times of their lives.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:213</sup>

There are examples that appear to contradict this ideal. An example of extreme marital strife among the Eldar is the case of [Eöl](/wiki/E%C3%B6l) and [Aredhel](/wiki/Aredhel), where Eöl tried to restrain his wife from living the life of her choice. As a result, Aredhel left Eöl without his knowing, and took their son, Maeglin, with her back to [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin). The end result was that Eöl sought revenge upon his own family, and while seeking to slay his rebellious son, slew his wife accidentally.<sup id="cite_ref-SilmMaeglin_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SilmMaeglin-7">[7]</a></sup> Another example of great discontent developed between Fëanor and Nerdanel after the theft of the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils). Nerdanel did not wish to be parted from all her children nor did she wish to follow her husband against the [Valar](/wiki/Valar)'s wishes. Fëanor's harsh response was that, if she would not follow him, she was an untrue wife for deserting both her husband and her children.<sup id="cite_ref-Feanor_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Feanor-8">[8]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:354</sup>

[Celegorm](/wiki/Celegorm)'s pursuit of [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) and [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin)'s attraction to [Idril](/wiki/Idril) are cases of elves who sought disinterested partners. The desire for these unwilling wives was mixed with a desire for power. While unrequited love was known to happen, few of the Eldar responded so negatively to it.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:211</sup> [Indis](/wiki/Indis) loved [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB) with secret admiration but remained contently unwed because he was married.<sup id="cite_ref-LawsA_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LawsA-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:238</sup> [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon), Idril's father, denied Maeglin's suit for Idril's hand because he believed that Maeglin sought power more than the love of his daughter.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:165</sup> In Celegorm's case, he was motivated to claim Lúthien as his bride to force her father, [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol), to ally with the [Fëanorians](/wiki/F%C3%ABanorians) during the [Siege of Angband](/wiki/Siege_of_Angband). However, [Huan](/wiki/Huan) and [Beren](/wiki/Beren) defended Lúthien, against the attempted bride-stealing by Celegorm and the later attack by [Curufin](/wiki/Curufin).<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Daily life {#Daily_life}

Elves preoccupy themselves with various arts, such as: smithwork, sculpture, weaving, music, lore, and healing. Males and females have equal skill in all things, not concerned with the bringing forth of children; however, the females often specialize in the arts of healing while the men go to war.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:213</sup> This is because the Elves believe that taking life interferes with the ability to preserve life. Women who hunted would not specialize in healing, and men who healed would refrain from hunting and only fight when absolutely necessary, for "the virtue . . . in this matter \[of healing\] was due . . . to their abstaining from hunting or war."<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:213–4</sup>

## Later life {#Later_life}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Jef_Murray_-_Cirdan.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/65/Jef_Murray_-_Cirdan.jpg/300px-Jef_Murray_-_Cirdan.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="491" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/65/Jef_Murray_-_Cirdan.jpg/450px-Jef_Murray_-_Cirdan.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/6/65/Jef_Murray_-_Cirdan.jpg 2x" data-file-width="489" data-file-height="800"/></a><figcaption><i><a href="/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan" title="Círdan">Círdan</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Jef_Murray" title="Jef Murray">Jef Murray</a></figcaption></figure>

Eventually, if they did not die in battle or from some other cause, Elves, such as Noldor and [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri), of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) grew weary and desired to go to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor), where the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) sheltered their kind. This was known as the sea-longing.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Those who wished to leave for the Undying Lands went by ships provided at the [Grey Havens](/wiki/Grey_Havens), where [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan) the Shipwright dwelt with his folk. Those, of any Elven people, who did not perish through bodily death or depart from Middle-earth across the sea would eventually fade. Fading occurred when their *[fëar](/wiki/F%C3%ABar "Fëar")* 'consumed' their bodies and the body became merely a memory of the *fëa*. Elves in this faded state were completely invisible to mortal eyes, except for those among Men "into whose minds they may [enter directly](/wiki/%C3%93sanw%C3%AB "Ósanwë")".<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:212</sup>

<h2><span id=".22Cycles_of_life.22_and_ageing"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&#34;Cycles_of_life&#34;_and_ageing">&#34;Cycles of life&#34; and ageing</span></h2>

The Elves distinguished between two distinct modes within their lifecycle: a period of growth (olmië) where they go from conception to physical maturity, and a period of life (coivië) where they live and acquire skill, knowledge, and wisdom.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

Elves had no beards, at least until their "third cycle of life", like [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan). [Mahtan](/wiki/Mahtan) was an exception, and had a beard in his early "second cycle".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:9</sup> The Elvish beardlessness could also be observed in Mannish lines with an Elvish strain (as in the princely house of [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth)), which lacked beards.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

While the three cycles are not specifically defined, the first cycle is likely childhood and adolescence, which ended at the 100th year, the second is adulthood which could continue for Ages, and the third is for extremely old Elves; Círdan was the most ancient known Elf in Middle-earth. However, Elves who were not ancient could enter the third stage sooner due to tragic life events. When Lúthien wilfully released her spirit to follow Beren, her father saw her die, and "a winter, as it were the hoar age of mortal Men, fell upon Thingol."<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-9">[9]</a></sup>

Apparently, beards, though rare, were the only sign of further natural physical ageing beyond maturity.

Elves did not physically age after they reached maturity, but they did age in a different sense than Men. They became ever more weary of the world and burdened by its sorrows.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Círdan seemed to be aged himself since he is described as looking old, save for the stars in his eyes; this may be due to all the sorrows he had seen and lived through since the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> He had been one of the Teleri on the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey) who tarried on the shores of Middle-earth for Thingol's sake, and at the [Valar](/wiki/Valar)'s behest, though he had greatly wished to go to Aman.<sup id="cite_ref-last_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-17">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:385–6</sup> Another aged elf was [Gwindor](/wiki/Gwindor), the people of [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond) had trouble recognizing him after he escaped from being a prisoner of [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) in the pits of [Angband](/wiki/Angband) for fourteen years.<sup id="cite_ref-Gwindor_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwindor-18">[18]</a></sup>

## Death and reincarnation {#Death_and_reincarnation}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/64/Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg/250px-Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="250" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/64/Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg/375px-Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/6/64/Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg/500px-Anna_Kulisz_-_Valar_-_Mandos.jpg 2x" data-file-width="900" data-file-height="900"/></a><figcaption><i>Valar - Mandos</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Anna_Kulisz" title="Category:Images by Anna Kulisz">Anna Kulisz</a></figcaption></figure>

Elves have "limitless serial longevity"<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> (often called [immortality](/wiki/Immortal "Immortal"), but true immortality is beyond Eä); like the Ainur, they are bound to Arda until its End. Elves are immune to all diseases, and they can recover from wounds which would normally kill a mortal Man.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:218–9</sup>

Nonetheless, Elves can be physically slain or die of grief and weariness. Death was unnatural for Elves; [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) intended for an Elf's spirit (*[fëa](/wiki/F%C3%ABa_and_hr%C3%B6a "Fëa and hröa")*) and body (*[hröa](/wiki/F%C3%ABa_and_hr%C3%B6a "Fëa and hröa")*) to remain united throughout the entire life of Arda, but this design was disrupted by evils of [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:330–331</sup> Should an Elf die, its spirit would be in a state "open to the direct instruction and command of the Valar", and would be summoned to the [Halls of Mandos](/wiki/Halls_of_Mandos) in Aman as soon as they were disembodied.<sup id="cite_ref-Laws_1-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Laws-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:219</sup> Elves could refuse the summons, but this would suggest that they were tainted. Elves who went to the Halls were, after a period of time, typically given the opportunity to be reincarnated into a body identical to the one that died.<sup id="cite_ref-P4i_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-P4i-21">[21]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:339</sup> If the Elf accepted the opportunity, the Valar would then create the new body for the Elf's spirit; Elven spirits had no power to build such bodies for themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-last_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-17">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:390–391</sup> But the Valar could, if an Elf committed evil acts and refused to repent or continued to feel ill-will towards others, delay the time of the reincarnation, impose conditions of an Elf's return, or refuse to re-embody an Elf altogether (as was done with Fëanor).<sup id="cite_ref-last_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-17">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:380, 389</sup><sup id="cite_ref-P4i_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-P4i-21">[21]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:339</sup> An Elven spirit could also choose to remain disembodied; the Valar had no authority to force Elves to reincarnate.<sup id="cite_ref-P4i_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-P4i-21">[21]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:339</sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:334</sup>

Initially, Elves who died in Middle-earth and were re-embodied in Aman could return to Middle-earth if they wished,<sup id="cite_ref-last_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-17">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:378, 381–382</sup> but few Elves did so, as the journey was dangerous and they risked dying again. However, while the Noldor were exiled in the First Age, the Valar physically barred travel between Aman and Middle-earth; the only Elf who died and was allowed to return to Middle-earth during this period was [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien), and through the grace of Ilúvatar, she returned as a mortal.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-P4i_21-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-P4i-21">[21]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:339–340</sup> After the Valar pardoned the exiled Noldor at the end of the First Age, travel from Aman to Middle-earth resumed. [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel), who died in the Fall of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), was reincarnated and returned to Middle-earth, most likely in the Second Age by way of Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-last_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-17">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:377–382</sup> After the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) and the removal of Aman and Tol Eressëa from the Circles of the World near the end of the Second Age, Ilúvatar decreed that Elves were no longer permitted to travel to Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-last_17-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-17">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:380–382</sup>
