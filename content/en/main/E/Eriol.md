**Eriol** was the [Mannish](/wiki/Men "Men") mariner who visited [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) and learned the histories of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Life in the Great Lands {#Life_in_the_Great_Lands}

Eriol was born as **Ottor**, in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglia_(peninsula)" title="wikipedia:Anglia (peninsula)">Anglia</a> in the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands), c. <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century" title="wikipedia:5th century">5th century AD</a>, to [Eoh](/wiki/Eoh).<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-2">[2]</a></sup> His father often told him stories of "wide waters", since his ancestors were sea-farers,<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:6–7</sup> one of whom visited [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) as a child in his sleep via [Olórë Mallë](/wiki/Ol%C3%B3r%C3%AB_Mall%C3%AB), the Path of Dreams.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:20</sup> Ottor's grandfather [Heden](/wiki/Heden) claimed to be a descendant of the god <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odin" title="wikipedia:Odin">Wóden</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Tyr_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tyr-5">[4]</a></sup>

Sometime during Ottor's childhood, his father and mother died in a siege of their home,<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:6–7</sup> with Eoh being killed by his brother [Beorn](</wiki/Beorn_(son_of_Heden)> "Beorn (son of Heden)").<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-2">[2]</a></sup> There, Ottor was taken as a slave, but ultimately managed to escape.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:6–7</sup> <sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup>

After his escape, Ottor "lived a life on the waters",<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 3]</a></sup> but later settled on the island of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heligoland" title="wikipedia:Heligoland">Heligoland</a>, where he married a woman called [Cwén](/wiki/Cw%C3%A9n). There, they had two sons, [Hengest and Horsa](/wiki/Hengest_and_Horsa), named after their grandfather Eoh (since their names share a similar meaning). However, after Cwén died, [sea-longing](/wiki/Sea-longing "Sea-longing") overcame Ottor, and he left his two young sons, and went sailing again.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-2">[2]</a></sup>

During his voyages, he stumbled upon an island inhabited by an old man, who taught him much about the lore of the hidden seas in the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/West" title="West">West</a>, and who told him of the [Magic Isles](/wiki/Magic_Isles), and of one very remote island far to the west - that is, [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa). The old man was later revealed to be the [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo) himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:6–7</sup>

### Arrival to Tol Eressëa {#Arrival_to_Tol_Eressëa}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/7/79/Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg/165px-Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="165" height="247" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/7/79/Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg/248px-Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/7/79/Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg/330px-Irina_Timofeeva_-_Kortirion.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1672" data-file-height="2504"/></a><figcaption><i>Kortirion</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Irina_Timofeeva" title="Category:Images by Irina Timofeeva">Irina Timofeeva</a></figcaption></figure>

Ultimately, however, after much searching for the island, Ottor managed to reach Tol Eressëa,<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:6–7</sup> and was afterwards called <i><b>Eriol</b></i> by the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) of the island.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:14–15</sup>

While exploring the [interior](/wiki/Alalmin%C3%B3r%C3%AB "Alalminórë") of the island, Eriol stumbled upon the city of [Kortirion](/wiki/Kortirion) and the [Cottage of Lost Play](/wiki/Cottage_of_Lost_Play) one summer evening. There, he was greeted by [Lindo](/wiki/Lindo), the lord of the Cottage, and his wife [Vairë](</wiki/Vair%C3%AB_(wife_of_Lindo)> "Vairë (wife of Lindo)"), who offered him shelter at their home.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:14–15</sup> That night, Lindo and Vairë told him the history of the Cottage of Lost Play,<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:18–20</sup> and of the city of Kortirion.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:16</sup>

The following day, Eriol met [Rúmil](</wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium> "Rúmil (elf of Tirion)"), the old door-ward of the Cottage, in his gardens,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[6]</a></sup> where he told Eriol the story of *[the Music of the Ainur](/wiki/The_Music_of_the_Ainur "The Music of the Ainur")*.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[7]</a></sup> Later that evening, Rúmil also told him the tale of *[the Coming of the Valar and the Building of Valinor](/wiki/The_Coming_of_the_Valar_and_the_Building_of_Valinor "The Coming of the Valar and the Building of Valinor")*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[8]</a></sup>

That night, Eriol for the second time heard the fluting of [Tinfang Warble](/wiki/Tinfang_Warble), a half-[fay](/wiki/Fays "Fays"), half-elven creature, which caused a great longing in his heart, that only the magic drink, called *[limpë](/wiki/Limp%C3%AB "Limpë")* could cure. However, Vairë told him that only the Lady of Tol Eressëa, [Meril-i-Turinqi](/wiki/Meril-i-Turinqi), might give it to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Mer_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mer-12">[9]</a></sup>

### Meeting with Meril-i-Turinqi {#Meeting_with_Meril-i-Turinqi}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0c/Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg/300px-Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="210" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/0c/Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg/450px-Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/0c/Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg/600px-Sandrine_Gestin_-_Meril.jpg 2x" data-file-width="700" data-file-height="490"/></a><figcaption><i>Meril</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Sandrine_Gestin" title="Category:Images by Sandrine Gestin">Sandrine Gestin</a></figcaption></figure>

Therefore, a few days later, [Littleheart](/wiki/Littleheart), one of the inhabitants of the Cottage, led Eriol to the home of Meril, located underneath the [tower of Ingil](/wiki/Tirin_na_Gilweth "Tirin na Gilweth") in a *[korin](/wiki/Korin "Korin")* of [elms](/wiki/Elms "Elms"). There, Eriol requested to taste *limpë*, seeking kinship and fellowship with the Elves and an end to his longing.<sup id="cite_ref-Mer_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mer-12">[9]</a></sup>

However, Meril denied him, telling him that it was dangerous for a <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Mortal" title="Mortal">mortal</a> Man to do so, as [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) made his [Children](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar "Children of Ilúvatar") different, and drinking *limpë* would erase his old desires but awake new ones; she also warned him that he would one day long for his lands again.<sup id="cite_ref-Mer_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mer-12">[9]</a></sup> To explain better she told him the stories of *[the Chaining of Melko](/wiki/The_Chaining_of_Melko "The Chaining of Melko")*<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[10]</a></sup> and of *[the Coming of the Elves and the Making of Kôr](/wiki/The_Coming_of_the_Elves_and_the_Making_of_K%C3%B4r "The Coming of the Elves and the Making of Kôr")*.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[11]</a></sup>

After that, Eriol stayed in the Cottage of Lost Play for some time, hearing the stories of *[the Theft of Melko and the Darkening of Valinor](/wiki/The_Theft_of_Melko_and_the_Darkening_of_Valinor "The Theft of Melko and the Darkening of Valinor")*<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[12]</a></sup> and of *[the Flight of the Noldoli](/wiki/The_Flight_of_the_Noldoli "The Flight of the Noldoli")*.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[13]</a></sup>

Before winter came to Kortirion that year, Eriol also visited many of the homes of the [Teleri](/wiki/Vanyar#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium "Vanyar") and the [Inwir](/wiki/Inwir) (the royal clan of the Teleri), who lived there, and learned more of the language, history and customs of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-Vea_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vea-17">[14]</a></sup>

### Winter in Tol Eressëa<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[note 4]</a></sup> {#Winter_in_Tol_Eressëa[note_4]}

One day during the winter, some time after Eriol's visit to Meril, there arrived a guest to the Cottage, and his name was [Gilfanon](/wiki/Gilfanon). He was a very wise and a very old [Gnome](/wiki/Gnomes "Gnomes") who lived at [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel), a village in the west of Tol Eressëa. Since Eriol wanted to know more about the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) and the [Moon](/wiki/Moon) and their making, Lindo suggested to him that he should travel with Gilfanon to his home, the [House of the Hundred Chimneys](/wiki/House_of_the_Hundred_Chimneys), for Gilfanon could tell him much of such things.<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-19">[15]</a></sup>

However, while Gilfanon agreed that Eriol should visit his home, he ultimately let Lindo tell *[the Tale of the Sun and Moon](/wiki/The_Tale_of_the_Sun_and_Moon "The Tale of the Sun and Moon")*,<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-19">[15]</a></sup> after which Vairë told the story of *[the Hiding of Valinor](/wiki/The_Hiding_of_Valinor "The Hiding of Valinor")*.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[16]</a></sup> The following night, Gilfanon told the story of the *[Travail of the Noldoli and the coming of Mankind](/wiki/Gilfanon%27s_Tale "Gilfanon's Tale")*.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[17]</a></sup>

Sometime during the winter, one of the children of the Cottage of Lost Play, called [Vëannë](/wiki/V%C3%ABann%C3%AB), asked Eriol to tell her about his own life,<sup id="cite_ref-Vea_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vea-17">[14]</a></sup> and in return Vëannë told him *[the Tale of Tinúviel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_Tin%C3%BAviel "The Tale of Tinúviel")*.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup>

Later, Gilfanon was supposed to tell the story of *[Turambar and the Foalókë](/wiki/Turambar_and_the_Foal%C3%B3k%C3%AB)*, but was not present at the [Tale-fire](/wiki/Tale-fire) that evening, so instead one [Eltas](/wiki/Eltas) recounted the tale instead.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[18]</a></sup>

The next day, Lindo and Vairë sent messengers to Meril, the Lady of Tol Eressëa, inviting her to the Cottage of Lost Play, since they were planning a great festival of tale-telling before Eriol departed for Tavrobel. This was done because Littleheart son of [Voronwë](/wiki/Voronw%C3%AB) needed aid in the telling of the three final tales, one of them being *[the Fall of Gondolin](</wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter)> "The Fall of Gondolin (chapter)")*, which he recounted on the first night of the festival.<sup id="cite_ref-Ear_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ear-23">[19]</a></sup>

That tale was followed by the tale of *[the Nauglafring](/wiki/The_Nauglafring "The Nauglafring")*, which was told by Gilfanon.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[note 5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[20]</a></sup>

All of these tales ultimately culminated in *[the Tale of Eärendel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_E%C3%A4rendel "The Tale of Eärendel")*,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[21]</a></sup> after which Eriol departed for Tavrobel.<sup id="cite_ref-Ear_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ear-23">[19]</a></sup>

### Departure to Tavrobel {#Departure_to_Tavrobel}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d4/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png/120px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="120" height="168" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d4/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png/180px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d4/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png/240px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Taurobel.png 2x" data-file-width="688" data-file-height="965"/></a><figcaption><i>Taurobel</i> by <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a></figcaption></figure>

Once Eriol arrived in Tavrobel, he was bidden by Gilfanon to write down all the stories he had heard in a book called *[Parma Kuluina](/wiki/Parma_Kuluina)* or the *Golden Book of Tavrobel*.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[note 6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[22]</a></sup> Since this was the final condition before drinking *limpë*, and because now he knew most of the Elves' history, he was finally allowed to drink it.<sup id="cite_ref-Tav_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tav-29">[23]</a></sup>

After drinking *limpë*, he was made young again, and afterwards married [Naimi](/wiki/Naimi) (or Nelmir, according to one text),<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-30">[24]</a></sup> the niece of Vairë, with whom he either had one son, [Heorrenda](/wiki/Heorrenda),<sup id="cite_ref-Tyr_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tyr-5">[4]</a></sup> or, according to another text, two sons: Heorrenda *and* [Hendwine](/wiki/Hendwine).<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-30">[24]</a></sup>

He also stayed in a nearby place called [Fladweth Amrod](/wiki/Fladweth_Amrod) for some time.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[25]</a></sup>

<br>

### Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

The manner of Eriol's death is uncertain, depending on the version of the events given in the outlines for the continuation of *The Book of Lost Tales*. In one earlier version, Eriol died in Tol Eressëa *before* the Faring Forth.<sup id="cite_ref-Tav_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tav-29">[23]</a></sup>

#### *Epilogue* {#Epilogue}

However, in another version of events (which Tolkien called the <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Epilogue_(The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Epilogue (The Book of Lost Tales) (page does not exist)">Epilogue</a></i>), following the [Faring Forth](/wiki/Faring_Forth) (the disastrous attempt by the Elves of Tol Eressëa to rescue their [lost kin](/wiki/Ilkorindi "Ilkorindi") in the East, in which the entire island of Tol Eressëa was dragged across the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean" title="wikipedia:Atlantic Ocean">Ocean</a> to be anchored off the coast of the Great Lands), Eriol died some time after the [Battle of the Heath of the Sky-roof](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Heath_of_the_Sky-roof) in which the invading Men from the Great Lands overran Tol Eressëa.

There, after fleeing across the rivers [Afros](/wiki/Afros) and [Gruir](/wiki/Gruir) with the remaining Elves of the Lonely Isle following the battle, he wrote down the last words of the *Golden Book* in the ruined Tavrobel:<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[26]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...Hark O my brothers, they shall say, the little trumpets blow; we hear a sound of instruments unimagined small. Like strands of wind, like mystic half-transparencies, <a href="/wiki/Gilfanon" title="Gilfanon">Gilfanon</a> Lord of <a href="/wiki/Tavrobel" title="Tavrobel">Tavrobel</a> rides out tonight amid his folk, and hunts the elfin deer beneath the paling sky. A music of forgotten feet, a gleam of leaves, a sudden bending of the grass, and wistful voices murmuring on the bridge, and they are gone.<br/><br/> But behold, Tavrobel shall not know its name, and all the land be changed, and even these written words of mine belike will all be lost; and so I lay down the pen, and so of the <a href="/wiki/Elves" title="Elves">fairies</a> cease to tell.
</p>

</blockquote>

<h4><span id="Other_versions_of_Eriol.27s_death"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_versions_of_Eriol&#39;s_death">Other versions of Eriol&#39;s death</span></h4>

According to yet another text, it was Eriol himself who was the main instigator of the Faring Forth. Eriol, longing for his home, sailed back to the Great Lands against the command of Meril-i-Turinqi, where he "preached" of the Faring Forth before he eventually died. However, his impatience and disobedience hastened the Faring Forth, and ultimately doomed it.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[27]</a></sup>

#### Legacy {#Legacy}

Eriol's legacy, however, continued in his sons - Hengest, Horsa and Heorrenda - who later conquered Tol Eressëa (which eventually became known as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a>), and from whom the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people" title="wikipedia:English people">English</a> preserved the true tradition of the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[28]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Eriol* is in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya), meaning "One who dreams alone", possibly being derived from the roots <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=ERE&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="ERE (page does not exist)">ERE</a> ("remain alone") + <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=OLO&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="OLO (page does not exist)">OLO</a> ("dream") or <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=LORO&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="LORO (page does not exist)">LORO</a> ("doze, slumber").<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[29]</a></sup>

According to one text, however, the meaning of *Eriol* ("One who dreams alone") was "punningly" derived by Lindo from <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%89ri&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Éri (page does not exist)">éri</a></i> ("alone") + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Olta&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Olta (page does not exist)">olta</a></i> ("dream").<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[30]</a></sup> However, the actual meaning of *Eriol* was "iron cliff", being the Qenya cognate of <i><b>Angol</b></i>, a name given to Ottor by the [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes) of Tol Eressëa after the "regions of his home" (i.e. <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglia_(peninsula)" title="wikipedia:Anglia (peninsula)">Anglia</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Iro_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iro-37">[31]</a></sup>

The name *Angol* consists of the elements *[ang](/wiki/Ang "Ang")* ("iron") + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%94l_(Gnomish_word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ôl (Gnomish word) (page does not exist)">ôl</a></i> ("cliff").<sup id="cite_ref-Iro_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iro-37">[31]</a></sup> There also appears another Qenya cognate of *Angol*, *Eriollo*, given in the *[Gnomish Lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon)*.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[32]</a></sup>

According to yet another text, there appear the variations <i><b>Eriolë</b></i> and <i><b>Erioldo</b></i>. In that same text, however, *Eriol* is instead given as a [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) form of the name.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[33]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Eriol's original name was <i><b>Ottor</b></i> (meaning "otter" in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English)),<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[34]</a></sup> but he called himself <i><b>Wǽfre</b></i> ("restless, wandering").<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-2">[2]</a></sup>

Another one of his names was <i><b>Sarothron</b></i>, meaning "voyager, seafarer" in Gnomish.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[35]</a></sup>

Vëannë, a child of [Mar Vanwa Tyaliéva](/wiki/Mar_Vanwa_Tyali%C3%A9va), also called him <i><b>Melinon</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup> a Qenya name likely derived from the root <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=MELE_(root)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="MELE (root) (page does not exist)">MELE</a> ("love").<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[36]</a></sup>

During Eriol's first meeting with Lindo, when asked by Lindo what was his name, Eriol simply named himself the <i><b>Stranger</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Win_3-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Win-3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:14–15</sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Earlier legendarium {#Earlier_legendarium}

In some of the outlines for the continuation of *The Book of Lost Tales*, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) decided to completely change the nature of the framework of the tales. In that reimagining of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Tol Eressëa ceased to be identified with England/<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles" title="wikipedia:British Isles">British Isles</a> - instead, it became a different island, and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain" title="wikipedia:Great Britain">Great Britain</a> became the island of [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany), while the mariner that came there was called [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), an Englishman sailing *from* England *to* Tol Eressëa.<sup id="cite_ref-Ael_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ael-43">[37]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

In a somewhat later version of the legendarium, beginning with the c. [1926](/wiki/1926) *[Sketch of the Mythology](/wiki/Sketch_of_the_Mythology)*, the name <i><b>Eriol</b></i> reappears as an [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) name of Ælfwine.<sup id="cite_ref-Ael_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ael-43">[37]</a></sup>

For more information, see: *[Ælfwine#Other names](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine#Other_names "Ælfwine")*.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The names of Eriol's sons are significant to the historical context of the Eriol story. In British folklore, Hengest and Horsa are the legendary leaders of the Anglo-Saxon invasion of Britain in the 5th century AD. By connecting them to the translator of the *Lost Tales*, Tolkien would have established a direct link between his legendarium and the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_of_Britain" title="wikipedia:Matter of Britain">Matter of Britain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-2">[2]</a></sup>

As stated previously, Eriol's original name was Ottor. [Dimitra Fimi](/wiki/Dimitra_Fimi) and [Andrew Higgins](/wiki/Andrew_Higgins) propose that Tolkien in his youth perhaps assumed (or was given) an [Animalic](/wiki/Animalic) name **Otter** for himself. In the year [1909](/wiki/1909) he wrote a note in Esperanto *Privata al LUTTRO* "Private to OTTER".<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[38]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[39]</a></sup>
