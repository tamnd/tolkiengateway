**Eric Valentine Gordon** ([14 February](/wiki/14_February) [1896](/wiki/1896) – [29 July](/wiki/29_July) [1938](/wiki/1938)) was a Canadian philologist who worked alongside [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) on various scholastic works and published books.

## Early life {#Early_life}

Gordon was educated at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_College,_British_Columbia" title="wikipedia:Victoria College, British Columbia">Victoria College</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University" title="wikipedia:McGill University">McGill University</a>. He also attended <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Oxford" title="wikipedia:University College, Oxford">University College</a> at [Oxford University](/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") (1920), and later taught at [Leeds University](/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") (1922-1931).<sup id="cite_ref-chance_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chance-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Works with Tolkien {#Works_with_Tolkien}

While Tolkien was teaching at [Leeds University](/wiki/Leeds_University), Gordon had come to Leeds and Tolkien wrote in [his diary](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_diaries "J.R.R. Tolkien's diaries") "Eric Valentine Gordon has come and got firmly established and is my devoted friend and pal."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Gordon worked with him on *[A Middle English Vocabulary](/wiki/A_Middle_English_Vocabulary)* and <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight" title="Sir Gawain and the Green Knight">Sir Gawain and the Green Knight</a></i>.

The two also began the *[Viking Club](/wiki/Viking_Club)* where they would read [Old Icelandic](/wiki/Old_Norse "Old Norse") sagas (and drink a lot of beer) with students and faculty, and invent original [Anglo-Saxon](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") songs. A collection of these was privately published as the book *[Songs for the Philologists](/wiki/Songs_for_the_Philologists)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In 1930, he married [Ida Pickles](/wiki/I.L._Gordon "I.L. Gordon"), a former student and fellow philologist and later he taught at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester" title="wikipedia:University of Manchester">Manchester University</a> (1932-1938).<sup id="cite_ref-chance_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chance-1">[1]</a></sup>

While Gordon died in 1938, yet another two works, to which Tolkien contributed, were published posthumously by Ida, who completed and revised them. The first was *[Pearl](</wiki/Pearl_(E.V._Gordon)> "Pearl (E.V. Gordon)")* (1953; Tolkien contributed to this book with a section, "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Form_and_Purpose&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Form and Purpose (page does not exist)">Form and Purpose</a>", in the introduction) and the second was *[The Seafarer](</wiki/The_Seafarer_(I.L._Gordon)> "The Seafarer (I.L. Gordon)")* (1960).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
