The **Elder Days**, also called the <i><b>Eldest Days</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> was a term that, during the [Second](../S/Second_Age.md) and [Third Ages](../T/Third_Age.md), properly referred to the times that preceded the [Downfall of Angband](../W/War_of_Wrath.md) at the very end of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md),<sup id="cite_ref-appb_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appb-3">[3]</a></sup> perhaps covering not only the entire [Age](../A/Ages.md), but also the events before the [Awakening of the Elves](../A/Awakening_of_the_Elves.md),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> by which its beginning was marked.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In that context, the [Middle Days](../M/Middle_Days.md) were the Second and Third Ages, whereas the [Younger Days](../Y/Younger_Days.md) were the foreseen [Dominion of Men](../D/Dominion_of_Men.md).<sup id="cite_ref-council_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-1">[1]</a></sup>

However, in the [Fourth Age](../F/Fourth_Age.md) the term began to be applied to all previous [Ages](../A/Ages.md), as the time before the Dominion of Men and the dwindling of the [Elves](Elves.md) and other races.<sup id="cite_ref-appb_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appb-3">[3]</a></sup> [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) for instance, referred to himself as the last King of the Elder Days, according to the latter sense.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> In this context, the Elder Days were also called the <i><b>Elvish World</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) notes that the word "Elder" has deliberately an archaic flavour, since it is now only applied to persons (Elders = seniors). He also pointed to an association with the poetic word *eld* 'old age, antiquity'.

Early English had the expression *Þe eldern dawes* 'the days of our forefathers, long ago,' i.e. *Days of the [Seniors](Elves.md)*, which was intended by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

The "very archaic" [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) name for the First Age that "came" to [Arundel Lowdham](../A/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham.md) was *frumaeldi*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
