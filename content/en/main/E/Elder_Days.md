The **Elder Days**, also called the <i><b>Eldest Days</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> was a term that, during the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and [Third Ages](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age"), properly referred to the times that preceded the [Downfall of Angband](/wiki/War_of_Wrath "War of Wrath") at the very end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age),<sup id="cite_ref-appb_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appb-3">[3]</a></sup> perhaps covering not only the entire [Age](/wiki/Ages "Ages"), but also the events before the [Awakening of the Elves](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> by which its beginning was marked.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In that context, the [Middle Days](/wiki/Middle_Days) were the Second and Third Ages, whereas the [Younger Days](/wiki/Younger_Days) were the foreseen [Dominion of Men](/wiki/Dominion_of_Men).<sup id="cite_ref-council_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-1">[1]</a></sup>

However, in the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age) the term began to be applied to all previous [Ages](/wiki/Ages), as the time before the Dominion of Men and the dwindling of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and other races.<sup id="cite_ref-appb_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appb-3">[3]</a></sup> [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) for instance, referred to himself as the last King of the Elder Days, according to the latter sense.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> In this context, the Elder Days were also called the <i><b>Elvish World</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") notes that the word "Elder" has deliberately an archaic flavour, since it is now only applied to persons (Elders = seniors). He also pointed to an association with the poetic word *eld* 'old age, antiquity'.

Early English had the expression *Þe eldern dawes* 'the days of our forefathers, long ago,' i.e. *Days of the [Seniors](/wiki/Elves "Elves")*, which was intended by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

The "very archaic" [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name for the First Age that "came" to [Arundel Lowdham](/wiki/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham "Alwin Arundel Lowdham") was *frumaeldi*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
