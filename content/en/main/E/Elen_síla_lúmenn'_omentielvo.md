<i><b>Elen síla lúmenn’ omentielvo</b></i> is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) greeting used by [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo) in the first meeting with [Gildor Inglorion](/wiki/Gildor_Inglorion). It translates as "A star shines on the hour of our meeting".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Without the vocal assimilation it would be *[Elen](/wiki/Elen) <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=S%C3%ADl-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Síl- (page does not exist)">síla</a> [lúmenna](/wiki/L%C3%BAme "Lúme") [omentielvo](/wiki/Omentie "Omentie")*, which Tolkien also translates as "A star shines upon the hour of the meeting of our ways".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

When someone asked [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) what *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* was about, and if it was an "allegory", he explained that he was just trying to create a situation where *Elen síla lúmenn’ omentielmo* would be a common greeting, and that the phrase long antedated the book. (The letter gives the form *omentielmo*, which was the early form used in the first edition).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
