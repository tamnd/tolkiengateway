<i><b>English and Welsh</b></i> is the title of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s inauguration speech for the O'Donnell Lectures.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien presented the paper on [21 October](../_/21_October.md) [1955](../_/1955.md) at the [University of Oxford](../U/University_of_Oxford.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The lecture was first published in *[Angles and Britons: O'Donnell Lectures](../A/Angles_and_Britons.md)* ([8 July](../_/8_July.md) [1963](../_/1963.md)). In [1983](../_/1983.md), it was reprinted in *[The Monsters and the Critics and Other Essays](../T/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics_and_Other_Essays.md)*.

## Contents {#Contents}

In a lengthy sidenote, Tolkien discusses his notions of "native tongue" as opposed to "cradle tongue", and of an inherited taste of language. The lecture sheds light on Tolkien's conceptions of the connections of race, ethnicity and language.
