**Eä** (pronounced <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈɛ.a]</span>), the **Created World**,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> is the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name for the universe, as a realization of the vision of the [Ainur](/wiki/Ainur). Thus, Eä is *the World that Is*, as distinguished from the [Timeless Halls](/wiki/Timeless_Halls) of [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) and the [Void](/wiki/Void), that have no material form.

## Creation {#Creation}

Eä was conceived in the [Music of the Ainur](/wiki/Music_of_the_Ainur) and then visualized in the beautiful [Vision](/wiki/Vision_of_Il%C3%BAvatar "Vision of Ilúvatar") that Ilúvatar showed to the Ainur to see for themselves what they sang. The Vision showed a world sustained inside the void, but without being part of it, and developing life on its own. The Ainur desired all this to be real, and Ilúvatar brought this into actuality by saying *[Eä](</wiki/Ea_(verb)> "Ea (verb)")* and sent His [Flame Imperishable](/wiki/Flame_Imperishable) into the Void to burn at the heart of the World. Thus Eä was set amid the void.<sup id="cite_ref-ainu_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ainu-3">[3]</a></sup>

When the Valar entered Eä they became part of it and could not leave it — willingly or not, until [the End](/wiki/The_End "The End").<sup id="cite_ref-ainu_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ainu-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> At the beginning of time, Eä was dark and unshaped, and the Ainur who entered it had to labor in the vast wastes for ages beyond knowledge or reckoning to fulfill the vision.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> During their labors, [Varda](/wiki/Varda) created the [stars](/wiki/Stars "Stars"),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and even [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB), [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB), and [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo) chiefly worked for the formation of [Arda](/wiki/Arda) at the beginning of its existence.<sup id="cite_ref-ainu_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ainu-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Annals_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Annals-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Other [spirits](/wiki/Spirits "Spirits") wandered around Eä, and [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) gathered some to his service.<sup id="cite_ref-Annals_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Annals-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Description {#Description}

Eä is described as being of "vast halls and spaces" burning with "wheeling fires" and histories unfolding amidst the "Deeps of [Time](/wiki/Time)", such as the waking of the [Children of Ilúvatar](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar) and the [Dominion of Men](/wiki/Dominion_of_Men).<sup id="cite_ref-ainu_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ainu-3">[3]</a></sup> Eä has vast regions beyond the thought of Elves and Men. However its shape, duration, extent and size are not described. Of all Eä, only [Arda](/wiki/Arda), the world inhabited by the Children, is known.

Eä is sustained into the Void without being part of it, separated from it by the [Walls of the World](/wiki/Walls_of_the_World).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> At the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), the Valar threw Melkor to the [Void](/wiki/Void) through the [Door of Night](/wiki/Door_of_Night).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

The [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and [Elves](/wiki/Elves) recognize that Eä functions with the *[axani](/wiki/Axan "Axan")*, the laws established by Ilúvatar that beings are directed to follow but can choose to disobey, as did the [Dark Lords](/wiki/Dark_Lord "Dark Lord") and their followers. But the *axani* are limited with the *[únati](/wiki/%C3%9Anat "Únat")*, physical laws that are impossible to break by all beings, no matter how powerful or evil.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

*[Eä](</wiki/Ea_(verb)> "Ea (verb)")* is the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) verb "to be", and was the word spoken by Eru Ilúvatar by which He created the universe.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In earlier works, the [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) word *[Ilu](/wiki/Ilu)* is translated as "world"<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> in the sense of the "universe", from the [root](/wiki/Root "Root") ILU "all, the whole".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

In the 1930s [Ambarkanta](/wiki/Ambarkanta), the World is described as a globe of air enclosed in [Vaiya](/wiki/Vaiya) and the [Ilurambar](/wiki/Ilurambar), the Walls of the World that separate Vaiya from nothingness and the Void.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> The [Ambarkanta maps](/wiki/Ambarkanta_maps) schematics are labelled as "ILU".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

As the word Ilu doesn't make a distinction between the concepts of "Arda" and "Eä" of the later *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, the two seem to be mostly equated; in that context, all the universe consists only of [Ambar](/wiki/Ambar) and the airs around it.

Eä first appeared in a later version of the Ainulindalë, which here introduced the new concept of Arda set within an indefinite vastness of the Creation.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> Around the same time, Tolkien changed the conception of Ilurambar to Eärambar.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

In the later [Round World version of the Silmarillion](/wiki/Round_World_version_of_the_Silmarillion), the text mentions that Eä is vast beyond measure with the stars belonging to other and remoter parts of its Great Tale. In the Beginning, the innumerable Ainur shaped Eä and labored amid remote stars and worlds; but we could know only those who followed [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) to Arda, which is of central importance in the [Great Tale](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") of Eä.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

## Outside the Legendarium {#Outside_the_Legendarium}

In *[Átaremma](/wiki/%C3%81taremma)*, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s translation of the Lord's Prayer, he renders "in Heaven" as *han Eä*, lit. "beyond Creation \[the Material Universe\]".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> <span></span>
