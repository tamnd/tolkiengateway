**Ellor Eshúrizel** was the second planet that [Michael George Ramer](/wiki/Michael_George_Ramer) had visited with his mind.

## History {#History}

Ramer thought it could be one of the worlds inhabited by the [En-keladim](/wiki/En-keladim), as it was designed by minds.<sup id="cite_ref-Notion_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Notion-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:206–7</sup> He described the world as follows:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>That immense plain with its silver floor all delicately patterned; the shapely cliffs and convoluted hills. The whole world was designed with such loveliness, not of one thought, but of many in harmony; though in all its shapes there was nowhere any to recall what we call organic life. There &#34;inanimate nature&#34; was orderly, symmetrical, unconfused, yet intricate, beyond my mind&#39;s unravelling, in its flowing modulations and recollections: a garden, a paradise of water, metal, stone, like the interwoven variations of vast natural orders of flowers. Eshúrizel! Blue, white, silver, grey, blushing to rich purples were its themes, in which a glint of red was like an apocalyptic vision of essential Redness, and a gleam of gold was like the glory of the <a href="/wiki/Sun" title="Sun">Sun</a>. And there was music, too. For there were many streams, water abundant – or some fairer counterpart, less wayward, more skilled in the enchantment of light and in the making of innumerable sounds. There the great water fall of <a href="/wiki/%C3%96sh%C3%BCl-k%C3%BCll%C3%B6sh" title="Öshül-küllösh">Öshül-küllösh</a> fell down its three hundred steps in a sequence of notes and chords of which I can only hear faint echoes now. I think the <a href="/wiki/En-keladim" title="En-keladim">En-keladim</a> dwell there.<sup id="cite_ref-Notion_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Notion-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:198–9</sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
