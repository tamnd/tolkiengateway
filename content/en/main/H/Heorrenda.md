**Heorrenda** was a son of a [Man](/wiki/Men "Men") [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol) and an [Elf](/wiki/Elf) [Naimi](/wiki/Naimi),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Hen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-3">[2]</a></sup> according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Ers_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ers-4">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Heorrenda was born in Tol Eressëa,<sup id="cite_ref-Ers_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ers-4">[3]</a></sup> and, according to one text, it was he who completed the *[Golden Book](/wiki/Golden_Book)*, in which were written the histories of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), that was kept in Tol Eressëa.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

After the [Faring Forth](/wiki/Faring_Forth),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup> the disastrous attempt by the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) of Tol Eressëa to save their [kin](/wiki/Ilkorindi "Ilkorindi") in the Great Lands which resulted in their total defeat and eventual fading, evil Men invaded Tol Eressëa, now anchored near the coast of the Great Lands.<sup id="cite_ref-Hay_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hay-8">[6]</a></sup>

Afterwards however, Heorrenda, alongside his half-brothers [Hengest](/wiki/Hengest) and [Horsa](/wiki/Horsa), led what would become the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people" title="wikipedia:English people">English</a> people and conquered Tol Eressëa, after which it became known as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Hay_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hay-8">[6]</a></sup> There, he would settle in what was once the village of [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel), now called *Gréata Hægwudu*, and which would eventually become known as the village of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Haywood" title="wikipedia:Great Haywood">Great Haywood</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup>

He, his half-brothers, and the English people as a whole, would ultimately be the only group of [Men](/wiki/Men) to remain friendly to the Elves, and to keep the "true tradition of the [fairies](/wiki/Elves "Elves")".<sup id="cite_ref-Hay_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hay-8">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Heorrenda* is in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) and of uncertain meaning.

It is found in old Germanic poetry (such as the Old English poem <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=D%C3%A9or_(poem)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Déor (poem) (page does not exist)">Déor</a></i>) and the first element has been interpreted as *heorra* meaning "a hinge, cardinal point"; an etymological connection with the Latin *corvus* and Greek *korax* (both meaning "raven") has been proposed, possibly in the sense of a pick used to play a stringed instrument, with *Heorrenda* meaning "minstrel".<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-3">[2]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Heorrenda was also known as <i><b>Heruven</b></i> in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya), with <i><b>Herwent</b></i> being its [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) equivalent. These two names were most likely phonological imitations of the name *Heorrenda* instead of being translations of its meaning.

The first element of *Heruven* is probably *[heru](/wiki/Heru "Heru")* ("lord"), while the second element of *Herwent* is most likely <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gwent&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gwent (page does not exist)">gwent</a>/gwenn/gwen</i> ("large, big; fine") - hence *Heruven* and *Herwent* might mean "great lord".<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-3">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In some of the later notes, **Heorrenda**, called "the harper", is described as being the son of [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) and [Earissë](/wiki/Eariss%C3%AB), the daughter of [Lindo](/wiki/Lindo). Here he has a brother called [Hlúdwine](/wiki/Hl%C3%BAdwine).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup>

### Later etymology {#Later_etymology}

In these texts, the Qenya name for *Heorrenda* is <i><b>Heruvendo</b></i> (<i><b>Horwin</b></i> being its Gnomish equivalent).

As with the earlier names (<a class="mw-selflink-fragment" href="#Etymology">see above</a>) *Heruvendo* likewise seems to be a phonological imitation of *Heorrenda*, corresponding even more closely than *Heruven*.<sup id="cite_ref-Lus_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lus-11">[9]</a></sup>

However, the first element *Hor-* in *Horwin* cannot be cognate with *Heru-* in *Heruvendo*. It is possible that *Horwin* might have been a slip for <i><b>Herwin</b></i>, though in the old German poem <i><a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudrun" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Kudrun</a></i>, the form *Horund* (or *Horant*) appears for the name *Heorrenda*, which might have inspired the Gnomish name of *Horwin*.<sup id="cite_ref-Lus_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lus-11">[9]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The name *Heorrenda* appears in the Old English poem *Déor*, where Heorrenda was a bard that has supplanted Déor, another bard and the main character of the poem, in their lord's favour.

In some of [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien)'s lectures on the poem *[Beowulf](</wiki/Beowulf_(poem)> "Beowulf (poem)")*, he named the unknown author of the poem as *Heorrenda*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup>
