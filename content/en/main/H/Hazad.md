**Hazad Longbeard** was a [Man](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Pre-Númenóreans") of [Agar](/wiki/Agar) who was the son of [Buldar](/wiki/Buldar) and [Elmar](/wiki/Elmar) during the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Hazad was described in his youth as being "broad, swarthy, short, though, harsh-tongued, heavy-handed, and quick to violence." <sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> Hazad’s sons are said to resemble him in his youth.

Though he found pride in the number of his descendants, his greatest joy was his beard, long over five feet and, unlike his sons, soft, “ruly to his hand,” and close to him all the time.

Hazad married late, due to the admiration he had for his late mother, Elmar. He fathered many children, including [Tal-Elmar](</wiki/Tal-Elmar_(character)> "Tal-Elmar (character)"), who was his seventeenth child.

Along with his youngest son, he delivered the ill message to [Mogru](/wiki/Mogru) of [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans "Númenóreans") ships approaching the Hills of Agar.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Some analysts have noticed the similarity of *Hazad* with [Dwarvish](/wiki/Khuzdul "Khuzdul") *[Khazâd](/wiki/Khaz%C3%A2d)*, "Dwarves", and [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) *hazad*, "seven". The name would then either mean "Dwarf (bearded, smaller one)" or "Seventh child", however, this remains speculation.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Hazad Longbeard originally had the name of **Tal-argan Longbeard**. In a penciled note [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) proposed that Buldar should be left out of the tale and Elmar be Hazad's wife.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>
