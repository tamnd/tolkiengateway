**Harad**, also known as the **South**, refers to the little-known lands in southern [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). The great region is located south of [Harondor](/wiki/Harondor) and [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). Very little is known of the [southern lands](/wiki/Dark_Land "Dark Land") beyond the great [Sea of Ringil](/wiki/Sea_of_Ringil).

## History {#History}

### Before the First Age {#Before_the_First_Age}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Lamp_of_the_Valar.jpg"><img src="/w/images/d/d3/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Lamp_of_the_Valar.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="298" height="450" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="298" data-file-height="450"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Ormal, The Lamp of the Valar&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

In the [primordial times](/wiki/Days_before_days "Days before days") of [Arda](/wiki/Arda), the southern land of Middle-earth was illuminated by one of the [two mighty lamps](/wiki/Two_Lamps "Two Lamps"), named [Ormal](/wiki/Ormal), atop a mighty pillar. Ormal was made by [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB), filled by [Varda](/wiki/Varda) and hallowed by [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) in the south of the world while the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) still dwelt on [Almaren](/wiki/Almaren). Both lamps were eventually destroyed by an assault by [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth"),<sup id="cite_ref-Rend_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rend-1">[1]</a></sup> and the destruction of Ormal created the southern Sea of Ringil.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

During the period in which Melkor bred monsters of diverse shapes and kinds that troubled [Endor](/wiki/Endor), his realm spread from [Utumno](/wiki/Utumno) over the southern lands of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

### First Age {#First_Age}

Some of the first [Men](/wiki/Men) leaving [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn), after the [awakening](/wiki/Awakening_of_Men "Awakening of Men") in [Hildórien](/wiki/Hild%C3%B3rien), eventually settled in Harad and became the ancestors of the [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Historians of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) believed that the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) came from lands south of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), that they turned north into [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) before they reached the coasts of Harad and were the first Men to cross the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

The coast south of Harad extended much farther west before the ruin of Beleriand.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

When the Men of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) began sailing east they explored the coasts of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), including the coast lands of Harad. The Númenóreans initially benefited the people in the lands they explored by teaching them many things about agriculture and craftsmanship.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> The Númenóreans later made settlements on the southern coasts, including the Havens of [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar).

In the 9th century of the Second Age, the great mariner [Aldarion](/wiki/Tar-Aldarion "Tar-Aldarion") sailed to the haven of [Vinyalondë](/wiki/Lond_Daer "Lond Daer") and then sailed along the coasts southwards, far beyond any place yet reached by the Númenóreans. On his return northwards he barely escaped being shipwrecked in the Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

After the reign of [Tar-Ciryatan](/wiki/Tar-Ciryatan), the Númenóreans began to set themselves up as lords in Middle-earth as they demanded tribute of goods and wealth, causing the oppression of the Haradrim. The Númenóreans expanded their control over Harad and Sauron did not at first dare to challenge them. But after the forging of the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power) and the emergence of the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl), Sauron began to attack the Númenórean settlements on the coast.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-9">[9]</a></sup>

After the forging of the Rings of Power almost all men east and south of the [Ered Luin](/wiki/Ered_Luin) were under [Sauron's](/wiki/Sauron "Sauron") rule. Sauron was both their king and their god. These men grew strong and built many towns and walls made from stone and they were fierce in war and armed with iron.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-12">[12]</a></sup>

Umbar was fortified in [2280](/wiki/Second_Age_2280 "Second Age 2280").<sup id="cite_ref-SA_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-13">[13]</a></sup>

[Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n), the king of Númenor, landed at [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar) in [3261](/wiki/Second_Age_3261 "Second Age 3261")<sup id="cite_ref-SA_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-13">[13]</a></sup> with a great fleet and the people on the coasts fled before them. Ar-Pharazôn and his host marched through Harad to meet Sauron's forces, but they fled from the Númenóreans and Sauron allowed himself to be taken to Númenor where he corrupted the King and his followers. Under Sauron's influence, the Númenóreans became even more ruthless to the locals of Harad, enslaving them and using them for sacrifices.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-9">[9]</a></sup>

During the stay of Sauron in Númenor many of the Númenóreans who sailed east from Númenor and made fortresses and dwellings upon the coasts of the southlands in Middle-earth were already bent to his will.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-12">[12]</a></sup>

The Númenóreans who lived in Harad survived the [drowning](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor") of Númenor in [3319](/wiki/Second_Age_3319 "Second Age 3319"). They were later called [Black Númenóreans](/wiki/Black_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) in Gondor,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> because they still served Sauron in Middle-earth after the drowning of Númenor. They fought with Sauron during the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance) against the Alliance of Elves and Men. Two of those númenórean lords, [Herumor](/wiki/Herumor) and [Fuinur](/wiki/Fuinur) rose to power among the Haradrim.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-12">[12]</a></sup>

### Third Age and later history {#Third_Age_and_later_history}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/db/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg/300px-L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="379" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/db/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg/450px-L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/db/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg/600px-L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Harad.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="1292"/></a><figcaption><i>Harad</i> by <a href="/wiki/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1" title="Lída Holubová">Lída Holubová</a></figcaption></figure>

The settlements of the Númenóreans beyond Umbar had been absorbed or had become hostile to Gondor and parts of Sauron's dominions because they were made by men that were already corrupted by Sauron in Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

King [Eärnil I](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnil_I) of Gondor laid siege by sea and land to Umbar and in [933](/wiki/Third_Age_933 "Third Age 933")<sup id="cite_ref-APTA_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APTA-17">[17]</a></sup> conquered Umbar, which became a great fortress of Gondor. Three years later in [936](/wiki/Third_Age_936 "Third Age 936")<sup id="cite_ref-APTA_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APTA-17">[17]</a></sup> Eärnil I was lost with many ships and men in a storm near Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

The Men of the Harad, led by the lords that had been driven from Umbar, came with great power against Umbar and laid siege to it. King [Ciryandil](/wiki/Ciryandil) fell in the siege of Umbar in [1015](/wiki/Third_Age_1015 "Third Age 1015").<sup id="cite_ref-APTA_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APTA-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

Umbar was under a siege for many years, but could not be taken because of the sea-power of Gondor. King [Ciryaher](/wiki/Ciryaher) of Gondor came from the north by sea and by land and crossed the river Harnen. His armies defeated the Men of the Harad and the kings of Harad were forced to acknowledge the overlordship of Gondor in [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050").<sup id="cite_ref-APTA_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APTA-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>

After the [Kin-strife](/wiki/Kin-strife), [Castamir](/wiki/Castamir)'s sons with others of their kin and with many people of the fleets sailed away from [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir) and seized Umbar in [1448](/wiki/Third_Age_1448 "Third Age 1448").<sup id="cite_ref-APTA_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APTA-17">[17]</a></sup> They made a refuge for all enemies of King <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Eldacar" title="Eldacar">Eldacar</a> and a Lordship independent of Gondor at Umbar. Umbar remained at war with Gondor for many lives of men and was a threat to the coastlands of Gondor and to all traffic on the sea. After Gondor lost Umbar to the rebels, Gondor's control over the Men of the Harad was loosened.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

King [Telumehtar](/wiki/Telumehtar_Umbardacil "Telumehtar Umbardacil") of Gondor conquered Umbar in [1810](/wiki/Third_Age_1810 "Third Age 1810"). During that war the last descendants of Castamir perished and Umbar was again held for a while by the kings of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-APTA_17-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APTA-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

After the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) the newly crowned king of the [Reunited Kingdom](/wiki/Reunited_Kingdom), [Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn"), made peace with the Men of Harad<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> and completely subdued Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Éomer_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Éomer-25">[25]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

Harad was located south of Mordor, with the [Mountains of Shadow](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath "Ephel Dúath") forming part of the northern border of Harad. The River Harnen flowed westward from the Mountains of Shadow to the [Bay of Belfalas](/wiki/Bay_of_Belfalas) and was Harad's border with South Gondor - a deserted region contested between [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) and Harad. The Harad Road ran from Harad northward through South Gondor and on to [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien).

To the north-east of Harad was [Khand](/wiki/Khand), a land that also had ties with Sauron. It is not known how far eastward or southward Harad stretched. On the west, Harad was bounded by the Bay of [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas) and the [Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer").

The northernmost part of Harad was called [Near Harad](/wiki/Near_Harad) and the southern part was [Far Harad](/wiki/Far_Harad). On the coast there was a natural harbour with a narrow cape curving around it. The coastal region around the harbour was known as Umbar, and the harbour was called the Havens of Umbar. The seafaring raiders known as the [Corsairs](/wiki/Corsairs_of_Umbar "Corsairs of Umbar") lived there. At the eastern end of the harbour was the City of the Corsairs.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> Somewhere in Harad was the inland city that [Berúthiel](/wiki/Ber%C3%BAthiel) came from.<sup id="cite_ref-Interview_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Interview-27">[27]</a></sup>

There were fortresses and dwellings upon the coasts in the south.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-12">[12]</a></sup> There were also fields<sup id="cite_ref-Éomer_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Éomer-25">[25]</a></sup> and "dark forests" with [apes](/wiki/Apes "Apes") in the south.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> In Harad lived also [camels](/wiki/Camels "Camels") and the gigantic [Oliphaunts](/wiki/Oliphaunts),<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Gate_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-30">[30]</a></sup> which were used by the Haradrim as moving war towers.

Harad was called the "[Sunlands](/wiki/Sunlands)" by the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"),<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-30">[30]</a></sup> suggesting the climate was warmer and sunnier. Even the constellations in Harad's night sky down in the southern hemisphere<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> were different from those in northern Middle-earth ([Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) had travelled far into Harad "where the stars are strange".<sup id="cite_ref-Council_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-32">[32]</a></sup>

The Men of Harad were also known as the *Haradrim* to the peoples of north-western Middle-earth. There were many tribes of the Haradrim often mutually hostile. The men of [Near Harad](/wiki/Near_Harad) were brown-skinned, with black hair and dark eyes,<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_30-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-30">[30]</a></sup> while the men of [Far Harad](/wiki/Far_Harad) were described as black-skinned with white eyes and red tongues like "[half-trolls](/wiki/Half-trolls "Half-trolls")".<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Harad* means "South" in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin).<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup> The word is derived from [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=KHYAR&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="KHYAR (page does not exist)">KHYAR</a>, and is cognate to [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *[hyarmen](/wiki/Hyarmen "Hyarmen")* ("south").<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup> It is also called [Haradwaith](/wiki/Haradwaith) which includes *[gwaith](/wiki/Gwaith "Gwaith")* ("people"). An early name of Harad was *(Sun)Harrowland*, which is derived from the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name of the Aethiopans, *Sigerhearwan*, and therefore meaning "Aethiopia" (See also: *[Sigelwara Land](/wiki/Sigelwara_Land)*).<sup id="cite_ref-Fangorn_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fangorn-36">[36]</a></sup> The [Men](/wiki/Men) of Harad were called [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim) ("Southern-host").<sup id="cite_ref-Council_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-32">[32]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Early variant names for Harad were *Harwan*, *Sunharrowland* and *Harrowland*.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup> In early drafts of *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)*, the portion of Harad directly south of Mordor was named the [Desert of Lostladen](/wiki/Desert_of_Lostladen), Lothlann, or Lothland.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup> In an early sketch of the chapter "[The Last Debate](/wiki/The_Last_Debate)", the region was called the Lothland desert,<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[39]</a></sup> to which [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) noted that the name *Lostladen* is likely related to the names *[Lothlann](/wiki/Lothlann)*/*Lothland* found in the [*Etymologies*](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies").<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[40]</a></sup>

In the earliest narrative of the legendarium in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, Tolkien mentioned some obscure descriptions of the lands of the South: [Melko](/wiki/Melko) raised the great tower [Helkar](/wiki/Helkar) to house the southern of the [Two Lamps](/wiki/Two_Lamps) fashioned by [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) to illuminate Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[41]</a></sup> In the original text, [Palisor](/wiki/Palisor), where the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) woke, is placed in the south of the world.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[42]</a></sup>

Tolkien mentioned the movement of Men, after the [War of Palisor](/wiki/Palisor "Palisor"), leading to the darkening of their skin.<sup id="cite_ref-Palisor_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palisor-43">[43]</a></sup> Additionally, it was revealed that [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) was unaware of where the beginning of Men should be, whether the north, south or east.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[44]</a></sup> After the death of [Nuin](/wiki/Nuin) at the bands of the Goblins through the treachery of Men, many kindreds of Men were driven to the southern forests.<sup id="cite_ref-Palisor_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palisor-43">[43]</a></sup> [Lindo](/wiki/Lindo) spoke of the "untrodden wilds" of the South.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[45]</a></sup> The southern lands, where the Sun burnt hot, had mighty tawny [cats](/wiki/Cats "Cats").<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[46]</a></sup> The southern lands also had a blue sky, whose color was surpassed by the eyes of [Eärendel](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel).<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[47]</a></sup>

Eärendel also travelled to the south. After the building of [Wingilot](/wiki/Wingilot), Eärendel searches for [Elwing](/wiki/Elwing) and is blown far to the South where he encounters [Wirilómë](/wiki/Wiril%C3%B3m%C3%AB) before escaping eastward.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[48]</a></sup> In his voyage wih [Voronwe](/wiki/Voronwe) on Wingilot to the south dark regions and fire mountains are mentioned.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[49]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[note 1]</a></sup> Additionally, In 1930, Tolkien wrote how Eärendil fought and slew [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant) in the South, which resulted in light coming to many places which had yet long been hid.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">[50]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In an interview from 1967, when asked what lay south of Harad, Tolkien replied "And south of Harad is Africa, the hot countries."<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[51]</a></sup> Given the descriptions of the inhabitants of Middle-earth's southern lands,<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_30-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-30">[30]</a></sup> the early etymologies of their name,<sup id="cite_ref-Fangorn_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fangorn-36">[36]</a></sup> and the overall shape of the southern lands as shown in the [Ambarkanta maps](/wiki/Ambarkanta_maps),<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">[52]</a></sup> it is likely that Harad may have been based on the lands of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa" title="wikipedia:North Africa">northern</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa" title="wikipedia:Northeast Africa">northeastern Africa</a> and perhaps the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East" title="wikipedia:Middle East">Middle East</a> in our world. In addition, apart from the peninsula and firth of Umbar, the shape of the coastline of Harad south of the mouth of the river Harnen on the [General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth) is similar to the shape of the coastline of the northwestern coast of Africa south of the city of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier" title="wikipedia:Tangier">Tangier</a>.
