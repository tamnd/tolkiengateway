The **Heath of the Sky-roof** was an upland heath located about a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_(unit)" title="wikipedia:League (unit)">league</a> from the town of [Tavrobel](../T/Tavrobel.md) in [Tol Eressëa](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md), according to the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*.

It was the location of a [great battle](../B/Battle_of_the_Heath_of_the_Sky-roof.md) during the [Faring Forth](../F/Faring_Forth.md), after which it was called the <i><b>Withered Heath</b></i>, presumably due to the destruction that has taken place during the battle.<sup id="cite_ref-Sky_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sky-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The place was also called the <i><b>High Heath</b></i>, and the <i><b>Heaven Roof</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-High_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-High-2">[2]</a></sup>

Its [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) name was <i><b>Ladwen-na-Dhaideloth</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-High_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-High-2">[2]</a></sup> glossed as the "Heath of the Sky-roof", consisting of the elements <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ladwen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ladwen (page does not exist)">ladwen</a></i> ("levelness, flatness; plain, heath; plane; surface") + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Na_(Gnomish_word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Na (Gnomish word) (page does not exist)">na</a></i> ("of the") + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Dai&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Dai (page does not exist)">dai</a></i> ("sky") + <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Teloth&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Teloth (page does not exist)">teloth</a></i> ("roofing, canopy, shelter").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> An alternative form of the name was <i><b>Ladwen Daideloth</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-High_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-High-2">[2]</a></sup>

Another name for the place was <i><b>Dor-na-Dhaideloth</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-High_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-High-2">[2]</a></sup> meaning "Land of Heaven Roof", the first element of the name being *[dôr](../D/Dôr.md)* ("land, country").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

G. L. Elkin, the Acting Director of the Shugborough Estate, has suggested to [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) that the Heath of the Sky-roof might have been inspired by Hopton Heath, where in 1643, a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hopton_Heath" title="wikipedia:Battle of Hopton Heath">battle</a> of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_English_Civil_War" title="wikipedia:First English Civil War">Civil War</a> was fought.

It was located a few miles away from <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Haywood" title="wikipedia:Great Haywood">Great Haywood</a> which served as an inspiration for Tavrobel.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Alternatively, according to [John Garth](../J/John_Garth.md), it might have been inspired by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase" title="wikipedia:Cannock Chase">Cannock Chase</a>, a small area of countryside in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire" title="wikipedia:Staffordshire">Staffordshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
