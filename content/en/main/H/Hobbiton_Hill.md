**Hobbiton Hill**,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> usually called simply **The Hill**,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> or the hill of Hobbiton<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> stood to the north of the town of [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) was tunneled into this hill by [Bungo Baggins](/wiki/Bungo_Baggins).<sup id="cite_ref-party_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-party-5">[5]</a></sup>

The earth removed in excavating Bag End was *shot* over the edge of the sudden fall in the hillside onto the ground forming the [Bagshot Row](/wiki/Bagshot_Row); it became the gardens and earthwalls of the other dwellings.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

A path outside Bag End led to a gate and towards the [Hill Road](/wiki/Hill_Road). The garden-path led to the bottom of the western side of the Hill where was a hedge; beyond it is a meadow, and a gate opening into a narrow lane.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

After [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) returned from the [Quest for Erebor](/wiki/Quest_for_Erebor), the younger [hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") believed that the Hill was full of secret tunnels filled with his legendary treasure, to explain his wealth.<sup id="cite_ref-party_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-party-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The name **Hill of Hobbiton** is used in a rewrite of the first chapter of The Hobbit, which was never incorporated in a published version of The Hobbit.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
