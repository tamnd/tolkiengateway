**Hebrew** is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages" title="wikipedia:Semitic languages">Semitic language</a>. The Old Testament, the first half of the Bible, was originally written in Hebrew.

While working on *[The Jerusalem Bible](/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Bible)* as a translator in the 1960s, Tolkien prepared himself learning a great amount of Hebrew.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> He explained to his grandson [Michael George](/wiki/Michael_George_Tolkien "Michael George Tolkien"): "I am at present immersed in Hebrew. If you want a beautiful but idiotic alphabet, and a language so difficult that it makes [Latin](/wiki/Latin) (or even Greek) seem footling but also glimpses into a past that makes Homer seem recent - that is the stuff! (I am hoping when I retire to get included in a new Bible-translation team that is brewing. I have passed the test: with a version of the Book of Jonah. Not from Hebrew direct!)".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Among the [books in his library](/wiki/List_of_books_in_Tolkien%27s_library "List of books in Tolkien's library"), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) had some Semitic-related publications, including *The Babylonian Story of the Deluge and the Epic of Gilgamish* and *Babylonisch-Assyrische Grammatik mit Übungsbuch*. Babylonian and Assyrian were classical Semitic languages, distantly related to Hebrew.

<h2><span id="In_Tolkien.27s_works"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Tolkien&#39;s_works">In Tolkien&#39;s works</span></h2>

In his [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Tolkien compared his [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) to the history of the Jewish people,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and despite their [Norse](/wiki/Norse) connections (using [Germanic](/wiki/Germanic) names and [Runes](/wiki/Runes)) their original language, [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul), displays Hebrew-like structure.

The same happens with the unrelated [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) language.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Hebrew script is an impure abjad, that uses letters for consonants whereas signs placed above or under them signify vowels. The [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) script is a pure abjad.
