**Hebrew** is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages" title="wikipedia:Semitic languages">Semitic language</a>. The Old Testament, the first half of the Bible, was originally written in Hebrew.

While working on *[The Jerusalem Bible](../T/The_Jerusalem_Bible.md)* as a translator in the 1960s, Tolkien prepared himself learning a great amount of Hebrew.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> He explained to his grandson [Michael George](../M/Michael_George_Tolkien.md): "I am at present immersed in Hebrew. If you want a beautiful but idiotic alphabet, and a language so difficult that it makes [Latin](../L/Latin.md) (or even Greek) seem footling but also glimpses into a past that makes Homer seem recent - that is the stuff! (I am hoping when I retire to get included in a new Bible-translation team that is brewing. I have passed the test: with a version of the Book of Jonah. Not from Hebrew direct!)".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Among the [books in his library](../L/List_of_books_in_Tolkien's_library.md), [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) had some Semitic-related publications, including *The Babylonian Story of the Deluge and the Epic of Gilgamish* and *Babylonisch-Assyrische Grammatik mit Übungsbuch*. Babylonian and Assyrian were classical Semitic languages, distantly related to Hebrew.

<h2><span id="In_Tolkien.27s_works"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Tolkien&#39;s_works">In Tolkien&#39;s works</span></h2>

In his [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), Tolkien compared his [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) to the history of the Jewish people,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and despite their [Norse](../N/Norse.md) connections (using [Germanic](../G/Germanic.md) names and [Runes](../R/Runes.md)) their original language, [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md), displays Hebrew-like structure.

The same happens with the unrelated [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) language.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Hebrew script is an impure abjad, that uses letters for consonants whereas signs placed above or under them signify vowels. The [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) script is a pure abjad.
