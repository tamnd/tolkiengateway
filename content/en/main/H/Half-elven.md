**Half-elven** ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) singular **Peredhel**, plural **Peredhil**), are the children of the [Union of Elves and Men](/wiki/Unions_of_Elves_and_Men "Unions of Elves and Men"). Half-elven are not a distinct race *per se*; rather, they were fertile offspring as the result of a union between [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and [Men](/wiki/Men). There are four recorded unions of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) with the [Edain](/wiki/Edain).

In the late [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) married [Beren](/wiki/Beren) and [Idril](/wiki/Idril) married [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-2">[2]</a></sup> These two lines of Peredhil were then joined by the marriage of [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) and [Elwing](/wiki/Elwing). Eärendil and Elwing were the first to receive the choice of fates which passed to their line. Their sons, [Elros](/wiki/Elros) and [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond), chose different fates and the line was sundered until the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).

In the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), when [Arwen Undómiel](/wiki/Arwen "Arwen") wed [Aragorn II Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn"),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> a distant descendant of Elros Tar-Minyatur, it united the long-sundered lines of those descended from both Elves and Men. There is also mention of an Elven bloodline in Prince [Imrahil](/wiki/Imrahil)<sup id="cite_ref-Debate_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Debate-4">[4]</a></sup> via the old tale of [Mithrellas](/wiki/Mithrellas) and [Imrazôr](/wiki/Imraz%C3%B4r).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:220–2</sup>

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/f/f3/Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg/225px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="225" height="329" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/f/f3/Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg/338px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/f/f3/Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg/450px-Jenny_Dolfen_-_Descendants_of_Thingol.jpg 2x" data-file-width="657" data-file-height="960"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Jenny_Dolfen" title="Jenny Dolfen">Jenny Dolfen</a> - <i>Descendants of Thingol</i></figcaption></figure>

The first bond between Elves and Men was the marriage of [Beren Erchamion](/wiki/Beren_Erchamion) and [Lúthien Tinúviel](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien_Tin%C3%BAviel), from which a single son, [Dior Eluchíl](/wiki/Dior_Eluch%C3%ADl), was born. Dior wed [Nimloth of Doriath](/wiki/Nimloth_of_Doriath), and they had three children: [twins](/wiki/Twins "Twins") [Eluréd](/wiki/Elur%C3%A9d) and [Elurín](/wiki/Elur%C3%ADn), and [Elwing the White](/wiki/Elwing "Elwing"). Dior wore the [Nauglamir](/wiki/Nauglamir) with the [Silmaril](/wiki/Silmaril), passed to him from his mother, thus awakening the oath of the [Fëanorians](/wiki/F%C3%ABanorians) and the [Second Kinslaying](/wiki/Second_Kinslaying).

The next bond came about through the union of [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor) son of [Huor](/wiki/Huor) and [Idril Celebrindal](/wiki/Idril_Celebrindal), from which another son, [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil), was born. While Eärendil was truly half-elven, his Adan father, Tuor, had been granted the fate of the elder race.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-2">[2]</a></sup> Eärendil and Elwing begat another pair of twin sons: [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) and [Elros](/wiki/Elros). Of the half-elves, Eärendil and Elwing were specifically given a choice in their fates when they journeyed to Valinor with the Silmaril to beg pardon for the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) and plead the plight of Elves and Men to the Valar. Only after that event was the choice passed to their children, to be counted as one of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) (Elves), thus being immortal, or one of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) (Men), thus being mortal.<sup id="cite_ref-Voyage_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Voyage-7">[7]</a></sup> Elrond Half-elven chose the fate of Elven-kind, but Elros chose the [Gift of Men](/wiki/Gift_of_Men), and none of [his descendants](/wiki/House_of_Elros "House of Elros") bore the title of "Half-Elven".

Elrond wed [Celebrían](/wiki/Celebr%C3%ADan), daughter of [Celeborn](/wiki/Celeborn) and [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel), and bore three children: twin sons [Elladan](/wiki/Elladan) and [Elrohir](/wiki/Elrohir), and daughter [Arwen Undómiel](/wiki/Arwen_Und%C3%B3miel). Aragorn and Arwen were parents to a son, [Eldarion](/wiki/Eldarion), as well as at least two daughters, thus leaving a remnant of the proud Elven lineage even after the Firstborn had long passed over the sea and out of time and memory. Though these children were long-lived, like Númenóreans had been and Eldarion ruled for at least 100 years,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> they were mortal.

There is no evidence that any other mixed bloodline shared in the choice of fate as Earendil's and Elwing's. The children of [Mithrellas](/wiki/Mithrellas), [Galador](/wiki/Galador) and [Gilmith](/wiki/Gilmith), were enobled but mortal like their father, as were their descendants.<sup id="cite_ref-Debate_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Debate-4">[4]</a></sup>

### The half-elven lines {#The_half-elven_lines}

Those sharing a divine heritage and didn't make a choice are bold faced; those who chose to be counted as an Elda rather than an Adan are italicized; those who were counted among the Edain (or who had not made the choice either way) are not. Their actual mathematical descent from elves is in a fraction in parentheses.

The important members of the half-elven lines were:

- **[Dior](/wiki/Dior)**, son of Man [Beren](/wiki/Beren) and Elf [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien), grandson of the [Maia](/wiki/Maia) [Melian](/wiki/Melian). (1/4)

<dl><dd>Dior and his descendants were not only half-elven, but also descendants of the divine bloodline of <a href="/wiki/Melian" title="Melian">Melian</a>; Dior was slain in his 30s, far below the lifespan of a mortal, therefore his intended nature, <a href="/wiki/Elven_life_cycle" title="Elven life cycle">longevity</a> and <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Mortality" title="Mortality">mortality</a> are not known.</dd></dl>

- *[Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil)*, son of the Man [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor) and Elf [Idril](/wiki/Idril) (1/2)

<dl><dd>Eärendil would rather have chosen the kindred of Men, but he chose the Elves for his wife Elwing&#39;s sake (she also chose the Elves). His fate was special; although he was not allowed to stay in <a href="/wiki/Valinor" title="Valinor">Valinor</a> with the Elves, he had to sail the heavens in his ship <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Vingil%C3%B3t%C3%AB" title="Vingilótë">Vingilótë</a>, wearing the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Silmaril" title="Silmaril">Silmaril</a> of Beren and Lúthien on his brow, <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Star_of_Earendil" title="Star of Earendil">shining brightly</a> as a sign of hope for <a href="/wiki/Middle-earth" title="Middle-earth">Middle-earth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Voyage_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Voyage-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

- *[Elwing](/wiki/Elwing)*, daughter of Dior and [Nimloth](/wiki/Nimloth_of_Doriath "Nimloth of Doriath"). (5/8)

<dl><dd>Elwing&#39;s family was slain or lost, with only Elwing surviving, given her choice of kindred and choosing the Eldar. As Eärendil sailed the heavens, Elwing resided in a white tower built northward on the borders of the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Sundering_Seas" title="Sundering Seas">Sundering Seas</a> and met him upon his return.<sup id="cite_ref-Voyage_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Voyage-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

- **[Eluréd](/wiki/Elur%C3%A9d)** and **[Elurín](/wiki/Elur%C3%ADn)**, sons of Dior and Nimloth. (5/8)

<dl><dd>While Dior was slain, Eluréd and Elurín were abandoned in the woods outside Doriath by the vengeful Noldor and were never found again, disappearing from history in a very young age.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> As with their father, their eventual nature remains unknown.</dd></dl>

- *[Elrond](/wiki/Elrond)*, son of Eärendil and Elwing. (9/16)

<dl><dd>His heirs would also have the free choice of kindred.</dd></dl>

- [Elros](/wiki/Elros), also son of Eärendil and Elwing. (9/16)

<dl><dd>The heirs of Elros were not given the choice of the children of Elrond; but their lifespan was multiple of that of normal Men. In later times, the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Kings_of_N%C3%BAmenor" title="Kings of Númenor">Kings of Númenor</a> regretted their forefather&#39;s choice, and this led to the <a href="/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor" title="Downfall of Númenor">downfall of Númenor</a>.</dd></dl>

- [Arwen](/wiki/Arwen), daughter of Elrond and [Celebrían](/wiki/Celebr%C3%ADan). (25/32)

<dl><dd>She could choose her kindred like her father; however unlike him, after living for millennia as an Elf, she ultimately chose the fate of Men and married <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a>, distant heir of Elros. Their son <a href="/wiki/Eldarion" title="Eldarion">Eldarion</a> and their daughters were not counted as Half-elven, but rather as <a href="/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain" title="Dúnedain">Dúnedain</a> restored, as the two Half-elven lines of Earendil were merged in the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Age" title="Fourth Age">Fourth Age</a>.</dd></dl>

- **[Elladan](/wiki/Elladan)** and **[Elrohir](/wiki/Elrohir)**, sons of Elrond and Celebrían. (25/32)

<dl><dd>Like their sister they &#34;shall live with the youth of the Eldar&#34; as immortals; until they made their choice, which they were bound to make only after their father took the <a href="/wiki/White_Ship" title="White Ship">White Ship</a> out from Middle-earth. Their choice(s) and fate(s) is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup></dd></dl>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In an older version of the *Quenta Silmarillion*, Manwë spoke this judgment to Eärendil:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>To Eärendel I remit the ban, and the peril that he took upon himself out of love for the Two Kindreds shall not fall on him; neither shall it fall upon Elwing who entered into peril for love of Eärendel: save only in this: they shall not ever walk again among Elves or Men in the Outer Lands. Now all those who have the blood of mortal Men, in whatever part, great or small, are mortal, unless other doom be granted to them; but in this matter the power of doom is given to me.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="/wiki/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings" title="The Lost Road and Other Writings">The Lost Road and Other Writings</a></i>, Part Two: Valinor and Middle-earth before The Lord of the Rings, Quenta Silmarillion</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) interprets it as that Dior would inherit his father's mortality, irrespective of his mother's nature and choice.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-elves" title="wikipedia:Half-elves">Half-elves</a> appear in [Norse](/wiki/Norse) mythology, the offspring of a man and an <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfar" title="wikipedia:Alfar">Alfr</a>; believed to be particularly beautiful and with magic powers.
