**Highday** (archaic form *Hihdei*) was the seventh and last day of the week in the [Shire Calendar](../S/Shire_Calendar.md). It represented the [Númenórean](../N/Númenórean.md) day *[Orbelain](../O/Orbelain.md)*, dedicated to the [Valar](../V/Valar.md). As such it was the chief day of the week and after noon [Hobbits](Hobbits.md) considered it a holiday with evening feasts.<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the reform of [Isengrim Took II](../I/Isengrim_Took_II.md),<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup> dates such as 1 [Lithe](../L/Lithe.md) and 1 [Yule](../Y/Yule.md) always fell on Highday. There was no month beginning with Highday, therefore it became a jesting idiom in the [Shire](../S/Shire.md) to say "on Friday the first (of [Summerfilth](../S/Summerfilth.md))" when referring to a fictitious day or unlikely events.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Highday* represents an archaic [Hobbitish](Hobbitish.md) form "Hihdei", a word preserved in the [Yellowskin](../Y/Yellowskin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup>

*Highday* is translated as Friday in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, however as it was the chief day of the week, its cultural significance rather corresponded to that of our Sunday. For this reason, when the poem *[The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late](../T/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Stayed_Up_Too_Late.md)* mentions a special pair of silver spoons and plates reserved for "Sunday", it should rather be understood as Highday, rather than [Sunday](../S/Sunday.md).<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
