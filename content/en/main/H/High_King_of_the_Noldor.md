The **High King of the Noldor** was the head of the [House of Finwë](House_of_Finwë.md) in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md). Its bearer was the nominal overlord of all the [Noldorin](../N/Noldor.md) realms, but had little practical authority due to the disunity of the Noldor. The [House of Fëanor](House_of_Fëanor.md) acknowledged the Kingship but paid it little heed, following their own agenda under the general leadership of [Maedhros](../M/Maedhros.md).

The founder of the [House of Finwë](House_of_Finwë.md) and first [King of the Noldor](../K/King_of_the_Noldor.md) was [Finwë](../F/Finwë.md), the lord of the [Tatyar](../T/Tatyar.md) who led his people in the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md) from [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md) into [the West](../A/Aman.md) to dwell in [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md). He was slain by [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md) at [Formenos](../F/Formenos.md).

His eldest son [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) succeeded to the kingship and led the [Rebellion of the Noldor](../R/Rebellion_of_the_Noldor.md) to Middle-earth to avenge his father's death and recover the [Silmarils](../S/Silmarils.md) from Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Fëanor died before establishing a realm, and the claim to the kingship passed to his eldest son [Maedhros](../M/Maedhros.md), who was captured by Morgoth.

[Fingon](../F/Fingon.md), son of [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md), rescued Maedhros from his captivity in a daring raid on [Thangorodrim](../T/Thangorodrim.md). Out of gratitude to Fingon, and regret that Fëanor had abandoned the other Noldor in [Araman](../A/Araman.md), Maedhros surrendered his house's claim to the kingship to Fingolfin. At that time, Maedhros said: "<i>If there lay no grievance between us, lord, still the kingship would rightly come to you, <b>the eldest</b> here <b>of the house of Finwë</b>, and not the least wise.</i>" Thus the kingship passed from the [House of Fëanor](House_of_Fëanor.md) to the [House of Fingolfin](House_of_Fingolfin.md), fulfilling the [Doom of Mandos](../D/Doom_of_Mandos.md) that the [Sons of Fëanor](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md) would become the *Dispossessed*, and the two Houses made peace. However, not all of the seven Sons of Fëanor accepted this in their hearts.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

**[Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md)** became the first High King of the Noldor, ruling from [Hithlum](Hithlum.md) as the "King of the North", mostly during the [Siege of Angband](../S/Siege_of_Angband.md). When Morgoth broke the siege in the [Dagor Bragollach](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md) and slew many of the Noldor, Fingolfin rode in great wrath to the gates of [Angband](../A/Angband.md) and was slain in single combat with Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruin-3">[3]</a></sup>

Fingolfin was succeeded by his eldest son [Fingon](../F/Fingon.md), whose short reign was consumed by endless war. He and Maedhros formed an [alliance](../U/Union_of_Maedhros.md) that brought together all the realms of the Noldor to make a [final assault](../N/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad.md) on Morgoth. The attempt ended in the slaughter of the Noldor and Fingon's death at the hands of [Gothmog](<../G/Gothmog_(balrog).md>), Lord of Balrogs.<sup id="cite_ref-Fifth_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fifth-4">[4]</a></sup>

Fingon's brother [Turgon](../T/Turgon.md) succeeded him, but he was High King in name only, as it was not possible for the "Hidden King" to rule the other Noldor from his [Hidden City](../G/Gondolin.md), the location of which was unknown even to his own kin. When [Maeglin](../M/Maeglin.md) betrayed this secret to Morgoth, the city swiftly [fell](../F/Fall_of_Gondolin.md) and Turgon with it.

Gondolin was the last of the Noldorin realms in Beleriand and Turgon was the last son of the [House of Fingolfin](House_of_Fingolfin.md). With his death, the Kingship passed to the [House of Finarfin](House_of_Finarfin.md), which was by this time reduced to a single dynast: [Ereinion Gil-galad](../G/Gil-galad.md), the young son of [Orodreth](../O/Orodreth.md). Gil-galad had been sent to take refuge with [Círdan](../C/Círdan.md) after the fall of [Minas Tirith](<../M/Minas_Tirith_(Beleriand).md>) in the aftermath of the Dagor Bragollach, and thus survived the ruin of the Noldor. Gil-galad was acclaimed High King when news of Turgon's death reached the [Isle of Balar](../I/Isle_of_Balar.md), to which he and Círdan had fled from the fall of the [Falas](../F/Falas.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Gil-galad lived to see the end of the [War of the Jewels](../B/Battles_of_Beleriand.md) and held the Kingship throughout the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md), far longer than any of his predecessors.

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

After the cataclysmic [War of Wrath](../W/War_of_Wrath.md) that ended the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) and destroyed Beleriand, the surviving [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md) who did not depart for the [Undying Lands](../U/Undying_Lands.md) established the realms of [Lindon](../L/Lindon.md) and later [Eregion](../E/Eregion.md) in northwestern Middle-earth. In Lindon, Gil-galad was acknowledged as the "High King of the Elves of the West".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> He was the chief enemy of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) in the [Dark Years](../D/Dark_Years.md), at the end of which he appointed Elrond as his vice-regent in [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

After the [Downfall of Númenor](../D/Downfall_of_Númenor.md), Gil-galad and [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) formed the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](../L/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men.md) to destroy Sauron. In the ensuing [war](../W/War_of_the_Last_Alliance.md), Gil-galad commanded the Elven forces, save for the host of [Silvan Elves](../S/Silvan_Elves.md) under [Amdír](../A/Amdír.md) and [Oropher](../O/Oropher.md). After the Last Alliance broke into Mordor and [besieged Barad-dûr](../S/Siege_of_Barad-dûr.md) for seven years, Sauron personally came forth and fought Gil-galad and Elendil. The battle ended in the deaths of all three, but Sauron's spirit and malice endured through the power of his [One Ring](../O/One_Ring.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Legacy {#Legacy}

Gil-galad was the last High King of the Noldor, for he fathered no children. With his death, the [House of Finwë](House_of_Finwë.md) was extinguished in the male line in Middle-earth. The only living descendants of Finwë remaining in Middle-earth at the dawn of the Third Age were [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md), [Celebrían](../C/Celebrían.md), [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md), and the descendants of [Elros](../E/Elros.md), who were counted among the race of Men.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the published version of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*, Gil-galad is said to be the son of Fingon.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruin-3">[3]</a></sup> However, in some of Tolkien's later notes, Gil-galad is said to be the son of Orodreth and thus a junior member of the [House of Finarfin](House_of_Finarfin.md),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> and Turgon's closest living relative in the male line (excluding the dispossessed House of Fëanor). Turgon's claim to the title of High King would be [problematic](../G/Gil-galad.md#Parentage) if Gil-galad were descended from Fingon, but the issue is resolved through descent from Orodreth, which may explain the change.
