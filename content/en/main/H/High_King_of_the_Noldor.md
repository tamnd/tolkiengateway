The **High King of the Noldor** was the head of the [House of Finwë](/wiki/House_of_Finw%C3%AB) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). Its bearer was the nominal overlord of all the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldor "Noldor") realms, but had little practical authority due to the disunity of the Noldor. The [House of Fëanor](/wiki/House_of_F%C3%ABanor) acknowledged the Kingship but paid it little heed, following their own agenda under the general leadership of [Maedhros](/wiki/Maedhros).

The founder of the [House of Finwë](/wiki/House_of_Finw%C3%AB) and first [King of the Noldor](/wiki/King_of_the_Noldor) was [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB), the lord of the [Tatyar](/wiki/Tatyar) who led his people in the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey) from [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen) into [the West](/wiki/Aman "Aman") to dwell in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor). He was slain by [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) at [Formenos](/wiki/Formenos).

His eldest son [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) succeeded to the kingship and led the [Rebellion of the Noldor](/wiki/Rebellion_of_the_Noldor) to Middle-earth to avenge his father's death and recover the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils) from Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Fëanor died before establishing a realm, and the claim to the kingship passed to his eldest son [Maedhros](/wiki/Maedhros), who was captured by Morgoth.

[Fingon](/wiki/Fingon), son of [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin), rescued Maedhros from his captivity in a daring raid on [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim). Out of gratitude to Fingon, and regret that Fëanor had abandoned the other Noldor in [Araman](/wiki/Araman), Maedhros surrendered his house's claim to the kingship to Fingolfin. At that time, Maedhros said: "<i>If there lay no grievance between us, lord, still the kingship would rightly come to you, <b>the eldest</b> here <b>of the house of Finwë</b>, and not the least wise.</i>" Thus the kingship passed from the [House of Fëanor](/wiki/House_of_F%C3%ABanor) to the [House of Fingolfin](/wiki/House_of_Fingolfin), fulfilling the [Doom of Mandos](/wiki/Doom_of_Mandos) that the [Sons of Fëanor](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor) would become the *Dispossessed*, and the two Houses made peace. However, not all of the seven Sons of Fëanor accepted this in their hearts.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

**[Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin)** became the first High King of the Noldor, ruling from [Hithlum](/wiki/Hithlum) as the "King of the North", mostly during the [Siege of Angband](/wiki/Siege_of_Angband). When Morgoth broke the siege in the [Dagor Bragollach](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach) and slew many of the Noldor, Fingolfin rode in great wrath to the gates of [Angband](/wiki/Angband) and was slain in single combat with Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruin-3">[3]</a></sup>

Fingolfin was succeeded by his eldest son [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon), whose short reign was consumed by endless war. He and Maedhros formed an [alliance](/wiki/Union_of_Maedhros "Union of Maedhros") that brought together all the realms of the Noldor to make a [final assault](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad "Nirnaeth Arnoediad") on Morgoth. The attempt ended in the slaughter of the Noldor and Fingon's death at the hands of [Gothmog](</wiki/Gothmog_(balrog)> "Gothmog (balrog)"), Lord of Balrogs.<sup id="cite_ref-Fifth_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fifth-4">[4]</a></sup>

Fingon's brother [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) succeeded him, but he was High King in name only, as it was not possible for the "Hidden King" to rule the other Noldor from his [Hidden City](/wiki/Gondolin "Gondolin"), the location of which was unknown even to his own kin. When [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin) betrayed this secret to Morgoth, the city swiftly [fell](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin") and Turgon with it.

Gondolin was the last of the Noldorin realms in Beleriand and Turgon was the last son of the [House of Fingolfin](/wiki/House_of_Fingolfin). With his death, the Kingship passed to the [House of Finarfin](/wiki/House_of_Finarfin), which was by this time reduced to a single dynast: [Ereinion Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad "Gil-galad"), the young son of [Orodreth](/wiki/Orodreth). Gil-galad had been sent to take refuge with [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan) after the fall of [Minas Tirith](</wiki/Minas_Tirith_(Beleriand)> "Minas Tirith (Beleriand)") in the aftermath of the Dagor Bragollach, and thus survived the ruin of the Noldor. Gil-galad was acclaimed High King when news of Turgon's death reached the [Isle of Balar](/wiki/Isle_of_Balar), to which he and Círdan had fled from the fall of the [Falas](/wiki/Falas).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Gil-galad lived to see the end of the [War of the Jewels](/wiki/Battles_of_Beleriand "Battles of Beleriand") and held the Kingship throughout the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), far longer than any of his predecessors.

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

After the cataclysmic [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) that ended the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) and destroyed Beleriand, the surviving [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) who did not depart for the [Undying Lands](/wiki/Undying_Lands) established the realms of [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) and later [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion) in northwestern Middle-earth. In Lindon, Gil-galad was acknowledged as the "High King of the Elves of the West".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> He was the chief enemy of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) in the [Dark Years](/wiki/Dark_Years), at the end of which he appointed Elrond as his vice-regent in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

After the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor), Gil-galad and [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) formed the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) to destroy Sauron. In the ensuing [war](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance "War of the Last Alliance"), Gil-galad commanded the Elven forces, save for the host of [Silvan Elves](/wiki/Silvan_Elves) under [Amdír](/wiki/Amd%C3%ADr) and [Oropher](/wiki/Oropher). After the Last Alliance broke into Mordor and [besieged Barad-dûr](/wiki/Siege_of_Barad-d%C3%BBr "Siege of Barad-dûr") for seven years, Sauron personally came forth and fought Gil-galad and Elendil. The battle ended in the deaths of all three, but Sauron's spirit and malice endured through the power of his [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Legacy {#Legacy}

Gil-galad was the last High King of the Noldor, for he fathered no children. With his death, the [House of Finwë](/wiki/House_of_Finw%C3%AB) was extinguished in the male line in Middle-earth. The only living descendants of Finwë remaining in Middle-earth at the dawn of the Third Age were [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel), [Celebrían](/wiki/Celebr%C3%ADan), [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond), and the descendants of [Elros](/wiki/Elros), who were counted among the race of Men.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the published version of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, Gil-galad is said to be the son of Fingon.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruin-3">[3]</a></sup> However, in some of Tolkien's later notes, Gil-galad is said to be the son of Orodreth and thus a junior member of the [House of Finarfin](/wiki/House_of_Finarfin),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> and Turgon's closest living relative in the male line (excluding the dispossessed House of Fëanor). Turgon's claim to the title of High King would be [problematic](/wiki/Gil-galad#Parentage "Gil-galad") if Gil-galad were descended from Fingon, but the issue is resolved through descent from Orodreth, which may explain the change.
