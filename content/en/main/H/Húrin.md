**Húrin Thalion** ("the Steadfast", "the Strong"),<sup id="cite_ref-CH3_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH3-3">[3]</a></sup> was the last [Lord of Dor-lómin](/wiki/Lord_of_Dor-l%C3%B3min) and one of the great heroes of [Men](/wiki/Edain "Edain") during the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age). He was the eldest son of [Galdor](</wiki/Galdor_(Lord_of_Dor-l%C3%B3min)> "Galdor (Lord of Dor-lómin)") and [Hareth](/wiki/Hareth), and the older brother of [Huor](/wiki/Huor). He was the father of [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin), [Urwen](/wiki/Urwen) and [Nienor](/wiki/Nienor).

Húrin was regarded as "the mightiest of warriors of mortal Men",<sup id="cite_ref-TS22_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS22-4">[4]</a></sup> but also known for being the titular character of the *[Narn i Chîn Húrin](</wiki/Narn_i_Ch%C3%AEn_H%C3%BArin_(tale)> "Narn i Chîn Húrin (tale)")*, "the Tale of the Children of Húrin". In the tale he was captured by [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) at the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad) and the Dark Lord attempted to convince him to reveal the secret location of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin). He refused, and in response the Dark Lord cursed his kin and imprisoned him on [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim). He was forced to watch the curse befall his children.<sup id="cite_ref-CH3_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH3-3">[3]</a></sup> The curse led to some of the greatest successes and tragedies of the First Age.

## History {#History}

### Early years {#Early_years}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/4b/Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg/300px-Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/4b/Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg/450px-Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/4b/Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg/600px-Mysilvergreen_-_Hurin_and_Huor_are_landing_in_Gondolin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="853"/></a><figcaption><i>Hurin and Huor are landing in Gondolin</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Mysilvergreen" title="Category:Images by Mysilvergreen">Mysilvergreen</a></figcaption></figure>

Húrin was born in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [441](/wiki/First_Age_441 "First Age 441") in [Hithlum](/wiki/Hithlum).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> His father was [Galdor](</wiki/Galdor_(Lord_of_Dor-l%C3%B3min)> "Galdor (Lord of Dor-lómin)") and his grandfather [Hador Lórindol](/wiki/Hador "Hador"), founder of the [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> His mother was [Hareth](/wiki/Hareth) of [Brethil](/wiki/Brethil) who was the daughter of [Halmir](/wiki/Halmir) the lord of the [Haladin](/wiki/Haladin).<sup id="cite_ref-TS18_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS18-7">[7]</a></sup> In his youth, him and his younger brother [Huor](/wiki/Huor) were raised in the forest of Brethil by [Haldir](</wiki/Haldir_(Chieftain_of_the_Haladin)> "Haldir (Chieftain of the Haladin)") their uncle. In [458](/wiki/First_Age_458 "First Age 458"),<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> the brothers joined a company of scouts but were separated from them after an [Orc](/wiki/Orc) ambush. They were pursued to the ford of [Brithiach](/wiki/Brithiach) and were saved by the Vala [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo) who caused a mist to rise and the two escaped into [Dimbar](/wiki/Dimbar). From there, [Eagles](/wiki/Eagles) flew them to the hidden city [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin). King [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) welcomed the brothers, remembering their grandfather Hador who was an [Elf-friend](/wiki/Elf-friend) as well as the words of Ulmo who told him to deal kindly to the House of Hador. Húrin remained with Turgon for a time until he and his brother wished to return to their people. Turgon was reluctant to let the brothers leave but the king granted their wish and the brothers swore oaths of secrecy.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> They returned to Dor-lómin sometime before [462](/wiki/First_Age_462 "First Age 462").

### Lord of Dor-lómin {#Lord_of_Dor-lómin}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Steamey_-_Hurin.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/1/19/Steamey_-_Hurin.jpg/300px-Steamey_-_Hurin.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="424" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/1/19/Steamey_-_Hurin.jpg/450px-Steamey_-_Hurin.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/1/19/Steamey_-_Hurin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="579" data-file-height="819"/></a><figcaption><i>Húrin</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Steamey" title="Category:Images by Steamey">Steamey</a></figcaption></figure>

In [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [462](/wiki/First_Age_462 "First Age 462"),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> orcs [invaded](/wiki/Assaults_on_Hithlum "Assaults on Hithlum") Hithlum and Galdor was slain defending the fortress of [Eithel Sirion](/wiki/Eithel_Sirion). Húrin who had newly come to manhood took command and drove off the Orcs. He returned to rule as the third [Lord of Dor-lómin](/wiki/Lord_of_Dor-l%C3%B3min). Two years later he married [Morwen Eledhwen](/wiki/Morwen "Morwen") of the [House of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor).<sup id="cite_ref-TS18_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS18-7">[7]</a></sup> Their son Túrin was born shortly thereafter, followed by a daughter, [Urwen](/wiki/Urwen). Urwen, better known as *[Lalaith](/wiki/Lalaith)* ("Laughter"), died of the plague when she was three.

In [472](/wiki/First_Age_472 "First Age 472"), the alliance known as [Union of Maedhros](/wiki/Union_of_Maedhros) led Elves, Dwarves, and Men to assault Angband. Húrin led his folk to join the host led by the High King [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon). In the battle, later named the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad) or Battle of Unnumbered Tears, the Union were defeated by the hosts of Morgoth. Many Elves and Men perished, including Fingon who was slain by a [Balrog](/wiki/Balrogs "Balrogs"). With their liege lord slain and facing a rout, Húrin and Huor convinced Turgon to withdraw and keep the secret of Gondolin. To defend his escape, the Men of Dor-lómin formed a rearguard near the [Pass of Sirion](/wiki/Pass_of_Sirion) and held it giving as much time for Turgon to gather the remainder of Fingon's people and the host of Gondolin to escape. The location of Gondolin remained secret but for the Men of the Dor-lómin this was their last stand for they would not forsake the Northlands and were slaughtered by the hosts of Morgoth. By the [Fen of Serech](/wiki/Fen_of_Serech), Huor was slain by an arrow through the eye, but Húrin remained steadfast crying *[Aurë entuluva!](/wiki/Aur%C3%AB_entuluva!)*([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin")) for each foe he slew with his axe, seventy times in all. He fought until he was buried under the weight of bodies of his enemies, then <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Gothmog" title="Gothmog">Gothmog</a> bound him and dragged him towards Angband.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

### The Curse of Morgoth {#The_Curse_of_Morgoth}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/60/Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg/300px-Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="466" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/60/Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg/450px-Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/6/60/Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg/600px-Alan_Lee_-_The_Words_of_H%C3%BArin_and_Morgoth.jpg 2x" data-file-width="993" data-file-height="1541"/></a><figcaption><i>The Words of Húrin and Morgoth</i> by <a href="/wiki/Alan_Lee" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

Húrin was brought before Morgoth, for he had learnt from his spies that Húrin was friends with Turgon, and was tortured for the secret of Gondolin's location. When he would not break, Morgoth cursed him and all his kin. The Dark Lord then chained Húrin to a chair high on the slopes of [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim) where, through Morgoth's sorcery, he could watch the tragedies that would befall his family.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> However Morgoth concealed much of Túrin's deeds and what Húrin saw was contorted, lies mixed with the truth.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;But upon all whom you love my thought shall weigh as a cloud of Doom, and it shall bring them down into darkness and despair. Wherever they go, evil shall arise. Whenever they speak, their words shall bring ill counsel. Whatsoever they do shall turn against them. They shall die without hope, cursing both life and death.&#39;
</p>

</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

For 28 years,<sup id="cite_ref-TS22_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS22-4">[4]</a></sup> Húrin watched the curse come upon Túrin and [Nienor](/wiki/Nienor), his daughter born shortly after his capture, and to those that they encounter. For instance Túrin's actions led to the [destruction](/wiki/Fall_of_Nargothrond "Fall of Nargothrond") of [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond), one of the last kingdoms of the Noldor. Now only Gondolin remained.

### Wanderings {#Wanderings}

After the death of his children Morgoth released Húrin, where he hoped he could further his malice. The broken man first returned to Hithlum, which was now ruled by [Easterlings](</wiki/Easterlings_(First_Age)> "Easterlings (First Age)"). Finding none of his kin, he travelled with outlaws to the mountains of [Echoriath](/wiki/Echoriath), searching for the entrance to Gondolin. At first Turgon refused him, but later recanted and sent Eagles to bring him to Gondolin. Húrin had already left for the Forest of Brethil, but Morgoth's curse had already ensnared Gondolin, for the Dark Lord's spies now knew the general location of the [Hidden Kingdom](/wiki/Gondolin "Gondolin").<sup id="cite_ref-TS22_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS22-4">[4]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/5/51/Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="203" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/5/51/Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/5/51/Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_H%C3%BArin_Finds_Morwen.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="865"/></a><figcaption><i>Húrin Finds Morwen</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

In the Forest of Brethil he found the graves of his children. His wife, Morwen, was there and she died shortly after. After burying his wife, Húrin was taken by march-wardens to the settlement of [Ephel Brandir](/wiki/Ephel_Brandir), the home of the [People of Haleth](/wiki/People_of_Haleth), with whom Túrin lived the last years of his life. Angry and grieving, he turned the People of Haleth against one another and ruined Ephel Brandir.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

Húrin continued on to the ruins of Nargothrond; beyond seeking for news and plunder, he had been an admirer of [Finrod](/wiki/Finrod).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> In the ruins he found the [Petty-dwarf](/wiki/Petty-dwarves "Petty-dwarves") [Mîm](/wiki/M%C3%AEm). The petty-dwarf had betrayed Túrin years ago and in revenge Húrin slaughtered him. Mîm had claimed the treasure of Nargothrond, but Húrin recovered the [Nauglamír](/wiki/Nauglam%C3%ADr) and journeyed eastwards.<sup id="cite_ref-TS22_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS22-4">[4]</a></sup>

Húrin reached the [Meres of Twilight](/wiki/Meres_of_Twilight) and was taken by the Elves who guarded the western borders of [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath) in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [502](/wiki/First_Age_502 "First Age 502").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> He was allowed to enter [Menegroth](/wiki/Menegroth), where in anger he threw the Nauglamír before King [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) and 'thanked' him for aiding his son. Thingol's wife, [Melian](/wiki/Melian), finally pierced through Húrin's madness and grief revealing the truth that Morgoth hid. Shamed by his actions, he picked up the Nauglamír to give to the king as a gift and memorial, then he left Menegroth no longer a thrall of Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-TS22_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS22-4">[4]</a></sup>

After leaving Menegroth, it is said Húrin, "bereft of all purpose and desire", cast himself into the [Great Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer").<sup id="cite_ref-TS22_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS22-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

It could be argued, that Húrin partly sowed the seeds of the destruction of [Doriath](/wiki/Ruin_of_Doriath "Ruin of Doriath") and [Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin "Fall of Gondolin") or escalated it. Furthermore Húrin's presence in Brethil led to the extinction of the [House of Haleth](/wiki/House_of_Haleth).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

His life, and the lives of his children would become known as the *[Narn i Chîn Húrin](</wiki/Narn_i_Ch%C3%AEn_H%C3%BArin_(tale)> "Narn i Chîn Húrin (tale)")*, "The Tale of the Children of Húrin". It was written by the Mannish poet [Dírhaval](/wiki/D%C3%ADrhaval) who lived in the [Havens of Sirion](/wiki/Havens_of_Sirion) and it was highly praised by the Eldar.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> Millennia later [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) makes a passing reference of him to [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo) at the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>But if you take it freely, I will say that your choice is right; and though all the mighty Elf-friends of old, Hador, and Húrin, and Túrin, and Beren himself were assembled together, your seat should be among them.
</p>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Book of Lost Tales {#Book_of_Lost_Tales}

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, <i><b>Úrin</b></i> the Steadfast was a lord of Men who resided in the woodlands. He had a wife, *Mavwin* and a son, *Túrin*. Different from many of his folk, he had been a friend of the Elves, most especially the Gnome *Egnor*, an Elf of the greenwood and renown huntsman. He also knew *Beren*, son of Egnor, and had once rendered him service in respect of *Damrod*, son of Beren.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup> He was said to have "marched with countless battalions" alongside Egnor, and been as a brother in arms to Egnor<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> which was emended to him being a brother in arms to Beren.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>

Úrin hearkened to the summons of the Gnomes and with his folk marched with the [Ilkorindi](/wiki/Ilkorindi) against Melko. In the ensuing Battle of Lamentation the people of Úrin were among the few kindreds of Men who did not flee from battle, and were instead slain except for Úrin who was made captive.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup>

Melko then attempted to constrain Úrin to take up his employ, and seek after Turgon as a spy. However, neither threats of torture nor promises of riches would cause Úrin to yield. In anger, Melko then cursed a doom of woe upon Úrin and his folk, magically bound Úrin to a lofty place in the mountains, and gave Úrin a measure of vision by which he might see those things which befell his wife and children and be powerless to aid. After Melko gloated that *“the life of Túrin thy son shall be accounted a matter for tears wherever Elves or Men are gathered for the telling of tales”*, Úrin replied that *“At least none shall pity him for this, that he had a craven for father.”* Shortly after, Mavwin gave birth to their daughter, *Nienóri*, and sent Túrin to foster with *Tinwelint*. Due to the renown of Úrin, and his bond with Beren, Tinwelint accepted Túrin not as a retainer, but as even a second child.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup>

As the years passed, the dread happenings of Mavwin, Túrin, and Nienóri were witnessed by Úrin, and though Melko continually tempted him to yield, Úrin refused. However, after the deaths of Túrin, and Nienóri, the doom of Úrin's folk had been utterly fulfilled and Melko thought to use him in a more subtle way. Melko put evil words of distrust to Úrin, and then released him.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup>

Úrin, bowed with years and sorrow, and bitter in spirit, then departed unmolested from Melko's lands. Around himself he gathered a band of exiled wild Elves who were fierce and lawless. These he led to the caves of the Rodothlim where they encountered Mîm the Dwarf and the cursed gold. Though Mîm warned them not to touch the treasure, Úrin's outlaws made to carry it off regardless. When Mîm then began cursing them for doing so, Úrin smote the Dwarf, and Mîm then lay one final curse upon the gold before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup>

Úrin then ordered his outlaws to carry the gold to the halls of Tinwelint. They murmured against doing so, but Úrin responded that Tinwelint, that "king of greed", would reward them with a much sweeter life for the treasure than all the gold in Valinor could get them out in the empty woods. The hoard was then arranged for transport, placed in sacks or shut in boxes of rough wood, and so great was it that Úrin's company could scarce bear it. Úrin eventually brought it to Tinwelint's hall, and there he was greeted with joy and amazement. Úrin rebuked this welcome though, and fiercely told Tinwelint and his court of the unfortunate fates of Túrin and Nienóri. He then revealed the vast treasure, mockingly gifted it to Tinwelint as payment in full his services, and insulted him. At this Tinwelint lost his patience, and chastised Úrin and ordered him to be gone. He would not, however, have Úrin slain out of remembrance for his greet deeds of valiance.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup>

Úrin departed and returned to Hisilómë where he died among Men. However, it was said that in death his [shade](/wiki/Shade "Shade") fared into the woods seeking Mavwin, and the two then haunted the woods about the fall of Silver Bowl, where their children had perished, bewailing them. The Elves of [Kôr](/wiki/K%C3%B4r)\], however, further tell that Úrin and Mavwin eventually fared to [Mandos](/wiki/Mandos), and there they prayed to [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) so that the Gods would have mercy on the unhappy fate of Túrin and Ninóri.<sup id="cite_ref-t&amp;f_24-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-t&amp;f-24">[24]</a></sup>

### Later versions {#Later_versions}

In [Concerning ... 'The Hoard'](/wiki/Concerning_..._%27The_Hoard%27) instead of going Nargothrond alone and leaving with only the Nauglamír, "Húrin gathered a great company of violent outlaws, and they came to Nargothrond and slew Mim, and carried off a great part of the dragon-treasure.". When he (and the outlaws) then goes to Doriath, he casts all the treasure "before the feet of King Thingol in a proud gesture of scorn". Though Thingol "answered with patience and courtesy", "Húrin laughed in contempt and went out, unmolested, into the night."<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>
