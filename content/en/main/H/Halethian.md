The **Halethian** language was the [language](/wiki/Mannish "Mannish") of the [Folk of Haleth](/wiki/Folk_of_Haleth), the Second House of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain).<sup id="cite_ref-Ros_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ros-1">[1]</a></sup>

Very little is known about it, and it died out completely sometime between [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [501](/wiki/First_Age_501 "First Age 501")<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and [542](/wiki/First_Age_542 "First Age 542").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Unlike the languages of the [Folk of Hador](/wiki/Folk_of_Hador) and [Bëor](/wiki/Folk_of_B%C3%ABor "Folk of Bëor"), which were somewhat intelligible to each other, the Halethian language was completely unrelated to the two, or, at any rate, it split off from the ancestor of the former two a very long time ago by the time that the Folk of Haleth reached [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand).<sup id="cite_ref-Haleth_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Haleth-6">[5]</a></sup>

The language was already going extinct even before [Túrin Turambar](/wiki/T%C3%BArin_Turambar), a tragic hero of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), came to [Brethil](/wiki/Brethil) in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [496](/wiki/First_Age_496 "First Age 496").<sup id="cite_ref-Ros_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ros-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

The term *Halethian* is likely a combination of the name *[Haleth](/wiki/Haleth)* (the legendary chieftainess of the Haladin in the First Age) + the suffix *-ian* ("from, related to, or like"): which ultimately traces its roots to the [Latin](/wiki/Latin) *-iānnus*, which forms adjectives of belonging or origin from a noun.

The Halethian language was also called the <i><b>language of the Folk of Haleth</b></i>.

Originally, the name "[Taliska](/wiki/Taliska)" referred to the language of the Houses of [Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor") and *Haleth* whereas it was the [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador) that spoke another language, which would eventually be conceived as [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic). In an early text by Tolkien, some words he labels as Taliska refer to terms which in the later version would be used by the [Haladin](/wiki/Haladin). However, later, in the essay "[Of Dwarves and Men](/wiki/Of_Dwarves_and_Men)" (published in *[The Peoples of Middle-Earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-Earth)*), Tolkien wrote that the Bëorians and Halethians did not speak related languages: The Hadorians still spoke "ancient Adûnaic", and the Bëorians had a closely related language; it was the language of Halethians that was unrelated to both of them (see [pre-Númenorean](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmenorean "Pre-Númenorean")).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>
