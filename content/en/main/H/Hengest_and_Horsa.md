**Hengest** and **Horsa** were the elder sons of [Eriol](../E/Eriol.md), and one of the [Mannish](../M/Men.md) conquerors of [Tol Eressëa](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md) following the [Faring Forth](../F/Faring_Forth.md), according to the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

Hengest and Horsa were born during the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century" title="wikipedia:5th century">5th century AD</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heligoland" title="wikipedia:Heligoland">Heligoland</a>, an island off the coast of the [Great Lands](../G/Great_Lands.md), to Eriol and his wife [Cwén](../C/Cwén.md).

While Hengest and Horsa were still children, their mother died, and their father went to seek [Tol Eressëa](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md), the island of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) in the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../W/West.md" title="West">West</a>.

After the brothers grew up, they sought vengeance on their uncle [Beorn](<../B/Beorn_(son_of_Heden).md>) for the murder of their grandfather [Eoh](../E/Eoh.md), and afterwards became great chieftains among their people.<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-1">[1]</a></sup>

### The Faring Forth {#The_Faring_Forth}

Following the [Faring Forth](../F/Faring_Forth.md), the disastrous attempt by the Elves of Tol Eressëa to save their [kin](../I/Ilkorindi.md) in the Great Lands which resulted in their total defeat and eventual fading, evil Men invaded Tol Eressëa, which was now anchored near the coast of the Great Lands.<sup id="cite_ref-War_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-War-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

However, some time after, Hengest and Horsa (alongside their half-brother [Heorrenda](Heorrenda.md)) invaded Tol Eressëa as well, which would in later days become known as <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-War_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-War-2">[2]</a></sup>

There, Hengest established his capital at what was once the city of [Kortirion](../K/Kortirion.md), which would later be known as *Warwíc* (in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md)), and eventually as <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick" title="wikipedia:Warwick">Warwick</a></i>; meanwhile Horsa established himself at what was once the town of [Taruithorn](../T/Taruithorn.md), which would later be known as *Oxenaford* (in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md)), and eventually as *[Oxford](../O/Oxford.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

Unlike all other Men that invaded the island, however, the sons of Eriol (Hengest, Horsa, and Heorrenda) were friendly to the Elves, and it is through them that the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people" title="wikipedia:English people">English people</a> retained the "true tradition of the [fairies](../E/Elves.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-War_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-War-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The names *Hengest* and *Horsa* are in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md), with *Hengest* meaning "stallion",<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> and *Horsa* meaning "horse".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The characters of Hengest and Horsa from *The Book of Lost Tales* were based on the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengist_and_Horsa" title="wikipedia:Hengist and Horsa">legendary leaders</a> of the Anglo-Saxon invasion of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain" title="wikipedia:Great Britain">Britain</a> in the 5th century AD.<sup id="cite_ref-Hen_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hen-1">[1]</a></sup>
