<i><b>Hrólfs saga kraka</b></i> (English: *Saga of King Hrolf kraki*) is an [Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse) [saga](/wiki/Norse_mythology "Norse mythology"). The saga is commonly held by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") scholars to be the main source for the idea behind the character [Beorn](/wiki/Beorn).<sup id="cite_ref-RME73_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RME73-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AH164_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AH164-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Plot {#Plot}

The tale describes the complex, violent history of the Danish royal family (the Skjöldungs), including the incestuous union that brings about the birth of Hrólf. As king, Hrólf gathers a band of berserkers and champions. The most famous of these was Böðvar Bjarki, a "bear-warrior" who could project a massive bear-spirit onto the field of battle while his body remained in a trance. The kingdom is plagued by manipulative sorceresses, eldrich curses, and conflicts with the Swedish King Aðils. At the end of the saga, Hrólf is betrayed from within by his half-sister Skúld, leading to an epic but fatal last stand.

## Background {#Background}

Tolkien is known to have created a short story based on the *Hrólfs saga kraka*, which he called *[Sellic Spell](/wiki/Sellic_Spell)*.<sup id="cite_ref-MB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MB-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [1933](/wiki/1933), Tolkien's pupil [Stella M. Mills](/wiki/Stella_M._Mills) published a translation *[The Saga of Hrolf Kraki](/wiki/The_Saga_of_Hrolf_Kraki)*. Mills dedicated the book to Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Drafts published in [John D. Rateliff's](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff "John D. Rateliff") *[The History of The Hobbit](/wiki/The_History_of_The_Hobbit)* reveal that Tolkien originally scribbled notes suggesting Beorn's shape-shifting was a curse that would be lifted after the [Battle of the Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies "Battle of Five Armies"). This directly mirrored the tragic magical curse of Bjarki's father, though Tolkien ultimately changed his mind for the final book, making the skin-changing a part of Beorn's natural being. Tolkien popularized the term **"**skin-changer" (*hamrammr* in Old Norse) for his character Beorn.
