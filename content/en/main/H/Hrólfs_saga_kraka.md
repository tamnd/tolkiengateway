<i><b>Hrólfs saga kraka</b></i> (English: *Saga of King Hrolf kraki*) is an [Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md) [saga](../N/Norse_mythology.md). The saga is commonly held by [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) scholars to be the main source for the idea behind the character [Beorn](../B/Beorn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-RME73_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RME73-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MB-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AH164_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AH164-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Plot {#Plot}

The tale describes the complex, violent history of the Danish royal family (the Skjöldungs), including the incestuous union that brings about the birth of Hrólf. As king, Hrólf gathers a band of berserkers and champions. The most famous of these was Böðvar Bjarki, a "bear-warrior" who could project a massive bear-spirit onto the field of battle while his body remained in a trance. The kingdom is plagued by manipulative sorceresses, eldrich curses, and conflicts with the Swedish King Aðils. At the end of the saga, Hrólf is betrayed from within by his half-sister Skúld, leading to an epic but fatal last stand.

## Background {#Background}

Tolkien is known to have created a short story based on the *Hrólfs saga kraka*, which he called *[Sellic Spell](../S/Sellic_Spell.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-MB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MB-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [1933](../_/1933.md), Tolkien's pupil [Stella M. Mills](../S/Stella_M._Mills.md) published a translation *[The Saga of Hrolf Kraki](../T/The_Saga_of_Hrolf_Kraki.md)*. Mills dedicated the book to Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Drafts published in [John D. Rateliff's](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) *[The History of The Hobbit](../T/The_History_of_The_Hobbit.md)* reveal that Tolkien originally scribbled notes suggesting Beorn's shape-shifting was a curse that would be lifted after the [Battle of the Five Armies](../B/Battle_of_Five_Armies.md). This directly mirrored the tragic magical curse of Bjarki's father, though Tolkien ultimately changed his mind for the final book, making the skin-changing a part of Beorn's natural being. Tolkien popularized the term **"**skin-changer" (*hamrammr* in Old Norse) for his character Beorn.
