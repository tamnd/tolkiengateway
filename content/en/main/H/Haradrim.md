**Haradrim** or the **Southrons** were the [Men](/wiki/Men) of [Harad](/wiki/Harad), descendants of the Men who [woke](/wiki/Awakening_of_Men "Awakening of Men") in the far eastern land of [Hildórien](/wiki/Hild%C3%B3rien) near the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Uttermost_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Uttermost-3">[1]</a></sup> They are the proud, doughty and warlike people of the Harad, in the south of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[2]</a></sup> Ancient enemies of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[3]</a></sup> they allied with [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) during the [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron) and [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 3]</a></sup> Eventually, many turned against Sauron, when [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) sent the [Wizards](/wiki/Wizards) to the southern lands of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-PMLast_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PMLast-8">[5]</a></sup>

## Origins {#Origins}

The ancestors of the Haradrim were the Men who awoke in Hildórien during the [age of the Sun](/wiki/Years_of_the_Sun "Years of the Sun"),<sup id="cite_ref-S12_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S12-9">[6]</a></sup> but stayed to the [East](/wiki/East) of Middle-earth, not joining the migration of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) to the [Uttermost West](/wiki/Uttermost_West)<sup id="cite_ref-Uttermost_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Uttermost-3">[1]</a></sup> after [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth), the original [Dark Lord](/wiki/Dark_Lord), came to corrupt them to his service whilst tricking them to abandon [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[7]</a></sup> A [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) ever after lay over all of humanity, even the Edain, wherein portions of the ancestors of [Harad](/wiki/Harad) later began migrating after coming into conflict with other men that fell under the Shadow.<sup id="cite_ref-S17_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S17-11">[8]</a></sup> Still some went West in search of the [Light of Valinor](/wiki/Light_of_Valinor) and servants of Morgoth hunted them, but the majority of mankind refused alike the summons of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and of Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-S17_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S17-11">[8]</a></sup>

The different peoples who moved to Harad, where the Sun is fierce because there are barely any clouds,<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-12">[9]</a></sup> played no role in the tales of the First Age.<sup id="cite_ref-S12_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S12-9">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[10]</a></sup> However, this sundering bereft them of the enlightenment and crafts that the Elves may have taught them,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[12]</a></sup> which made them vulnerable to their two later woes: [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[13]</a></sup> Historians of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) believed that the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) came from lands south of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), that they turned north into [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) before they reached the coasts of Harad and were the first Men to cross the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), possibly near [Cair Andros](/wiki/Cair_Andros).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[14]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

At first, for many centuries, the Haradrim were independent peoples, generally isolated from the rest of the world. However, in the Second Age they became increasingly caught between the ambitions of two great powers — namely [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) — a circumstance which lasted thousands of years.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-18">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Coasts_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coasts-19">[16]</a></sup> Sailing East, the Númenóreans explored the coasts of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), including the coast lands of Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-Coasts_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coasts-19">[16]</a></sup> The Númenóreans initially benefited the natives they contacted, by teaching them many things about agriculture and craftsmanship<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-20">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[18]</a></sup> and later made settlements on the southern coasts, including the construction of the great city of [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[19]</a></sup>

After the reign of [Tar-Ciryatan](/wiki/Tar-Ciryatan), the Númenóreans began to set themselves up as lords in Middle-earth as they demanded tribute of goods and wealth, causing the oppression of the Haradrim<sup id="cite_ref-SA_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-23">[20]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[21]</a></sup> as with many other peoples whose lands included a coastline.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[22]</a></sup> Often Haradrim were killed or sold into slavery.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[23]</a></sup> At first, Sauron didn't dare to challenge the Númenóreans as they expanded their control over Harad, until after the forging of the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power) and the emergence of the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-20">[17]</a></sup> Then, Sauron came as a ruler of almost all men east and south of the [Ered Luin](/wiki/Ered_Luin), as both their king and their god.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-18">[15]</a></sup> These men grew strong and built many towns and walls made from stone and they were fierce in war and armed with iron.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-18">[15]</a></sup> However, those of Harad who sought freedom and rejected Sauron would venture to the woods or mountains in fear of being pursued by him.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-18">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[24]</a></sup>

In c. [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1600](/wiki/Second_Age_1600 "Second Age 1600"), [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) sent two [Wizards](/wiki/Wizards) to the southern and eastern lands of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-PMLast_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PMLast-8">[5]</a></sup> The Valar suspected there was a rebellion of good Haradrim but no leadership — ultimately they were very successful there in Harad. They arrived before Sauron's first [war against the Elves](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron "War of the Elves and Sauron") and even on this war the Wizards had some influence.<sup id="cite_ref-NM3_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM3-28">[25]</a></sup> They became known as [Morinehtar](/wiki/Morinehtar) and [Rómestámo](/wiki/R%C3%B3mest%C3%A1mo).<sup id="cite_ref-PMLast_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PMLast-8">[5]</a></sup> Because of their influence among the Haradrim, Sauron took a long time to [attack Eregion](/wiki/Sack_of_Eregion "Sack of Eregion"), for his dominance and recruiting of forces was not entirely successful.<sup id="cite_ref-NM3_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM3-28">[25]</a></sup>

In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3261](/wiki/Second_Age_3261 "Second Age 3261"), [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n), the [King of Númenor](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor), landed at Umbar with a great fleet and the people on the coasts fled before them.<sup id="cite_ref-SA_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-23">[20]</a></sup> Ar-Pharazôn and his host marched through Harad to meet Sauron's forces, but they fled from the Númenóreans and Sauron allowed himself to be taken to Númenor where he corrupted the King and his followers.<sup id="cite_ref-Influence_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Influence-29">[26]</a></sup> Under Sauron's influence, the Númenóreans became even more ruthless to the locals of Harad, enslaving them and using them for human sacrifices.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-20">[17]</a></sup>

In the following centuries the Haradrim were influenced by Sauron and [Black Númenóreans](/wiki/Black_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), those evil High Men who stayed in Harad and survived the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-Influence_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Influence-29">[26]</a></sup> The Haradrim became enemies of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), as the Kingdom's southern borders were close to their lands.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[27]</a></sup> Shortly before the end of the Age, two Númenórean lords, [Herumor](/wiki/Herumor) and [Fuinur](/wiki/Fuinur), rose to great power amongst the Haradrim.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_18-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-18">[15]</a></sup>

However around the same time, the two Wizards, always operating in the East and the South, were able to hinder Sauron's operations, leading to his defeat in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance).<sup id="cite_ref-PMLast_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PMLast-8">[5]</a></sup> When Sauron's dominions of the Second Age fell with his demise in the War of the Last Alliance the Haradrim were freed from his direct control but not free of the effects of his long work on them, to which evil and darkness for the West had set in their hearts.<sup id="cite_ref-UT|6a_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT|6a-31">[28]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[29]</a></sup> Additionally, some of the Haradrim, and the other servants of Sauron, turned from evil and became subject to the heirs of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil).<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_18-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-18">[15]</a></sup>

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

The great cape and land-locked firth of Umbar had been Númenórean land since days of old; but it was a stronghold of the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men), who were afterwards called the Black Númenoreans, corrupted by Sauron, and who hated above all the followers of Elendil.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup> After the fall of Sauron their race swiftly dwindled or became merged with the Men of Middle-earth, including those of Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup> There was even intermarriage: [Tarannon Falastur](/wiki/Tarannon_Falastur), [King of Gondor](/wiki/King_of_Gondor), married a high-born Black Númenórean lady from an inland city in Harad; she is remembered as [Queen Berúthiel](/wiki/Queen_Ber%C3%BAthiel).<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[31]</a></sup> However, most Black Númenórean intermarriage was with the Haradrim, and they became increasingly indistinguishable from the indigenous peoples.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> The Haradrim did not trouble the [Realms in Exile](/wiki/Realms_in_Exile) for many centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-herbs_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-herbs-36">[33]</a></sup>

When Gondor took the haven of Umbar from the Black Númenóreans, in the year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1015](/wiki/Third_Age_1015 "Third Age 1015") they followed the Black Númenóreans against Umbar to recapture it.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup> Their great power was not enough against the sea-power of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup> The attempts lasted for 35 years until, in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050"), [Ciryaher](/wiki/Ciryaher) defeated the Haradrim force by sending troops by land.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup> They acknowledged the overlordship of Gondor; the kings of Harad did homage and their sons were given as hostages in the court of the [King of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor "Kings of Gondor").<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppA_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-37">[34]</a></sup> The sons of [Castamir](/wiki/Castamir) and others of his kin, having fled from Gondor in 1447, set up a small kingdom in Umbar, and there made a fortified haven and later called themselves the [Corsairs of Umbar](/wiki/Corsairs_of_Umbar).<sup id="cite_ref-Blood_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blood-38">[35]</a></sup> The Corsairs of Umbar continued to make war upon Gondor, attacking its ships and coasts when they had opportunity.<sup id="cite_ref-Blood_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blood-38">[35]</a></sup> Additionally, Castamir's progeny married women of the Harad - continuing the Numenorean [Line of Elros](/wiki/Line_of_Elros), though of which was spent after three generations.<sup id="cite_ref-Blood_38-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blood-38">[35]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[36]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[note 4]</a></sup>

Later, however, kings of Harad who were in league with Umbar rebelled against Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1634](/wiki/Third_Age_1634 "Third Age 1634"), [Minardil](/wiki/Minardil) was slain at Pelargir by the Corsairs of Umbar, who were led by the great-grandsons of Castamir: [Angamaitë](/wiki/Angamait%C3%AB) and [Sangahyando](/wiki/Sangahyando).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup> [Telumehtar](/wiki/Telumehtar) who raided his coasts even as far as the [Anfalas](/wiki/Anfalas), gathered his forces and in 1810 took Umbar by storm.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> In that war the last descendants of Castamir perished, and Umbar was again held for a while by the kings, however, the [Battle of the Plains](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Plains) that befell Gondor resulted in Umbar being lost again, wherein it fell into the hands of the Men of the Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup>

The Haradrim later were allied with the [Wainriders](/wiki/Wainriders), a confederation of [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings), and the Men of [Khand](/wiki/Khand).<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> During that time they conquered Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> The simultaneous assaults from the north and the south brought the South-kingdom close to destruction.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> The Haradrim crossed the river [Poros](/wiki/Poros) into [South Ithilien](/wiki/South_Ithilien), but the [Southern Army](/wiki/Southern_Army), destroyed them.<sup id="cite_ref-annals_35-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annals-35">[32]</a></sup> In the later years three great fleets were prepared in Umbar and Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-stew_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-41">[37]</a></sup> Around the [Long Winter](/wiki/Long_Winter) they assailed the [coasts](/wiki/Southern_Fiefs "Southern Fiefs") of Gondor, even [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan).<sup id="cite_ref-stew_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-41">[37]</a></sup> The Haradrim occupied the fief [South Gondor](/wiki/South_Gondor) and there was a long fight along the Poros.<sup id="cite_ref-stew_41-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-41">[37]</a></sup> Stirred by emissaries of Sauron, they attempted once more to invade into [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2885](/wiki/Third_Age_2885 "Third Age 2885")) but a combined force of Gondorians and [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) defeated them at the [Crossing of Poros](/wiki/Crossings_of_Poros "Crossings of Poros").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-42">[38]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[39]</a></sup>

In the meantime, it was said that the two Wizards, Morinehtar and Rómestámo ensured that the forces of the East and the South did not outnumber the West, thus helping secure victory for the [Free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples) in the subsequent War of the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-PMLast_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PMLast-8">[5]</a></sup> At some time before the War, Gandalf also wandered in the South, for unspecified errands, and he became known as *Inkā-nūs* among the suspicious Haradrim, who saw him as a spy from [the North](/wiki/Gondor "Gondor").<sup id="cite_ref-Incanus_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Incanus-44">[40]</a></sup>

At the time of that War, the Haradrim were in league with Sauron and fought alongside his [Orc](/wiki/Orcs "Orcs") army. The Haradrim were among the forces led by the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) that attacked [Osgiliath](/wiki/Osgiliath) on [20 June](/wiki/20_June), [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), at the beginning of the War.<sup id="cite_ref-Great_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-45">[41]</a></sup> More Haradrim continued to come up the [Harad Road](/wiki/Harad_Road) to Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-herbs_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-herbs-36">[33]</a></sup> Regiments of Haradrim joined the host from [Minas Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul) marching to the [Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Pelennor_Fields).<sup id="cite_ref-MinasTirith_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MinasTirith-46">[42]</a></sup> Sauron's forces besieged [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith), and the Mûmakil of Harad were used to bring forward war-towers and siege-engines to test the City's defences.<sup id="cite_ref-MinasTirith_46-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MinasTirith-46">[42]</a></sup> During the battle, the chief leader of the Haradrim army bore a standard of a [Black Serpent](/wiki/Black_Serpent) on a red field; he was slain by King [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden) of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan).<sup id="cite_ref-Great_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-45">[41]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-fields_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fields-47">[43]</a></sup>

The Haradrim rallied around their [Mûmakil](/wiki/Oliphaunts "Oliphaunts") which the horses of the Rohirrim feared to approach. As the battle wore on, more Haradrim including Men from Far Harad were sent onto the field by [Gothmog](</wiki/Gothmog_(Lieutenant_of_Morgul)> "Gothmog (Lieutenant of Morgul)").<sup id="cite_ref-fields_47-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fields-47">[43]</a></sup> The Corsairs were prevented from reaching the battle, having been defeated by [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and the [Dead Men of Dunharrow](/wiki/Dead_Men_of_Dunharrow).<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[44]</a></sup> Haradrim also fought at the [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon) on March 25.<sup id="cite_ref-Influence_29-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Influence-29">[26]</a></sup> Sauron's army of Men, Orcs and Trolls outnumbered the Men of the West by more than ten to one, but the battle ended once the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring) was destroyed in [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) and Sauron was vanquished.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[45]</a></sup> The Haradrim held out long and were among the last of Sauron's forces to be defeated.<sup id="cite_ref-fields_47-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fields-47">[43]</a></sup> Some of the Haradrim fled or surrendered, though others banded together in their hatred and fought on stalwartly.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[46]</a></sup>

### Fourth Age {#Fourth_Age}

After the War of the Ring the newly crowned king of the [Reunited Kingdom](/wiki/Reunited_Kingdom), Elessar, made peace with the Men of Harad and completely subdued Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Éomer_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Éomer-51">[47]</a></sup> Embassies travelled from Harad to Aragorn's court, and although Aragorn's reign was largely one of peace, it was occasionally necessary for him and [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer), [King](/wiki/Kings_of_Rohan "Kings of Rohan") of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan), to travel to the southlands to maintain peace with Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[48]</a></sup>

It is possible that later generations of Haradrim, having the memory of the two Wizards who wandered among them, followed [magic](/wiki/Magic "Magic") "cults" that lasted long into the Fourth Age or later.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter211_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter211-53">[49]</a></sup>

## Traits and culture {#Traits_and_culture}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/41/Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg/300px-Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="498" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/41/Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg/450px-Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/41/Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg/600px-Alan_Lee_-_Oliphaunt.jpg 2x" data-file-width="602" data-file-height="999"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Oliphaunt&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Alan_Lee" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

The "fierce dark men of the South."<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[50]</a></sup> are of various ethnicities and cultures; some are organized into kingdoms.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_33-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-33">[30]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-herbs_36-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-herbs-36">[33]</a></sup> The Haradrim were described by a messenger as "cruel and tall.",<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-55">[51]</a></sup> "a grim folk, and not easily daunted by shade or blade."<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[52]</a></sup> Some of the Men in the south had weapons of iron.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">[53]</a></sup> At the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields), the Haradrim bore scimitars that glittered like stars.<sup id="cite_ref-fields_47-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fields-47">[43]</a></sup> Those of Harad had tamed the massive *[Mûmakil](/wiki/Oliphaunts "Oliphaunts")* beasts and used them in warfare and, like their masters, were decorated with scarlet and gold. They even strapped towers on their backs, used by Haradrim archers and spearmen.<sup id="cite_ref-herbs_36-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-herbs-36">[33]</a></sup>

The people of [Far Harad](/wiki/Far_Harad) are described as black-skinned, but there is also a group of them described as "black men like [half-trolls](/wiki/Half-trolls "Half-trolls") with white eyes and red tongues" and "troll-men".<sup id="cite_ref-fields_47-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fields-47">[43]</a></sup> It is unclear whether these were just large Men who are being compared to Trolls or an implication of crossbreeding between the two races.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">[54]</a></sup> The Haradrim from [Near Harad](/wiki/Near_Harad) were those most familiar and most closely encountered during the War of the Ring:<sup id="cite_ref-Scarlet_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Scarlet-59">[55]</a></sup> when [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir) ambushed a company of Haradrim on the [North Road](/wiki/North_Road), Frodo and Sam witnessed a Harad warrior crashing at their feet, a man with "brown skin", with black plaits of hair braided with gold,<sup id="cite_ref-Scarlet_59-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Scarlet-59">[55]</a></sup> a scarlet tunic, as do the other Haradrim, and a gold collar.<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-12">[9]</a></sup> He was armed with a scimitar and garbed with a corslet of brazen scales. Their standards are scarlet, and their great beasts, the *Mûmakil*, have scarlet and gold trappings.<sup id="cite_ref-Scarlet_59-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Scarlet-59">[55]</a></sup> They carry round spiked shields, painted yellow and black.<sup id="cite_ref-herbs_36-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-herbs-36">[33]</a></sup> One of the great chieftains of the Haradrim also bore a [Black Serpent](/wiki/Black_Serpent) as his emblem, against a field of red.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">[56]</a></sup>

### Languages {#Languages}

Very little is known about the language(s) of Harad though the word "Mûmak", the name of the great war-<a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Oliphaunt" title="Oliphaunt">oliphaunts</a> of Harad, is stated to come directly from a language of Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-Language_61-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Language-61">[57]</a></sup> To the [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) the voices of the Haradrim sounded harsh, like shouts of beasts.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_55-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-55">[51]</a></sup> Albeit having a meaning in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) ("fate"), the name *[umbar](</wiki/Umbar_(word)> "Umbar (word)")* is said to be adapted from the indigenous languages of Harad and not from an [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) or [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">[58]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppendixF_63-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppendixF-63">[59]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Names_64-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Names-64">[60]</a></sup>

[Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) stated that his name in "the south" is *Incánus*, which is apparently "alien", that is neither [Westron](/wiki/Westron), nor [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish), nor explicable by the surviving tongues of Northern Men. A note in the [Thain's Book](/wiki/Thain%27s_Book) states that it is a form adapted to Quenya of a word in the tongue of the Haradrim thought *Inkā-nūsh* (or possibly *Inkā-nūs*), meaning "North-spy".<sup id="cite_ref-Incanus_44-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Incanus-44">[40]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Haradrim* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, consisting of the elements *[harad](/wiki/Harad "Harad")* ("south") + *[rim](/wiki/Rim "Rim")* ("host") thus meaning "South-people".<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">[61]</a></sup> Additionally, the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) named the land and its people *Haradwaith*, "South-folk", from the Sindarin *harad* ("south") and *gwaith* ("people").<sup id="cite_ref-Language_61-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Language-61">[57]</a></sup> The [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) called the area the *Sunlands*, and the people *[Swertings](/wiki/Swertings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-12">[9]</a></sup>

*Barangils* is a [Gondorian](/wiki/Gondorian) term for the people of Harad; 'swerting' derives from *swart* ("swarthy").<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">[62]</a></sup> The Elvish name seems to contain **baran** ("dark, swart, dark-brown") (BARÁN) and possibly the suffix **-il** also seen in **ernil** ("prince").<sup id="cite_ref-UT|6a_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT|6a-31">[28]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Early variant names for this people were the *Barangils*, the *Haradwaith*, and *Haradrians*.<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">[63]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">[64]</a></sup> In early drafts of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, anglicized names for the Haradrim in the text, were *Silharrows* and *Harwan*, which according to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) were derived from the name for the Aethiopians.<sup id="cite_ref-Fangorn_69-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fangorn-69">[65]</a></sup> "Men of Sunharrowland" (or "of Harrowland") are other two anglicized names.<sup id="cite_ref-Harrowland_70-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Harrowland-70">[66]</a></sup> In early drafts of *The Lord of the Rings*, *Harns* was a Sindarin equivalent for the term Haradrim.<sup id="cite_ref-71" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-71">[67]</a></sup> It's based on [harn](/wiki/Harn "Harn") ’southern’ (KHYAR-) and is an English plural.<sup id="cite_ref-72" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-72">[68]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">[69]</a></sup> Additionally, Haradwaith has the anglicized name *(Sun)Harrowland*.<sup id="cite_ref-Fangorn_69-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fangorn-69">[65]</a></sup> For more explanation on those names, see [below](#The_"Sigelwaran").

In the earliest narrative of the legendarium in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, Tolkien mentioned the movement of Men, after the [War of Palisor](/wiki/Palisor "Palisor"). After [Nuin](/wiki/Nuin) died at the bands of the Goblins through the treachery of Men, many kindreds of Men were driven to the eastern deserts and the southern forests, whence came dark and savage peoples.<sup id="cite_ref-Palisor_74-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palisor-74">[70]</a></sup>

Additionally, it was revealed that Manwë was unaware of where the beginning of Men should be, whether the north, south or east.<sup id="cite_ref-75" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-75">[71]</a></sup> In *[The Book of Lost Tales Part Two](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two)*, [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) and [Voronwe](/wiki/Voronwe)'s voyage on [Vingilótë](/wiki/Vingil%C3%B3t%C3%AB) to southern Haradwaith where there is mention of Tree-men and Pygmies.<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">[72]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-77" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-77">[note 5]</a></sup>

### Earlier account of the Two Wizards {#Earlier_account_of_the_Two_Wizards}

In the published works, all the Wizards were originally sent in the year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1000](/wiki/Third_Age_1000 "Third Age 1000") as emissaries to Middle-earth along with [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) and [Radagast](/wiki/Radagast).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_42-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-42">[38]</a></sup> The above article gives information based on a late essay by Tolkien concerning the two Wizards, published in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*.

However in some earlier time, when asked about the other two Wizards, Tolkien speculated that they went to the distant regions, including the lands of Harad, far out of Númenórean range. Whether together or independent of each other, the two may have fallen from their appointed task, wherein they may have founded 'magic' cults amongst the peoples of the East and South, which existed beyond the downfall of the [Lord of the Rings](/wiki/Sauron "Sauron").<sup id="cite_ref-Letter211_53-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter211-53">[49]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) linked the Haradrim with ancient Aethiopians.<sup id="cite_ref-Fangorn_69-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fangorn-69">[65]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Harrowland_70-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Harrowland-70">[66]</a></sup> In an interview from 1966, Tolkien likened [Berúthiel](/wiki/Ber%C3%BAthiel) to the giantess <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ska%C3%B0i" title="wikipedia:Skaði">Skaði</a> of Norse mythology, since they both shared a dislike for "seaside life".<sup id="cite_ref-Interview_78-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Interview-78">[73]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-79" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-79">[74]</a></sup> Additionally, Tolkien scholar [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) stated in reference to the 'black men like half-trolls' passage from *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)* that Tolkien was attempting to write like a medieval chronicler in describing the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim)'s encounter with a Haradrim: "\[...\] and when medieval Europeans first encountered sub-Saharan Africans, they were genuinely confused about them, and rather frightened."<sup id="cite_ref-African_80-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-African-80">[75]</a></sup> He noted that Tolkien had pointed out in his early scholarly works "the ancient English seemed to have a belief in fire-demons, who naturally enough had skin like soot – their word for them, ‘harwan’, is related to Latin ‘carbo’, ‘soot,’ or carbon."<sup id="cite_ref-African_80-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-African-80">[75]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-81" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-81">[76]</a></sup> Shippey concluded by remarking that, "An Anglo-Saxon meeting an African for the first time might then really wonder - for a moment, from a distance - whether this was a demon from his own mythology. This doesn't mean that Tolkien shared the mythology, or the mistake."<sup id="cite_ref-82" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-82">[77]</a></sup>

<h3><span id="The_.22Sigelwaran.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="The_&#34;Sigelwaran&#34;">The &#34;Sigelwaran&#34;</span></h3>

Much of Tolkien's influence for Harad and the Haradrim came about from his essay *[Sigelwara Land](/wiki/Sigelwara_Land)*, in which he examined the etymology of *Sigelwaran* (and the more usual form *Sigelhearwan*) — the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) word for Ethiopians.<sup id="cite_ref-CG_83-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-83">[78]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TB1_84-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TB1-84">[79]</a></sup> The essay was originally published in two parts: part one appeared in *Medium Ævum* (Vol. 1, No. 3) in [1932](/wiki/1932), and part two appeared in *Medium Ævum* (Vol. 3, No. 2) in [1934](/wiki/1934).<sup id="cite_ref-TB1_84-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TB1-84">[79]</a></sup> The article investigated why there was a distinct and several times attested [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name for the Ethiopians (namely **Sigelwaran, Sigelhearwan**)<sup id="cite_ref-CG_83-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-83">[78]</a></sup> as actual biblical names were usually adapted, not translated.<sup id="cite_ref-85" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-85">[80]</a></sup>

Tolkien remarked that the word includes elements not current in Old English and argues from there on that it must be older and preserved at least a name, "if no more, from the vanished native mythology or its borderland of half-mythical geography."<sup id="cite_ref-86" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-86">[81]</a></sup> As the initial element **Sigel** ’Sun’ comes into question, which is attested in *[Beowulf](</wiki/Beowulf_(poem)> "Beowulf (poem)")* and has furthermore cognates in other languages, as e.g. the name of the s-rune.<sup id="cite_ref-87" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-87">[82]</a></sup> Another candidate is **sigel**, **sigle** describing "a round jewel or golden ornament",<sup id="cite_ref-Gimm_88-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gimm-88">[83]</a></sup> originating from Latin **sigillum**, which in its turn refers to "a small image or figure, the impress of a stamp or seal."<sup id="cite_ref-89" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-89">[84]</a></sup> Tolkien then suggested that the two words had mutual influence on each other; and he remarks on the usage of *gimm* ’precious stone, jewel’ for the sun.<sup id="cite_ref-Gimm_88-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gimm-88">[83]</a></sup> For the second element **hearwa** he discusses several primitive candidates all having to do with the colour ’black’, so that the name may mean something akin to ’those who were made black by the sun’.<sup id="cite_ref-Vol._3_p._110_90-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vol._3_p._110-90">[85]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-91" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-91">[86]</a></sup> As he concluded, "such guesswork is naturally inconclusive \[...\] giving insight into English and northern tradition and imagination."<sup id="cite_ref-Vol._3_p._110_90-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vol._3_p._110-90">[85]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-92" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-92">[87]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

### Films {#Films}

<b>2001-03: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Haradrim appear briefly in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers">The Two Towers</a></i> when Frodo and <a href="/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee" title="Samwise Gamgee">Sam</a> witness a raid on one of their columns by <a href="/wiki/Faramir" title="Faramir">Faramir&#39;s</a> rangers. They are featured more prominently in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">The Return of the King</a></i>, in which the battle between the <a href="/wiki/Rohirrim" title="Rohirrim">Rohirrim</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Oliphaunts" title="Oliphaunts">Mûmakil</a> is a major action sequence. While the <i><a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Return of the King">book</a></i> depicts the Southron army as primarily cavalry armed with scimitars, we see no horsemen in the movie: the Haradrim fight almost exclusively from platforms mounted on the backs of their monstrous <a href="/wiki/Oliphaunts" title="Oliphaunts">Oliphaunts</a>. They have also adorned the animals&#39; tusks with spikes and shafts that crush and impale numerous enemy horsemen. The costumes of the Haradrim in the movie are Middle Eastern in style and dark brown or black in colour, while a companion book, <i>Lord of the Rings: Creatures</i>, stated that the Haradrim were based on 12th century Saracens.<sup id="cite_ref-93" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-93">[88]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2012-14: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Ringwraith of Harad, also known as the Dark Headsman was a <a href="/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl" title="Nazgûl">Nazgûl</a> who was revived by <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a> and summoned to <a href="/wiki/Dol_Guldur" title="Dol Guldur">Dol Guldur</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-94" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-94">[89]</a></sup> He was once a King of the Haradrim that was given a <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Ring_of_Power" title="Ring of Power">Ring of Power</a> by Sauron the deceiver and was corrupted to serve him. The Witch-king and the other Ringwraiths were buried in a dungeon in the <a href="/wiki/High_Fells_of_Rhudaur" title="High Fells of Rhudaur">High Fells of Rhudaur</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Fornost" title="Battle of Fornost">fall</a> of Angmar. Later, Gandalf, following <a href="/wiki/Galadriel" title="Galadriel">Galadriel</a>&#39;s advice, visits the High Fells and discovers that the Nazgûl&#39;s cells were empty, broken open from the inside. Through this investigation, he is convinced that the Necromancer in Dol Guldur is indeed <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>. In the <a href="/wiki/Attack_on_Dol_Guldur" title="Attack on Dol Guldur">Attack on Dol Guldur</a>, The Witch-King, along with the other Nazgûl, participates by fighting <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a> and <a href="/wiki/Elrond" title="Elrond">Elrond</a>, members of the <a href="/wiki/White_Council" title="White Council">White Council</a>. In the end, he and the other Nine are driven back to <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Mordor</a> by Galadriel and her <a href="/wiki/Phial_of_Galadriel" title="Phial of Galadriel">phial</a>, his master also eventually following suit.</dd></dl>
