**Hawks** were the swiftest birds of prey,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> smaller than [Eagles](../E/Eagles.md), but just as sharp-eyed.

## History {#History}

[Spirits](../S/Spirits.md) in the shapes of hawks and eagles were the servants of [Manwë](../M/Manwë.md) Súlimo, continually flying over [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) to gather information for their master.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> During the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) noticed hawks flying high in the sky above many flocks of *[crebain](../C/Crebain.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) noted that one of two possible readings for the unintelligible entry <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=PHI&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="PHI (page does not exist)">PHI</a> in *[The Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)* could be "hawk" (the other being "haste").<sup id="cite_ref-Etym_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Etym-4">[4]</a></sup> [Carl F. Hostetter](../C/Carl_F._Hostetter.md) and [Patrick H. Wynne](../P/Patrick_H._Wynne.md) argue that "hawk" is the more likely reading, especially given the onomatopoeic suitability of the form of the base to the cry of a hawk, and the possible relation to the root <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=PHILIK&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="PHILIK (page does not exist)">PHILIK</a>, "small bird".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word for "hawk" would then be *fion*, plural *fiondi*.<sup id="cite_ref-Etym_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Etym-4">[4]</a></sup>

In a linguistic manuscript dating from the 1930s, appears the [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) gloss *haro* ("hawk").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
