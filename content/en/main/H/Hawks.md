**Hawks** were the swiftest birds of prey,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> smaller than [Eagles](/wiki/Eagles), but just as sharp-eyed.

## History {#History}

[Spirits](/wiki/Spirits) in the shapes of hawks and eagles were the servants of [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) Súlimo, continually flying over [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) to gather information for their master.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) noticed hawks flying high in the sky above many flocks of *[crebain](/wiki/Crebain "Crebain")*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) noted that one of two possible readings for the unintelligible entry <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=PHI&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="PHI (page does not exist)">PHI</a> in *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)* could be "hawk" (the other being "haste").<sup id="cite_ref-Etym_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Etym-4">[4]</a></sup> [Carl F. Hostetter](/wiki/Carl_F._Hostetter) and [Patrick H. Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_H._Wynne) argue that "hawk" is the more likely reading, especially given the onomatopoeic suitability of the form of the base to the cry of a hawk, and the possible relation to the root <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=PHILIK&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="PHILIK (page does not exist)">PHILIK</a>, "small bird".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word for "hawk" would then be *fion*, plural *fiondi*.<sup id="cite_ref-Etym_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Etym-4">[4]</a></sup>

In a linguistic manuscript dating from the 1930s, appears the [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) gloss *haro* ("hawk").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
