**Hanstovánen**, formerly **Amnos**, was the beaching place of the ship [Mornië](<../M/Mornië_(ship).md>) in the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*.

## History {#History}

Mornië, the ship that ferried the dead, was often beached at Amnos, a northern harbour at the end of the path that led there from [Mandos](Halls_of_Mandos.md). As the [Noldoli](../N/Noldoli.md) travelled along the coasts of [Eldamar](../E/Eldamar.md) during their [exile](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md) from [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md), they came to Amnos before they reached the [Helkaraksë](Helkaraksë.md). A servant of [Vefántur](../V/Vefántur.md) saw them there and asked that they turn back. When they refused, he stood on a tall rock and spoke the [Prophecies of Amnos](../D/Doom_of_Mandos.md), which warned of hardships to come. The prophecy was named for Amnos, as that was the place where it was spoken. Thus, this place was later called *Hanstovánen*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

*Hanstovánen* was also written as *Mornielta* and *Vane Hansto*, but some of these were struck out, and *Hanstovánen* appears to be the final form, being the one used in the narrative.<sup id="cite_ref-notes_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-notes-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:170</sup>

The name *Amnos* was emended from *Emnon*, which was an emendation from *Morniento*.<sup id="cite_ref-notes_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-notes-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:170</sup> In the following Lost Tale, the location is referred as the strands of <i><b>Amnor</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

A prophecy by [Amnon](../A/Amnon.md) referenced by [Turgon](../T/Turgon.md) in *[The Tale of the Fall of Gondolin](<../T/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter).md>)* resembled the Prophecies of Amnos, but here Amnon was a prophet rather than a location. A few other notes for Lost Tales mention a Prophecy of Amnon, but these do not specify whether Amnon was a person or place.<sup id="cite_ref-notes_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-notes-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:172</sup>

In *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*, the [Doom of the Noldor](../D/Doom_of_the_Noldor~13942.md) is given in the lands of [Araman](../A/Araman.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
