The **hair of Galadriel** was described as golden touched with silver and seemed to shine with the [light](/wiki/Light "Light") of the [Two Trees of Valinor](/wiki/Two_Trees_of_Valinor).

## History {#History}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395753">.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider{margin-top:1rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem;color:var(--tg-color-subtle);display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:space-between}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider:not(:empty){gap:1rem;white-space:nowrap}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::before,.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::after{content:"";height:1px;width:100%;background-color:var(--tg-border-color-base)}</style><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...and her hair was held a marvel unmatched. It was golden like the hair of her father and of her foremother <a href="/wiki/Indis" title="Indis">Indis</a>, but richer and more radiant, for its gold was touched by some memory of the starlike silver of her mother, and the Eldar said that the light of the Two Trees had been snared in her tresses.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="/wiki/Unfinished_Tales" title="Unfinished Tales">Unfinished Tales</a></i>, &#34;<a href="/wiki/The_History_of_Galadriel_and_Celeborn" title="The History of Galadriel and Celeborn">The History of Galadriel and Celeborn</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Many thought that this first gave to [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) the idea of imprisoning and blending the light of the Trees, that later became the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils). He admired her hair so much that he requested a tress three times, but she refused each time and would not give him even a single strand.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Millennia after, [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) of the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) was asked which [gift](/wiki/Gifts_of_Galadriel "Gifts of Galadriel") he would like before leaving [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien), and he responded:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39; There is nothing, Lady Galadriel&#39;, said Gimli, bowing low and stammering. &#39;Nothing, unless it might be - unless it is permitted to ask, nay, to name a single strand of your hair, which surpasses the gold of the earth as the stars surpass the gems of the mine. I do not ask for such a gift. But you commanded me to name my desire&#39;.
</p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Galadriel's response surprised all of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves): she granted Gimli's wish and gave him three golden strands of her hair, which Gimli promised to set in crystal as a "*pledge of goodwill between the Mountain and the Wood until the end of days.*"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the early drafts of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, there is no dialogue between Galadriel and Gimli when she is giving her gifts to the Company.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In the following draft, when Gimli is asked, he answers to Galadriel that he is contented with having seen her. Then she took a brooch off herself, an emerald set in gold, and gave it to him. Gimli tried to apply the name *[Elfstone](/wiki/Elfstone)* to himself, but he was stopped by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), who decided to use that name for [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
