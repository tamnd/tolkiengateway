The river **Hoarwell**, or **Mitheithel** was a river traversing eastern [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md).

## Course {#Course}

It began in the northern [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) west of the [Rhimdath](../R/Rhimdath.md) River and about 100 miles north of [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md). The Hoarwell flowed west past the [Ettenmoors](../E/Ettenmoors.md) and then curved southward, skirting the western edge of the [Trollshaws](../T/Trollshaws.md). Flowing under the [Last Bridge](../L/Last_Bridge.md) on the [East-West Road](../E/East-West_Road.md), it coursed southward until it was joined by the [Bruinen](../B/Bruinen.md) or "Loudwater". The Hoarwell turned south-west until at [Tharbad](../T/Tharbad.md) it met with the [Glanduin](../G/Glanduin.md), forming the fenland called the [Swanfleet](../S/Swanfleet.md) or *Nîn-in-Eilph*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Beyond this point the combined waters were called the Greyflood or [Gwathló](../G/Gwathló.md).<sup id="cite_ref-AppendixD_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppendixD-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After the founding of the realm of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) in [3320](../S/Second_Age_3320.md),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> the Hoarwell was within its boundaries.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [861](../T/Third_Age_861.md),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> when Arnor was divided into the splinter-kingdoms of [Arthedain](../A/Arthedain.md), [Cardolan](../C/Cardolan.md) and [Rhudaur](../R/Rhudaur.md), the river was within the confines of Rhudaur.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Around [1150](../T/Third_Age_1150.md),<sup id="cite_ref-TA1150_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1150-8">[8]</a></sup> the [Fallohides](../F/Fallohides.md), one of the three kinds of [Hobbits](Hobbits.md), crossed over the Misty Mountains north of Rivendell, travelled down the river Hoarwell and eventually settled in Eriador.<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-9">[9]</a></sup> In the same year, the [Stoors](../S/Stoors.md), another kind of Hobbits, left their homes at the bank of the river [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) and migrated to the west,<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-9">[9]</a></sup> crossed the Misty Mountains over the [Redhorn Pass](../R/Redhorn_Gate.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA1150_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1150-8">[8]</a></sup> and followed the course of the Loudwater southwards<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-9">[9]</a></sup> and settled in the [Angle](../A/Angle.md) between the rivers Hoarwell and Loudwater or in [Dunland](../D/Dunland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-TA1150_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1150-8">[8]</a></sup>

When [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) became powerful about [1300](../T/Third_Age_1300.md), some of the hobbits moved westwards over the Hoarwell and many settled at [Bree](../B/Bree.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

Around [1409](../T/Third_Age_1409.md),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> the Stoors that had settled in the Angle fled westwards over the Hoarwell and southwards, because of the war with Angmar.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

In May [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md),<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> [Thorin and Company](../T/Thorin_and_Company.md) crossed the Hoarwell on the Last Bridge (and later that night encountered the trolls).<sup id="cite_ref-Mutton_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mutton-1">[1]</a></sup>

On [11 October](../_/11_October.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md),<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> [Glorfindel](../G/Glorfindel.md) drove the [Black Riders](../N/Nazgûl.md) from the Last Bridge and left a beryl on the roadway to show that the path was safe.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Two days later,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> after finding the elf-stone, [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) led the hobbits across the bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

## Note {#Note}

The name of the river between the confluence of the Loudwater with the Hoarwell and the confluence of the Glanduin with the combined river is not named on [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s [The First Map of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md), [The Third Map of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Third_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) or the Map of the North of Middle-earth that he drew during the writing of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*. However, it is named "River Hoarwell" and "Mitheithel" on the [General Map of Middle-earth](../G/General_Map_of_Middle-earth.md) and [The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age](../T/The_West_of_Middle-earth_at_the_End_of_the_Third_Age.md) map, respectively, which [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) drew for earlier published editions of *The Lord of the Rings* and for *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)*.

The wording in the narrative of *The Lord of the Rings* and in its Appendix A is ambiguous if that part of the river was called the Greyflood or continued to be called the Hoarwell or Mitheithel. [Strider](../A/Aragorn.md) tells the Hobbits that the Hoarwell joins the Loudwater "away in the South" and that "Some call it the Greyflood after that". However he does not specify if that means that "some" call it the Greyflood already immediately after the confluence of the two rivers or after some other point further downstream from the confluence. Appendix A mentions that the Glanduin flows into the Greyflood and that the Greyflood and the Glanduin were the southern border of Eriador. It also mentions that [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) at is greatest extent included all of Eriador, except "the lands east of Greyflood and the Loudwater, in which lay Rivendell and [Hollin](../E/Eregion.md)". However, if the river continued to be called the Hoarwell after its confluence with the Loudwater and if it only started to be called the Greyflood after the confluence of the combined river with the Glanduin, then Hollin was not east of the Greyflood, because the Glanduin formed the southern border of Hollin,<sup id="cite_ref-AppendixD_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppendixD-3">[3]</a></sup> except at the point of the confluence of the Glanduin and the Hoarwell at the very beginning of the Greyflood.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The English name *Hoarwell* has the sense of "hoary spring". It is a combination of *hoar* ("greyish-white") and [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *well* ("well", "spring", "stream").<sup id="cite_ref-UI_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-18">[18]</a></sup>

The [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name *Mitheithel* has the same meaning: "hoary spring".<sup id="cite_ref-UI_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-18">[18]</a></sup> It is composed of *[mith](../M/Mith.md)* ("pale grey") and *[eithel](../E/Eithel.md)* ("spring", "source").<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the 1937 version of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* the Hoarwell was described as a rushing *red* river coming from the mountains *in front* of the company. In the 1966 edition the adjective "red" was removed and the river came from the north in order to match the geography of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.
