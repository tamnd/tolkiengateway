The **house of Swan** is an isolated concept in the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*.

[Tuor](../T/Tuor.md) referred to himself as belonging to "the house of the Swan of the sons of the [Men of the North](<../M/Men_of_the_North_(First_Age).md>)".<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:160</sup> It may be supposed that [Peleg](../P/Peleg.md) and [Indor](../I/Indor.md) (Tuor's father and grandfather respectively in this stage of the legendarium) also belonged to this house; or, more probably, Tuor may have invented it for himself, as he had personally chosen the [Swan](../S/Swans.md) as emblem.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:151</sup> Later, the house Tuor founds in [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md) is called the "[house of the Wing](House_of_the_Wing.md)" and its emblem was the wing of a "swan or gull".<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:172</sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In a [prose fragment](../P/Prose_Fragments_Following_the_Lost_Tales.md) following the Lost Tales, [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md) changed the name and explored the concept more deeply: after the [Battle of Unnumbered Tears](../B/Battle_of_Unnumbered_Tears.md), a great part of the kindreds of Men dwelt in [Dor-lómin](../D/Dor-lómin.md) about the waters of the lake [Mithrim](../M/Mithrim.md), and they were friends of both the [Gnomes](../G/Gnomes.md) and the [Dark Elves](../I/Ilkorindi.md). They were called <i><b>Tunglin</b></i> ([Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) for "folk of the Harp"), as their joy was in the wild music.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In the later [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), the motif of the swan remained: in *[Of Tuor and his Coming to Gondolin](../O/Of_Tuor_and_his_Coming_to_Gondolin.md)* is said that the swan was the token of Tuor's foster-father [Annael](../A/Annael.md) and his folk, who were indeed "men of the North", dwelling in [Mithrim](../M/Mithrim.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondolin_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondolin-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:205</sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
