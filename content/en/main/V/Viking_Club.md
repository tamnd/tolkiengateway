The **Viking Club** was a club for philologists and historians specializing in Germanic and Scandinavian studies.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It was founded by [E.V. Gordon](/wiki/E.V._Gordon) and [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) when the two were professors at [Leeds University](/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") in the 1920s. At meetings of the club students and faculty would gather to read [Old Icelandic](/wiki/Old_Norse "Old Norse") sagas and drink together in an informal setting. Members of the club also invented original songs and poems in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic), [Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse) and other extinct Germanic languages. A collection of these works was privately published as *[Songs for the Philologists](/wiki/Songs_for_the_Philologists)*.

Similar groups have continued at Leeds University since the time of Tolkien and Gordon. As of [2015](/wiki/2015) the successor of the Viking Club was known as the Old Norse Reading Group, and associated with Gordon's distant successor, Alaric Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
