The **Viking Club** was a club for philologists and historians specializing in Germanic and Scandinavian studies.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It was founded by [E.V. Gordon](../E/E.V._Gordon.md) and [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) when the two were professors at [Leeds University](../U/University_of_Leeds.md) in the 1920s. At meetings of the club students and faculty would gather to read [Old Icelandic](../O/Old_Norse.md) sagas and drink together in an informal setting. Members of the club also invented original songs and poems in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md), [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md), [Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md) and other extinct Germanic languages. A collection of these works was privately published as *[Songs for the Philologists](../S/Songs_for_the_Philologists.md)*.

Similar groups have continued at Leeds University since the time of Tolkien and Gordon. As of [2015](../_/2015.md) the successor of the Viking Club was known as the Old Norse Reading Group, and associated with Gordon's distant successor, Alaric Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
