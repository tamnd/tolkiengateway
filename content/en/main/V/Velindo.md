**Velindo** was a [fay](/wiki/Fays "Fays") of [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB)'s folk, according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") associated with *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Veli_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Veli-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Velindo was sent by [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) into the [world](/wiki/Middle-earth "Middle-earth") in order to fetch "some of the good heavy red gold of the [dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves")". However, there he grew prideful due to the adoration of the Dwarves and [Men](/wiki/Men) owing to his skills as a craftsman.

Therefore, he never returned to Aulë, but rather set up on his own - and he grew famous, but as the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) faded so did his own power and fame, yet he never became wicked.

Ages later, the mariner [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol) asked [Rúmil](</wiki/R%C3%BAmil_(elf_of_Tirion)#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium> "Rúmil (elf of Tirion)"), an Elf of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa), if the person of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayland_the_Smith" title="wikipedia:Wayland the Smith">Wéland</a> (a renowned smith in the Germanic legends) is the same as Aulë, but Rúmil corrected him, saying rather that *Wéland* is actually Velindo.<sup id="cite_ref-Veli_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Veli-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Velindo* is in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya), with <i><b>Gwilion</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-Veli_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Veli-1">[1]</a></sup> being its [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) equivalent - however, their meaning is unclear.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Velindo's name in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) was <i><b>Wéland</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-Veli_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Veli-1">[1]</a></sup> or <i><b>Welund</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Fea_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fea-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In another contemporary text, the Germanic smith Wéland is instead identified with the [Gnome](/wiki/Gnomes "Gnomes") [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor).

That text depicts an outline of a story placed during the wars of the Elves with [Melko](/wiki/Melko), in which Fëanor was captured by a man called <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ni%C3%B0ad&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Niðad (page does not exist)">Niðad</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup> King of the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Niaroth&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Niaroth (page does not exist)">Niaroth</a>. However, Fëanor killed Niðad's sons<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 2]</a></sup> and managed to escape by crafting artificial wings for himself; he also made love to the king's daughter <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Beaduhilde&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Beaduhilde (page does not exist)">Beaduhilde</a> and fled with her, but ultimately lost her in woods.

Eventually Beaduhilde (who was now pregnant) forgave Fëanor, and in spite of her father's wrath at her pregnancy, she still managed to obtain Niðad's blessing upon her son.<sup id="cite_ref-Fea_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fea-3">[3]</a></sup>
