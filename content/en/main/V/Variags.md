The **Variags** were a little-known people who dwelt in the land of [Khand](/wiki/Khand).

## History {#History}

The *Variags* came from *Khand*, a land southeast of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It is not clear if the *Variags* are synonymous with the "men of Khand" in general, or if they are a specific group or tribe of those men. Records in Gondor mention "peoples of Khand"<sup id="cite_ref-Northmen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Northmen-2">[2]</a></sup> and it is known that the land of *Khand* was inhabited by *Variags*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) the eastern [Wainriders](/wiki/Wainriders) had been spreading southward beyond Mordor and were in conflict with their neighbours further south, the peoples of Khand. Later [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) arranged a peace and an alliance between the Wainriders and the peoples of Khand and prepared an attack on Gondor, that should be made at the same time from the north and from the south. In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1944](/wiki/Third_Age_1944 "Third Age 1944") the Wainriders and the "men of Khand" mustered a great army on the southern shores of the [Sea of Rhûn](/wiki/Sea_of_Rh%C3%BBn) and moved as fast as they could along the northern side of the [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui) and defeated the northern army of Gondor in a [battle](/wiki/Disaster_of_the_Morannon "Disaster of the Morannon") north of the [Morannon](/wiki/Morannon)<sup id="cite_ref-Northmen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Northmen-2">[2]</a></sup> and killed King [Ondoher](/wiki/Ondoher) and both his sons, [Artamir](/wiki/Artamir) and [Faramir](</wiki/Faramir_(son_of_Ondoher)> "Faramir (son of Ondoher)").<sup id="cite_ref-Wainriders_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainriders-4">[4]</a></sup> Afterwards they pursued the withdrawing survivors of the northern army of Gondor into the north of [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien), defeated the reargurad of the northern army of Gondor and killed the new commander of the northern army of Gondor, [Minohtar](/wiki/Minohtar), the son of the sister of King Ondoher.<sup id="cite_ref-Northmen_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Northmen-2">[2]</a></sup> They believed that they had defeated Gondor, set up a camp and feasted and revelled, but Eärnil, the Captain of the Southern Army, who had destroyed the army of Harad that had crossed the Poros moved north with the Southern Army as quickly as possible, gathered the retreating Northern Army of Gondor, defeated the Wainriders in the [Battle of the Camp](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Camp) and drove the surviving enemies into the [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes) where a great part of them perished.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainriders_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainriders-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) the Variags fought at the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields)<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-6">[6]</a></sup> on [15 March](/wiki/15_March) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> They were held in reserve in [Osgiliath](/wiki/Osgiliath) along with armies of [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) and [Southrons](/wiki/Southrons). After the death of the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) they were sent onto the battlefield by [Gothmog](</wiki/Gothmog_(Lieutenant_of_Morgul)> "Gothmog (Lieutenant of Morgul)"). Their ranks were among [troll-men](/wiki/Troll-men "Troll-men") and [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) when the knights of [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth) ran and drove them back.<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of the name *Variags* is unknown. J.R.R. Tolkien wrote in his [instructions](/wiki/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings") for translations of *The Lord of the Rings* into other languages that all names that were not inlcuded in a list in those instructions should not be translated. In contrast to the entry for *Corsairs* in this list where his instruction was to translate the name, the *Variags* were not included in this list.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

A draft version of what would later become appendix F mentions that the name *Variag* is a word from "the speech of Men of the East and allies of Sauron".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Wayne Hammond &amp; Christina Scull pointed out that in the "old Russian chronicle of Nestor" the name *Variags* is used for the people, which are usually named *Varangians* and which were Scandinavian rovers that overran parts of "Russia" and reached Constantinople in the 9th and 10th centuries. A group of *Varangians* formed the <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangian_Guard" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Varangian Guard</a> of the Byzantine Emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

Miryam Librán-Moreno stated that *Varyag* is the Russian name for the Varangians. The Varangians were Viking Northmen who moved eastwards and southwards through Russia, Belarus and Ukraine mainly in the 9th and 10th centuries. They regularly raided Byzantine territory and made three expeditions against Constantinople (Byzantium) in A.D. 860, 907 and 941.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

[David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) pointed out that according to Webster's Third New International Dictionary of the English Language the english word *variag* comes from the russian word *Varyag*, which is of Scandinavian origin and akin to Old Norse word *Vaeringjar*, and is another name for a Varangian. He thinks it is probable that J.R.R. Tolkien meant "barbarian mercenary" when he used the word Variag.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

[Jason Fisher](/wiki/Jason_Fisher) stated that the source of the russian word *varyag* is Germanic with the Old English cognate *wǽrgenga* ultimately sourced from Old Norse *væringi, váringi*, from Proto- Norse \*váringr, believed to derive from vár “vow, oath, pledge” and ganga “to go”, in the sense of foreigners pledged to service.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

According to Bosworth-Toller's Anglo-Saxon Dictionary the Old English word *wer-genga* means "a stranger who seeks protection in the land to which he has come". It occurs with the spelling *wærgenga* in Cædmon’s Metrical Paraphrase of parts of the Holy Scripture in Anglo-Saxon.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

It has been suggested that Tolkien adopted the Slavic term to indicate that the Variags were possibly mercenaries serving a possible Lord of Khand.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>
