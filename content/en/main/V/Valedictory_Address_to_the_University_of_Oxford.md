**Valedictory Address to the University of Oxford** refers to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s retirement speech from the [University of Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford), delivered on [5 June](/wiki/5_June) [1959](/wiki/1959) in the Merton College Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The valedictory remained unpublished until [1979](/wiki/1979), when the speech was included in *[J.R.R. Tolkien, Scholar and Storyteller: Essays in Memoriam](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_Scholar_and_Storyteller "J.R.R. Tolkien: Scholar and Storyteller")*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> A revised version was published in *[The Monsters and the Critics and Other Essays](/wiki/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics "The Monsters and the Critics")* (1983).

In his speech Tolkien observes that as he never delivered the expected inaugural address upon assuming the [Rawlinson and Bosworth Chair](/wiki/Rawlinson_and_Bosworth_Professor_of_Anglo-Saxon "Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon") 34 years earlier, he was in the awkward position of delivering an inaugural address upon retirement.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Excerpt {#Excerpt}

> *Fourteen more years have now passed, and I still have nothing special to say. Nothing, that is, of the kind proper to inaugurals -as far as I can judge by those that I have read: the products of minds more sanguine, or more efficient and magisterial than mine.* <br>
>
> *The diagnosis of what is wrong, and the confident prescription of the cure; the wide view, the masterly survey; plans and prophecies: these have never been in my line.* <br>
>
> *I would always rather try to wring the juice out of a single sentence, or explore the implications of one word than try to sum up a period in a lecture, or pot a poet in a paragraph. And I am afraid that what I would rather do is what I have usually done.*
