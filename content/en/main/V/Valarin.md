**Valarin**, also called the **Eldest Speech**,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was the [language](../L/Languages.md) of the [Ainur](../A/Ainur.md).

## Description {#Description}

As angelic beings with the ability to communicate telepathically, the Ainur had no need for a spoken language. However, it appears that Valarin was adopted as part of their assumption of physical forms.

Valarin was extremely alien to the ears of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), sometimes to the point of genuine displeasure.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The [Valar](Valar.md) therefore learned [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) and used it to converse with the Elves, or with each other if Elves were present. Very few elves ever learned the Valarin, only adopting some of the Valarin words into Quenya. The [Vanyar](Vanyar.md) adopted more words from Valarin into their dialect *[Quendya](../Q/Quendya.md)* than the [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md), as they lived closer to the Valar.

Valarin is unrelated to all the other languages of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) as it arose outside of [Arda](../A/Arda.md), and except for a few words (mainly proper names) nothing is known of the language. The language is also noted for its long words. For example, the Valarin word for [Telperion](../T/Telperion.md) is *[Ibrīniðilpathānezel](../T/Telperion.md#Other_names)*.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">[3]</a></sup>

[Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md), before the growth of his discontent, is said to have learned more of this tongue than any others before his time, and his knowledge must at any rate have far surpassed the little that is now recorded; but what he knew he kept to himself, and he refused to transmit it even to the [Lambengolmor](../L/Lambengolmor.md) because of his quarrel with the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In older versions of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* and in the [Lhammas](../L/Lhammas.md), Valarin is further subdivided in [Oromëan](../O/Oromëan.md), [Aulëan](../A/Aulëan.md) and [Melkian](../M/Melkian.md). In this conception, all [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) languages arose from Oromëan, but this view was later dropped.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Helge Fauskanger](../H/Helge_Fauskanger.md) has noted suggestions that Valarin might have been influenced by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language" title="wikipedia:Akkadian language">ancient Babylonian</a>:

"*\[...\] some feel that the general style of Valarin is reminiscent of such words as "Etemenanki", the name of the great tower (ziggurat) of Babylon. However, such views are purely conjectural, and we may rightly ask why Tolkien would use Babylonian as a model for the language of the gods of his mythos. More likely he simply aimed for a very peculiar style, since this is supposed to be a language wholly independent of the Elvish language family, and moreover a tongue developed and spoken by superhuman beings.*"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
