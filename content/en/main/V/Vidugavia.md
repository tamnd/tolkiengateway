**Vidugavia** was a prince of the [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen) who called himself "[King of Rhovanion](/wiki/King_of_Rhovanion)" and ruled a realm that was located between [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood) and the [River Running](/wiki/River_Running).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Vidugaiva was an ally of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor)'s King [Rómendacil II](/wiki/R%C3%B3mendacil_II) and had helped him in the war against the [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings). In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1250](/wiki/Third_Age_1250 "Third Age 1250") Prince [Valacar](/wiki/Valacar) was sent by his father, King Rómendacil II, to the court of Vidugavia as an ambassador to learn the language, manners and policies of the Northmen. Valacar eventually loved the Northern lands and people and married Vidugavia's daughter [Vidumavi](/wiki/Vidumavi).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-2">[2]</a></sup>

Vidugavia was apparently the father of other children, since he was also an ancestor of [Marhari](/wiki/Marhari).<sup id="cite_ref-Children_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Children-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) suggests that *Vidugavia* is the latinized spelling of the [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic) name *Widugauja*, which means "wood-dweller".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

[Jim Allan](/wiki/Jim_Allan) proposes that the name is a combination of the elements *vidu* ("wood", "forest") and *gavia* ("one of a district"). The element *gavi* means district, so *gavia* could mean "lord" (in the sense of lord of a district) or simply "man" (in the sense of a countryman). The name could be translated as "Wood-lord" or "Wood-man". The name appears in the forms *Witugouwo*, *Witicho*, *Witege* or *Wittich* in german literature as the name of a son of Weland Smith.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Gothic name *Widugauja* does not appear in historical records, but *Vidigabius* was the name of a king of the Alamanni from the fourth century and according to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes" title="wikipedia:Jordanes">Jordanes</a>, an Eastern Roman writer from the sixth century who is believed to be of Gothic descent, *Vidigoia* was the name of an old hero of the Goths.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
