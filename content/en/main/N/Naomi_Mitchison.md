**Naomi Mary Margaret Mitchison** ([November 1](/wiki/1_November "1 November"), [1897](/wiki/1897) – [January 11](/wiki/11_January "11 January"), [1999](/wiki/1999)) was a Scottish novelist and poet.

## History {#History}

Mitchison was educated in Oxford, and contributed verse to *[Oxford Poetry](/wiki/Oxford_Poetry)* for [1915](/wiki/1915) and [1916](/wiki/1916) (in the former, in company with [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s "[Goblin Feet](/wiki/Goblin_Feet)"). Eventually, she became a prolific writer of books and articles, including *Graeme and the Dragon* ([1954](/wiki/1954), illustrated by [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes)) and <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=To_the_Chapel_Perilous&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="To the Chapel Perilous (page does not exist)">To the Chapel Perilous</a></i> ([1955](/wiki/1955), which Tolkien disliked).<sup id="cite_ref-RG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RG-1">[1]</a></sup>

For many years, Mitchison was a fan of Tolkien's works, and they had a long-time correspondance. She became a proofreader of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* when [George Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/George_Allen_and_Unwin "George Allen and Unwin") sent her advance proofs of the first two volumes. Of *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*, Mitchison wrote "It is timeless and will go on".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Upon the release of the volume, she wrote the article "One Ring to Bind Them" in <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman" title="wikipedia:New Statesman">New Statesman and Nation</a></i> ([18 September](/wiki/18_September) [1954](/wiki/1954)) reviewing the book. Although she criticised some vague aspects, and scientific uncertainties of the Secondary World, she praised its geographic, cultural, historical and linguistic depth.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

However, later in life she became increasingly critical of Tolkien's works, critizing the "grandiose style" of *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)* and the lack of a feminine perspective in *The Lord of the Rings* and finding *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* simplistic.<sup id="cite_ref-RG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RG-1">[1]</a></sup>
