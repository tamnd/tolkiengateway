**nér** (pl. **neri**) means "man" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), referring to an adult male – [elf](/wiki/Elves "Elves"), [mortal](/wiki/Men "Men"), or of other [speaking race](/wiki/Races "Races").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

From [PQ](/wiki/PQ) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Nere&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nere (page does not exist)">nere</a></i>, <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=N%C4%93r&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nēr (page does not exist)">nēr</a></i> ([root](/wiki/Root "Root") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=N%C3%8AR&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="NÊR (page does not exist)">NÊR</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The [CE](/wiki/CE) form <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Nd%C3%A6%5Er&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ndæ^r (page does not exist)">ndæ^r</a></i> from PQ <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ndere&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ndere (page does not exist)">ndere</a>/<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Nd%C3%AAro&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ndêro (page does not exist)">ndêro</a></i> "bridegroom" (from [root](/wiki/Root "Root") N-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=DER&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="DER (page does not exist)">DER</a>) is also given<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Nér (and its ancient form *Ndér* has similarities with several Indo-European words for "man, husband, mister". In Albanian it's <i><a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/njer" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">njer</a></i>, in Ancient Greek <i><a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%80%CE%BD%CE%AE%CF%81" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">ανήρ</a></i> (a-nér), stem *ανδρ-* (andr-). In Sanskrit it's <i><a class="external text" href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%B0#Sanskrit" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">nara</a></i>. <span></span>
