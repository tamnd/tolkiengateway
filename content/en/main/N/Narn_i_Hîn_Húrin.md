**Narn i Hîn Húrin** ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "**Tale of the Children of Húrin**"), also called "**The Narn**", is a chapter in *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)*, a posthumously published work of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) edited and published by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien). It is a long story of all that happened to [Húrin](/wiki/H%C3%BArin) and his children [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin) and [Nienor](/wiki/Nienor), after Húrin was cursed by [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth). In 2007, Christopher Tolkien published *[The Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)*, wherein Tolkien completed the story and completed a single narrative of this tale with minimal editorial presence.

## Story {#Story}

The story elaborates on what is told of these characters in the published *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, starting with the childhood of Túrin, continuing through the captivity of his father in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad), Túrin's exile in [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), to Túrin's time in [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond) and ending with his suicide after having slain [Glaurung](/wiki/Glaurung).

The story has some inconsistencies when compared with *The Silmarillion*, and at points there are gaps and multiple versions. This is because [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") never finished the story during his lifetime, and his son [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) had to choose from all the work to create a consistent narrative for *The Silmarillion*.

The story of the *Narn* continues in the *Later Narn*, which is published in *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)*, and in the "Wanderings of Húrin", a text which was found to be too different in style from the rest of *The Silmarillion*, but which continues the *Narn* after Túrin's death. It ends with Húrin's eventual release and the bad deeds which follow him. This story was finally published in *[The War of the Jewels](/wiki/The_War_of_the_Jewels)*.

## Publishing notes {#Publishing_notes}

Note that the title of the *Narn* is wrongly given as **Narn i Hîn Húrin** in the published *Silmarillion* and *Unfinished Tales*. This was an editorial decision by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) which he later regretted, done only to prevent people from pronouncing *[Chîn](/wiki/H%C3%AEn "Hîn")* like the English "chin".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
