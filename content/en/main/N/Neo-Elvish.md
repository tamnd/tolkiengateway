**Neo-[Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md)** and/or **Neo-[Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md)** (collectively also called **Neo-[Elvish](../E/Elvish.md)**) represent attempts of post-Tolkien use of the Elvish languages. The attempts include a productive standardization, regularization and even reconstruction of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s languages with the intent to be taught, studied and be used in [fanfic](../F/Fanfic.md) compositions or even in dialogue. The terms Neo- arose so as to distinguish those attempts from the [canonical](../C/Canon.md) creations by Tolkien.

It must be noted that Neo-Elvish does not refer to original *a priori* creations; Neo-Elvish forms and grammar emerge from comparative and reconstructive methods from the canonical sources, albeit with varying arbitrarity.

## Overview of the problem {#Overview_of_the_problem}

In his youth, Tolkien composed a grammar on the language called [Goldogrin](../G/Goldogrin.md). He also attempted to compose an [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) grammar which he left unfinished. Other than these, he did not leave behind a definite set of rules for his later languages (which we would accept as "canonical") because he did not intend to create them with the purpose to be usable. As a result, the information gathered posthumously from his notes might seem like fluid and fragmentary to someone who seeks to see a possible 'whole picture'. The absence of a final, canonical Quenya or Sindarin has the consequence that anyone attempting to compile a grammar would eventually create a conventional and subjective grammar.

In order to 'fill the gaps', such a person would rely on speculation, personal instinct and widely subjective interpretation. Coping with Tolkien's continuous mutually exclusive revisions, the student would be forced to become selective and follow some priorities; this usually means a tendecy to reject Tolkien's older, temporary or 'anomalous' conceptions in favor of those that (for whatever reason) seem most stable and plausibly canonical.

For example, [Helge Fauskanger](../H/Helge_Fauskanger.md)'s grammar of Quenya, being such an attempt, is described as a: "*synthetic and regularized form of Quenya formed by the selective piecing-together of evidences from across decades of Tolkien's successive versions and elaborations of Quenya*".<sup id="cite_ref-resources_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-resources-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Attempts to study or write in [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) date back to at least the 1970s. An example composition of that era is *Valinorenna* by <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Bj%C3%B6rn_From%C3%A9n&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Björn Fromén (page does not exist)">Björn Fromén</a>, published in the fanzine *[Palantiren](../P/Palantiren.md)* 3 in [1973](../_/1973.md), using vocabulary only from words found in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Attempts continued the following decades, thanks to the publication of more of Tolkien's works, including the *[History of Middle-earth](../H/History_of_Middle-earth.md)* series, with most importantly, *[The Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)* ([1987](../_/1987.md)), and the work of the [Elvish Linguistic Fellowship](../E/Elvish_Linguistic_Fellowship.md).

Such publications allowed the students to infer some grammar rules from Tolkien's writings, and also made known the etymological-derivational mechanics in which the Elvish languages work. It was thus made possible to create Sindarin words from Quenya cognates or artificially derive new words from related ones, or from [Proto-Quendian](../P/Proto-Quendian.md) [roots](../R/Root.md).

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/D/David_Salo.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5b/David_Salo.jpg/300px-David_Salo.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="460" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/5/5b/David_Salo.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="391" data-file-height="600"/></a><figcaption><a href="../D/David_Salo.md" title="David Salo">David Salo</a> has made attempts for the standardization of Sindarin and is often cited as an example of neo-Elvish proponents.</figcaption></figure>

Attempts to write in Tolkien's languages include [fanfiction](../F/Fanfiction.md), tattoos or creations written in [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md), translations of Tolkien's works into Elvish,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> translation of pre-existing literature such as Biblical passages, Christian prayers, original short poems and stories etc. Linguistic plays included the composition of (Neo-)Quenya poems without containing the very frequent vowel *a*<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> or the translation of *[Namárië](Namárië.md)* to (Neo-)[Telerin](../T/Telerin.md)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> or (Neo-)Sindarin.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Of course, the resulting 'literature' by no means represents a universal homogeneous consensus on Neo-Elvish. Each work reflects the personal understanding and preferences of its author at the time of writing and the conventions used can be mutually exclusive among authors.

Similar attempts were made to reconstruct Elvish grammar rules, such as inferring a full working pronominal system based on Primitive Quendian evidence;<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> a system according to which the Sindarin verbs are possibly conjugated;<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> or the usage of [Noldorin](Noldorin.md) language of *The Etymologies* to create Sindarin forms. Such interpretations were partially obsoleted by later publications of Tolkien's papers, but offered a framework at their time.

The most prominent appearance of Neo-Elvish was in [Peter Jackson's *The Lord of the Rings*](../P/Peter_Jackson's_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) movie trilogy, which made use of linguist [David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md)'s reconstructions. The movie also featured original lyrics in (Neo-)Elvish, (Neo-)[Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md), (Neo-)[Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md) and other languages. However, the scarcity of information on these languages necessitated wholly original <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_priori_(languages)" title="wikipedia:A priori (languages)">a priori</a></i> creation of vocabulary and grammar rules.

The release of the films also boosted a temporary interest in studying and using Elvish.

## Criticism {#Criticism}

While the understanding of [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md)'s languages is a direct result of serious study and [Tolkienology](../T/Tolkienology.md) and is solely based on canonical linguistic evidence, the quest for Neo-Elvish borders to [fanon](../F/Fanon.md); critics speak about the "*problematic and illusory nature of \[Neo-Elvish\] as anything other than an approximation and introduction to the study of Tolkien's own linguistic inventions*".<sup id="cite_ref-resources_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-resources-1">[1]</a></sup>

Critics say that Tolkien's intention did not move much further than simple creativity and experimentation and his languages are fluid in their very nature; standardizing or enriching them would only result in new original creations. They even claim that, since finalization was never a goal of Tolkien, uncovering the full range of variation in Tolkien's conception of his languages will only make a complete, definite form less rather than more achievable.

Other common fields of criticism have to do with

- subjective conventions which eventually are presented and passed over and finally adopted as facts by newcomers to the field;
- the arbitrary distinction of canon; a preference towards "mature Elvish" and rejection of early [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) and [Noldorin](Noldorin.md) (whereas Tolkien never specifically divided the continuity of his languages as such);
- the "artificial" distinction between "valid", LotR-style forms vs "obsolete", inconsistent early forms.
- the simultaneous adoption of those very same Qenya and Noldorin sources to supplement the (Neo-)Quenya and Sindarin arsenal;
- sources that 'promote' Neo-Elvish, are usually criticized for providing inadequate references; making no distinction between proper Quenya/Sindarin sources; selective or artificial normalizations; selective imports from early Elvish; not making clear the points where the author made personal interpretations. Thus, these sources deprive the reader of the whole image.

As an example, *[A Gateway to Sindarin](../A/A_Gateway_to_Sindarin.md)* was a recent target of criticism for attempting to present a standard ("fabricated") Sindarin, yet being subject to the above failures. Among others, the book describes a plural form of Sindarin gerunds, while such a function was never described or appeared in Tolkien's writings.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Vocabulary {#Vocabulary}

As mentioned earlier neo-Elvish vocabulary is not *a priori*, but it is derived from the existing canon by means of reconstruction or analogy. Methods to create a needed word is:

*(Neo-Elvish words are marked with a #)*

- Analyse the etymology of a compound word and isolate the words it is composed of.

<dl><dd><dl><dd>Examples are #<i>hantale</i> &#34;thanksgiving&#34; from <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Eruhantale.md" title="Eruhantale">Eruhantale</a></i>, which can further be analysed to derive from #<i>hanta-</i> &#34;to thank&#34; (cf. <i>maptale</i> &#34;seizure&#34; from <i>mapta-</i> &#34;to seize&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> Another example is #<i><a href="../C/Corma.md" title="Corma">corma</a></i> &#34;ring&#34; from the compound <i><a href="../C/Corma.md" title="Corma">Cormacolindor</a></i> &#34;<a class="mw-redirect" href="../R/Ring-bearer.md" title="Ring-bearer">Ring-bearers</a>&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup></dd></dl></dd></dl>

- Combine a known word-stem with a known [affix](../../category/A/Affixes.md).

<dl><dd><dl><dd>For example the early #<i>tyelpea</i> Q &#34;silver (adj)&#34; from <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../T/Tyelpe.md" title="Tyelpe">tyelpe</a></i> and the common adjectival ending <i>-ea</i> (cf. <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Laurea&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Laurea (page does not exist)">laurea</a></i> &#34;golden&#34;).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> The word has been superceded by canonical <i>telpina</i> and <i>telemna</i><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup></dd></dl></dd></dl>

- Create a meaningful compound from existing Elvish words.

<dl><dd><dl><dd>An example is Q #<i>lapselunga</i> for &#34;pregnant&#34; (lit. &#34;baby-heavy&#34;); compound of <i>lapse</i> &#34;babe&#34; and <i>lunga</i> &#34;heavy&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-newwords_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newwords-14">[14]</a></sup></dd></dl></dd></dl>

- Borrow words from earlier versions of the languages ([Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md), [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md), [Noldorin](Noldorin.md)) and "update" them according to (mostly) regular changes made by Tolkien in the process.

<dl><dd><dl><dd>The Q word #<i>vandil</i> for &#34;staff&#34;, from early Quenya <i>vandl</i><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> since no word can end in <i>-dl</i> in canonical Quenya. This is based on the Qenya word <i>findl</i>, later updated by Tolkien as <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Findil&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Findil (page does not exist)">findil</a></i>.</dd>
<dd>The <a class="external text" href="http://www.jrrvf.com/hisweloke/sindar/online/english.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Sindarin Dictionary</a> maintained by <a href="../D/Didier_Willis.md" title="Didier Willis">Didier Willis</a> includes a version containing Noldorin words from the <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Etymologies.md" title="Etymologies">Etymologies</a></i> &#34;normalized&#34; to Sindarin according to how similar words appear in <i>LotR</i> or post-<i>LotR</i> texts. For example such Noldorin words as <i>bein, bui, bior, camland, rhoeg</i> have been updated to Sindarin as <i>#bain, #boe, #bŷr, #cae, #camlann, #raeg</i> although they haven&#39;t been confirmed so by published Tolkien texts.</dd></dl></dd></dl>

- Translate or derive a cognate from Sindarin to Quenya or vice-versa and create supposed cognates.

<dl><dd><dl><dd>The <i><a href="../K/King&#39;s_Letter.md" title="King&#39;s Letter">King&#39;s Letter</a></i> contains the Sindarin word <i>egor</i> &#34;or&#34;. Some authors have derived <i>egor</i> from possible <a href="../O/Old_Sindarin.md" title="Old Sindarin">Old Sindarin</a> *<i>(h)ekr(a)</i>. This word could have the supposed Quenya reflex #<i>ecar</i> or #<i>herca</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-newwords_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newwords-14">[14]</a></sup> Note however that the word has been superceded by the canonical <i>hya</i><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> which is unrelated</dd></dl></dd></dl>

- Derive a word from a [Primitive Elvish](../P/Primitive_Elvish.md) root and [affixes](../../category/A/Affixes.md).

<dl><dd><dl><dd>An example is #<i>yungwa</i> for &#34;appliance&#34;, supposed derivative of PE *#<i>yukmā</i> from the attested root YUK &#34;use&#34; and the frequent nominative affix <i><a href="../_/-ma.md" title="-ma">-ma</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> The word mirrors the canonical Quenya process <i>km</i> &gt; <i>ngw</i> as in the word <i><a href="../T/Tengwa.md" title="Tengwa">tengwa</a></i> (from PE <i>tekmā</i>).</dd></dl></dd></dl>

Around [2000](../_/2000.md), some wordlists of already established neo-vocabulary have been compiled online in order to facilitate authors who might need useful words for newer compositions, and likewise establish a fan "canon" or word repository.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>
