**NID** is a [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) [root](/wiki/Root "Root") meaning "press, thrust, force (in a given direction)".<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Development in Primitive Quendian {#Development_in_Primitive_Quendian}

From NID was possibly derived **inidō**, which meant "the mind in its purposing faculty, the will".<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup> This may have been a compound with **IN** ("mind", "inner self")<sup id="cite_ref-pe17_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:155</sup> and the genitive suffix "-ō". In this origin, inidō would loosely translate to "(the directive) force of the inner self".

Inidō shifted into *in'dō*, and then *[indo](/wiki/Indo "Indo")* with a verbal form *indu-*.<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Development in Quenya {#Development_in_Quenya}

Through the [Noldorin Quenya](/wiki/Noldorin_Quenya) phonetic development of (d &gt; ř &gt; r),<sup id="cite_ref-pe17_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:129</sup> NID became the basic verb <i><b>nír</b></i> with the same meaning.<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>

Nír had very broad application, and could refer to any type of pressure including physical pressure exerted by or against inanimate objects. In the case of force by a rational will (a faculty of the [fëa](/wiki/F%C3%ABa "Fëa") and [Ainur](/wiki/Ainur)), the more specific *indu-* was used.<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Development in Sindarin {#Development_in_Sindarin}

In [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) NID shifted phonologically to **nîð**, and narrowed in meaning to only rational purpose or will.<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>

*Indu* shifted phonologically (indu &gt; ind &gt; inn), and *inn* came to mean some *particular* purpose or intention of an individual.<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Development in Telerin {#Development_in_Telerin}

In [Telerin](/wiki/Telerin) *nīde* had the same meaning as S. *nîð*, and *indo* had the same meaning as S. *inn*. The verb *nid-* had the same meaning as Q. *nir*.<sup id="cite_ref-pe22_165_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe22_165-1">[1]</a></sup>
