**Nogrod** was one of two great underground [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") [cities](/wiki/Dwarf_realms "Dwarf realms") in the [Ered Luin](/wiki/Blue_Mountains "Blue Mountains"), the other being [Belegost](/wiki/Belegost), that prospered during the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age). It was home to the [Dwarves of Nogrod](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Nogrod).

## Geography {#Geography}

Nogrod was delved in the eastern side of the north-central part and of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) southeast of [Mount Dolmed](/wiki/Dolmed "Dolmed")<sup id="cite_ref-Sindar_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sindar-1">[1]</a></sup> where the Dwarf-road of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) crossed into [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador),<sup id="cite_ref-MI_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MI-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> guarding one of the only passes over the Blue Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> not far from lake [Nenuial](/wiki/Nenuial).<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The city was built sometime during the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees) when the western [Fathers of the Dwarves](/wiki/Fathers_of_the_Dwarves) awoke from beneath the [Ered Luin](/wiki/Ered_Luin).<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-2">[2]</a></sup>

Nogrod traded throughout Beleriand and the Dwarves were employed for delvings and crafts, most famously the [Nauglamir](/wiki/Nauglamir) for King [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> [Eöl](/wiki/E%C3%B6l) the Dark Elf often went there, as did his son [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin).<sup id="cite_ref-Sindar_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sindar-1">[1]</a></sup>

At the end of the First Age, Nogrod was ruined in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and around the [fortieth year](/wiki/Second_Age_40 "Second Age 40") of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) the Dwarves of the Blue Mountains began to migrate to [Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Moria "Moria"), abandoning Nogrod and [Belegost](/wiki/Belegost).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> However, there always remained some Dwarves on the eastern side of the Blue Mountains in days afterwards.<sup id="cite_ref-Concerning_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Concerning-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Nogrod* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name; it was originally known as <i><b>Novrod</b></i> which means "hollow delving" aka <i><b>Hollowbold</b></i>, like its original [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) name <i><b>Tumunzahar</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-Sindar_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sindar-1">[1]</a></sup> and the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) translation <i><b>Návarot</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

*Novrod* was altered to *Naug*rod under the influence of the similar-sounding word *[naug](/wiki/Naug "Naug")* ("dwarf").<sup id="cite_ref-el_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-el-12">[12]</a></sup> Therefore while the name **Hollowbold** is provided as the translation of *Nogrod*<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> it is not a literal translation; the new name means rather "Dwarf dwelling".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

The second element of *Novrod/Nogrod* is Sindarin *groth/grod* ("delving, underground dwelling").<sup id="cite_ref-el_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-el-12">[12]</a></sup>

In the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) phase of the language, it contains the element *[naug](/wiki/Naug "Naug")* ("dwarf"). The second element *-rod* is not explained, but a note by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) points to entry <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=ROD&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="ROD (page does not exist)">ROD</a>, an etymological [root](/wiki/Root "Root") meaning "cave". Relevant Noldorin words include *rhond* ("cave") and *rhaud* ("hollow, cavernous").<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earliest version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, Nogrod was located "a very long journey southward beyond the wide forest on the borders of those great heaths nigh Umboth-muilin the Pools of Twilight, on the marches of [Tasarinan](/wiki/Tasarinan)".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

In *[The Lay of the Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)* the Dwarves of Nogrod were traders, bringing wine of [Dor-Winion](/wiki/Dor-Winion) "that is bruised from the berries of the burning South" by long ways to the lands of the North, including Doriath.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

On the Eastward Extension of [the First 'Silmarillion' Map](/wiki/The_First_%27Silmarillion%27_Map "The First 'Silmarillion' Map"), the [Dwarf-road](/wiki/Dwarf-road_of_Beleriand "Dwarf-road of Beleriand") crosses the Blue Mountains below Mount Dolmed, then turns to the south and continues until the edge of the map with the direction "Southward in East feet of Blue Mountans are Belegost and Nogrod".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> In the second version of [the Earliest *Annals of Beleriand*](/wiki/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Beleriand "The Earliest Annals of Beleriand")<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> and in [the Later *Annals of Beleriand*](/wiki/The_Later_Annals_of_Beleriand "The Later Annals of Beleriand"),<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> Nogrod was in the east of the Blue Mountains and far south of Beleriand.

The position of Belegost was later moved by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) to the north of Mount Dolmed on a photocopy of [the Second 'Silmarillion' Map](/wiki/The_Second_%27Silmarillion%27_Map "The Second 'Silmarillion' Map").<sup id="cite_ref-MI_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MI-3">[3]</a></sup> This corresponds with its position in the *[Quenta Silmarillion](</wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road)> "Quenta Silmarillion (Lost Road)")* from [1937](/wiki/1937),<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> in the revised version of [the Later *Quenta Silmarillion*](/wiki/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion "The Later Quenta Silmarillion"),<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> and in the *[Grey Annals](/wiki/Grey_Annals)*.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>

Although the locations of Nogrod and Mount Dolmed are not indicated on the *[General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth)* and on the map of *[The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age](/wiki/The_West_of_Middle-earth_at_the_End_of_the_Third_Age)*, Mount Dolmed can be identified as the westernmost mountain in the middle of the part of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains), which is north of the [Gulf of Lune](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lune) by using the position of Mount Dolmed relative to the positions of the Hill of Himring and the highland of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Taur-nu-Fuin" title="Taur-nu-Fuin">Taur-nu-Fuin</a> on the *[Map of Beleriand and the Lands to the North](/wiki/Map_of_Beleriand_and_the_Lands_to_the_North)* and determining the position of Nogrod and Mount Dolmed relative to the positions of their respective remains after the War of Wrath, the island of [Himling](/wiki/Himring "Himring") and the island of [Tol Fuin](/wiki/Tol_Fuin),<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> on [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien)'s original map of The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> This location is on a similar latitude as lake Nenuial.
