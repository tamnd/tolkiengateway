**Númenórean Sindarin**<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup> was a dialect of [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) spoken by the [Númenóreans](Númenóreans.md) and later the [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md) in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).

## History {#History}

### Before the Downfall {#Before_the_Downfall}

When the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) settled in [Númenor](Númenor.md), the common speech of the new realm derived in the most part from the language of the [People of Hador](../P/People_of_Hador.md), giving rise to the [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> In the northwestern parts of the island, however, the newly settled [Edain](../E/Edain.md) were mainly descended from the [Bëorians](../B/Bëorians.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> These people had early abandoned their own language for [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) and they preserved this tradition in [Númenor](Númenor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> Thus a dialect of [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md), Númenórean Sindarin, evolved, especially in [Andustar](../A/Andustar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> There was relatively little change in this version of [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) due to continued contact between the [Númenóreans](Númenóreans.md) and the [Eldar of Eressëa](../E/Eldar_of_Eressëa.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup>

Although relations between the [Númenórean rulers](../K/King_of_Númenórë.md) and the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) declined in the later history of [Númenor](Númenor.md),<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup> Sindarin was kept alive, and survived in secret.

The [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md), who held dominions on the coast of the [Westlands](../W/Westlands.md), used this tongue for all the names of places that they gave in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup> All men of [Númenórean](Númenórean.md) descent and those taught to read and write used Sindarin, even as a daily tongue among themselves. In some families, Sindarin even became the native tongue. Sindarin was not taught those of lesser race, as it was held a mark of Númenórean descent.<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

### After the Downfall {#After_the_Downfall}

After the Downfall, Sindarin was the regular spoken tongue of the [Exiles of Númenor](../E/Exiles_of_Númenor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> When they arrived, few among the colonists on the coasts remembered Sindarin since the neglected [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) was used as a *lingua franca* among the Men of Middle-earth, which developed into the [Westron](../W/Westron.md) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

The [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md) of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) and [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md); the kings and high lords, and indeed all those of Númenórean blood in any degree, used this tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Many of the [Men of Gondor](../M/Men_of_Gondor.md) could also speak it, a notable distinction and characteristic among the [Dúnedain of the South](../D/Dúnedain_of_the_South.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup> Sindarin had long ceased to be a "first language" in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), but was learned in youth (by those claiming [Númenórean](Númenórean.md) descent) from loremasters, and used by them as a mark of rank and high-blood.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup> For example, the [Stewards of Gondor](../S/Stewards_of_Gondor.md) belonged to a [family](../H/House_of_Húrin.md) of the ancient Faithful who used (beside the [Common Speech](../C/Common_Speech.md)) the Sindarin tongue after the fashion of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup>

Being a learned language in Gondor, Sindarin had changed very little since the Downfall<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup> and schools taught an ancient form (regarded in high esteem).<sup id="cite_ref-atani_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-9">[9]</a></sup>

[Westron](../W/Westron.md) became used more and more by the [Dúnedain of Gondor](../D/Dúnedain_of_Gondor.md) themselves, so that at the time of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), Númenórean Sindarin was known to only a small part of the peoples of Gondor (and spoken daily by fewer); they dwelt mostly in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) and the adjacent townlands, and in the land of the tributary princes of [Dol Amroth](../D/Dol_Amroth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup> Númenórean Sindarin was used to be polite, especially in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md).<sup id="cite_ref-quenya_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quenya-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Peculiarities {#Peculiarities}

Although taught an archaic language, the [Men of Gondor](../M/Men_of_Gondor.md) used a corrupted version in daily speech (some sounds were altered) distinguising it from the Sindarin proper as spoken by the [Elves](../E/Elves.md).<sup id="cite_ref-atani_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-9">[9]</a></sup> Despite some differences, the dialects of the Gondorians and the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) were mutually intelligible.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Phonetical {#Phonetical}

Like any acquired language, the second language's sound range is directly influenced by the speaker's original sound range. Westron did not possess *ch*<sup id="cite_ref-CE49_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE49-11">[11]</a></sup> or *y*,<sup id="cite_ref-AppE_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppE-12">[12]</a></sup> and pronounced them differently.

The *y* was pronounced by Sindarin Elves as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet" title="wikipedia:International Phonetic Alphabet">IPA</a> \[y\], a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_front_rounded_vowel" title="wikipedia:Close front rounded vowel">close front rounded vowel</a>. Of all languages, only Sindarin had this sound, so it was problematic for speakers of other tongues. Gondorians generally pronounced it as an *i*,<sup id="cite_ref-AppE_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppE-12">[12]</a></sup> though it was sometimes substituted with an *e*, as in the Gondorian plural for <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Onod&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Onod (page does not exist)">onod</a></i>, *ened* (rather than the usual *enyd*).<sup id="cite_ref-L144_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-13">[13]</a></sup>

Also frequent in Sindarin was the *ch*, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/velar_fricative" title="wikipedia:velar fricative">velar fricative</a>, which the Gondorians also encountered among [pre-Númenórean](../P/Pre-Númenórean.md) placenames such as [Erech](../E/Erech.md)<sup id="cite_ref-L297_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-14">[14]</a></sup> and [Eilenach](../E/Eilenach.md).<sup id="cite_ref-CE51_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE51-15">[15]</a></sup>

The most notable use of the voiceless velar fricative was in the name of Gondor's new northern neighbour, [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md). Originally envisioned as *Rochand*, in Gondor this became *Rohan*, because in the middle of words, *ch* developed to *h*; however at the end of words, it became *c (k)* (so *roch* “horse” on its own would become [\*](Neo-Elvish.md)*roc*), because in that position it had been articulated more forcefully in the correct pronunciation already.<sup id="cite_ref-CE49_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE49-11">[11]</a></sup> In the beginning of words, *ch* had become *h* in Standard Sindarin already, so no differences in the Gondorian pronunciation are to be expected.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Though [the tongue of the Éothéod](../R/Rohanese.md) did possess the voiced *ch*, it adopted the southern use.<sup id="cite_ref-CE49_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE49-11">[11]</a></sup>

### Philological {#Philological}

Another notable difference from regular Sindarin was purely <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology" title="wikipedia:Philology">philological</a>. Those Gondorians learned in lore wished to speak like [Noldor](Noldor.md), and the Sindarin they spoke in the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) was [North Sindarin](North_Sindarin.md). At least one feature from North Sindarin was reintroduced: whereas "true Sindarin of the Elves" changed both the voiced and voiceless combination of a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/sonorant" title="wikipedia:sonorant">sonorant consonant</a> and a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fricative_consonant" title="wikipedia:Fricative consonant">spirant</a> to a long sonorant, the Gondor Sindarin retained the spirant. Thus in the case of the former, *[malt](../M/Malt.md)* ("gold") and *[orn](../O/Orn.md)* ("tree") became *[Mallorn](../M/Mallorn.md)*, in Gondor this remained *Malthorn*.<sup id="cite_ref-VT4227_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT4227-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

Another example is the word [rath](../R/Rath.md), which originally meant 'climb', but came to be applied to long streets (and was much later used in [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md) to name streets such as [Rath Celerdain](../R/Rath_Celerdain.md) and [Rath Dínen](../R/Rath_Dínen.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup>
