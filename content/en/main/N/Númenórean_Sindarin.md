**Númenórean Sindarin**<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup> was a dialect of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) spoken by the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) and later the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).

## History {#History}

### Before the Downfall {#Before_the_Downfall}

When the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) settled in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), the common speech of the new realm derived in the most part from the language of the [People of Hador](/wiki/People_of_Hador), giving rise to the [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> In the northwestern parts of the island, however, the newly settled [Edain](/wiki/Edain) were mainly descended from the [Bëorians](/wiki/B%C3%ABorians).<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> These people had early abandoned their own language for [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and they preserved this tradition in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> Thus a dialect of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), Númenórean Sindarin, evolved, especially in [Andustar](/wiki/Andustar).<sup id="cite_ref-Wife_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wife-3">[3]</a></sup> There was relatively little change in this version of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) due to continued contact between the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) and the [Eldar of Eressëa](/wiki/Eldar_of_Eress%C3%ABa).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup>

Although relations between the [Númenórean rulers](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3r%C3%AB "King of Númenórë") and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) declined in the later history of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor),<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup> Sindarin was kept alive, and survived in secret.

The [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful), who held dominions on the coast of the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands), used this tongue for all the names of places that they gave in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup> All men of [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) descent and those taught to read and write used Sindarin, even as a daily tongue among themselves. In some families, Sindarin even became the native tongue. Sindarin was not taught those of lesser race, as it was held a mark of Númenórean descent.<sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

### After the Downfall {#After_the_Downfall}

After the Downfall, Sindarin was the regular spoken tongue of the [Exiles of Númenor](/wiki/Exiles_of_N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> When they arrived, few among the colonists on the coasts remembered Sindarin since the neglected [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) was used as a *lingua franca* among the Men of Middle-earth, which developed into the [Westron](/wiki/Westron) tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Atani_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atani-5">[5]</a></sup>

The [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor); the kings and high lords, and indeed all those of Númenórean blood in any degree, used this tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Many of the [Men of Gondor](/wiki/Men_of_Gondor) could also speak it, a notable distinction and characteristic among the [Dúnedain of the South](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_the_South).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup> Sindarin had long ceased to be a "first language" in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), but was learned in youth (by those claiming [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) descent) from loremasters, and used by them as a mark of rank and high-blood.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup> For example, the [Stewards of Gondor](/wiki/Stewards_of_Gondor) belonged to a [family](/wiki/House_of_H%C3%BArin "House of Húrin") of the ancient Faithful who used (beside the [Common Speech](/wiki/Common_Speech)) the Sindarin tongue after the fashion of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup>

Being a learned language in Gondor, Sindarin had changed very little since the Downfall<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup> and schools taught an ancient form (regarded in high esteem).<sup id="cite_ref-atani_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-9">[9]</a></sup>

[Westron](/wiki/Westron) became used more and more by the [Dúnedain of Gondor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Gondor) themselves, so that at the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), Númenórean Sindarin was known to only a small part of the peoples of Gondor (and spoken daily by fewer); they dwelt mostly in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) and the adjacent townlands, and in the land of the tributary princes of [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-languages_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-languages-4">[4]</a></sup> Númenórean Sindarin was used to be polite, especially in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith).<sup id="cite_ref-quenya_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quenya-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Peculiarities {#Peculiarities}

Although taught an archaic language, the [Men of Gondor](/wiki/Men_of_Gondor) used a corrupted version in daily speech (some sounds were altered) distinguising it from the Sindarin proper as spoken by the [Elves](/wiki/Elves).<sup id="cite_ref-atani_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atani-9">[9]</a></sup> Despite some differences, the dialects of the Gondorians and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) were mutually intelligible.<sup id="cite_ref-Elendil_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elendil-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Phonetical {#Phonetical}

Like any acquired language, the second language's sound range is directly influenced by the speaker's original sound range. Westron did not possess *ch*<sup id="cite_ref-CE49_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE49-11">[11]</a></sup> or *y*,<sup id="cite_ref-AppE_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppE-12">[12]</a></sup> and pronounced them differently.

The *y* was pronounced by Sindarin Elves as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet" title="wikipedia:International Phonetic Alphabet">IPA</a> \[y\], a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_front_rounded_vowel" title="wikipedia:Close front rounded vowel">close front rounded vowel</a>. Of all languages, only Sindarin had this sound, so it was problematic for speakers of other tongues. Gondorians generally pronounced it as an *i*,<sup id="cite_ref-AppE_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppE-12">[12]</a></sup> though it was sometimes substituted with an *e*, as in the Gondorian plural for <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Onod&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Onod (page does not exist)">onod</a></i>, *ened* (rather than the usual *enyd*).<sup id="cite_ref-L144_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-13">[13]</a></sup>

Also frequent in Sindarin was the *ch*, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/velar_fricative" title="wikipedia:velar fricative">velar fricative</a>, which the Gondorians also encountered among [pre-Númenórean](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean "Pre-Númenórean") placenames such as [Erech](/wiki/Erech)<sup id="cite_ref-L297_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-14">[14]</a></sup> and [Eilenach](/wiki/Eilenach).<sup id="cite_ref-CE51_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE51-15">[15]</a></sup>

The most notable use of the voiceless velar fricative was in the name of Gondor's new northern neighbour, [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan). Originally envisioned as *Rochand*, in Gondor this became *Rohan*, because in the middle of words, *ch* developed to *h*; however at the end of words, it became *c (k)* (so *roch* “horse” on its own would become [\*](/wiki/Neo-Elvish "Neo-Elvish")*roc*), because in that position it had been articulated more forcefully in the correct pronunciation already.<sup id="cite_ref-CE49_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE49-11">[11]</a></sup> In the beginning of words, *ch* had become *h* in Standard Sindarin already, so no differences in the Gondorian pronunciation are to be expected.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Though [the tongue of the Éothéod](/wiki/Rohanese "Rohanese") did possess the voiced *ch*, it adopted the southern use.<sup id="cite_ref-CE49_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE49-11">[11]</a></sup>

### Philological {#Philological}

Another notable difference from regular Sindarin was purely <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology" title="wikipedia:Philology">philological</a>. Those Gondorians learned in lore wished to speak like [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor), and the Sindarin they spoke in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) was [North Sindarin](/wiki/North_Sindarin). At least one feature from North Sindarin was reintroduced: whereas "true Sindarin of the Elves" changed both the voiced and voiceless combination of a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/sonorant" title="wikipedia:sonorant">sonorant consonant</a> and a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fricative_consonant" title="wikipedia:Fricative consonant">spirant</a> to a long sonorant, the Gondor Sindarin retained the spirant. Thus in the case of the former, *[malt](/wiki/Malt "Malt")* ("gold") and *[orn](/wiki/Orn "Orn")* ("tree") became *[Mallorn](/wiki/Mallorn)*, in Gondor this remained *Malthorn*.<sup id="cite_ref-VT4227_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT4227-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

Another example is the word [rath](/wiki/Rath "Rath"), which originally meant 'climb', but came to be applied to long streets (and was much later used in [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) to name streets such as [Rath Celerdain](/wiki/Rath_Celerdain) and [Rath Dínen](/wiki/Rath_D%C3%ADnen)).<sup id="cite_ref-UT_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-2">[2]</a></sup>
