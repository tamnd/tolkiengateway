The **North Germanic languages**, **Norse** or **Scandinavian languages** make up one of the three branches of the Germanic languages, a sub-family of the Indo-European languages, along with the West Germanic languages and the extinct East Germanic languages like [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md).

In a letter to [Naomi Mitchison](Naomi_Mitchison.md), [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) wrote that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The <a href="../L/Language_of_Dale.md" title="Language of Dale">language of Dale</a> and the <a href="../L/Long_Lake.md" title="Long Lake">Long Lake</a> would, if it appeared, be represented as more or less Scandinavian in character; but it is only represented by a few names, especially those of the Dwarves that came from that region. These are all Old Norse Dwarf-names.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../L/Letter_144.md" title="Letter 144">Letter 144</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

Most of the non-English names in *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* are Norse (mostly from a list of Dwarves found in *Voluspa* and the *Gylfaginning*)<sup id="cite_ref-intro_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-1">[1]</a></sup> although anglicized, such as [Gandalfr](../G/Gandalf.md), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../D/Durin.md" title="Durin">Durinn</a>, [Dvalinn](../D/Dwalin.md), [Þrór](../T/Thrór.md), [Eikinskjaldi](../O/Oakenshield.md), [Jarknasteinn](../A/Arkenstone.md), [Beorn](../B/Beorn.md), [Bard](../B/Bard.md), [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md) and [Golfimbul](../G/Golfimbul.md).

In the backstory developed for *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, Tolkien explained that [Durin's Folk](../D/Durin's_Folk~15256.md), after migrating to [Erebor](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md), adopted the language of Dale for their names when interacting with [Men](../M/Men.md) because their secretive nature prevented them from using [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Names in *The Lord of the Rings* like [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md), [Narvi](Narvi.md) and [Forn](../T/Tom_Bombadil.md) are also Norse.

Tolkien never gives the "original" Dalish forms, however the Norse translations fit conveniently their meaning; for example Dwarf relations - like father-son or brothers- have rhyming or similar names (cf. [Glóin](../G/Glóin.md)-[Gróin](../G/Gróin.md)-[Óin](../_/Óin.md), [Thrór](../T/Thrór.md)-[Frór](../F/Frór.md)-[Grór](../G/Grór.md), [Fíli](../F/Fíli.md)-[Kíli](../K/Kíli.md) etc)<sup id="cite_ref-intro_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-1">[1]</a></sup> possibly reflecting a similar relationship in their original forms.

Norse used [Runes](../R/Runes.md) for a writing system, however Tolkien in *The Hobbit* used [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) runes.
