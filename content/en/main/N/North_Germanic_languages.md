The **North Germanic languages**, **Norse** or **Scandinavian languages** make up one of the three branches of the Germanic languages, a sub-family of the Indo-European languages, along with the West Germanic languages and the extinct East Germanic languages like [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic).

In a letter to [Naomi Mitchison](/wiki/Naomi_Mitchison), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Language_of_Dale" title="Language of Dale">language of Dale</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Long_Lake" title="Long Lake">Long Lake</a> would, if it appeared, be represented as more or less Scandinavian in character; but it is only represented by a few names, especially those of the Dwarves that came from that region. These are all Old Norse Dwarf-names.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Letter_144" title="Letter 144">Letter 144</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Most of the non-English names in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* are Norse (mostly from a list of Dwarves found in *Voluspa* and the *Gylfaginning*)<sup id="cite_ref-intro_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-1">[1]</a></sup> although anglicized, such as [Gandalfr](/wiki/Gandalf "Gandalf"), <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin" title="Durin">Durinn</a>, [Dvalinn](/wiki/Dwalin "Dwalin"), [Þrór](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r "Thrór"), [Eikinskjaldi](/wiki/Oakenshield "Oakenshield"), [Jarknasteinn](/wiki/Arkenstone "Arkenstone"), [Beorn](/wiki/Beorn), [Bard](/wiki/Bard), [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) and [Golfimbul](/wiki/Golfimbul).

In the backstory developed for *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Tolkien explained that [Durin's Folk](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk), after migrating to [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"), adopted the language of Dale for their names when interacting with [Men](/wiki/Men) because their secretive nature prevented them from using [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Names in *The Lord of the Rings* like [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli), [Narvi](/wiki/Narvi) and [Forn](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil "Tom Bombadil") are also Norse.

Tolkien never gives the "original" Dalish forms, however the Norse translations fit conveniently their meaning; for example Dwarf relations - like father-son or brothers- have rhyming or similar names (cf. [Glóin](/wiki/Gl%C3%B3in)-[Gróin](/wiki/Gr%C3%B3in)-[Óin](/wiki/%C3%93in), [Thrór](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r)-[Frór](/wiki/Fr%C3%B3r)-[Grór](/wiki/Gr%C3%B3r), [Fíli](/wiki/F%C3%ADli)-[Kíli](/wiki/K%C3%ADli) etc)<sup id="cite_ref-intro_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-1">[1]</a></sup> possibly reflecting a similar relationship in their original forms.

Norse used [Runes](/wiki/Runes) for a writing system, however Tolkien in *The Hobbit* used [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) runes.
