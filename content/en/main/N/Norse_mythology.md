**Norse mythology** refers to the collection of the sagas and legends of Scandinavia. Most of it is found in the [Poetic](../P/Poetic_Edda.md) and the <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prose_Edda" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Prose Edda</a>. [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) borrowed many Norse concepts and storylines for his own [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), like [the Shieldmaiden](../_/Éowyn.md) and the race of [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md). The names of many of Tolkien's Dwarves, as well as that of [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md), are taken from the *[Völuspá](../V/Völuspá.md)*.

[Elves](../E/Elves.md) also appear in the Norse sagas. Tolkien's Elves have the human-like stature and beautiful appearance of the Norse elves, rather than the miniature fairy-like size of elves in later folklore. He also borrowed the concept of [Light Elves](../C/Calaquendi.md) and [Dark Elves](../M/Moriquendi.md) from the Norse. However, in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) this does not refer to morality or appearance but to whether an Elf has seen the holy light of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md) and the [Two Trees](../T/Two_Trees.md).

Other creatures in the legendarium are also derived from Norse myths. [Trolls](../T/Trolls.md) frequently appear in Scandinavian legends, originally as a negative synonym for the Jotuns (giants) and later as a distinct creature. Tolkien derived the [Wargs](../W/Wargs.md) from the [Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md) word *vargr* and its [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) equivalent *wearg*, a word meaning both "wolf" and "strangler, choker". The giant wolves of Norse mythology, such as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenrir" title="wikipedia:Fenrir">Fenrir</a>, were referred to as *vargr*. Additionally, Tolkien's [Barrow-wights](../B/Barrow-wights.md) take inspiration from the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draugr" title="wikipedia:Draugr">draugar</a></i> of Norse mythology, undead creatures that lurk in tombs and barrows guarding treasure.

Outside the legendarium, Tolkien attempted to write his own versions of Scandinavian sagas such as *[The Story of Kullervo](../T/The_Story_of_Kullervo.md)* and *[The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrun](../T/The_Legend_of_Sigurd_and_Gudrun.md)*. The influence and themes of these stories can be seen in Tolkien's tales of ill-fated heroes such as [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md) and in the conflicts of his heroes against cunning and powerful [Dragons](../D/Dragons.md).
