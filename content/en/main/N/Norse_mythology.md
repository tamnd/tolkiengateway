**Norse mythology** refers to the collection of the sagas and legends of Scandinavia. Most of it is found in the [Poetic](/wiki/Poetic_Edda "Poetic Edda") and the <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prose_Edda" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Prose Edda</a>. [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) borrowed many Norse concepts and storylines for his own [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), like [the Shieldmaiden](/wiki/%C3%89owyn "Éowyn") and the race of [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves). The names of many of Tolkien's Dwarves, as well as that of [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), are taken from the *[Völuspá](/wiki/V%C3%B6lusp%C3%A1)*.

[Elves](/wiki/Elves) also appear in the Norse sagas. Tolkien's Elves have the human-like stature and beautiful appearance of the Norse elves, rather than the miniature fairy-like size of elves in later folklore. He also borrowed the concept of [Light Elves](/wiki/Calaquendi "Calaquendi") and [Dark Elves](/wiki/Moriquendi "Moriquendi") from the Norse. However, in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) this does not refer to morality or appearance but to whether an Elf has seen the holy light of [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) and the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees).

Other creatures in the legendarium are also derived from Norse myths. [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls) frequently appear in Scandinavian legends, originally as a negative synonym for the Jotuns (giants) and later as a distinct creature. Tolkien derived the [Wargs](/wiki/Wargs) from the [Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse) word *vargr* and its [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) equivalent *wearg*, a word meaning both "wolf" and "strangler, choker". The giant wolves of Norse mythology, such as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenrir" title="wikipedia:Fenrir">Fenrir</a>, were referred to as *vargr*. Additionally, Tolkien's [Barrow-wights](/wiki/Barrow-wights) take inspiration from the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draugr" title="wikipedia:Draugr">draugar</a></i> of Norse mythology, undead creatures that lurk in tombs and barrows guarding treasure.

Outside the legendarium, Tolkien attempted to write his own versions of Scandinavian sagas such as *[The Story of Kullervo](/wiki/The_Story_of_Kullervo)* and *[The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrun](/wiki/The_Legend_of_Sigurd_and_Gudrun)*. The influence and themes of these stories can be seen in Tolkien's tales of ill-fated heroes such as [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin) and in the conflicts of his heroes against cunning and powerful [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons).
