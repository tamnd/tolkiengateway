**Nebrachar** was a place of [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md)<sup id="cite_ref-manuscripts_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-manuscripts-1">[1]</a></sup> that appears in a [poem](../D/Dir_avosaith_a_gwaew_hinar.md) written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) that he used as an example of language invention in his lecture *[A Secret Vice](../A/A_Secret_Vice.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-monsters_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monsters-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of *Nebrachar* is not known, but [Dimitra Fimi](../D/Dimitra_Fimi.md) and [Andrew Higgins](../A/Andrew_Higgins.md) speculated in *[A Secret Vice: Tolkien on Invented Languages](../A/A_Secret_Vice%253A_Tolkien_on_Invented_Languages.md)* that the name might mean "near a place of slaughter" from the [Noldorin](Noldorin.md) roots <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=NEB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="NEB (page does not exist)">NEB</a></i> ("near")<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=RHACH&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="RHACH (page does not exist)">RHACH</a></i> ("carnage, slaughter")<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-secret_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secret-5">[5]</a></sup>
