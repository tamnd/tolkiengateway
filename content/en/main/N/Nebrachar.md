**Nebrachar** was a place of [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs)<sup id="cite_ref-manuscripts_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-manuscripts-1">[1]</a></sup> that appears in a [poem](/wiki/Dir_avosaith_a_gwaew_hinar "Dir avosaith a gwaew hinar") written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) that he used as an example of language invention in his lecture *[A Secret Vice](/wiki/A_Secret_Vice)*.<sup id="cite_ref-monsters_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monsters-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of *Nebrachar* is not known, but [Dimitra Fimi](/wiki/Dimitra_Fimi) and [Andrew Higgins](/wiki/Andrew_Higgins) speculated in *[A Secret Vice: Tolkien on Invented Languages](/wiki/A_Secret_Vice:_Tolkien_on_Invented_Languages)* that the name might mean "near a place of slaughter" from the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) roots <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=NEB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="NEB (page does not exist)">NEB</a></i> ("near")<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=RHACH&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="RHACH (page does not exist)">RHACH</a></i> ("carnage, slaughter")<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-secret_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secret-5">[5]</a></sup>
