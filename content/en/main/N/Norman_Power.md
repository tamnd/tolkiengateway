Canon<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> **Norman Sandiford Power** ([31 October](../_/31_October.md) [1916](../_/1916.md) - May [1993](../_/1993.md)) received his B.A. at Worcester College, [Oxford](../O/Oxford.md), in 1938. He went on to be associated with the parish of Ladywood in [Birmingham](../B/Birmingham.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup>

Power attended [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s invitational speech at the Lovelace Society in [1938](../_/1938.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

By the end of the life of Tolkien, they renewed their contact (at least one letter is recorded, see [Letter to Norman Power](../L/Letter_to_Norman_Power.md)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>):

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>How strange that I had no further contact with Tolkien until the nineteen-seventies when I had already been Vicar of Ladywood for over twenty years. Tolkien began to write to me about his old homes, and then, with amazing kindness, about my own scribbling.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Norman Power, <i><a href="../T/Tolkien_Centenary_Conference_1992.md" title="Tolkien Centenary Conference 1992">Tolkien Centenary Conference 1992</a></i></cite></p>
</blockquote>
