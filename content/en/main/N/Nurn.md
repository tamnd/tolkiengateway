**Nurn** was a region in the south of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md). The great Lake [Núrnen](../S/Sea_of_Núrnen.md), which was fed by four rivers, was located in that region.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-1">[1]</a></sup> Núrn was much more fertile than [Gorgoroth](../P/Plateau_of_Gorgoroth.md), a literal desert, and was able to sustain fields to grow food.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-2">[2]</a></sup>

It is probable that Nurn was not inhabited during the reign of king [Hyarmendacil I](../H/Hyarmendacil_I.md) of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), because Mordor was desolate during his time.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

At the time of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), slaves worked fields by the dark, sad waters of Lake Núrnen to feed the armies of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-2">[2]</a></sup>

The region of Nurn most likely escaped the desolation caused by the eruption of [Mount Doom](../O/Orodruin.md) at the end of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md) because it was much further away from Mount Doom than [Barad-dûr](../B/Barad-dûr.md)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> or [Udûn](<../U/Udûn_(valley).md>).<sup id="cite_ref-Map_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

After the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), King [Elessar](../A/Aragorn.md) of the [Reunited Kingdom](../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md) of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) and [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) liberated the slaves of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md) and gave them all the land around Lake Núrnen to be their own.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Nurn*, appearing in the compound *[Núrnen](../S/Sea_of_Núrnen.md#Etymology)* ("sad-water"), apparently means "sad" in [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Gap of Nurn, a gap between the Eregwath (&#34;Thorn of Shadows&#34; in Sindarin), the mountain spur that branched off eastwards from the <a href="../E/Ephel_Dúath.md" title="Ephel Dúath">Ephel Dúath</a> and the Ered Angûrath (&#34;Mountains of Slow Death&#34; in Sindarin), the mountain spur that branched off southwestwards from the <a href="../E/Ered_Lithui.md" title="Ered Lithui">Ered Lithui</a>, connect the plateau of Gorgoroth to Nurn. Small grasses and scrubs extend from Nurn almost to the plains. The land descends from the Gap of Nurn to the lower plains of Nurn. The tower of Barad-wath at the eastern end of the Eregwath and the town of Ostigurth monitor the Gap of Nurn.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> The Ruinen stream flows from three intermittent sources near the southeastern end of the Eregwath, in the Gap of Nurn and near the southwestern end of the Ered Angûrath that meet at the town of Rul southeastwards in the direction of the Sea of Nurnen. The Dúaduin river flows from the Ered Litui northeastwards by the town of Nûrumurl to the town of Urlurtsu Nurn where it makes a bend and then flowes southeastwards in the direction of the Sea of Nurn.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth%253A_Shadow_of_Mordor.md" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor">Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Nurn is a location in the game that the player can visit. Nurn was originally the domain of the Tribesmen of Nurn whom were descended from Corsairs, who have settled around the Sea of Nurn following Gondor’s retreat hundreds of years before the events of the story.</dd></dl>
