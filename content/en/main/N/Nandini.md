**Nandini** (or the <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dryads" title="wikipedia:Dryads">dryads</a></b>) were the [fays](../F/Fays.md) of the valleys,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ndr_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ndr-2">[1]</a></sup> according to the early version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

They arrived to the world with [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) and [Yavanna](../Y/Yavanna.md), along with many other types of fays.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Nandini* is in [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md), being the plural form of *nandin* ("dryad");<sup id="cite_ref-Nan_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nan-5">[4]</a></sup> the [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) cognate of *nandin* was <i><b>nandir</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ndr_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ndr-2">[1]</a></sup>

The word *nandin* is most likely related to the word <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Nan(d)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nan(d) (page does not exist)">nan(d)</a></i> ("woodland"), which was derived from the root <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=NA%C5%98A&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="NAŘA (page does not exist)">NAŘA</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Nan_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nan-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> See also the related Gnomish words <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Nand_(Gnomish_word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nand (Gnomish word) (page does not exist)">nand</a></i> or <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Nann&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nann (page does not exist)">nann</a></i> ("a field acre").<sup id="cite_ref-Ndr_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ndr-2">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In a later version of the legendarium from *[The Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)* (1930s), the term "dryad" is instead applied to the *[tavari](../T/Tavari.md)*, a related group of beings.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>
