**Nindalf**, or **Wetwang** in [Westron](/wiki/Westron), was a wide region of swampy land of <sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup> pathless fen<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup> on the east side of the river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin)<sup id="cite_ref-Map_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-3">[3]</a></sup> near the foot of the falls of [Rauros](/wiki/Rauros) and the mouths of the [Entwash](/wiki/Entwash)<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup>.

At the Nindalf the Anduin divided into many channels and formed marshlands in the east of the main channel of the river. Opposite of the Nindalf, on the west side of the Anduin the river Entwash flowed into the Anduin by many mouths in a great inland delta.<sup id="cite_ref-Farewell_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farewell-4">[4]</a></sup> The [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes) lay further east and may have been an extension of Wetwang.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-3">[3]</a></sup>

On [16 February](/wiki/16_February) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019")<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> [Celeborn](/wiki/Celeborn) recommended to the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) that those heading to [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) should leave the river above the Falls of [Rauros](/wiki/Rauros) and cross the Entwash before it separates into many mouths in the marshes.<sup id="cite_ref-Farewell_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farewell-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Nindalf* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, which means "Wetwang"<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup> or "wet flat"<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-6">[6]</a></sup>. It is a compound of *[nîn](/wiki/N%C3%AEn "Nîn")* ("wet")<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-6">[6]</a></sup> and <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Talf&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Talf (page does not exist)">talf</a></i> ("flat field"<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup> or "flat area"<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-6">[6]</a></sup>).

The archaic meaning of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/wang#Old_English" title="wiktionary:wang">wang</a></i> (modern <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/wong#English" title="wiktionary:wong">wong</a></i>) means "field" or "flat area".<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetwang" title="wikipedia:Wetwang">Wetwang</a> is a real-life place-name in Yorkshire.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup> [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) was in command of an outpost of the Humber Garrison in Roos in Yorkshire in 1917<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> and 1918<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>, which is not far from Wetwang.
