The **Noldor**, or **Ñoldor**, were those of the second clan of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) who came to [Aman](../A/Aman.md)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>. The Noldor were held to be the noblest of the Elves, and indeed of all peoples in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), in lore and in craftsmanship. In [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md), their wisdom and skill flourished exceedingly, and ever they hungered for deeper knowledge and greater mastery of the arts, so that in many matters they even outstripped their own teachers. They cherished also a love of words, and were ever shifting in speech, striving to devise names most fitting for all things. This rich inheritance of knowledge they later passed on to [Men](../M/Men.md) during their [Exile](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md).

Their first [King](../K/King_of_the_Noldor.md) was [Finwë](../F/Finwë.md), who was slain by [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md). In the wake of his death and the theft of the [jewels](../S/Silmarils.md), Finwë’s son [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) led the Noldor in rebellion, vowing vengeance upon Morgoth. The war of the Noldor against Morgoth comprises many of the greatest tales of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).

## Origins {#Origins}

According to legend, the clan was founded by [Tata](../T/Tata.md), the second Elf to awake at [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md). With him were his spouse Tatië and their 54 companions, and this clan became known as the <i><b>Tatyar</b></i>. [Finwë](../F/Finwë.md), the first Ñoldo to come to [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md) with [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md), became their King, and led most of them to Valinor. Out of the original 56 Tatyar who awoke at Cuiviénen, 28 remained at the place of their awakening, becoming [Avari](../A/Avari.md), while the other original 28 and their offspring continued on their [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md).

## History {#History}

### Elder Days {#Elder_Days}

#### In Valinor {#In_Valinor}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/K/Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/9/98/Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg/300px-Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="386" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/9/98/Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg/450px-Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/9/98/Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg/600px-Konstantin_Zahariev_-_A_Noldorin_Prince.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1028"/></a><figcaption><i>A Noldorin Prince</i> by Konstantin Zahariev</figcaption></figure>

The Noldor were accounted the greatest of the Elves and all the peoples in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) in lore and crafts. In Valinor, their knowledge and skill became great, and they had always a strong desire for more knowledge and skill in art, surpassing even their teachers in many things. The Noldor had also a love for words, and were changeful in speech, endeavouring to find suitable names for all things. They were beloved of [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) the Smith, and were the first to discover and carve gems. Their chief dwelling-place was the city of [Tirion](../T/Tirion.md) upon [Túna](../T/Túna.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamar_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamar-8">[8]</a></sup> Among the wisest of the Noldor was [Rúmil](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Tirion).md>), creator of the first writing system and author of many books of lore.<sup id="cite_ref-Unchaining_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Unchaining-9">[9]</a></sup> [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md), son of Finwë and [Míriel](../M/Míriel.md), was the greatest of their craftsmen, "mightiest in skill of word and of hand",<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamar_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamar-8">[8]</a></sup> and creator of the [Silmarils](../S/Silmarils.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Silmarils_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silmarils-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Melkor](../M/Melkor.md) harboured a hatred for the Elves, despising their joy and blaming them for his downfall, and he sought to deceive them by feigning friendship. The Noldor, eager for knowledge, listened to him most readily of the kindreds. So he went often among them, offering counsel, and the Noldor hearkened to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Unchaining_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Unchaining-9">[9]</a></sup> During this time, Fëanor created the Silmarils, and Melkor, desiring them greatly, sought ever to spread ideas and rumours among the Noldor. But amid his seemingly good advice, Melkor sowed lies to turn one House of the Noldor against another, and in the end the peace in Tirion was poisoned. After threatening his half-brother [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md), believing falsely that Fingolfin intended to usurp the throne, Fëanor was banished from Tirion by the [Valar](../V/Valar.md). With him went his [seven sons](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md), and his father Finwë, King of the Noldor. Fingolfin remained behind as the ruler of the Noldor of Tirion.<sup id="cite_ref-Silmarils_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silmarils-10">[10]</a></sup>

But Melkor had yet other designs to accomplish. Soon after, with the aid of [Ungoliant](../U/Ungoliant.md), he destroyed the [Two Trees](../T/Two_Trees.md),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> and coming to [Formenos](../F/Formenos.md) he killed Finwë, stole the Silmarils, and departed from Aman. Fëanor, driven by grief and desiring vengeance, rebelled against the Valar by coming back into Tirion and making a speech before the Noldor, in which he persuaded them to leave Valinor, follow Melkor to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), and wage war against him for the recovery of the Silmarils. He swore a terrible [oath](../O/Oath_of_Fëanor.md), as did his [sons](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md), to pursue Melkor and reclaim the Silmarils at all costs. Fëanor then claimed the kingship of the Noldor since his father was dead. Although the greater part of the Noldor still held Fingolfin as their leader, they followed Fëanor into [Exile](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Exile_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Exile-12">[12]</a></sup>

#### Exile {#Exile}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md" title="Exile of the Noldor">Exile of the Noldor</a></b></div></div>

Fëanor led the Noldor from Tirion, his host in the front, followed by the hosts of Fingolfin and [Finarfin](../F/Finarfin.md). He led them to Alqualondë, seeking to persuade the Teleri to join with the Noldor in rebellion. But the Teleri did not wish to depart with the Noldor, nor aid them with the providing of ships. Angered, Fëanor assembled his host, and they took the ships by force, committing the first [kinslaying](../K/Kinslaying_at_Alqualondë.md). The host of Fingolfin, coming upon the battle and mistakenly believing the Teleri had attacked the Noldor, joined in the struggle, and many of the Teleri were slain. Taking the ships, the Noldor continued their journey northwards. A messenger from the Valar came later and delivered the [Doom of Mandos](../D/Doom_of_Mandos.md), pronouncing judgement on the Noldor for the Kinslaying and rebellion and warning that if they proceeded their oath would betray them, and moreover they would be slain or tormented by grief. At this, some of the Noldor who had no hand in the Kinslaying, including [Finarfin](../F/Finarfin.md) son of Finwë and [Indis](../I/Indis.md), returned to Valinor, and the Valar forgave them. Other Noldor led by Fingolfin remained determined to leave Valinor for Middle-earth, not desiring to be separated from their kin and fearing the Valar's judgement, for some among them had participated in the kinslaying. Among those that continued were [Fingon](../F/Fingon.md), [Turgon](../T/Turgon.md), [Finrod](../F/Finrod.md), and [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Exile_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Exile-12">[12]</a></sup>

The Noldor suffered greatly during the cold journey, and began to mutter against Fëanor. Fearing treachery, Fëanor took his sons and those he deemed loyal to him, departing in the ships and crossing the sea to Middle-earth. He left behind those led by Fingolfin, his half-brother. Upon his arrival in Middle-earth, Fëanor had the ships burned. When the Noldor led by Fingolfin discovered their betrayal, they went farther north and crossed the sea at the treacherous [Helcaraxë](../H/Helcaraxë.md), at the cost of many lives.<sup id="cite_ref-Exile_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Exile-12">[12]</a></sup>

#### In Middle-earth {#In_Middle-earth}

##### Arrival {#Arrival}

Fëanor's company encamped in the region of [Mithrim](../M/Mithrim.md) and was soon attacked by the host of Morgoth. The battle became known as a [Dagor-nuin-Giliath](../D/Dagor-nuin-Giliath.md), and was a great victory for the Noldor. But Fëanor rode too far from his host during the battle, pursuing the remnant of the [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) in a vengeful mood. Several [Balrogs](../B/Balrogs.md), including their Lord [Gothmog](<../G/Gothmog_(balrog).md>), issued from [Angband](../A/Angband.md) and fought him. Despite battling valiantly, Fëanor was mortally wounded and would have been captured and taken to Angband had it not been for the swift arrival of his sons. However, Fëanor died whilst being carried back to his own people.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-13">[13]</a></sup>

Because Fëanor had taken the ships and left the Noldor led by his half-brother on the west side of the sea, much enmity remained between the Houses of the Noldor. The feud was at last settled when [Fingon](../F/Fingon.md), son of Fingolfin, saved [Maedhros](../M/Maedhros.md), Fëanor's son, from Morgoth's imprisonment. Maedhros was due to succeed Fëanor, but he regretted his part in the Kinslaying and waived his claim to the kingship of the Noldor to his uncle Fingolfin, who became the first [High King of the Noldor](../H/High_King_of_the_Noldor.md) in Middle-earth. The [House of Fëanor](../H/House_of_Fëanor.md) became known as *the Dispossessed* after this.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-13">[13]</a></sup>

##### Kingdom of the Noldor {#Kingdom_of_the_Noldor}

In [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md), the Noldor made alliance with the [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Realms_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Realms-14">[14]</a></sup> and later with the [Edain](../E/Edain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> Fingolfin reigned long in the land of [Hithlum](../H/Hithlum.md), and his younger son [Turgon](../T/Turgon.md) later ruled in his Hidden City of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md). The Sons of Fëanor, under Maedhros, ruled the lands of [East Beleriand](../E/East_Beleriand.md), while [Finrod Felagund](../F/Finrod_Felagund.md), Finarfin's son, was the [King of Nargothrond](../K/King_of_Nargothrond.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Realms_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Realms-14">[14]</a></sup>

Fingolfin's reign was marked by warfare against Morgoth and in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [60](../F/First_Age_60.md), after their victory in [Dagor Aglareb](../D/Dagor_Aglareb.md), the Noldor [besieged Angband](../S/Siege_of_Angband.md), the fortress of Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Yet in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [455](../F/First_Age_455.md),<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> the Siege was broken by Morgoth in the [Dagor Bragollach](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md), and many of the Noldor were slain or scattered. Fingolfin rode in despair to Angband and challenged Morgoth to [single combat](../F/Fall_of_Fingolfin.md). He dealt Morgoth seven wounds but perished, and was succeeded by his eldest son [Fingon](../F/Fingon.md), who became the second [High King of the Noldor](../H/High_King_of_the_Noldor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

##### Demise {#Demise}

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [472](../F/First_Age_472.md),<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> Maedhros [organised](../U/Union_of_Maedhros.md) an attack on Morgoth and this led to the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](Nirnaeth_Arnoediad.md), the Battle of Unnumbered Tears. Betrayed by the new-come [Easterlings](../E/Easterlings.md), the forces of the Noldor, Sindar and Edain were utterly defeated. Fingon the Valiant was slain; he was succeeded by his brother [Turgon](../T/Turgon.md) as the third [High King of the Noldor](../H/High_King_of_the_Noldor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Nirnaeth_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nirnaeth-20">[20]</a></sup>

Morgoth scattered the remaining forces of the [Sons of Fëanor](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md), and in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [495](../F/First_Age_495.md), [Nargothrond](Nargothrond.md) was also defeated.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> Turgon had withdrawn to Gondolin which was kept hidden from both Morgoth and other Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-Nirnaeth_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nirnaeth-20">[20]</a></sup> In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [510](../F/First_Age_510.md), Gondolin was betrayed by [Maeglin](../M/Maeglin.md) and [sacked](../F/Fall_of_Gondolin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> During the attack, Turgon was killed; however, many of his [people escaped](../E/Exiles_of_Gondolin.md) and found their way to the [Mouths of Sirion](../M/Mouths_of_Sirion.md). Turgon was the last surviving male descendant of Fingolfin, so [Gil-galad](../G/Gil-galad.md), great-grandson of Finarfin, became the fourth and last [High King of the Noldor](../H/High_King_of_the_Noldor.md) in Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup>

Finally, the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../H/Host_of_the_West.md" title="Host of the West">Host of the West</a> came down to Middle-earth and in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [545](../F/First_Age_545.md) to [587](../F/First_Age_587.md), the [War of Wrath](../W/War_of_Wrath.md) was fought and Morgoth was cast into the Void.<sup id="cite_ref-End_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-End-24">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> As a result of the cataclysmic war, Beleriand sank into the sea, except for a part of [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md) (Lindon),<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> and a few [islands](../T/Tol_Morwen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> The defeat of Morgoth marked the end of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) and the start of the [Second](../S/Second_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-End_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-End-24">[24]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

Most of the Noldor sailed back to [Tol Eressea](../T/Tol_Eressea.md) at the end of the First Age; but some, like [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) daughter of Finarfin, or [Celebrimbor](../C/Celebrimbor.md), grandson of Fëanor, refused the pardon of the Valar and remained in Middle-earth. Gil-galad, last heir of the kings of the Noldor in exile, and acknowledged as "High King of the Elves of the West",<sup id="cite_ref-SA_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-28">[28]</a></sup> founded a new kingdom at Lindon, and ruled throughout the Second Age, longer than any of his predecessors.

[Gil-galad](../G/Gil-galad.md)'s people were mainly Noldor<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> (especially from [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md), but also from the [Houses of Finarfin](../H/House_of_Finarfin.md) and [Fëanor](../H/House_of_Fëanor.md)); early in the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md), most of the Noldor that remained in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) lived in Lindon.<sup id="cite_ref-SA_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-28">[28]</a></sup> During this time, the few [Exiles](../E/Exiles.md) that remained also lived in Lindon.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>

In [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [750](../S/Second_Age_750.md), some Noldor learned that [mithril](../M/Mithril.md) was found in [Moria](../M/Moria.md) under the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md), so they went to [Eregion](../E/Eregion.md). Because of their legacy as craftsmen, they were less unfriendly to the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) than the Sindar. They became the [Gwaith-i-Mírdain](../G/Gwaith-i-Mírdain.md) under [Celebrimbor](../C/Celebrimbor.md) and formed the closest friendship ever between Elves and Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-SA_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA-28">[28]</a></sup> Although war soon befell Eriador along with Celebrimbor's death, remnants of Eregion, including many Noldor, either followed Elrond to establish Imladris or fled to [Lothlórien](../L/Lothlórien.md).

Gil-galad perished in the War of the Alliance at the end of the Second Age, and so ended the High Kingship of the Noldor. He fathered no children; however, the line of the High-elven kings, including the High Kingship of the Noldor, was alone preserved in the descendants of [Elros](../E/Elros.md) and [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md).<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>

### Later history {#Later_history}

After the fall of [Gil-galad](../G/Gil-galad.md) (during the time of the [Kings of Arnor](../K/Kings_of_Arnor.md)), there was still a remnant of the Noldor who dwelt at the [Grey Havens](../G/Grey_Havens.md) of further inland in Lindon who constituted the largest population of Noldor remaining in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-32">[32]</a></sup> However, in Eriador, the chief dwelling of the Noldor was Imladris.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-33">[33]</a></sup>

In the late [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), the Noldor in Middle-earth were greatly diminished, as most had passed over the Sea.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup> If any remained in the early [Fourth Age](../F/Fourth_Age.md), they were few.<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-32">[32]</a></sup>

After the [Dominion of Men](../D/Dominion_of_Men.md) was taken up by [Aragorn II Elessar](../A/Aragorn_II_Elessar.md), the last of the Noldor set sail from [Mithlond](../M/Mithlond.md) and left Middle-earth forever.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-33">[33]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The Noldor were the Second Clan of the Elves in both order and size, the other clans being the [Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md), a smaller group, and the [Teleri](../T/Teleri.md), a much larger one. The Noldor typically had grey eyes and dark hair, save for the members of the golden-haired [House of Finarfin](../H/House_of_Finarfin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup>

From their beginning the Noldor (meaning "Those who Know") were always distinguished, by their knowledge of the things that are and were in this world, and by their desire to learn more.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup> They were the most skillful of these Clans, and were specially loved by [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md), from whom they learned many things, although less than they wanted to know. Their skills included the developing of their language, the building of great towers and halls, and gem extraction.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[39]</a></sup> One of their most famous artefacts were the crystallic [Fëanorian lamps](../F/Fëanorian_lamps~17189.md); the craft to make them was lost to Middle-earth when they left.

The Noldor were the proudest of the Elves; in the words of the [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md), they came to Middle-earth because "they needed room to quarrel in". It was this pride that Melkor used to turn the Houses of [Fëanor](../H/House_of_Fëanor.md) and [Fingolfin](../H/House_of_Fingolfin.md) against each other. The pride of the Noldor also led to their [Fall](../F/Fall_of_the_Noldor.md) and [Exile](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md) (and their continued quarrels in Middle-earth, as noted by the Sindar).

Of the three clans of the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md) they are also the people who most favours swords and shields as their weapons.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[40]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[41]</a></sup>

The Sindar often called the Noldor <i><b>Lechind</b></i> (sing. <i><b>Lachend</b></i>), which meant 'flame-eyed' due to the piercing brightness of their eyes. This was the distinctive feature of all the [Elves that had dwelt in Aman](../C/Calaquendi.md), whose eyes thus deeply impressed [those of Middle-earth](../_/Úmanyar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-WJC_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJC-42">[42]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:384</sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

**Noldor** is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) name that meant "the Wise" (in the sense of possessing knowledge, not sagacity or sound judgment),<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[43]</a></sup> derived from the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) [root](../S/Sundocarme.md) [NGOL](NGOL.md) ("knowledge, wisdom, lore").<sup id="cite_ref-WJC_42-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJC-42">[42]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:383</sup> This name was given to the Second Clan by the others and was accepted and used as their regular and proper name by all the *Eldarin* members of the clan throughout their later history.<sup id="cite_ref-WJC_42-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJC-42">[42]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:383</sup>

Its singular form is <i><b><a href="Noldo.md" title="Noldo">Noldo</a></b></i> and the adjective form is <i><b>Noldorin</b></i>.

## Other names {#Other_names}

The spelling *Ñoldor*, rather than *Noldor*, was often used by Tolkien in his later writings. The grapheme Ñ is used here to represent the phoneme \[ŋ\]—the velar nasal sound at the end of the English word *sing*—rather than the palatal nasal \[ɲ\], as in the Spanish word *año*. This is due to vacillation on whether or not initial \[ŋ\] (written ñ) from \[ng\] became \[n\] or not in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md). Even in earlier forms the name *Ñoldo/Noldo* came from the same [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) stem \*ñgolodō, which led to *Ñoldo* in [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) and *Golodh* in [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md).

The Noldor were called <i><b><a class="mw-redirect" href="../G/Golodhrim.md" title="Golodhrim">Golodhrim</a></b></i> (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ɡɔ:ˈlɔð.rim]</span>) by [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md)-speakers and <i><b>Goldoi</b></i> by the [Falmari](../F/Falmari.md) of [Aman](../A/Aman.md). Both of these terms are cognates of the [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *Noldor,* meaning 'wise, learned ones'. The Sindarin singular <i><b><a href="../G/Golodh.md" title="Golodh">Golodh</a></b></i> (<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈɡɔ.lɔð]</span>) had the plural form <i><b><a class="mw-redirect" href="../G/Gelydh.md" title="Gelydh">Gelydh</a></b></i> (<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈɡɛ.lyð]</span>, or in Gondorian pronunciation <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈɡɛ.lið]</span>, archaically <i><b><a class="mw-redirect" href="../G/Gœlydh.md" title="Gœlydh">Gœlydh</a></b></i> <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈɡø.lyð]</span>)<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[44]</a></sup>. The class plural, <i><b>Golodhrim,</b></i> was used for the people as a whole.<sup id="cite_ref-WJA_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJA-45">[45]</a></sup> Later the Sindar replaced these with <i><b>Gódhil</b></i> (sing. <i><b>Gódhel</b></i>) or <i><b>Gódhellim</b></i> (<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ɡoːˈðel.lim]</span>), which, in turn, was a modernised form of *Ódhil* or *Ódhellim*, Sindarin name for the [Amanyar](../A/Amanyar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-WJA_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJA-45">[45]</a></sup>

The Noldor were called the "**Deep-elves**", a loose translation of *Noldor*, where 'deep' means 'wise, learned'.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[46]</a></sup> Other names used for the Noldor included the Wise, the Golden, the Valiant, the Sword-elves, the Elves of the Earth, the Foes of Melkor, the Skilled of Hand, the Jewel-wrights, the Companions of Men, the Followers of Finwë<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[47]</a></sup>, and Loremasters.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[48]</a></sup> It is also told that the Noldor were often called the Lispers by other native speakers of Quenya (such as the [Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md)), since the Noldor generally did not retain the sound "th" \[θ\] in their speech.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[49]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="../G/Gnomes.md" title="Gnomes">Gnomes</a></b></div></div>

In the earliest versions of Tolkien's [Legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), the Noldor were called '**Noldoli'** or '**[Gnomes](../G/Gnomes.md)'**. They were still called *Gnomes* in early editions of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*. They were also the ones who spoke the language that later became [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) (then called *Gnomish* or *Noldorin*).
