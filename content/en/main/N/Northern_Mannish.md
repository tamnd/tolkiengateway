**Northern** refers to the [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md) language or languages spoken by the [Northmen](Northmen.md) of [Rhovanion](../R/Rhovanion.md).

As the Northmen were distantly related to the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) and the [Númenóreans](Númenóreans.md), their language(s) had common elements with [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) and [Westron](../W/Westron.md).

## Origins and history {#Origins_and_history}

The first [Men](../M/Men.md) who migrated <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../W/West.md" title="West">West</a> in the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) spoke at least two languages. One was spoken by the northern group, however most people of the [Greater Folk](../H/House_of_Hador.md) of that group stayed behind while traversing [Greenwood the Great](../G/Greenwood_the_Great.md) in Rhovanion; those were the ancestors of the Northmen. As such, their language was related to that of their cousins, the Edain of the [House of Beor](../H/House_of_Beor.md) and the [House of Hador](../H/House_of_Hador.md), who reached [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) in the West.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-relations_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relations-2">[2]</a></sup>

Those folk had close relations with the [Longbeards](../L/Longbeards.md), the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) who occupied the mountains and hills around Rhovanion. In the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) they had mutual respect and warm friendship, and the Longbeards adopted the speech of Men, keeping their own language to themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-relations_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relations-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The Dwarves even made their own names from Northern elements, or even fabricated names that sounded Northern.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The custom apparently reached the [Dwarves of Moria](../D/Dwarves_of_Moria.md), and the Dwarf signing as [Narvi](Narvi.md).

By the Third Age, the Kings of Gondor recognised the common ancestry with the Northmen and their languages.<sup id="cite_ref-App_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-6">[6]</a></sup>

As the earliest known location of the Hobbits was the Vales of Anduin (c. T.A. 1050), they were in contact with the Éothéod. The name Kuduk (Hobbit) itself for example is believed to be derived from the Northern kûd-dûkan (Holbytlan; hole builders). During their Wandering Days, the Hobbits retained some peculiar words from old in their dialect, even after adopting Westron.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

A prominent Northern language was the [language of Dale](../L/Language_of_Dale.md); the Dwarves of Erebor bore external names in that language.<sup id="cite_ref-App_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

As Westron is rendered with modern English, Northern is rendered with archaic Germanic languages, such as [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md) (in the case of Northmen of Rhovanion), [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) (Éothéod and the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md)) and [Norse](Norse.md) ([Dale](../D/Dale.md) and [Esgaroth](../E/Esgaroth.md)).

Many Dwarven characters, as well as [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md), bear Norse names, representing their contact with the language of Dale. Most of them are taken from the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Dvergatal&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Dvergatal (page does not exist)">Dvergatal</a>, an ancient Norse listing the names of many dwarves.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) suggests that "those Dwarf-names in *The Hobbit* \[from *[Völuspá](../V/Völuspá.md)*\] provided the whole starting-point for the Mannish languages in Middle-earth".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
