**Nob** was a [hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") of [Bree](/wiki/Bree).

## History {#History}

Nob lived at the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) and probably the beginning of the [Fourth](/wiki/Fourth_Age "Fourth Age"). He worked for [Barliman Butterbur](/wiki/Barliman_Butterbur) at *[The Prancing Pony](/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony)* with [Bob](/wiki/Bob), where he served food and tended to any other needs guests might have.

On [September 29](/wiki/29_September "29 September"), [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [four Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Travellers "Travellers") came to *The Prancing Pony*.<sup id="cite_ref-Sign_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sign-1">[1]</a></sup> When one of them, [Meriadoc Brandybuck](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck), went missing after a night stroll, Nob found him near the house of [Bill Ferny](/wiki/Bill_Ferny).<sup id="cite_ref-Strider_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Strider-3">[3]</a></sup> Realizing the [danger](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl"), Nob helped the Hobbits to disguise their beds, and kept watch at night. Nonetheless, the Inn was attacked.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-4">[4]</a></sup>

When those four hobbits returned on [October 28](/wiki/28_October "28 October") [the following year](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Nob still worked at *The Prancing Pony*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In historical English *nob* is slang to denote disrespect for a high society person, cf. *knob* "head".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

[Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond "Wayne G. Hammond") and [Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull "Christina Scull") think that *Nob* was perhaps chosen rhyme with *Bob*, and that it does not appear to be recognized diminutive or nickname.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> It is also possible that *Nob* was a nickname of a person named Robert; Tolkien's review of M. Förster's *Die Französierung des englischen Personennamenschatzes* mentions the name *Nob* in one line with better known names as *[Hob](/wiki/Hob_Hayward "Hob Hayward")* and *Rob*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
