The **Ninth of the [Nazgûl](Nazgûl.md)** was never differentiated in Tolkien's works, but several adaptations did attribute individual traits to this specific Nazgûl.

[Iron Crown Enterprises](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md) constructed an elaborate non-[canonical](../C/Canon.md) backstory for [Middle-earth Role Playing](../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md), identifying the character as *Ûvatha the Horseman*, a backstory which was also used in the [Middle-earth Collectible Card Game](../M/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game.md).<sup id="cite_ref-ICE_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICE-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

[Decipher](../D/Decipher.md), for their [Trading Card Game](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game.md), instead limited this individualization to naming the character *[Úlairë](Nazgûl.md#Other_names) Nertëa* ([Q.](../Q/Quenya.md) Ninth Nazgûl), and assigning the epithet of *Black Horseman*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

While some other adaptations also individualized the Nine, none are known to have assigned specific numbers.

## Backstory in Middle-earth Role Playing {#Backstory_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing}

**Uvatha** was born as Ûvathar Achef in [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1970&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1970 (page does not exist)">1970</a> to a [Variag](../V/Variags.md) prince from [Khand](../K/Khand.md) who lived a nomadic life in exile. He was a skilled horseman and warrior from a young age.

In [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1988&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1988 (page does not exist)">1988</a>, Ûvatha's charge at the Battle of Noz Peka (Knife River) helped restore his family to the throne of Upper Khand. When his father Kîonid died, his uncle Mîonid took the throne and ordered Ûvatha's execution. Ûvatha escaped and earned the support of the king of Lower Khand, who made him warlord of his army in [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1999&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1999 (page does not exist)">1999</a>. The following year, Ûvatha deposed his king and conquered his uncle's domain, uniting Khand for the first time in recorded history. In [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_2002&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 2002 (page does not exist)">2002</a>, he accepted a [Ring of Power](../R/Rings_of_Power.md) from [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md), becoming the ninth to fall under Sauron's sway.

For the next 1260 years, Ûvatha's people became one of Sauron's most brutal and effective tools of conquest. They extended the Shadow to the north and south of their plateau kingdom, vying with the [Númenóreans](Númenóreans.md) for influence in [Harad](../H/Harad.md). Ûvatha and his warriors also fought alongside the warriors of Ren the Unclean, leading to the Chey conquest of the Khargagis Ahar.

His reign was interrupted in [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [3262](../S/Second_Age_3262.md) when [Ar-Pharazôn](../A/Ar-Pharazôn.md) invaded and captured Sauron. Ûvatha hid in his secret hold in his birthplace of Ôlbamarl, in the mountains of northern Khand, only to emerge after the [Downfall of Númenor](../D/Downfall_of_Númenor.md). He acted as Sauron's messenger, and passed into shadows as Sauron was overthrown in the [War of the Last Alliance](../W/War_of_the_Last_Alliance.md).

Ûvatha reappeared around [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1050](../T/Third_Age_1050.md), returning to Ôlbamarl. Fifty years later, he killed the current king and crowned himself King of the Variags, beginning a 540-year reign of terror. His empire expanded rapidly across northeastern Harad and southern Rhûn. When he left for Mordor in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1640](../T/Third_Age_1640.md) to prepare for Sauron's return, his kingdom declined.

Ûvatha returned to Khand in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_1854&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 1854 (page does not exist)">1854</a>, contributing to a chain of events that led to the first major Easterling migration into Rhovanion. This included the [Wainriders](../W/Wainriders.md), who had pushed into Rhovanion and fought [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) for over 40 years. This plot allowed Sauron to weaken the kingdom without revealing his presence. Ûvatha returned to Mordor in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1940](../T/Third_Age_1940.md), leaving his kingdom vulnerable to a new threat from the east. He joined the other Nazgûl in the attack on [Minas Ithil](../M/Minas_Morgul.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2000](../T/Third_Age_2000.md), returning to Khand two years later to begin his final reign as king.

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2951](../T/Third_Age_2951.md), Uvatha and two other Ringwraiths, [Khamûl](../K/Khamûl.md) and [Adûnâphel](../S/Seventh_of_the_Nazgûl.md), flew to [Dol Guldur](../D/Dol_Guldur.md) to reopen the citadel. Ûvatha served as a messenger between Dol Guldur and Mordor, coordinating the mobilization of Sauron's armies.

During the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), Ûvatha rode with the Black Riders in search of the One Ring. After their defeat at the [Bruinen](../B/Bruinen.md), he returned to Dol Guldur, where he took up a Fell Beast and resumed his role as a messenger. He then fought at the [Battle of the Morannon](../B/Battle_of_the_Morannon.md), where he perished with Sauron's defeat.<sup id="cite_ref-ICE_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICE-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Weaponry in Middle-earth Role Playing {#Weaponry_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing}

Beyond a [Morgul-knife](../M/Morgul-knife.md), the Ringwraith is noted to have possessed the following weapons:

- **Horse-tamer**: Reddish narig-wooden lance.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **Stormless Bow**: Short bow inlaid with gold.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
