**Narn i Chîn Húrin**, the sorrowful **Tale of the Children of Húrin**, tells of the tragic lives of [Túrin Turambar](/wiki/T%C3%BArin "Túrin") and his sister [Nienor](/wiki/Nienor).<sup id="cite_ref-Turin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Known also as *Narn e-'Rach Morgoth* (Tale of the Curse of Morgoth), it formed part of the wider matter *Narn e-mbar Hador* ("Tale of the [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador)") and the *[Atanatárion](/wiki/Atanat%C3%A1rion)*, preserved in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) also preserved a copy of the story and brought to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor); Narn i Chîn Húrin was shown by [Pengolodh](/wiki/Pengolodh) to [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Narn i [Chîn](/wiki/H%C3%AEn "Hîn") Húrin* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), directly meaning "Tale of the Children of Húrin".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The **Tale of Grief** was a name for the *Narn i Chîn Húrin*.<sup id="cite_ref-Turin_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turin-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The concept of the narrative referring to another longer tale of the life of Túrin is present since early stages of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"). Appears for first time in *[Sketch of the Mythology](/wiki/Sketch_of_the_Mythology)* as the "Children of Húrin".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The same name is given to it in the *[Quenta Noldorinwa](/wiki/Quenta_Noldorinwa)*, and it is also called the "Tale of Grief".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
