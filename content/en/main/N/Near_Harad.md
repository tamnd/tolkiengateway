**Near Harad** was an indefinite region of [Harad](../H/Harad.md) that lay to the south of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md), beyond the River [Harnen](../H/Harnen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Near Harad was home to [camels](../C/Camels.md) and, in the South, [Oliphaunts](../O/Oliphaunts.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Its inhabitants were enemies of Gondor during parts of its history. Once men of Near Harad made an alliance with those of [Khand](../K/Khand.md) and with the [Wainriders](../W/Wainriders.md) to attack Gondor. King [Ondoher](../O/Ondoher.md) anticipated their strike, and created a [Southern Army](../S/Southern_Army.md) to defend his land against them. Under the command of [Eärnil](../E/Eärnil_II.md), the Southern Army destroyed the army of Harad in [South Ithilien](../S/South_Ithilien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-4">[4]</a></sup> It seems likely that many of the [Haradrim](../H/Haradrim.md) who troubled Gondor, actually were men of Near Harad.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-1997: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><b>Amrûn</b> (meaning &#34;Sunrise&#34; in Sindarin), known as Korb Taskral (meaning &#34;Dwelling of the Stone King&#34; in Haradaic) before S.A. 2280, has been the capital of one of the more powerful kingdoms of the Haruze (Haradrim) in the region of Near Harad.<sup id="cite_ref-Amrûn_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amrûn-5">[5]</a></sup> It is located at the confluence of the river Ode Pezar with the river Harnen<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and at the meeting of three major trade roads, the Rath Khand, the Men Harad, and the Men Harnen<sup id="cite_ref-Amrûn_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amrûn-5">[5]</a></sup>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Pezarsan</b> (meaning &#34;Realm of Pezar&#34; in Haradaic) is a region in Near Harad east of the city of Amrûn. Its population is concentrated around the river Ode Pezar and the paved expanse of the Rak Harmal. There are a number of towns and cities and roving clans of nomads. The economy of Pezarsan consists of farming the riverside, the herding sheep and goats and trade along the caravan routes to the east and south. The lands of Pezarsan are ruled by local Haruze (Haradrim) governments in T.A. 1640.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Chelkar</b> (meaning &#34;Sheltered Home&#34; in Haradaic) is an upland region in northeastern Near Harad. It is centred on the river Ode Auchel Chelkar has borders with Harondor, Nurn and Khand. The Ode Auchel and a number of small streams that flow into it from the mountains drain the lands south of the Auducar Nurn and the north of the rugged hills of the Ausk Dubar. The green valleys of Chelkar are narrow and bordered by rolling hills and support small towns. The air in Chelkar is less blazing hot than in the lowlands.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Lurmsakûn</b> (meaning &#34;Basin of Flowing Waters&#34; in Haradaic) is a region of rivers and oases in eastern Near Harad with a border to the plateau of Khand and the foothills of the Ered Harmal. It is ruled by Haruze (Haradrim) rulers that swore allegiance to Gondor at the height of the power of Gondor. During most of the Third Age the lands of Lurmsakûn were contested between the kings of Lurmsakûn and the kings of the Chey and the kings of the Variags.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The land called Near Harad by <a href="../G/Gondor.md" title="Gondor">Gondorians</a> is known to its inhabitants as &#34;Shagâna&#34;, the Fair Land. The first explorable half of Shagâna, the valley of river &#34;Ikorbân&#34;, was added with the <i>Legacy of <a href="../M/Morgoth.md" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a></i> expansion in 2024. The second half, &#34;Mûr Ghala, the Ridge of Plenty&#34;, was released with the <i>Kingdoms of Harad</i> expansion in 2025. Unlike the scorched arid lands of &#34;Jarmát&#34;, or <a href="../F/Far_Harad.md" title="Far Harad">Far Harad</a>, Shagâna has more temperate climate and features a mix of river valleys, wetlands, and forests as well as arid highlands, deserts, and rocky canyons. <a href="../A/Avari.md" title="Avari">Kintai</a> <a href="../E/Elves.md" title="Elves">elves</a> and <a href="../B/Blacklocks.md" title="Blacklocks">Temámir</a> <a href="../D/Dwarves.md" title="Dwarves">dwarves</a> live in Shagâna along with the humans but remain secluded and rarely deal with the outsiders.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Shagâna had contended with invaders for much of its history, whether they came from <a href="Númenor.md" title="Númenor">Númenor</a>, <a href="../G/Gondor.md" title="Gondor">Gondor</a>, <a href="../U/Umbar.md" title="Umbar">Umbar</a>, <a href="../K/Khand.md" title="Khand">Khand</a>, or <a href="../F/Far_Harad.md" title="Far Harad">Far Harad</a>. For much of the <a href="../T/Third_Age.md" title="Third Age">Third Age</a>, a sovereign kingdom of Hamât ruled much of Shagâna, though its exact reach ebbed and flowed over time. It finally fell around <a href="../T/Third_Age.md" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="../T/Third_Age_2820.md" title="Third Age 2820">2820</a>, when all of Shagâna was conquered by the southern Empire of Ordâkh from <a href="../F/Far_Harad.md" title="Far Harad">Far Harad</a>, who brought with it forced worship of the Church of <a href="../S/Sauron.md" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>. The Shagâni were oppressed but remembered their history, and when news came of the defeat of Sauron and the death of the <a href="../B/Black_serpent.md" title="Black serpent">Ordâkhai Hûl (King)</a> were quick to push out the Ordâkhai and begin to reassert their independence. The Empire of Ordâkh, though diminished in land and power, was quick to reassert itself under a new Hûl, leading to renewed hostilities in Shagâna by summer of <a href="../T/Third_Age_3019.md" title="Third Age 3019">Third Age 3019</a>.</dd></dl>
