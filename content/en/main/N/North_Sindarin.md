**North Sindarin**, also called **Mithrimin** or just **Mithrim**,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> is an extinct dialect of [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md). It was spoken in [Mithrim](../M/Mithrim.md) and the highlands of [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md).

## History {#History}

The [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md) of [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) were divided in several groups, and their language had developed some dialects. North Sindarin, the flavour of Sindarin spoken by the *Mithrim*, the northernmost group of the Sindar, differed from the Sindarin of Beleriand proper in many aspects. It was this language which was adopted by the exiled [Noldor](Noldor.md) after their return to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), and by their mortal allies, the [House of Bëor](../H/House_of_Bëor.md). During this time North Sindarin was changed much, partially due to the adoption of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) features, and partially due to the love of the Noldor for making linguistic changes. [Beren](../B/Beren.md)'s heritage was clear to [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md) of [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md) as he spoke the North Sindarin of his homeland.

North Sindarin retained many features of Archaic Sindarin which had been lost in the Sindarin of Beleriand proper, but also went through several changes of its own: lenition occurred far less in this dialect than in the other dialects.

After the end of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), the survivors of Beleriand's realms generally adopted the more southern variants of Sindarin, but several proper names uninterpretable in normal Sindarin which remained in use during the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) show North Sindarin influence.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In Tolkien's earliest writings, there was a language called [Ilkorin](../I/Ilkorin.md), the language of the Ilkorindi (the [Dark Elves](../D/Dark_Elves.md) who stayed in the [Great Lands](../M/Middle-earth.md)). After Tolkien evolved his mythology, the background and setting of the stories changed, and Ilkorin was replaced by [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md), the language of the [Sindar](../S/Sindar.md).

[Edward Kloczko](../E/Edward_Kloczko.md) observed that the few words mentioned as "Mithrim" fit to the phonological changes and principles seen in Ilkorin: for example Mithrim has *ô* where Sindarin has *au*, and retains final *-v* and *-m* where Sindarin has final *-w*; he theorized that Tolkien recycled parts of Ilkorin as Mithrim.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### The case of cw {#The_case_of_cw}

Sindarin descends from [Common Telerin](../C/Common_Telerin.md), where the primitive sound *kw* (seen in Quenya as *qu*) was simplified in *p* (cf. Quenya *[quár](../Q/Quár.md)* against Telerin *[Pār](../P/Pār.md)* and Sindarin *[paur](../P/Paur.md)*).

Ilkorin, which was conceived by Tolkien for an earlier version of the setting, maintains primitive *kw*, which stays *cw* or *c* in some Ilkorin words such as *alch* (<a href="../L/LAK.md" title="LAK">ALÁK<sup>w</sup>Â</a>), *côm* (<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=KWAM&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="KWAM (page does not exist)">K<sup>w</sup>AM</a>), *cwess* (<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=KWESS&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="KWESS (page does not exist)">K<sup>w</sup>ESS</a>), *salch* (<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=SAL%C3%81K&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="SALÁK (page does not exist)">SALÁK<sup>w</sup>Ê</a>): a phonetic retention that would be unexpected in a Sindarin dialect. (Also, some ancestral sounds extinct in Sindarin were changed into the viable sounds "g" and "go" in Ilkorin.)

Assuming North Sindarin is not, linguistically speaking, completely identical to Ilkorin, and thus as in other Sindarin dialects the "kw" sound was not conserved, the aforementioned words may exist in the forms of <i>*alph, *pôm, *pess, *salph</i> in North Sindarin.

### Mithrimin and Doriathrin {#Mithrimin_and_Doriathrin}

The word *[lómin](../L/Lómin.md)* (cf. [Dor-Lómin](../D/Dor-Lómin~15272.md)) was a variant of *lómen* "echoing", both in [Doriathrin](../D/Doriathrin.md),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> another dialect of Tolkien's early legendarium; Doriathrin shows adjectives ending in *-in* instead of standard Sindarin <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=-en&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="-en (page does not exist)">-en</a></i> (<i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../D/Dungorthin.md" title="Dungorthin">Dungorthin</a>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Muilin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Muilin (page does not exist)">muilin</a></i>).

Tolkien later redefined *lómin* as a Mithrimin word as an example of the absence of [a-affection](../A/A-affection.md) in the dialect,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> this absence resulting in unusual *-in* endings.

Likewise, the ending *-in* is attested in Ilkorin (<i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ermabin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ermabin (page does not exist)">Ermabin</a></i>) but also *-en* (<i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%BAren&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Thúren (page does not exist)">thúren</a>, <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Mablosgen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mablosgen (page does not exist)">Mablosgen</a></i>). <span></span>
