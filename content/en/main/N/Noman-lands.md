The **Noman-lands** were a desolate region<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup> southeast of the [Dead Marshes](../D/Dead_Marshes.md) and northwest of the [Desolation of the Morannon](../D/Desolation_of_the_Morannon.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Marshes_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marshes-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Southeast of the Dead Marshes lay arid moors with dead peats and wide flats of dry cracked mud that were followed by barren and pitiless long shallow rising slopes.<sup id="cite_ref-Marshes_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marshes-2">[2]</a></sup>

[Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) and [Sam](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md), led by [Gollum](../G/Gollum.md) passed through the Noman-lands on [2 March](../_/2_March.md) and [3 March](../_/3_March.md), [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ChronNotes_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ChronNotes-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup> On their journey to the [Black Gate](../B/Black_Gate.md), upon reaching the living lands and entering the desolate ones that stretched out in front of the Gate, the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../H/Host_of_the_West.md" title="Host of the West">Host of the West</a> could see the "[marshes](../D/Dead_Marshes.md) and the desert that stretched north and west to the [Emyn Muil](../E/Emyn_Muil.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> This desert was almost certainly the Noman-lands.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The Noman-lands appear on the [first map](../T/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) that [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) drew while he wrote *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* and on [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md)'s [1943 map](../T/The_1943_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md). On these maps they are located southwest of the Dead Marshes and northwest of [Dagorlad](../D/Dagorlad.md) which was just northeast of the [Morannon](../M/Morannon.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Map_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-9">[8]</a></sup> Although these early conceptions place the Noman-lands between Sarn Gebir (Emyn Muil) and the Gates of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md), this does not align with later conceptions and maps, in which the geography is rearranged and the Dead Marshes are moved southwest, so that they lie in the region between the Emyn Muil and the gates previously occupied by the Noman-lands. This geography appears on the *[Map of Rohan, Gondor, and Mordor](../M/Map_of_Rohan,_Gondor,_and_Mordor.md)*, and the Noman-lands do not appear there or in any other published maps.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WRchanges_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WRchanges-10">[9]</a></sup>

Frodo's journey through the Noman-lands is not included in the earliest manuscript for *[The Two Towers](../T/The_Two_Towers.md)*, where only the dreadful passage through the marshes is mentioned. However, the later, expanded passages, including those in the published version, also reference the Noman-lands, and two days<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 2]</a></sup> were added to the [Tale of Years](../T/Tale_of_Years.md) and account for the time spent on this journey.<sup id="cite_ref-WRchanges_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WRchanges-10">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ChronNotes_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ChronNotes-5">[5]</a></sup>

### Early names {#Early_names}

In a draft of the chapter *Farewell to Lórien*, the Noman-lands were called <i><b>Uvanwaith</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup> This name is not glossed, but has been conjectured to be derived from the negative prefix [ú-](../_/Ú-.md); *waith*, the lenited form of [gweith](../G/Gweith.md) (which can refer to "people", or "region"); and the middle component, although more difficult to identify, has been suggested by Roman Rausch to be <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Anw&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Anw (page does not exist)">anw</a> "male", or a lenited form of [man](<../M/Man_(word).md>) "someone".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[11]</a></sup> Other variants of this region's name appearing in earlier drafts include "Nomenlands", "Nomen's Land", "Noman-land(s)" and "No Man's Land".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup> In the first edition of *The Lord of the Rings*, the region is referred to as the "Nomen's land", rather than the Noman-lands of later editions.<sup id="cite_ref-Map_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-9">[8]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Hammond and Scull suggest that the name was derived from *no man's land*, a name for the ground that lies between the trenches of one army and the trenches of the enemy army, which J.R.R. Tolkien was familiar with, because he participated in the Battle of the Somme in 1916.<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[14]</a></sup>
