The **[Nazgûl](#Etymology)** ([Black Speech](/wiki/Black_Speech) for "**Ringwraiths**"), also known as the **Nine Riders** or **Black Riders** (or simply **the Nine**), were [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)'s "most terrible servants" in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). They were mortal [Men](/wiki/Men) who had been turned into wraiths by their [Nine Rings](/wiki/Nine_Rings) of power.

## History {#History}

### Origins {#Origins}

Sometime during the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) (after the year [1697](/wiki/Second_Age_1697 "Second Age 1697")<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup>) Sauron gave [nine Rings of Power](/wiki/Nine_Rings "Nine Rings") to nine mortal [Men](/wiki/Men). It is said that three of the Nine were great lords of [Númenorean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenoreans "Númenoreans") race.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

The bearers of the rings gained great wealth and prestige and became "*mighty in their day, kings, sorcerers, and warriors of old*". The rings enabled them to turn invisible and see the [Unseen](/wiki/Unseen). Their lives were prolonged so they seemed unending, but life became unendurable to them. Over time, depending on their native strength and their initial good or evil will, they succumbed to the powers of the rings and the domination of the [One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). Their bodily forms faded until they became permanently invisible and turned into [wraiths](/wiki/Wraiths "Wraiths").<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[4]</a></sup>

Known as the Nazgûl, they first appeared around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [2251](/wiki/Second_Age_2251 "Second Age 2251")<sup id="cite_ref-AppB1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB1-6">[5]</a></sup> and were soon established as Sauron's principal servants. However, the circumstances of this appearance are unknown, as are their actions during the [Dark Years](/wiki/Dark_Years), during Sauron's capture to [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), and in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance). When Sauron was overthrown in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441") at the hands of the [Last Alliance](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men "Last Alliance of Elves and Men"), the wraiths faded into the shadows.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB1_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB1-6">[5]</a></sup>

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

The One Ring which dominated the Nazgûl was taken from Sauron and then it was lost in [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) for the following centuries; both Sauron and the wraiths lost their powers and remained hidden.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[4]</a></sup> However around [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050") of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) Sauron returned in the form of a shadow in [Greenwood the Great](/wiki/Mirkwood "Mirkwood"), triggering also the return of his servants. [About 50 years later](/wiki/Third_Age_1100 "Third Age 1100") [the Wise](/wiki/The_Wise "The Wise") of that Age discovered the [shadow](/wiki/Shadow "Shadow") of [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur) and theorized that it was one of the Nazgûl.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-7">[6]</a></sup>

But it was around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1300](/wiki/Third_Age_1300 "Third Age 1300") when evil things, like the [Orcs of the Misty Mountains](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains), multiplied again, and the Nazgûl definitely reappeared; it was then when [Lord of the Nazgûl](/wiki/Witch-king "Witch-king") gathered evil [Men](/wiki/Men) and founded the Witch-kingdom of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar). From there he led Sauron's forces against the mannish kingdom of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1409](/wiki/Third_Age_1409 "Third Age 1409").<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-7">[6]</a></sup>

Meanwhile [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) was weakened by the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) and [War](/wiki/Wainrider/Balchoth_War "Wainrider/Balchoth War"), and it was believed that Sauron's plan was to open the way to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor); indeed as the watch on Mordor stopped and Gondor's borders withdrew, it is believed that the Nazgûl freely re-entered Mordor around [1856](/wiki/Third_Age_1856 "Third Age 1856") or later.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-8">[7]</a></sup>

The Witch-king was eventually [defeated](/wiki/Battle_of_Fornost "Battle of Fornost") in battle in [1975](/wiki/Third_Age_1975 "Third Age 1975") and [sometime later](/wiki/Third_Age_1980 "Third Age 1980") returned to [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), gathering the other Nazgûl in preparation for the return of Sauron to that realm.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-8">[7]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2000](/wiki/Third_Age_2000 "Third Age 2000"), they issued by [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol), began a two-year siege against [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil) and [captured it](/wiki/Second_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil "Second Fall of Minas Ithil"). The city thereafter became their stronghold Nazgûl, from where they directed the rebuilding of Sauron's armies, also acquiring [one of](/wiki/Ithil-stone "Ithil-stone") the *[palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri "Palantíri")* for the Dark Lord.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-7">[6]</a></sup>

During the [Watchful Peace](/wiki/Watchful_Peace), when Sauron hid from the power of the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council), the Nazgûl also remained hidden in [Morgul Vale](/wiki/Morgul_Vale), allowing some peaceful time for the first [Ruling Stewards](/wiki/Ruling_Stewards) of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>

But after his defeat in [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur), Sauron returned to Mordor in [2942](/wiki/Third_Age_2942 "Third Age 2942") and declared himself openly in [2951](/wiki/Third_Age_2951 "Third Age 2951"). Three of the Nazgûl were sent to his fortress at Dol Guldur to garrison that outpost.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-7">[6]</a></sup>

In [3017](/wiki/Third_Age_3017 "Third Age 3017") Sauron commanded the Ringwraiths to recover [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") of Power from "Baggins of the Shire". Disguised as horse riders clad in black (hence the term *Black Riders*), they sought out [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) who, as [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) had revealed, had the One Ring in his possession.

The [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) of [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain") spoke of a rider who had come to offer Sauron's friendship and ask about [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits). As a token of their friendship he asked for a "trifle", a ring, "the least of rings", that the [thief](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") had stolen.<sup id="cite_ref-council_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-10">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 2]</a></sup>

### War of the Ring {#War_of_the_Ring}

Sauron decided to assault Gondor directly. In [attack](/wiki/Sauron%27s_attack_on_Osgiliath "Sauron's attack on Osgiliath") on [Osgiliath](/wiki/Osgiliath) he let the Nazgûl lead, causing terror with the [Black Shadow](/wiki/Black_Shadow) among the [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians)<sup id="cite_ref-council_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-10">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup> and making them think that they are the vanguard of Sauron's military force. However this was a feigned move of Sauron, who intended them to send them invisible to look for the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-hunt_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunt-13">[11]</a></sup>

The Nazgûl were given black horses (stolen from [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan)) for transportation and set off to find "[the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")", on their way learning from a [Squint-eyed southerner](/wiki/Squint-eyed_southerner) that [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) was not a true ally of Sauron. Failing to find it, they returned to the [Wold](/wiki/Wold) empty-handed; messengers from Sauron sent them to [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard). There they encountered [Gríma Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma_Wormtongue), who told them of the location of the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-hunt_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunt-13">[11]</a></sup>

Crossing the [Fords of Isen](/wiki/Fords_of_Isen), they reached [Sarn Ford](/wiki/Sarn_Ford) at evening and drove off the guard of [Rangers of the North](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North) and five pursued the Rangers eastward, then returned to guard the [Greenway](/wiki/Greenway). The other four entered the Shire before the dawn of [23 September](/wiki/23_September); at nightfall [Khamûl](/wiki/Kham%C3%BBl) came to [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) and interrogated [Hamfast Gamgee](/wiki/Hamfast_Gamgee) asking for "[Baggins](/wiki/Baggins)", not knowing that [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins), the [Ring-bearer](/wiki/Ring-bearer), had left Bag End on his way out of Hobbiton that same day.<sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup>

On [24 September](/wiki/24_September) a Rider approached Frodo and his companions near the [Woody End](/wiki/Woody_End), when a [Wandering Company](/wiki/Wandering_Companies "Wandering Companies") of [Elves](/wiki/Elves) approached from [Emyn Beraid](/wiki/Emyn_Beraid), chanting the name of "<a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Elbereth" title="Elbereth">Elbereth</a>", and the rider fled away.<sup id="cite_ref-Three_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Three-14">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78–9</sup> The [next day](/wiki/25_September "25 September") a Rider came to [Bamfurlong](/wiki/Bamfurlong) and approached [Farmer Maggot](/wiki/Farmer_Maggot), asking about Baggins. The farmer directed him to Hobbiton. The Rider said that the individual had already left Hobbiton and offered Maggot gold if he would inform the rider if he saw Baggins. The farmer felt chill and was enraged by his trespassing and threatened him with his [dogs](/wiki/Dogs "Dogs"), which, however, yelped and ran. The Rider, infuriated by his defiance, hissed and rode away.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>

On [26 September](/wiki/26_September), two Riders came to *[The Prancing Pony](/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony)* at [Bree](/wiki/Bree) and asked [Nob](/wiki/Nob) for "Baggins", making the dogs and geese noisy. Nob, terrified, slammed the door on them, and the Riders continued all the way to [Archet](/wiki/Archet).<sup id="cite_ref-strid_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strid-16">[14]</a></sup>

Three days later, on [29 September](/wiki/29_September) at night, the hobbits arrived at the *Prancing Pony*.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[15]</a></sup> On the same day, three Riders arrived in Bree and were informed by a spy from Isengard about the events with the Hobbits that took place at the *Prancing Pony* and guessed that the One Ring is present. One Rider was sent to find the Witch-king, but was stopped and driven away by the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain), so that he only met the Witch-king on the next day.<sup id="cite_ref-PrancingRC_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PrancingRC-18">[16]</a></sup> After [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo), [Pippin](/wiki/Pippin), and Sam returned from the big common-room to the parlour of the *Prancing Pony*, [Merry](/wiki/Merry) went outside for a walk, followed a shadow to the last house of the road in the dark, but began to feel terrified. After he had turned around to run back something came behind him and he fell over and became unconscious. However, [Nob](/wiki/Nob) saw two men on the road lifting something and shouted.<sup id="cite_ref-strid_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strid-16">[14]</a></sup> When he reached the spot on the road<sup id="cite_ref-strid_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strid-16">[14]</a></sup> the two Riders had disappeared<sup id="cite_ref-PrancingRC_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PrancingRC-18">[16]</a></sup> and he only found the unconscious Merry lying there<sup id="cite_ref-strid_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strid-16">[14]</a></sup>. Merry and Nob returned to the parlour and informed Frodo, Pippin, Sam, and [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) about what had happened. As a result, they decided to not go back to their rooms, but to spend the night in the parlour and to bar its window and door.<sup id="cite_ref-strid_16-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strid-16">[14]</a></sup>

In the early hours before dawn of [30 September](/wiki/30_September), the two Riders rode through Bree and may have attacked the *Prancing Pony* while other Riders attacked [Crickhollow](/wiki/Crickhollow).<sup id="cite_ref-PrancingRC_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PrancingRC-18">[16]</a></sup> When Aragorn and the Hobbits got up and returned to their bedrooms they discovered that the windows had been forced open, the beds had been tossed about, the bolsters had been slashed and the brown mat had been torn to pieces. At around ten o'clock Aragorn and the Hobbits left Bree eastwards on the East Road.<sup id="cite_ref-knife_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-knife-19">[17]</a></sup>

The Riders pursued [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) from Bree and attacked him at night on [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop), where he defended himself with his [magic](/wiki/Magic "Magic").<sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup> Four Riders followed Gandalf as he escaped to the north.<sup id="cite_ref-council_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-10">[9]</a></sup> The others later attacked [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and the [hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") while they were camping under Weathertop, and the Witch-king [wounded](/wiki/Morgul-wound "Morgul-wound") [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) with a [Morgul-knife](/wiki/Morgul-knife).<sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-knife_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-knife-19">[17]</a></sup>

On [11 October](/wiki/11_October) [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018")<sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup> three Riders were guarding the [Last Bridge](/wiki/Last_Bridge) of [Mitheithel](/wiki/Mitheithel), expecting [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and the [hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") on their journey to [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell). Their plan was foiled by [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel), who drove them away.<sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Flight_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-20">[18]</a></sup> Two days later, some of them pursued Frodo across the [Ford of Bruinen](/wiki/Ford_of_Bruinen).<sup id="cite_ref-b3_12-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b3-12">[10]</a></sup> [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) and [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) released a flood and the waters of [Bruinen](/wiki/Bruinen) swept away and drowned the horses of the Riders.<sup id="cite_ref-council_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-council-10">[9]</a></sup>

The Ringwraiths were forced to return to Mordor to regroup.<sup id="cite_ref-Disguises_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Disguises-21">[19]</a></sup> They reappeared later mounted on [flying creatures](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl-birds "Nazgûl-birds"),<sup id="cite_ref-White_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-22">[20]</a></sup> at which point they were referred to as **winged Nazgûl**.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[21]</a></sup>

By the conclusion of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), all of the Nine Nazgûl were destroyed. The [Lord of the Nazgûl](/wiki/Witch-king "Witch-king") himself was slain by [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn), the niece of King [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden) (with help from [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck")) during the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields).<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[22]</a></sup> The remaining eight Ringwraiths attacked the Army of the West during the last battle at the [Black Gate](/wiki/Black_Gate).<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[23]</a></sup> However, when [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) put on the ring in the fires of [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom), Sauron ordered the remaining Nazgûl to fly with all possible speed to Mount Doom to intercept Frodo. They arrived too late, with the Ring falling into the fire along with the hapless Gollum. The Nazgûl were caught in the firestorm of the erupting mountain and were destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[24]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

### Appearance {#Appearance}

To mortals, the Nazgûl appeared as shadowy Men hidden by black hoods and cloaks that reached down to their boots.<sup id="cite_ref-Three_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Three-14">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:74</sup> Without their cloaks, they were shapeless,<sup id="cite_ref-MeetingsTwo_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MeetingsTwo-27">[25]</a></sup> and invisible to all but the wearer of [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring").<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[4]</a></sup> When the Witch-king removed his hood, his crown could be seen, although his head was invisible and the red fires shone through it.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-28">[26]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:829</sup> During the Battle of the Pelennor Fields, the deadly gleam of his eyes could be seen.<sup id="cite_ref-PelennorPage_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PelennorPage-29">[27]</a></sup>

When Frodo put on the Ring at Weathertop, he could see the forms of the Nazgûl clearly. They were tall, and beneath their black cloaks, they were robed in grey. Their hair was grey, and they wore silver helms, although the Witch-king wore a crown. Their eyes were merciless and piercing, and their hands were haggard. The Witch-king held a [knife](/wiki/Morgul-knife "Morgul-knife"), and the knife and his hand shone with a pale light.<sup id="cite_ref-Visible_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Visible-30">[28]</a></sup>

### Powers and abilities {#Powers_and_abilities}

The chief power of the Nazgȗl was the unreasoning fear they struck in living things. Their powers and the fear that they inspired are greatly increased in darkness.<sup id="cite_ref-L210_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L210-31">[29]</a></sup> During the [Siege of Gondor](/wiki/Siege_of_Gondor), their cries were filled with evil and horror. When the Ringwraiths passed overhead unseen, even the bravest dropped their weapons from their nerveless hands or cowered on the ground due to the fear that overcame them. As a blackness entered their minds, they no longer thought about fighting, and only thought of hiding, crawling away, and death.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-28">[26]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:823</sup> Even when they could not be seen or heard, their terrible presence could be felt, and those nearby felt a sense of dread.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[30]</a></sup> Animals also feared them, and the only creatures that endured them were horses bred for the service of Sauron<sup id="cite_ref-MeetingsTwo_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MeetingsTwo-27">[25]</a></sup> and the [winged beasts](/wiki/Fell_beasts "Fell beasts") that were given to them when their horses were destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-White_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-22">[20]</a></sup> The evil aura that surrounded them was known as the *[Black Breath](/wiki/Black_Breath)*, and those that came under its shadow fell ill and eventually passed into silence and died.<sup id="cite_ref-Houses_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Houses-33">[31]</a></sup>

The Ringwraiths did not have great physical power against fearless opponents.<sup id="cite_ref-L210_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L210-31">[29]</a></sup> The Nazgûl also used physical weapons. At Weathertop, they bore steel swords, and the Witch-king had a knife that he stabbed Frodo with.<sup id="cite_ref-Visible_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Visible-30">[28]</a></sup> A shard of the [Morgul-knife](/wiki/Morgul-knife) remained in Frodo's shoulder, and was moving deeper before it was removed by [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond). It caused Frodo to begin to fade into the [wraith-world](/wiki/Unseen "Unseen"), but the shard was unable to pierce his heart and bring him completely under the control of the Nazgȗl.<sup id="cite_ref-MeetingsTwo_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MeetingsTwo-27">[25]</a></sup> The Ringwraiths were also known to use deadly black darts.<sup id="cite_ref-HousesPage_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HousesPage-34">[32]</a></sup> The Witch-king used a black mace during the Battle of the Pelennor Fields.<sup id="cite_ref-PelennorPage_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PelennorPage-29">[27]</a></sup>

The Nazgûl could not be slain by arrows,<sup id="cite_ref-White_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-22">[20]</a></sup> and only weapons endowed with a special power, such as the [blade of Westernesse](/wiki/Blade_of_Westernesse "Blade of Westernesse") that Merry used, could do them any great damage.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[33]</a></sup> Any blades that pierced the Witch-king were destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-20">[18]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:198</sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Attack_of_the_Wraiths.jpg"><img src="/w/images/1/10/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Attack_of_the_Wraiths.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="278" height="450" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="278" data-file-height="450"/></a><figcaption><i>The Attack of the Wraiths</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

The Nazgûl existed mostly in the wraith-world and required their cloaks to give them form during their interactions with the living.<sup id="cite_ref-MeetingsTwo_27-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MeetingsTwo-27">[25]</a></sup> When their steeds and disguises were destroyed, they were forced to return to Sauron to receive new ones.<sup id="cite_ref-Disguises_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Disguises-21">[19]</a></sup>

The Nazgûl had poor vision in the world of light, but they were aware of the presence of other beings, and their perception grew stronger in the dark. They could detect living things by the smell of their blood, and they were drawn by the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-knife_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-knife-19">[17]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:189</sup> They could also see beings with a presence in the wraith-world, such as the wearer of the One Ring, as well as certain powerful Elves with control over the Unseen, such as [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel). Anyone who could see into the wraith-world could see the Nazgûl, as Frodo did on Weathertop<sup id="cite_ref-MeetingsTwo_27-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MeetingsTwo-27">[25]</a></sup> and at the Ford of Bruinen.<sup id="cite_ref-FlightPage_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FlightPage-36">[34]</a></sup>

The [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) could also affect objects and people with his power, and was able to strike Frodo dumb and shatter the dagger that Frodo had gotten in the [Barrow-downs](/wiki/Barrow-downs) by raising his hand,<sup id="cite_ref-FlightPage_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FlightPage-36">[34]</a></sup> and he also weakened both hearts and stone with terrifying words of power while attacking the [Gate](/wiki/Great_Gate_of_Minas_Tirith "Great Gate of Minas Tirith") of [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith). He also caused flames to run along his sword.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-28">[26]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:828–9</sup> Whether other Nazgûl could perform similar feats is unknown.

## Identities {#Identities}

Only a few of the Nazgûl are named or identified individually. Their leader was the [Witch-king of Angmar](/wiki/Witch-king "Witch-king"), and his second in command was named [Khamûl](/wiki/Kham%C3%BBl). Khamûl was a lord of [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings),<sup id="cite_ref-Black_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Black-37">[35]</a></sup> and was the only Nazgûl known by his name. Three of them were [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans).<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-3">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Nazgûl* means "ringwraiths" in the [Black Speech](/wiki/Black_Speech). It is a compound of *[nazg](/wiki/Nazg "Nazg")* ("ring") and *[gûl](/wiki/G%C3%BBl "Gûl")* ("\[evil\] spirit under control of Sauron").<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[36]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Among their many names and titles were: the Ringwraiths,<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-39">[37]</a></sup> the Black Riders,<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[38]</a></sup> the Fell Riders,<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[39]</a></sup> the Nine Riders,<sup id="cite_ref-FlightPage_36-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FlightPage-36">[34]</a></sup> the Nine,<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-39">[37]</a></sup> the Nine Servants of the Lord of the Rings,<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[40]</a></sup> the Black Wings,<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[41]</a></sup> Winged Messenger(s),<sup id="cite_ref-White_22-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-22">[20]</a></sup> the Shadows,<sup id="cite_ref-HousesPage_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HousesPage-34">[32]</a></sup> the Shriekers<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[42]</a></sup>, the Nine Ring-slaves<sup id="cite_ref-:0_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-45">[43]</a></sup>, and the Black Rider(s) of the Air<sup id="cite_ref-:0_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-45">[43]</a></sup>.

An [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) name given for the Ringwraiths is <i><b>Úlairi</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[44]</a></sup> Linguists have remarked that it is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) plural name of unknown meaning and etymology.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[45]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[46]</a></sup> [Michael Martinez](/wiki/Michael_Martinez), analysing the word, has tentatively suggested that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p> <i>Úlairi</i> is a compound formed from <i>Ú</i> + <i>lai</i> + <i>ri</i> with probable meaning of &#34;un (bad or immoral)&#34; + &#34;shadow&#34; + &#34;ones&#34;. In other words, the name may mean something like &#34;those who are in/of unnatural shadow&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[47]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

### Films {#Films}

<b>1978: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Nine are clad in brown and black, and have red eyes. The attack on <i><a href="/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony" title="The Prancing Pony">The Prancing Pony</a></i> is their deed, not that of any accomplices. After the attack, they cast off their hoods, revealing the black armour and hideous masks they wear beneath their cloaks.</dd></dl>

<b>1980: <a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)"><i>The Return of the King</i> (1980 film)</a></b>

<dl><dd>The Nine are skull headed demons, who ride winged horses. The <a href="/wiki/Witch-king" title="Witch-king">Witch-king</a> himself rides a dragon-like creature, and has no face. Only a suspended crown and two red eyes can be seen. The Nazgûl have the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Red_Eye" title="Red Eye">Red Eye</a> of <a href="/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr" title="Barad-dûr">Barad-dûr</a> rather than the emblem of <a href="/wiki/Minas_Morgul" title="Minas Morgul">Minas Morgul</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2001-03: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Nazgûl serve as the main antagonists in the first half of <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">the first film</a>. Their dialogue is changed; the conversation with <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Hamfast_Gamgee" title="Hamfast Gamgee">Hamfast Gamgee</a> is omitted, and the conversation with <a href="/wiki/Farmer_Maggot" title="Farmer Maggot">Farmer Maggot</a> is reduced. In the <a href="/wiki/Unseen" title="Unseen">Unseen</a>, they are pale white ghostly creatures.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>After the death of the Witch-king in <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">the third film</a>, the other eight are taken out by <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Eagles" title="Eagles">Eagles</a> and debris from <a href="/wiki/Mount_Doom" title="Mount Doom">Mount Doom</a>, however, nothing is told of their individual fates.</dd></dl>

<b>2012-14: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Witch-king and the other Ringwraiths were buried in a dungeon in the <a href="/wiki/High_Fells_of_Rhudaur" title="High Fells of Rhudaur">High Fells of Rhudaur</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Fornost" title="Battle of Fornost">fall</a> of Angmar. The Witch-king initially appears in Dol Guldur, where he briefly fights <a href="/wiki/Radagast" title="Radagast">Radagast</a> with his Morgul-blade; he drops it and Radagast takes it, ultimately bringing it to <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a>, who then shows to the <a href="/wiki/White_Council" title="White Council">White Council</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rivendell" title="Rivendell">Rivendell</a>. Later, Gandalf, following Galadriel&#39;s advice, visits the High Fells and discovers that the Nazgûl&#39;s cells were empty, broken open from the inside. Through this investigation, he is convinced that the Necromancer in Dol Guldur is indeed Sauron.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>During the <a href="/wiki/Attack_on_Dol_Guldur" title="Attack on Dol Guldur">Attack on Dol Guldur</a> they appeared in armour, ghostly versions of their mortal forms. The Nine do battle with Galadriel, <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a>, and Elrond. During the confrontation between Galadriel and Sauron, their living forms seem to <a href="/wiki/Unseen" title="Unseen">phase in and out of reality</a> as holograms. Eventually they are driven to Mordor, with their master following suit.</dd></dl>

The player is later able to find the corpse of the fell beast in the <a href="/wiki/Brown_Lands" title="Brown Lands">Brown Lands</a> and it becomes clear that a Nazgûl is not far. At night, the Wraith ambushes the player who manages to drive him away with the use of fire.</dd></dl>
