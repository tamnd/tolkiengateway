**Nobottle** was a town in the [Westfarthing](../W/Westfarthing.md) of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md), west of [Needlehole](Needlehole.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/c/c6/CdxIconArticleDisambiguation.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content">See also: <a href="../H/Hardbottle.md" title="Hardbottle">Hardbottle</a></div></div>

[David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) suggests that the name represents a possible [Old Hobbitish](../O/Old_English.md) etymology \**Neowebotl*, therefore meaning "new dwelling".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

*Bottle* in an element in English place-names meaning "large dwelling".<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup> Old English <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bold#Old_English" title="wiktionary:bold">bold</a></i> or <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/botl#Old_English" title="wiktionary:botl">botl</a></i> mean "building, house, dwelling".

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The name was one of those proposed by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) to his father while drawing [A Part of the Shire](../A/A_Part_of_the_Shire.md) in [1943](../_/1943.md), mainly derived from his large collection of books on English place-names.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> However that time Christopher thought that the name referred to an absence of inns, due to some remoteness and poverty of the place.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobottle" title="wikipedia:Nobottle">Nobottle</a> is an actual place-name in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire" title="wikipedia:Northamptonshire">Northamptonshire</a>, England (erroneously listed as being in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland" title="wikipedia:Northumberland">Northumberland</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup> Similarly named actual places (apparently with the same etymology) include <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbold#Places" title="wikipedia:Newbold">Newbold</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobold" title="wikipedia:Nobold">Nobold</a>.
