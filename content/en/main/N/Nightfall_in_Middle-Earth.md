<i><b>Nightfall in Middle-Earth</b></i> is an album by [Blind Guardian](/wiki/Blind_Guardian) that was released in [1998](/wiki/1998).

The album is a concept album based on the [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) book *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. Many of the tracks from the album are not complete songs, but are merely narratives to further the story. Blind Guardian owes many of its lyrics to Tolkien's work, but this album is exclusively dedicated to *The Silmarillion*. The lyrics range from the quest for the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils) until the dawn of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).

"Nightfall in Middle-Earth" was the first album Blind Guardian released in the United States. The result was so positive that Century Media started to release old Blind Guardian hits in the U.S. (records such as "Follow the Blind" and "Battalions of Fear").<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

The track "Mirror Mirror" is included in *Blind DVD: Imaginations Through The Looking Glass*. This DVD was rated by many different music-specialized sites as "a masterpiece".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Thematic references {#Thematic_references}

1. In *"War of Wrath"*, [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) advises his master [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) to flee the triumphant [Valar](/wiki/Valar) in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath).<sup id="cite_ref-S|24_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S|24-5">[5]</a></sup> Morgoth sends him away and reflects on the events leading up to his defeat.
2. In *"Into the Storm"*, Morgoth and [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant), fleeing from [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) after having destroyed the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> struggle for the possession of the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils).
3. *"Lammoth"* is the scream of Morgoth after being attacked by Ungoliant in [Lammoth](/wiki/Lammoth).<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup>
4. In *"Nightfall"*, [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor) and his [Seven Sons](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor "Sons of Fëanor") mourn the destruction wrought by Morgoth, including the slaying of [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB), Fëanor's father, and swear to get revenge on him, in spite to the Valar's disapproval.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup>
5. *"The Minstrel"* is most likely about [Maglor](/wiki/Maglor), son of Fëanor, who composed the song "[Noldolantë](/wiki/Noldolant%C3%AB)".
6. In *"The Curse of Fëanor"*, Fëanor expresses his wrath and anger and relates the misdeeds he commits, especially the [Kinslaying](/wiki/First_Kinslaying "First Kinslaying"), in pursuit of Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup>
7. In *"Captured"*, Morgoth addresses the captive [Maedhros](/wiki/Maedhros), Fëanor's son, and chains him to the [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim) mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-8">[8]</a></sup>
8. In *"Blood Tears"*, Maedhros relates the horrors of his captivity and his deliverance by [Fingon](/wiki/Fingon).<sup id="cite_ref-:1_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-8">[8]</a></sup>
9. *"Mirror Mirror"* recounts how [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon), in view of inevitable defeat, builds the city of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), aided by [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo) ("The Lord of Water").<sup id="cite_ref-:1_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-8">[8]</a></sup>
10. In *"Face the Truth"*, [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin) reflects about the destiny of the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor).
11. In *"Noldor (Dead Winter Reigns)"*, [Mandos](/wiki/Mandos) issues the [Doom of Mandos](/wiki/Doom_of_Mandos) after the [Kinslaying](/wiki/Kinslaying_at_Alqualond%C3%AB "Kinslaying at Alqualondë") at [Alqualondë](/wiki/Alqualond%C3%AB), and Fingolfin recounts his Noldor army's passage from the icy waste of [Helcaraxë](/wiki/Helcarax%C3%AB) and the [Prophecy of the North](/wiki/Prophecy_of_the_North) about the Noldor's fate; he reflects on his own and his people's guilt and foreshadows their ultimate defeat.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup>
12. *"The Battle of Sudden Flame"* talks about the [Dagor Bragollach](/wiki/Dagor_Bragollach), in which Morgoth breaks the [Siege of Angband](/wiki/Siege_of_Angband) using his [Balrogs](/wiki/Balrogs) and [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons).<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">[9]</a></sup>
13. *"Time Stands Still (At the Iron Hill)"* is about Fingolfin riding to the gates of [Angband](/wiki/Angband) to challenge Morgoth to a duel. Fingolfin wounds Morgoth seven times, and manages one last stab to Morgoth's foot before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">[9]</a></sup>
14. *"The Dark Elf"* refers to [Eöl](/wiki/E%C3%B6l) who seduced [Turgon's sister](/wiki/Aredhel "Aredhel") and fathered [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin), who would eventually betray Gondolin.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
15. In *"Thorn"*, Maeglin reflects on his situation and decides to betray Gondolin to Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
16. *"The Eldar"* is Elvenking [Finrod Felagund](/wiki/Finrod "Finrod")'s farewell to his people, dying from wounds received in saving his human friend [Beren](/wiki/Beren) from a [werewolf](/wiki/Werewolf "Werewolf").<sup id="cite_ref-:3_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-12">[12]</a></sup>
17. In *"Nom the Wise"*, Beren mourns his friend Finrod. *Nóm* means "wise" and was the name given to Finrod by Beren's forefather [Bëor](/wiki/B%C3%ABor).
18. In *"When Sorrow Sang"*, Beren sings about his love to the Elven princess [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) and his death at the teeth of Morgoth's wolf [Carcharoth](/wiki/Carcharoth).<sup id="cite_ref-:3_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-12">[12]</a></sup>
19. *"Out on the Water"* refers to the last dwelling-place of Beren and Lúthien, [Dor Firn-i-Guinar](/wiki/Dor_Firn-i-Guinar).
20. In *"The Steadfast"*, Morgoth curses his captive [Húrin](/wiki/H%C3%BArin) who steadfastly refused to reveal the secret location of Gondolin.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-13">[13]</a></sup>
21. In *"A Dark Passage"*, Morgoth ponders his triumph in the [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](/wiki/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad). The song also relates the origins of the kindred of men and Morgoth's curse on Húrin to be witness to his children's tragic fate.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-13">[13]</a></sup>
22. *"Final Chapter (Thus ends...)"* concludes the album, speaking of Morgoth's victory by the "treachery of man" in the Nirnaeth Arnoediad<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> but also of the [hope](/wiki/Estel "Estel") for a new day.<sup id="cite_ref-S|24_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S|24-5">[5]</a></sup>

- **Bonus Tracks**

1. *"Harvest of Sorrow"* has [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin) mourn the loss of his sister [Niënor](/wiki/Ni%C3%ABnor).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>
2. *"The Tides of War"* is about the burning of the swan ships at [Losgar](/wiki/Losgar) sung from the perspective of Feanor and Fingolfin.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup>
