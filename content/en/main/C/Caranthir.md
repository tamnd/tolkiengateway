**Caranthir** was a [Noldorin](../N/Noldor.md) prince and one of the [seven sons](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md) of [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md). Along with the other sons of Fëanor, he swore the [Oath of Fëanor](../O/Oath_of_Fëanor.md), joined the [Exile of the Noldor](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md), and settled in [East Beleriand](../E/East_Beleriand.md).

His severe words to [Angrod](../A/Angrod.md) at an early [council of the Noldor](Council_in_Mithrim.md) influenced the decision of the Noldor to choose [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md) as [High King of the Noldor](../H/High_King_of_the_Noldor.md).

He was first among the Noldor to reestablish contact with the [Dwarves of the Blue Mountains](../D/Dwarves_of_the_Blue_Mountains.md), and through mutually beneficial trade with them became very rich. Later he had mixed relations with [Men](../M/Men.md): originally he looked upon them with disinterest, but after witnessing the valour of the [people of Haleth](../H/Haladin.md), he came to some measure of respect for them.

When Morgoth broke the [Siege of Angband](../S/Siege_of_Angband.md) the sons of Fëanor were scattered, with Caranthir retreating south. After some years they regrouped, and made alliance with the newly-come [Men from the East](<../E/Easterlings_(First_Age).md>). One of their two great chieftains, [Ulfang](../U/Ulfang.md), swore allegiance to Caranthir, but they were faithless and betrayed the Eldar, assuring their defeat in the [Battle of Unnumbered Tears](../B/Battle_of_Unnumbered_Tears.md).

Caranthir, along with his brothers [Celegorm](Celegorm.md) and [Curufin](Curufin.md), later perished in the [Second Kinslaying](../S/Second_Kinslaying.md) when they attacked [Dior](../D/Dior.md) of [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md) in an attempt to reclaim his [Silmaril](../S/Silmaril.md).

Caranthir's most distinguishing features were his exceptionally dark black hair, ruddy complexion, and a personality that was harsh, haughty, and quick to anger.

## History {#History}

### Life in Valinor {#Life_in_Valinor}

**Caranthir** was a Noldorin prince, one of the seven sons of Fëanor and [Nerdanel](../N/Nerdanel.md), born in [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md) during the Time of the [Two Trees](../T/Two_Trees.md). His appearance was notable, being as black-haired as of his grandfather [Finwë](../F/Finwë.md), but having the ruddy complexion of his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-Feanor_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Feanor-2">[2]</a></sup> At some point he married, but nothing of his wife or their family is known.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Fëanor and his sons abode seldom in one place for long, but travelled far and wide upon the confines of Valinor, going even to the borders of the Dark and the cold shores of the [Outer Sea](../O/Outer_Sea.md), seeking the unknown. Often they were guests in the halls of [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md).<sup id="cite_ref-S|Princes_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S|Princes-4">[4]</a></sup>

During the [unrest of the Noldor](../U/Unrest_of_the_Noldor.md) and the strife between Fëanor and his half-brother [Fingolfin](../F/Fingolfin.md), Fëanor was banished from [Tirion](../T/Tirion.md) and took up residence in [Formenos](../F/Formenos.md); he was followed by his father, King Finwë, and his sons.<sup id="cite_ref-Unrest_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Unrest-5">[5]</a></sup>

Along with Finwë, the sons of Fëanor did not attend the [high feast](../H/High_feast.md) and were out riding when Formenos was attacked by [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md) and [Ungoliant](../U/Ungoliant.md) after the pair had [killed the Two Trees](../D/Darkening_of_Valinor.md). They attempted to return, but as they approached they were thrown from their horses and lay upon their faces, blind and without strength. Once the cloud passed and they could move again, they entered Formenos and found Finwë dead, the house ravaged, and the Silmarils stolen.

They then sped to the [Ring of Doom](../R/Ring_of_Doom.md) where [Maedhros](../M/Maedhros.md) informed [Manwë](../M/Manwë.md) and, unknowingly, also Fëanor who kept himself hidden during the conversation. Fëanor reacted by cursing both Melkor and the summons of Manwë, and then fleeing into the night; the sons of Fëanor followed him, dismayed that he might slay himself.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Fëanor and his sons soon returned to Tirion where during a [great speech by Fëanor](../S/Speech_of_Fëanor.md), they took a [terrible and blasphemous oath](../O/Oath_of_Fëanor.md) of enmity and vengeance against all or any who dares to claim any part or right in the Silmarils, and led most of the Noldor into exile.

To obtain the ships needed to pursue Morgoth into [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), Fëanor's host seized them from the [Falmari](../F/Falmari.md), [killing many when they resisted](../K/Kinslaying_at_Alqualondë.md), and thus came under the [Doom of Mandos](../D/Doom_of_Mandos.md).

After a long march northwards, the Noldor drew close to the [Helcaraxë](../H/Helcaraxë.md), a land bridge deemed impassable, and the narrow strait between Aman and Middle-earth they intended to sail. With too few ships remaining to ferry all the Noldor at once, and the fear of treachery between their houses, Fëanor and his sons stole away in them, abandoning the host of Fingolfin.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-7">[7]</a></sup>

### Return to Middle-earth {#Return_to_Middle-earth}

After landing at the [Firth of Drengist](../F/Firth_of_Drengist.md), and burning the ships to prevent any from sailing back, Fëanor's host traveled north and encamped on the northern shore of [Lake Mithrim](../L/Lake_Mithrim.md) where they were attacked unaware by a host of Morgoth. In [the battle that followed](../D/Dagor-nuin-Giliath.md), Fëanor's forces were swiftly victorious, and pursued the routed orcs over the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../M/Mountains_of_Shadow.md" title="Mountains of Shadow">Mountains of Shadow</a> into [Ard-galen](../A/Ard-galen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Return_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-8">[8]</a></sup>

Though Morgoth's assaulting forces were destroyed, Fëanor recklessly drew far ahead of his host in pursuit of the remnant orcs, and was ambushed and mortally wounded at the confines of [Dor Daedeloth](../D/Dor_Daedeloth.md). He was rescued by his sons, and just before his death he laid it on them to uphold their oath and to avenge him. Shortly after, Maedhros was taken captive after feigning to treat with Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-Return_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Return-8">[8]</a></sup>

The host of Fëanor remained at northern shore of Lake Mithrim until the arrival of Fingolfin's host, having successfully crossed the Helcaraxë, with the [first sunrise](../Y/Years_of_the_Sun.md) in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [1](../F/First_Age_1.md). To avoid open hostilities with Fingolfin's larger forces, the remaining sons of Fëanor relocated to the southern shore of Lake Mithrim.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> The hatred between the houses began to be assuaged in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [5](../F/First_Age_5.md), when Maedhros was rescued by Fingolfin's son [Fingon](../F/Fingon.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [7](../F/First_Age_7.md) the Noldor held a [great common council](Council_in_Mithrim.md). Here, [Angrod](../A/Angrod.md), son of [Finarfin](../F/Finarfin.md), returned from embassy to [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md) bearing the words of King [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md). The Noldor found these words cold, but Caranthir, who loved not the Sons of Finarfin, took especial umbrage:

At this Angrod became exceedingly wroth and left the council. And though Maedhros rebuked Caranthir, his words troubled the hearts of the Noldor, who feared that the sons of Fëanor would ever be like to burst forth in rash word or violence. Thus, when it came to selecting an [overlord of the Exiles](../H/High_King_of_the_Noldor.md), all save few chose Fingolfin. Maedhros then subsequently waived his claim to the title, and shortly after relocated the House of Fëanor to [East Beleriand](../E/East_Beleriand.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

### Establishment in Thargelion {#Establishment_in_Thargelion}

The people of Caranthir took the easternmost area which lay between the river [Gelion](../G/Gelion.md) and the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) and fortified the mountains east of [Maglor's Gap](../M/Maglor's_Gap.md). In the valley between Mount [Rerir](../R/Rerir.md) and the Blue Mountains, shadowed by them in all directions save the south, lay [Lake Helevorn](../L/Lake_Helevorn.md), and it was here that Caranthir had his abode.<sup id="cite_ref-LRQS_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LRQS-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§118</sup> On the western slope of Mount Rerir itself was constructed a fortress.<sup id="cite_ref-LRQS_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LRQS-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§142</sup>

The Sindar had previously called this area *Talath Rhúnen* ("East Vale"), however the Noldor referred to it as *[Thargelion](../T/Thargelion.md)* ("(Land) beyond Gelion") or *Dor Caranthir* ("Land of Caranthir").<sup id="cite_ref-LRQS_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LRQS-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§118</sup>

### Interaction with the Dwarves {#Interaction_with_the_Dwarves}

In about [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [150](../F/First_Age_150.md) the people of Caranthir first climbed the Blue Mountains and gazed eastward unto [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md), which to them seemed wide and wild. They soon after made contact with the [Dwarves of the Blue Mountains](../D/Dwarves_of_the_Blue_Mountains.md), who had ceased their traffic into Beleriand since the [onslaught of Morgoth](../F/First_Battle_of_Beleriand.md) and the coming of the Noldor.<sup id="cite_ref-ga114_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ga114-13">[13]</a></sup>

There was little love between them, for the Dwarves were secretive and quick to resentment, and Caranthir was haughty and scarce concealed his scorn for their unlovely appearance, and his people followed their lord. Yet, as they both feared and hated Morgoth, alliance was made and from it both had great profit. The smiths and masons of the Dwarves learned many secrets of craft from the Noldor, making them renowned among their kin, while the Noldor obtained great wealth of [iron](../I/Iron.md) and their armouries became filled with weapons and harness of war. Further, until the power of Maedhros was overthrown, all Dwarven traffic first passed through the hands of Caranthir, and from this he won great riches.<sup id="cite_ref-ga114_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ga114-13">[13]</a></sup>

Due to Caranthir's friendship with the [Dwarves of Belegost](../D/Dwarves_of_Belegost.md), [Eöl](../E/Eöl.md) in turn much more often visited [Nogrod](../N/Nogrod.md).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

### Arrival of Men {#Arrival_of_Men}

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [310](../F/First_Age_310.md), the [first Men in Beleriand](../H/House_of_Bëor.md) were encountered in [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md) by [Finrod](../F/Finrod.md). As the [Green-elves](../G/Green-elves.md) of that region were troubled by them, Finrod persuaded them to carry onward, and they settled in [Estolad](../E/Estolad.md). Soon after, in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [312](../F/First_Age_312.md), a second group of Men, the [Haladin](../H/Haladin.md), arrived and, after they met the unfriendship of the Green-elves, they turned north and settled in Thargelion where the people of Caranthir paid little heed to them.<sup id="cite_ref-men_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-men-15">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§13</sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [375](../F/First_Age_375.md) Morgoth, angered that he had not been able to estrange the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) from the Elves through lies, decided to do them hurt. He sent out an Orc raiding party which passed secretly over the Blue Mountains, north of the leaguer, before then moving south and re-entering Beleriand in stealth via the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../D/Dwarf-road.md" title="Dwarf-road">Dwarf-road</a>. The Orcs then [set upon the Haladin](../B/Battle_of_the_Gelion-Ascar_stockade.md), who were forced to fall back into a makeshift defence where for seven days they held out. Just as the Orcs made their last assault, and broke through the stockade, Caranthir with his host came down from the north and drove the Orcs into the rivers. Caranthir, now seeing the valour of the Edain, looked kindly upon them and did their leader, a woman named [Haleth](../H/Haleth.md), great honour. He offered her recompense for her father and brother who had been slain, and further said this to her: *"If you will remove and dwell further north, there you shall have the friendship and protection of the Eldar and free lands of your own."* Haleth, like her people, was proud though, and unwilling to be guided or ruled; she thus thanked Caranthir, but declined and chose instead to remove her people to Estolad.<sup id="cite_ref-men_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-men-15">[15]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§23-27</sup>

### War renewed {#War_renewed}

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [455](../F/First_Age_455.md), Morgoth broke the [Siege of Angband](../S/Siege_of_Angband.md) with a [massive assault](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md) against the Noldor.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> The Orcs broke through the [Pass of Aglon](../P/Pass_of_Aglon.md) and, led by the [dragon](../D/Dragon.md) [Glaurung](../G/Glaurung.md), breached Maglor's Gap. They took the fortress upon the west slopes of Mount Rerir, ravaged all Thargelion, defiled Lake Helevorn and came far into East Beleriand. Caranthir fled and joined the remnant of his people to the scattered folk of Amrod and Amras. They then retreated to the south and established a watch and some strength of war upon [Amon Ereb](../A/Amon_Ereb.md) where they had aid of the Green-elves.<sup id="cite_ref-LRQS_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LRQS-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§142</sup>

But the Orcs came not into Ossiriand or South Beleriand.<sup id="cite_ref-LRQS_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LRQS-12">[12]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§142</sup> At some point prior to [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [462](../F/First_Age_462.md), Morgoth's army in East Beleriand was broken by Thingol on the borders of Doriath. Part had fled away south never to return to him, part retreated north and was stricken by Maedhros, and those that ventured near the mountains were hunted by the Dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [463](../F/First_Age_463.md) [Swarthy Men](<../E/Easterlings_(First_Age).md>) came out of Eriador, passing north of the Blue Mountains, and entered into [Lothlann](../L/Lothlann.md). They were not unexpected, as Maedhros had been warned by the Dwarves of their coming from out of the further East. They were not a unified people, having many houses with little love among them. But Maedhros, seeing the weakness of the Elves and Edain, and the seemingly inexhaustible store of Angband, made alliance with them and gave them dwellings. Among these Men, the two chieftains with the greatest followings were [Bór](../B/Bór.md) and [Ulfang](../U/Ulfang.md). While Bór swore loyalty to Maedhros and Maglor, and was presumably given lands within Lothlann,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[note 1]</a></sup> the folk of [Ulfang](../U/Ulfang.md) were given lands within Thargelion<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup>, and he and his sons, [Uldor](../U/Uldor.md), [Ulfast](../U/Ulfast.md), and [Ulwarth](../U/Ulwarth.md), swore allegiance to Caranthir.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [468](../F/First_Age_468.md), following the successful [Quest of the Silmaril](../Q/Quest_of_the_Silmaril.md), new hope arose that Morgoth was not unconquerable, and the [Union of Maedhros](../U/Union_of_Maedhros.md) was formed. Among these preparations, the Men of Bór and of Ulfang were marshalled and trained for war, and given fair arms, and they summoned yet more of their kinsfolk out of the East.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> In spring of the following year, the Union made its first trial of strength, and soon succeeded in driving the Orcs out of Beleriand and reestablishing the frontiers as they had been during the Siege.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/H/Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg/300px-Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="424" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg/450px-Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg/600px-Helena_Stepanova_-_Tears_Unnumbered_-_Caranthir.jpg 2x" data-file-width="707" data-file-height="1000"/></a><figcaption><i>Tears Unnumbered - Caranthir</i> by <a class="mw-redirect" href="../H/Helena_Stepanova.md" title="Helena Stepanova">Helena Stepanova</a></figcaption></figure>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [472](../F/First_Age_472.md), Maedhros launched his final assault upon Angband. His plan was simple: marching openly with his main host from the east, he anticipated the armies of Morgoth to draw forth in response; at that point then Fingon would issue from the west and thus break the might of Morgoth as between hammer and anvil. In midsummer, on the day the battle was to be fought, Maedhros' host was hindered by the guile of Uldor: first he gave a false warning of attack from Angband, and then he tarried, claiming that not all his men were willing to march. By the fourth day, once Maedhros finally arrived, Morgoth had already done great harm to the western host of Fingon, having drawn them forth into a trap, beaten them back, and now had them surrounded. As Maedhros' vanguard came upon these Orcs, Morgoth then loosed all his remaining forces of Angband, including [wolf-riders](../W/Wolf-rider.md), [Balrogs](../B/Balrog.md) and dragons, led by Glaurung. These new forces drove between the hosts of Fingon and Maedhros, preventing them from union. Yet even then the Elves might have prevailed were it not for the treachery of Men. While many of the Easterlings deserted and fled, the plots of Ulfang were finally revealed and Uldor turned his forced against Maedhros, attacking him from the rear, and came near even to the standard of Maedhros. Uldor, who was the leader of this treason, was himself slain by Maglor, and Uldor's brothers by the forces of Bór, but yet more strength of evil men came from forces Uldor had hidden in the hills. Now assailed on three sides, the sons of Fëanor drew together, gathered what remnant of Noldor and Dwarves they could, and hewed their way out, escaping towards Mount [Dolmed](../D/Dolmed.md). Though none of the sons of Fëanor were slain, all of them were wounded.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup>

### Last deeds in Beleriand {#Last_deeds_in_Beleriand}

After the battle, the power of the sons of Fëanor was broken. Their realms and fortresses in East Beleriand were taken and garrisoned by Orcs, and they retreated to into the woods.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [506](../F/First_Age_506.md), he perished along with his brothers [Celegorm](Celegorm.md) and [Curufin](Curufin.md) during the [Second Kinslaying](../S/Second_Kinslaying.md), the attack by the sons of Fëanor on [Menegroth](../M/Menegroth.md) in an attempt to recover a Silmaril from King [Dior Eluchíl](../D/Dior.md) of Doriath.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

Physically, Caranthir was notable for two characteristics: his exceptionally dark hair (inherited from his father's side) and his ruddy complexion (inherited from his mother's side). These became the bases of his father-name and mother-name respectively.

Caranthir's personality was noted as being harsh, haughty, and quick to anger. His words to Angrod not only diminished the standing of the House of Fëanor at the early council of the Noldor, but remained with Angrod, and the bitterness of them contributed to his confessing the deeds of the Noldor to Thingol many years later.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup>

*Caranthir* is the [Sindarized](../S/Sindarized.md) form of *Carnistir*, being a compound of *caran* (“red”) and *þir* ("face").<sup id="cite_ref-vt41_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt41-27">[26]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Caranthir had the sobriquet of "the dark".<sup id="cite_ref-S|Princes_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S|Princes-4">[4]</a></sup>

Caranthir's [father-name](../F/Father-name.md) was <i><b>Morifinwë</b></i> ("Dark Finwë"), for he was black-haired as his grandfather. The short form of this name was <i><b>Moryo</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Feanor_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Feanor-2">[2]</a></sup>

His [mother-name](../A/Amilessë.md) was <i><b>Carnistir</b></i> ("Red-face") which was a compound of *carnë* and *stîre*.<sup id="cite_ref-vt41_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt41-27">[26]</a></sup>

In *Ælfwine’s translation of the Quenta into Old English; Old English equivalents of Elvish names*, his name is given as <i><b>Colþegn Nihthelm</b></i> and defined as "\[Cranthir the Dark. O.E. col 'coal', þegn 'thane'; niht-helm lit. 'night-helm', but poetically referring to 'cover of night'\]"<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[27]</a></sup>

An earlier variant of his name was **Cranthir** ([N.](../N/N..md) "perfect");<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:161</sup> it was alternatively spelled as **Cranthor**.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In [The Nauglafring](../T/The_Nauglafring.md), from the [second volume](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two.md) of *[The History of Middle-earth](../T/The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)*, the only mention of *Cranthor* is the attack on Dior's realm to reclaim the Silmaril, and his subsequent death beside Celegorm.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup>

In [The Lay of the Children of Húrin](../T/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_Húrin.md), from the [third volume](../L/Lays_of_Beleriand.md), Flinding ([Gwindor](../G/Gwindor.md)) tells [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md) about the Sons of Fëanor who live secretly in the forests of the East as unflinching foes of Morgoth, including "dark Cranthir".<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup>

In [The Earliest 'Silmarillion'](../T/The_Earliest_'Silmarillion'.md), from the [fourth volume](../T/The_Shaping_of_Middle-earth.md), an early version has the Sons of Fëanor succeed in recovering the Silmaril after attacking Doriath and slaying Dior; however, owing to the curse of the gold, they then quarrelled for it, until only Maglor was left. This was very soon after emended to Elwing escaping with the Silmaril.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup>

In [The Earliest Annals of Beleriand](../T/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Beleriand.md), from the fourth volume, the sons of *Ulfand* (*Uldor*, *Ulfast*, and *Ulwar*) were most beloved by Cranthir among Sons of Fëanor, and they swore fealty to him. In the Battle of Unnumbered Tears, Uldor betrayed the Sons of Fëanor to Morgoth, and fell on their right flank. It was Cranthir who then slew Uldor.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[32]</a></sup>

In one version of the [Battle of Sarn Athrad](../B/Battle_of_Sarn_Athrad.md), the Dwarf army (likely of Nogrod) was defeated and "almost entirely destroyed by Caranthir".<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup>

While in the published Silmarillion Caranthir is recorded as the fourth son of Fëanor, in *[The Shibboleth of Fëanor](../T/The_Shibboleth_of_Fëanor.md)* Curufin is explicitly numbered as the fourth.<sup id="cite_ref-Feanor_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Feanor-2">[2]</a></sup> This would then imply Caranthir to be the fifth son instead.

In *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)*, a footnote regarding [Finrod](../F/Finrod.md)'s [epessë](../E/Epessë.md), *Felegund*, states that "This nick-name was probably actually given to Finrod not by Dwarves but by the Sons of Fëanor at least partly in derision. There was no great love between the Sons of Fëanor and the children of Finarphin, though they hid their enmity at need. (As when Curufin and **Caranthir** dwelt in the shelter of Nargothrond after the defeat of the Elves by Morgoth in the North). Moreover the Sons of Fëanor had much communication with the Dwarves of Nogrod and Belegost."<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup> It may be assumed that this was intended to refer to *Celegorm* instead.
