The "**Clarendon Chaucer**" was the working title of an edition by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Geoffrey_Chaucer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Geoffrey Chaucer (page does not exist)">Geoffrey Chaucer</a>'s works for the Clarendon Press, which was never completed (a manuscript suggests that <i><b>Selections from Chaucer&#39;s Poetry and Prose</b></i> might have been intended as the published title). While at the [University of Leeds](/wiki/University_of_Leeds), Tolkien worked with <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=George_Gordon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="George Gordon (page does not exist)">George Gordon</a> on the edition, under the supervision of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Kenneth_Sisam&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Kenneth Sisam (page does not exist)">Kenneth Sisam</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) have noted that the existence of the "Clarendon Chaucer" was largely unknown before the publication of their *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Companion and Guide](/wiki/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Companion_and_Guide)* ([2006](/wiki/2006)).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [2019](/wiki/2019), John M. Bowers' book *[Tolkien's Lost Chaucer](/wiki/Tolkien%27s_Lost_Chaucer)* explores this abandoned project and sheds much light on its genesis, evolution, and final fate.

In [2024](/wiki/2024), Bowers' follow-up book *[Tolkien on Chaucer, 1913-1959](/wiki/Tolkien_on_Chaucer,_1913-1959)* publishes substantial materials from this work.
