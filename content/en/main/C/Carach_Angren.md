**Carach Angren** or **Isenmouthe** was a pass in the northwest of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). The pass was formed where spurs reaching out from the ranges of the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath) and the [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui) met, leaving only a narrow passage between the [Plateau of Gorgoroth](/wiki/Plateau_of_Gorgoroth) and the smaller valley of [Udûn](</wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)> "Udûn (valley)") to the north.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Appearance {#Appearance}

As the passage from Gorgoroth to Udûn, Carach Angren was heavily fortified, and both the rocky spurs that overlooked it carried forts and towers. Across the passage itself, a wall of earth had been built, and a great ditch had been dug across the opening spanned by a single bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> A great fence of pointed iron posts closed the gap leading into Udûn.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Both *Carach Angren* and *Isenmouthe* mean "Iron-mouth": "*It was so called because of the great fence of pointed iron posts that closed the gap leading into Udûn, like teeth in jaws.*"<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name *Carach Angren* comes from <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Carach&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Carach (page does not exist)">carach</a></i> ("jaws", "rows of teeth")<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and *[angren](/wiki/Angren "Angren")* ("iron").

*Isen* is an old English variant form of iron; and mouthe represents [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *mūða* &lt; *mūð* "opening, mouth" especially used of the mouths of rivers, but also applied to other openings.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup>

Despite the Old English, the name is not to be understood as [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese), but rather as archaic [Westron](/wiki/Westron), translation of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *Carach Angren*.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b><a href="/wiki/1987" title="1987">1987</a>-<a href="/wiki/1996" title="1996">96</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the <i><a href="/wiki/Teeth_of_Mordor_(book)" title="Teeth of Mordor (book)">Teeth of Mordor</a></i> module in the <i>Fortresses of Middle-earth</i> series it is described that <a href="/wiki/Isildur" title="Isildur">Isildur</a> began a grand plan to seal off Mordor and that the construction of Carach Angren began before the end of <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1" title="Third Age 1">1</a> and was completed during the reign of <a href="/wiki/R%C3%B3mendacil_I" title="Rómendacil I">Rómendacil I</a> (<a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_492" title="Third Age 492">492</a> - <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_541" title="Third Age 541">541</a>). It is mentioned that by <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1640" title="Third Age 1640">1640</a> the last citadel guarding Mordor was abandoned. It is said that in the same year of the closing of the Dúnadan fortresses guarding Mordor Sauron sent all <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Ringwraiths" title="Ringwraiths">Ringwraiths</a> except the <a href="/wiki/Witch-king" title="Witch-king">Witch-king</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kham%C3%BBl" title="Khamûl">Khamûl</a> to lake <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/N%C3%BArnen" title="Núrnen">Núrnen</a> with the mission to ready Mordor for his return. It is described that they sent scouts north who found that the Gondorian fortresses were abandoned.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In the <a href="/wiki/Gorgoroth_(book)" title="Gorgoroth (book)">Gorgoroth</a> campaign module it is described that the garrison of Carach Angren succumbed to the Great Plague and only a skeleton crew was left in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1635" title="Third Age 1635">1635</a>. All contact was lost with these soldiers and their fate was unknown. It is described that the Ringwraith Indûr Dawndeath met the other Ringwraiths except the Witch-king in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1640" title="Third Age 1640">1640</a> in the castle of Ostigurth to prepare the Black Land for Sauron&#39;s return. It is mentioned that Orcs entered Carach Angren in T.A. 1642 and found that the Gondorian soldiers had blocked the Great Gate by sabotaging its lifting mechanism. It is mentioned that Indûr took control of Carach Angren in T.A. 1652 and remained there and that he joined his companions in the siege of Minas Ithil in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2000" title="Third Age 2000">2000</a>. Carach Angren is described to consist of a wall with the Great Gate, three middle towers, the tower of Tarmorost (High Black Fort) and the tower of Fuinost (Gloom-Fort). The wall rests on the top of a stone dike (47 feet high, extending 100 feet from the base of the wall). The wall itself is over forty feet high and consists of stone with toothed parapets on its top. The Great Gate consists of double portals made of one foot thick hollow steel on the north and south side of the gatehouse. Inside the gate is a set of porticulli. When the door is closed great masonry fangs are lowered on the outside to further seal the gate. A 1,224 ton stone rests in the western side of the gate in an alcove. It sits on massive rollers and can be slid to seal Carach Angren in a last ditch effort. The three middle towers consist of three levels. Tarmorost is the easternmost end tower of the wall. It stands on a shelf of rock with the peak of the tower nearly 220 feet above the floor of the plateau. Tarmorost consists of five levels. Fuinost is the westernmost end tower of the wall. Fuinost is eighty feet high, but stands on a ridge of stone with the peak of the tower 170 feet above the floor of the plateau. Fuinost also consists of five levels. It is mentioned that the basic garrison of Carach Angren consists of 162 Men, 54 Olog-hai, 81 Uruk-hai and 243 Orcs.</dd></dl>
