<i><b>Cram</b></i> was a biscuit-like substance<sup id="cite_ref-Home_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Home-1">[1]</a></sup> made by the [Men of Dale](/wiki/Men_of_Dale)<sup id="cite_ref-Farewell_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farewell-2">[2]</a></sup> and the [Lake-men](/wiki/Lake-men)<sup id="cite_ref-Home_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Home-1">[1]</a></sup>. It was said to be nutritious, and was used as sustenance on long journeys, as it was by [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) on the last stage of their [quest](/wiki/Quest_for_Erebor "Quest for Erebor").<sup id="cite_ref-Home_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Home-1">[1]</a></sup> When [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) first saw a *[lembas](/wiki/Lembas "Lembas")* cake, he looked at it with a doubtful expression and thought that it was *cram*, but when he tasted a bit, he ate the whole cake with relish.<sup id="cite_ref-Farewell_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farewell-2">[2]</a></sup>

Basic *cram* was a biscuitish, flavourless, uninspiring food.<sup id="cite_ref-Home_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Home-1">[1]</a></sup>. It was made of compressed flour, but sometimes milk and honey was added to it to make it more palatable.<sup id="cite_ref-LR_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the fictitious poem *[Perry-the-Winkle](/wiki/Perry-the-Winkle)*, written by [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> the [Lonely Troll](/wiki/Lonely_Troll) fed [Perry](</wiki/Perry-the-Winkle_(hobbit)> "Perry-the-Winkle (hobbit)") "cramsome bread" and "cram-some cake".<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Cram* is probably a word in a [Mannish](/wiki/Mannish) language.<sup id="cite_ref-Cram_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cram-7">[7]</a></sup>

The Oxford English Dictionary contains the word "cram-cake", which means a "fried-cake" or "pancake".<sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)*, *cram* is said to be a [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) word (the precursor of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin)), from a root (KRAB-) meaning "press".<sup id="cite_ref-LR_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-3">[3]</a></sup> This entry seems to have been added in late [1937](/wiki/1937) or early [1938](/wiki/1938).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become the chapter "[A Knife in the Dark](/wiki/A_Knife_in_the_Dark)" mentions that <i><a class="mw-selflink selflink">Cram</a></i> was "a word in the [language](/wiki/Language_of_Dale "Language of Dale") of the men of Dale and the Long-lake".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> This version seems to have been written before late summer 1938.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> In the final published version of the chapter "[Farewell to Lórien](/wiki/Farewell_to_L%C3%B3rien)", [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) says after trying *[lembas](/wiki/Lembas "Lembas")* that he "thought it was only a kind of *cram*" which the Dale-men would make for travel.<sup id="cite_ref-Farewell_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farewell-2">[2]</a></sup>
