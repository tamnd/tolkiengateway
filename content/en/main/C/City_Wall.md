The **City Wall** was the protective stone wall that enclosed [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) on its lowest level. Its only opening was the [Great Gate](/wiki/Great_Gate_of_Minas_Tirith "Great Gate of Minas Tirith") that pierced the wall at its easternmost point.<sup id="cite_ref-Tirith_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tirith-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The city of Minas Anor (later Minas Tirith) was probably built after the founding of the realm of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320") before [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3429](/wiki/Second_Age_3429 "Second Age 3429").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Minas Anor was built as a small fort on the summit of Amon Anor mostly to guard [Rath Dínen](/wiki/Rath_D%C3%ADnen), the Tombs of the Kings and was originally of less importance than its eastern counterpart [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil) ([S.](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin") 'Tower of the Moon').<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> It is thus probable that Minas Anor did not have all seven of its levels at its earliest stage and that the City Wall was built later.

In the great [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields) on [15 March](/wiki/15_March) [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> the host of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) did not attempt to breach the City Wall; instead they concentrated upon breaking the Great Gate.<sup id="cite_ref-Siege_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Siege-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

On a plan of Minas Tirith, drawn on the back of a typescript page, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote "**Othram** or City Wall" to the left of the words "Great Gate".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Although the phrase "City Wall" was used in *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)*,<sup id="cite_ref-Tirith_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tirith-1">[1]</a></sup> the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word *Othram* was not included.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2003: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Although the text of <i>The Return of the King</i> states that the outer wall of Minas Tirith was &#34;dark&#34; (see quotation above) in the movie, as in other depictions of the City, the wall is white.</dd></dl>
