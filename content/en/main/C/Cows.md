**Cows**, also known by the archaic plural **Kine** and occasionally as **Oxen**, were pasture animals in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).

## History {#History}

Cows were domesticated for farm use in most of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md). They produced milk, which was used as a beverage in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Being generally stronger than [horses](../H/Horses.md), cows also pulled the heavier wagons and wains in Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-MT_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MT-4">[4]</a></sup>

Yet still, there were undomesticated oxen. The wild-oxen of the East were, according to legend, descended from the [Kine of Araw](../K/Kine_of_Araw.md), the herd of [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md).<sup id="cite_ref-AA_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AA-5">[5]</a></sup> [Vorondil the Hunter](../V/Vorondil_the_Hunter.md) hunted them in the far fields of [Rhûn](../R/Rhûn.md), and of their horns he made the [Great Horn](../G/Great_Horn.md) of Gondor, which served as an heirloom to the House of Stewards until its destruction.<sup id="cite_ref-MT_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MT-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Cows are a major source of food even in today's world. Their use as farm animals seems little more than logical. [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) spent most of his life in [**Ox**ford](../O/Oxford.md), and cows would also play a role in other stories, such as *[Farmer Giles of Ham](../F/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham.md)*, where [the Giant](../T/The_Giant.md) steps on [Farmer Giles](../F/Farmer_Giles.md)' favourite cow, [Galathea](../G/Galathea.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

The white oxen of [Rhûn](../R/Rhûn.md) were likened by Tolkien to an <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurochs" title="wikipedia:Aurochs">aurochs</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> a bovine species common in the ancient and medieval world until its extinction.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

An "elvish" word for "bull" is *[mundo](../M/Mundo.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> The [Root](../S/Sundocarme.md) MBUD, which lists among its derivatives a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word *mundo* meaning "snout, nose, cape",<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> could perhaps be related to *mundo*.

In [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md), the word for "ox" is <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Yakko&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Yakko (page does not exist)">yakko</a></i> or *yako*, "cow" is <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Yaksi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Yaksi (page does not exist)">yaksi</a></i> or *yakse*. In [Gnomish](../N/Noldorin.md), "milch cow" is <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gach&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gach (page does not exist)">gach</a></i>, "cow" is <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gacha&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gacha (page does not exist)">gacha</a></i>, and "a yoke ox - bullock" is *gagron*. Other Gnomish names are *mûs* or *mui* ("cow"); *muir* ("heifer"); <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=M%C3%BB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mû (page does not exist)">mû</a></i> ("ox"), *muin* ("kine, cattle"); and <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Mugli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mugli (page does not exist)">mugli</a></i> ("calf").<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In Tolkien's juvenile language [Nevbosh](../N/Nevbosh.md), the word for cow is *woc*, which is both the reverse of *cow*, and an alteration of the French word *vache*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
