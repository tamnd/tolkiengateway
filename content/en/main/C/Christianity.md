Tolkien belonged to the **Roman Catholic Church**. Raised in the church by his mother [Mabel](/wiki/Mabel_Tolkien "Mabel Tolkien"), he would remain a devout Catholic till his death. His faith was an essential part of his life, which formed an aspect of his writings.

<h2><span id="Tolkien.27s_life"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Tolkien&#39;s_life">Tolkien&#39;s life</span></h2>

### Mabel Tolkien {#Mabel_Tolkien}

The parents of John Ronald Reuel Tolkien were <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism" title="wikipedia:Anglicanism">Anglican</a>. He was baptized four weeks after his birth, on [31 January](/wiki/31_January) [1892](/wiki/1892) at the Anglican [Bloemfontein](/wiki/Bloemfontein) Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:13</sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup> Some time after becoming widowed, [Mabel Tolkien](/wiki/Mabel_Tolkien) began to visit a Roman Catholic church: <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Anne%27s_Church,_Birmingham" title="wikipedia:St Anne&#39;s Church, Birmingham">St Anne</a>. After receiving formation there, in June [1900](/wiki/1900) she was received into the Catholic Church. Her conversion was not accepted by her family or the Tolkiens, who belonged to other Christian denominations, and Mabel lost the only help she had from her father and brother-in-law. Despite the pressure and the economic distress, she began to instruct her two sons in the new faith.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:23–24</sup> Looking for a better place of worship, Mabel felt attracted by the [Birmingham Oratory](/wiki/Birmingham_Oratory), where the family met Father [Francis Morgan](/wiki/Francis_Morgan). This priest became a friend of the family, who would go on to support them. On [Christmas](/wiki/Christmas) of [1903](/wiki/1903), Tolkien received his First Communion.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:26–28</sup>

Despite the help of Father Francis, their mother had to work very hard to raise her children and have them schooled. Finally she fell ill of diabetes and died in 1904. Tolkien himself wrote nine years later: "*My own dear mother was a martyr indeed, and it is not to everybody that God grants so easy a way to his great gifts as he did to [Hilary](/wiki/Hilary_Tolkien "Hilary Tolkien") and myself, giving us a mother who killed herself with labour and trouble to ensure us keeping the faith.*"<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:31</sup> Even sixty years later, he would refer to his mother as an example to others,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> showing that she would always be one of the pillars of his faith.

### Father Francis {#Father_Francis}

In her will, Mabel appointed Father Francis as the guardian of her children, both because of her trust in him and to ensure their continued Catholic education.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:32</sup> The boys lived for four years with an aunt, but they spent most of the day in the Oratory, as it was nearby. They served Father Francis' early mass, and participated enthusiastically in the parish activities.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Birmingham_Oratory.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/42/Birmingham_Oratory.jpg/300px-Birmingham_Oratory.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/42/Birmingham_Oratory.jpg/450px-Birmingham_Oratory.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/42/Birmingham_Oratory.jpg/600px-Birmingham_Oratory.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="960"/></a><figcaption>Birmingham Oratory as in 2009</figcaption></figure>

Morgan's guardianship was not only economical and spiritual, but also intellectual: the Catholic environment of the Oratory, motivated by the anti-modernist reform of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pius_X" title="wikipedia:Saint Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> would have a great impact on Tolkien that can be seen later in his life.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup> Such reform will later be considered by him as the greatest reform of our time, surpassing anything achieved by the Second Vatican Council.<sup id="cite_ref-Pope_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pope-6">[5]</a></sup>

When Tolkien was seventeen, he began a love interest with [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Bratt), but Father Francis ordered him to wait till his coming of age (21) to even talk to her. Despite that strict situation, Tolkien observed the order with perfect obedience, and on [8 January](/wiki/8_January) of [1913](/wiki/1913), he and Edith got engaged. The recovered relationship increased Tolkien's religious fervour, and began a diary entitled "<i>JRRT and <a href="/wiki/Edith_Tolkien" title="Edith Tolkien">EMB</a> in account together, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_maiorem_Dei_gloriam" title="wikipedia:Ad maiorem Dei gloriam">AMDG</a></i>", in which he accounted, among other things, his performance of religious duties.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup> The only condition Tolkien asked of Edith was that she convert to Catholicism, which she accepted despite the inconvenience that it would bring her. They would marry on [22 March](/wiki/22_March) [1916](/wiki/1916), with the blessing of Father Francis.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:78–79</sup>

### The Inklings {#The_Inklings}

When he returned to Oxford as a professor in [1925](/wiki/1925), he was an active lay man, both with the chaplaincy of the University and the Catholic communities of [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

Tolkien met [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis) while working at the Oxford University. They became close friends when Lewis was still an atheist, and religion was one of the main topics in their conversations. In [1929](/wiki/1929), Lewis was convinced with theism, although he could not accept Christianity, yet. In [19 September](/wiki/19_September) [1931](/wiki/1931), Tolkien, Lewis and [Hugo Dyson](/wiki/Hugo_Dyson) had a dinner, followed by a walk that ran till 4 a.m. There Lewis was moved by Tolkien's ideas that the truth inherent to all myths and its relation with the historical *myth* of Christ's Resurrection.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:146–148</sup> Lewis' conversion supported a new step in their friendship, although Tolkien always regretted that Lewis did not become a Roman Catholic: Lewis chose Anglicanism, which Tolkien considered a distortion of the previous Catholicism.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:151</sup>

In the following years, a literary club was formed around Lewis, called '[The Inklings](/wiki/The_Inklings)', which he describes as "*a sort of informal club \[...\] the qualifications (as they have informally evolved) are a tendency to write, and Christianity*".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup> His secretary [Walter Hooper](/wiki/Walter_Hooper) will later compare it with the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Movement" title="wikipedia:Oxford Movement">Oxford Movement</a>, an intellectual movement begun a century before, in which their members also shared their Christian passion for Truth with conversation and comradery.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 2]</a></sup>

After Lewis's death, Tolkien once refused an invitation to write an obituary for him on religious grounds, stating that *a Catholic could not possibly say anything sincere about Jack's books without giving widespread offence* due to Lewis' bad understanding on denominational differences. However, he also imagined a reconciliation between Lewis and the Virgin Mary: *According to my fashion and in a remote way this is an ingredient in the meeting of [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) and [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel)*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup>

### Spiritual life {#Spiritual_life}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Oxford_Oratory.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Oxford_Oratory.jpg/300px-Oxford_Oratory.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="450" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/3b/Oxford_Oratory.jpg/450px-Oxford_Oratory.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/3/3b/Oxford_Oratory.jpg/600px-Oxford_Oratory.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1366" data-file-height="2048"/></a><figcaption>Mass at the Oxford Oratory Church of St Aloysius Gonzaga</figcaption></figure>

For the many years Tolkien lived in Oxford, he had a routine life, in which he attended daily mass very early in the morning. He usually went with his children by bike to the 7:30am mass, served at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Oratory" title="wikipedia:Oxford Oratory">St Aloysius Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:115</sup> His wife Edith did not like at first that Tolkien took the children to church, as her view of Catholicism had declined over the years, even giving up attending mass. Sometimes the couple had tough discussions about this, which ended after a reconciliation in [1940](/wiki/1940), with Edith leaving any resentment towards religion and resuming interest in church affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:157</sup>

Humphrey Carpenter describes Tolkien's religion as "one of the deepest and strongest elements in his personality", affecting him both with great joy and sadness. This led him to have a strict spiritual practice, being very scrupulous when attending communion, which he denied himself if he had not confession before.<sup id="cite_ref-Bio_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bio-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:128</sup> It is probable that this excessive scrupulousness was episodic, not something typical of his daily life as presented by Carpenter: Tolkien did explain he always liked to go to Confession before receiving Communion,<sup id="cite_ref-Sayer_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sayer-13">[11]</a></sup> but attending daily mass would imply many occasions of missing Confession. No spiritual director would allow this behaviour and it would contradict the own importance Tolkien gave to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequent_Communion" title="wikipedia:Frequent Communion">Frequent Communion</a>:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The only cure for sagging or fainting faith is Communion. Though always itself, perfect and complete and inviolate, the Blessed Sacrament does not operate completely and once for all in any of us. Like the act of Faith it must be continuous and grow by exercise. Seven times a week is more nourishing than seven times at intervals.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Letter_250" title="Letter 250">Letter 250</a> (dated <a href="/wiki/1_November" title="1 November">1 November</a> <a href="/wiki/1963" title="1963">1963</a>)</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

In any case, Tolkien did have a crisis of deep sadness, as he describes in one occasion in which was helped by [Humphrey Havard](/wiki/Robert_Havard "Robert Havard"), his personal doctor and fellow Catholic Inkling:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>All right now, but I&#39;ve been in a very bad state. Humphrey came here and told me that I must go to confession and that he would come early on Sunday morning to take me to confession and communion. That&#39;s the sort of doctor to have.<sup id="cite_ref-Sayer_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sayer-13">[11]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

### Spiritual recommendations {#Spiritual_recommendations}

In his [published letters](/wiki/Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien "Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien"), Tolkien deals many times with religious topics, and gave some spiritual advice, especially to his sons. He told his son [Michael](/wiki/Michael_Tolkien "Michael Tolkien") about the importance of the Eucharist:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Out of the darkness of my life, so much frustrated, I put before you the one great thing to love on earth: the Blessed Sacrament. [...] There you will find romance, glory, honour, fidelity, and the true way of all your loves on earth, and more than that: Death: by the divine paradox, that which ends life, and demands the surrender of all, and yet by the taste—or foretaste—of which alone can what you seek in your earthly relationships (love, faithfulness, joy) be maintained, or take on that complexion of reality, of eternal endurance, which every man’s heart desires.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Letter_43" title="Letter 43">Letter 43</a> (dated <a href="/wiki/6_March" title="6 March">6</a>-<a href="/wiki/8_March" title="8 March">8 March</a> <a href="/wiki/1941" title="1941">1941</a>)</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/3d/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="357" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/3d/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png/450px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/3/3d/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png/600px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Gloria_in_Excelsis_Deo_Excerpt.png 2x" data-file-width="714" data-file-height="849"/></a><figcaption>Excerpt of the <i>Gloria in Excelsis Deo</i>, transcribed by Tolkien with Tengwar</figcaption></figure>

He even explains that this was why he believed in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primacy_of_Peter" title="wikipedia:Primacy of Peter">Petrine claims</a>, because along the history of Christianity, only the Pope had defended the Eucharist, giving it the main place as Christ intended.<sup id="cite_ref-Pope_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pope-6">[5]</a></sup>

Tolkien placed importance on prayer, considering that God was most ready to attend the prayers of the least worthy supplicants if they pray for others.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup> While his son [Christopher](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien "Christopher Tolkien") was in the war, Tolkien advised him to remember his <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_angel" title="wikipedia:Guardian angel">guardian angel</a>, as well as to recite memorised prayers in Latin: the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Patri" title="wikipedia:Gloria Patri">Gloria Patri</a>, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_in_excelsis_Deo" title="wikipedia:Gloria in excelsis Deo">Gloria in Excelsis</a>, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_117" title="wikipedia:Psalm 117">Laudate Dominum</a>, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_113" title="wikipedia:Psalm 113">Laudate Pueri Dominum</a> (which he especially liked), the Sunday psalms, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnificat" title="wikipedia:Magnificat">Magnificat</a>, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litany_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="wikipedia:Litany of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Litany of Loreto</a>, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_tuum_praesidium" title="wikipedia:Sub tuum praesidium">Sub tuum praesidium</a>, and even the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="wikipedia:Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a> in case he could not attend mass.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>

### Rejection of liturgical reform {#Rejection_of_liturgical_reform}

Tolkien agreed "in the abstract" with the gradual liturgical reforms of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_Movement" title="wikipedia:Liturgical Movement">Liturgical Movement</a>, but already in [1956](/wiki/1956) he expressed his sadness of knowing that some ceremonies and modes would never hear again.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[14]</a></sup> In that year, the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_reforms_of_Pope_Pius_XII#Easter_Vigil" title="wikipedia:Liturgical reforms of Pope Pius XII">new Easter Vigil</a> had just been introduced, but in the following decade came a more significant liturgical reform after the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="wikipedia:Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>. The change of the Latin language with vernacular was rejected by Tolkien, both in theory and practice, as his grandson [Simon](/wiki/Simon_Tolkien "Simon Tolkien") tells:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I vividly remember going to church with him in <a href="/wiki/Bournemouth" title="Bournemouth">Bournemouth</a>. He was a devout Roman Catholic and it was soon after the Church had changed the liturgy from Latin to English. My Grandfather obviously didn&#39;t agree with this and made all the responses very loudly in Latin while the rest of the congregation answered in English. I found the whole experience quite excruciating, but My Grandfather was oblivious. He simply had to do what he believed to be right.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Simon_Tolkien" title="Simon Tolkien">Simon Tolkien</a>, &#34;<a class="external text" href="http://www.simontolkien.com/mygrandfather" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">My Grandfather</a>&#34; in <i>The Mail On Sunday</i>, <a href="/wiki/23_February" title="23 February">23 February</a> <a href="/wiki/2003" title="2003">2003</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

He did agree that the Church was a living organism that grew and changed with the ages, but precisely for that, the research for a simplified and primitive Christianity was a Protestant regression, "*and primitiveness is no guarantee of value and is and was in great part a reflection of ignorance*".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[15]</a></sup> On the other hand, he frequently complained about how the translations of the new liturgy were inaccurate or in bad or clumsy English.<sup id="cite_ref-Sayer_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sayer-13">[11]</a></sup> But language was not the only change that affected Tolkien; he was saddened more by the spiritual decay that came thereafter: the irreverence and immodesty within churches, as well as unprepared priests. The first time he noticed that people no longer made genuflections, he was so disappointed that he rose up and stomped out of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[16]</a></sup>

<h2><span id="Tolkien.27s_works"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Tolkien&#39;s_works">Tolkien&#39;s works</span></h2>

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>His creation, as he himself said, was a kind of sub-creation under the inspiration and aegis of almighty God. His grand themes — good and evil, truth and falsehood, power and glory and honor and sacrifice — all flow forth from his Christian faith and his decidedly sacramental view of the world. For Tolkien, all the world is shot through with meaning by a Creator who loves mankind and is manifest in His works.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>John Daniel Davidson<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[17]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Tolkien himself admitted, "*The Lord of the Rings* is of course a fundamentally religious and Catholic work, unconsciously so at first, but consciously in the revision."<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[18]</a></sup> Possible examples include as follows:

- Tolkien makes his world monotheistic with a single [God](/wiki/Eru "Eru") who created all things by simply speaking it into being, which is consistent with the Christian view of the creation<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[19]</a></sup>
- Tolkien writes, "Or more important, I am a Christian (which can be deduced from my stories), and in fact a Roman Catholic. The latter 'fact' perhaps cannot be deduced; though one critic (by letter) asserted that the invocations of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Elbereth" title="Elbereth">Elbereth</a>, and the character of [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) as directly described (or through the words of [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) and [Sam](/wiki/Sam)) were clearly related to Catholic devotion to Mary. Another saw in waybread ([lembas](/wiki/Lembas "Lembas"))= viaticum and the reference to its feeding the *will* (vol. III, p. 213) and being more potent when fasting, a derivation from the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist" title="wikipedia:Eucharist">Eucharist</a>. (That is: far greater things may colour the mind in dealing with the lesser things of a fairy-story.)"<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[20]</a></sup>

### Invented languages {#Invented_languages}

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d8/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png/200px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="199" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d8/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d8/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png/400px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Credo.png 2x" data-file-width="955" data-file-height="950"/></a><figcaption><i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="wikipedia:Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a></i> in Latin, transcribed by Tolkien with Tengwar</figcaption></figure>

The early *[Qenya Lexicon](/wiki/Qenya_Lexicon)* (written in [1915](/wiki/1915), before Tolkien had written any of the [Lost Tales](/wiki/Lost_Tales)) included a remarkable set of religious words, not only related with the later stories (like *[Vala](/wiki/Vala)*, *[Ainu](/wiki/Ainu)*), but also with explicit Christian themes. Thus we have *evandilyon* ("gospel"), *tarwe* ("crucifix"), *valmandui* ("heaven and hell"), *anusta* ("monastery"), among others.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[21]</a></sup> We cannot know if he ever used this vocabulary or if it was just composed as an expression of his own daily life. In the later years, his Elvish [languages](/wiki/Languages "Languages") focused only on his stories, so this vocabulary was left behind.

However, years later, when he invented the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) script in early [1930s](/wiki/1930 "1930"), he enjoyed transcribing poems and texts, including many excerpts from the Roman Missal and other prayers.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[22]</a></sup>

He used again his invented languages with religious purposes in the decade of [1950](/wiki/1950), when his languages were more mature, as he had finished *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. Translating directly from the Latin versions, he made [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) translations of the Lord's Prayer (*[Átaremma](/wiki/%C3%81taremma)*), the Ave Maria (*[Aia María](/wiki/Aia_Mar%C3%ADa)*), Sub tuum praesidium (*[Ortirielyanna](/wiki/Ortirielyanna)*) and Gloria in excelsis (*[Alcar mi Tarmenel na Erun](/wiki/Alcar_mi_Tarmenel_na_Erun)*). Other unfinished translations are the Quenya *[Litany of Loreto](/wiki/Litany_of_Loreto)* and *[Alcar i Ataren](/wiki/Alcar_i_Ataren)*, and the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[Ae Adar Nín](/wiki/Ae_Adar_N%C3%ADn)*.

## Tolkien’s Influence {#Tolkien’s_Influence}

In his first encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas* (15 May 2026), Pope Leo XIV explicitly drew upon the moral vision of J.R.R. Tolkien, quoting *The Lord of the Rings* to illuminate the Church’s teaching on human dignity in the age of artificial intelligence. In paragraph 213 of the encyclical, the Pope cites Gandalf’s counsel from *The Return of the King*, emphasizing the responsibility of each person to resist the evils of their own time through small acts of fidelity:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The twentieth-century Catholic author J.R.R. Tolkien, in the words of a protagonist in one of his novels, described our responsibility in this way: &#39;It is not our part to master all the tides of the world, but to do what is in us for the succour of those years wherein we are set, uprooting the evil in the fields that we know, so that those who live after may have clean earth to till.&#39;
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i>Magnifica Humanitas</i>, §213 <sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[23]</a></sup> </cite></p>
</blockquote>
