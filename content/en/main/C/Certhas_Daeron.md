## History {#History}

In [Y.T.](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees "Years of the Trees") [1350](/wiki/Year_of_the_Trees_1350 "Year of the Trees 1350"),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Dairon (better known as [Daeron](/wiki/Daeron)), the Minstrel and Loremaster of [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), reorganised the cirth and added new ones, making the extension of the cirth known as Certhas Daeron, used for inscribing names in [Menegroth](/wiki/Menegroth). The [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) working for [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) liked them and adopted them, making them known also in the East, beyond the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains).

The nature and the timeframe of the standardization however are obscure; some point the similarities between <em style="font-style: normal; font-family: &#39;Tengwar Annatar&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Quenya&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Sindarin&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Formal&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Parmaite&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Elfica&#39;; font-size: 1.5em;">q</em> and <em style="font-style: normal; font-family: &#39;Tengwar Annatar&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Quenya&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Sindarin&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Formal&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Parmaite&#39;, &#39;Tengwar Elfica&#39;; font-size: 1.5em;">1</em> with <em style="font-family: &#39;Cirth Erebor&#39;; font-style: normal;">1</em> and <em style="font-family: &#39;Cirth Erebor&#39;; font-style: normal;">8</em> as signs that Daeron was influenced by the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar), therefore this must not have occurred before the return of the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor). Unlike the previous system, the reversal of the certh had a phonemic significance: reversed cirth were softer versions of their originals. This also gives us another piece of information: perhaps [lenited](/wiki/Prestanneth "Prestanneth") consonants must have started to occur in Sindarin around that time.

## Alterations {#Alterations}

We know the sound of *mh* (soft *m*) was needed at some later time<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and the most appropriate solution was to reverse the certh for *m* to indicate its softening, but it could not be reversed (presumably it was the certh <em style="font-family: &#39;Cirth Erebor&#39;; font-style: normal;">5</em>); therefore *m* was given to <em style="font-family: &#39;Cirth Erebor&#39;; font-style: normal;">6</em> (which until then had a value unknown to us), and <em style="font-family: &#39;Cirth Erebor&#39;; font-style: normal;">5</em> got the value of *hw*.

The same process took place with *r*, *l* etc..

Maybe at that time, also the distinct cirth for semivowels and umlauts (sounds like *w*, *y* and *œ*), were employed.
