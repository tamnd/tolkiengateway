**Crows** are black omnivorous birds sometimes associated with battles due to their taste for carrion.

## History {#History}

Crows were known of in [Aman](../A/Aman.md), with [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) applying "jail-crow" as a term of insult against [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The crows encountered by [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) and the [Dwarves](../T/Thorin_and_Company.md) during the quest to [Erebor](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> were mostly harmless. During the scouting expedition to the [Front Gate](../F/Front_Gate.md) of the Lonely Mountain the crows were considered "ominous" and [Balin](../B/Balin.md) distrusted them,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> though later he called them merely "nasty suspicious-looking creatures at that, and rude as well".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The most feared variety in the [Westlands](../W/Westlands.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) was the large type known as *[crebain](Crebain.md)*. On [8 January](../_/8_January.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md) the [Fellowship](../F/Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md) entered [Hollin](../H/Hollin.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Great_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-5">[5]</a></sup> and were spied upon by these large crows from [Fangorn](../F/Fangorn_Forest.md) and [Dunland](../D/Dunland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> The [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) were aware that [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md) made use of *crebain*; on [30 February](../_/30_February.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Great_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-5">[5]</a></sup> [Éomer](../_/Éomer.md) told [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) that Saruman's "birds of ill omen are abroad in the sky".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Crows may actually just be another name for Ravens as they looked and acted exactly the same. (Balin certainly distinguished between them, preferring Ravens over Crows.)

## Etymology {#Etymology}

A [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word for "crow" is *quáko*, derived from [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) *k(a)wāk*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Another Quenya word for "crow" is *korko*, and the [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md) cognate of the same meaning is *corch*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> In [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md), one of [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s very early conceptions of an [Elven](../E/Elvish.md) language, the word for "crow" is *crunc* (pl. *crunghin*).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) also had its own terms for a crow, specifically *khô* in "common" gender singular ("crow", as a species/creature), the plural *khôi* ("crows"), and the dual (pair-plural) *khôwat* ("a pair of crows"). Tolkien's notes on Adûnaic vocabulary and grammar also indicate that the more archaic forms of the term were likely *khaw*, later *khâw*, eventually evolving into *khâu* (with the Adûnaic diphtong *âu*), while the plural of these older forms would apparently be *khâwî(m)*. However, both *khâu* and *khâwî(m)* would likely be declensions in the subjective case of the noun, rather than in the nominative case. The objective case of the noun would be *khôwu*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s plot notes for the second phase of writing *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*, there was no distinction between crows and ravens as ominous birds.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> When the scouting party went to view the [Front Gate](../F/Front_Gate.md) [Balin](../B/Balin.md) did not like the ravens nearby, stating that they looked "like spies of evil".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> However, when the Company needed to learn of the death of [Smaug](../S/Smaug.md) Tolkien "rehabilitated" the ravens, making them friendly to the Dwarves, and replaced all negative raven-references with crows.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>
