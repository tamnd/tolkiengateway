The **Carrock** was a stony eyot in the upper reaches of the River [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), to the north of the [Old Ford](/wiki/Old_Ford).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The river looped around the rock although there was a ford of stones (probably the [Ford of Carrock](/wiki/Ford_of_Carrock)) to the eastern bank, while there was a swirling channel under its western cliff. At the base was a little cave with adjacent steps that led to a flat space upon the top.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

[Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) apparently had several such eyots, which [Beorn](/wiki/Beorn) called carrocks, but he referred to that particular one as "the Carrock" being closer to [his home](/wiki/Beorn%27s_Hall "Beorn's Hall"). Beorn built stairs reaching to its top, and sometimes would be seen climbing to the top in the form of a [bear](/wiki/Bears "Bears") to look at the [Moon](/wiki/Moon) or the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains).<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) were placed on the summit by the [Eagles](/wiki/Eagles). They bathed and rested at the cave.

According to [Bilbo's](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") account of his journey, [Radagast the Brown](/wiki/Radagast "Radagast")'s home, [Rhosgobel](/wiki/Rhosgobel), was near the Carrock.<sup id="cite_ref-Queer_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Queer-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The use of the name *Carrock* might be inspired by [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/carreg" title="wiktionary:carreg">carreg</a></i> "rock, stone".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> or [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/carr#Old_English" title="wiktionary:carr">carr</a></i> "especially applied to isolated rocks standing in the sea just off the Scottish and Northumbrian coasts" and by the fact that the root Celtic word is itself an anomaly that has caused scholars of the Celtic languages much puzzlement. The various forms of it in celtics langages do not follow the normal laws of sound-changes which is the kind of thing that attracted Tolkien’s attention.<sup id="cite_ref-medwed_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-medwed-5">[5]</a></sup>

*Carrock* therefore is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tautological_place_names" title="wikipedia:List of tautological place names">tautological</a> compound of a Celtic word for "rock" and the english word "rock";<sup id="cite_ref-medwed_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-medwed-5">[5]</a></sup> this principle is exemplified also in the names *[Chetwood](/wiki/Chetwood)* and *[Bree-hill](/wiki/Bree-hill)*.<sup id="cite_ref-N_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) experimented with a number of names before he settled upon the *Carrock* — he first considered *Sorneldin*, then *Sinrock*, then *Lamrock* before making his final choice.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
