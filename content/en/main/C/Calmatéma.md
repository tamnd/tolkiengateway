**Calmatéma** was one of the four series of the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) writing system. As with all of the series it consisted of six characters in each of six *[tyeller](/wiki/Tyell%C3%AB "Tyellë")* or 'grades'. This particular series took its name from the first of its signs, *[calma](/wiki/Calma "Calma")* (literally meaning 'lamp', but here simply representing the character 'c' or 'k').

The Calmatéma was applied either to the third or fourth series of the Fëanorian writing system. The [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) language used the third series as the Calmatéma while [Westron](/wiki/Westron) used the fourth. In either case half of the symbols in this series had one bow or *[lúva](/wiki/L%C3%BAva "Lúva")* while the other half had two bows. All bows in this series curved upwards and to the left from a stem, or *[telco](/wiki/Telco "Telco")*, which was either lowered, raised, or shortened. If the Calmatéma was the third series all bows were open; if the Calmatéma was the fourth series all bows were closed with a bar across their opening.

The names of the letters in Tengwar were established in Quenya and had become standardised by the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). The first four signs in the third series, the Calmatéma for Quenya, were *[calma](/wiki/Calma "Calma")*, *[anga](/wiki/Anga "Anga")*, *[harma](/wiki/Harma "Harma")* and *[anca](/wiki/Anca "Anca")*, each represented a variant of the basic 'k' sound: 'k', 'g', 'ch' and 'gh', respectively. The fifth sign, *[noldo](/wiki/Noldo "Noldo")* (earlier *ngoldo*), represented 'ng', while the sixth, *[anna](/wiki/Anna "Anna")* was commonly used for consonantal 'y'. In Westron, where the Calmatéma was the fourth series, the first four signs had the Quenya names *[quesse](/wiki/Quesse "Quesse")*, *[ungwe](/wiki/Ungwe "Ungwe")*, *[hwesta](/wiki/Hwesta "Hwesta")* and *[unque](/wiki/Unque "Unque")* although they represented the same 'k', 'g', 'ch' and 'gh' sounds.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
