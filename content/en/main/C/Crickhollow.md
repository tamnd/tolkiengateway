**Crickhollow** was a location, perhaps a small settlement, in [Buckland](../B/Buckland.md), located a short way to the north-east of [Brandy Hall](../B/Brandy_Hall.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Its most notable spot was an isolated house standing back from the lane in the middle of a wide lawn. It was surrounded by low trees inside an outer hedge, and there were no other dwellings nearby. This house was occasionally occupied with [Brandybuck](../B/Brandybuck_Family~66350.md) [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) who grew tired of the often crowded [Brandy Hall](../B/Brandy_Hall.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Conspiracy_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conspiracy-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

[Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) was instructed by [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) to find a reason why he should leave [Hobbiton](../H/Hobbiton.md) (and [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md)) and meet him in [Bree](../B/Bree.md). In the summer of [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md), [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) bought this house as an excuse for leaving [Hobbiton](../H/Hobbiton.md) to the east.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/1/14/Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="276" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/1/14/Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="414"/></a><figcaption><a href="../T/Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a> - Bathing at Crickhollow</figcaption></figure>

While Frodo, [Sam](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md), and [Pippin](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) moved out, [Merry](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) and [Fatty Bolger](../F/Fredegar_Bolger.md) were already in the house at Crickhollow, making preparations for their arrival. They were the only Hobbits who knew that Frodo would begin a journey to the east, so they made preparations for their departure as well.

On [25 September](../_/25_September.md), Frodo and his company arrived at the lodgings and met Merry and Fatty. The next day Frodo and his party departed for the [Old Forest](../O/Old_Forest.md). It was decided that to hide Frodo's departure, Fatty would stay at Crickhollow to maintain the pretence that Frodo was still in residence.<sup id="cite_ref-Conspiracy_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conspiracy-2">[2]</a></sup> Frodo spent only one night; at [dawn](../_/26_September.md), they picked up the ponies from a nearby stable and rode to the [High Hay](../H/High_Hay.md).

In the early hours of [30 September](../_/30_September.md), three [Black Riders](../N/Nazgûl.md) came to the house in Crickhollow,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but Fatty Bolger had already escaped. He had run to the nearest house, and from there he spread the news of an invasion, which travelled swiftly to [Brandy Hall](../B/Brandy_Hall.md) and resulted in the rousing of the hobbits of [Buckland](../B/Buckland.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

After the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), Merry and Pippin lived together for some time at Crickhollow.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) noted that the first element is obsolete that the second element *hollow* is a small depression in the ground.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-7">[7]</a></sup> J.R.R. Tolkien wrote in a [letter](<../L/Letter_to_Jenny_Hall_(28_February_1966).md>) in 1966 that the name Crickhollow was meant to resemble <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crickhowell" title="wikipedia:Crickhowell">Crickhowell</a>, which is a city in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales" title="wikipedia:Wales">Wales</a>.

Based on this, [David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) has suggested a speculative [Old Hobbitish](../O/Old_English.md) form \**Crycholh* from which Crickhollow derives. The obscure element *cryc* could represent, as can be expected in [Stoorish](../S/Stoorish.md), a [Celtic](Celtic.md) (<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_language" title="wikipedia:British language">Old Brythonic</a>) word for "hill". The placename would therefore mean "low place by the hill".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

It is not clear in *The Lord of the Rings* whether Crickhollow was a village or a region occupied by a solitary house. The [Encyclopedia of Arda](../E/Encyclopedia_of_Arda.md) mentions Crickhollow as a village.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> [Robert Foster](../R/Robert_Foster.md) describes Crickhollow as a "place in Buckland"<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> whereas [Karen Fonstad](../K/Karen_Fonstad.md) believes that Crickhollow is just the name of Frodo's house.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
