**Crickhollow** was a location, perhaps a small settlement, in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland), located a short way to the north-east of [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Its most notable spot was an isolated house standing back from the lane in the middle of a wide lawn. It was surrounded by low trees inside an outer hedge, and there were no other dwellings nearby. This house was occasionally occupied with [Brandybuck](/wiki/Brandybuck_Family "Brandybuck Family") [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) who grew tired of the often crowded [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall).<sup id="cite_ref-Conspiracy_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conspiracy-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

[Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) was instructed by [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) to find a reason why he should leave [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) (and [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")) and meet him in [Bree](/wiki/Bree). In the summer of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) bought this house as an excuse for leaving [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) to the east.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/1/14/Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="276" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/1/14/Ted_Nasmith_-_Bathing_at_Crickhollow.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="414"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a> - Bathing at Crickhollow</figcaption></figure>

While Frodo, [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee"), and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") moved out, [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Fatty Bolger](/wiki/Fredegar_Bolger "Fredegar Bolger") were already in the house at Crickhollow, making preparations for their arrival. They were the only Hobbits who knew that Frodo would begin a journey to the east, so they made preparations for their departure as well.

On [25 September](/wiki/25_September), Frodo and his company arrived at the lodgings and met Merry and Fatty. The next day Frodo and his party departed for the [Old Forest](/wiki/Old_Forest). It was decided that to hide Frodo's departure, Fatty would stay at Crickhollow to maintain the pretence that Frodo was still in residence.<sup id="cite_ref-Conspiracy_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conspiracy-2">[2]</a></sup> Frodo spent only one night; at [dawn](/wiki/26_September "26 September"), they picked up the ponies from a nearby stable and rode to the [High Hay](/wiki/High_Hay).

In the early hours of [30 September](/wiki/30_September), three [Black Riders](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl") came to the house in Crickhollow,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but Fatty Bolger had already escaped. He had run to the nearest house, and from there he spread the news of an invasion, which travelled swiftly to [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall) and resulted in the rousing of the hobbits of [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

After the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), Merry and Pippin lived together for some time at Crickhollow.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") noted that the first element is obsolete that the second element *hollow* is a small depression in the ground.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-7">[7]</a></sup> J.R.R. Tolkien wrote in a [letter](</wiki/Letter_to_Jenny_Hall_(28_February_1966)> "Letter to Jenny Hall (28 February 1966)") in 1966 that the name Crickhollow was meant to resemble <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crickhowell" title="wikipedia:Crickhowell">Crickhowell</a>, which is a city in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales" title="wikipedia:Wales">Wales</a>.

Based on this, [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) has suggested a speculative [Old Hobbitish](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") form \**Crycholh* from which Crickhollow derives. The obscure element *cryc* could represent, as can be expected in [Stoorish](/wiki/Stoorish), a [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) (<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_language" title="wikipedia:British language">Old Brythonic</a>) word for "hill". The placename would therefore mean "low place by the hill".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

It is not clear in *The Lord of the Rings* whether Crickhollow was a village or a region occupied by a solitary house. The [Encyclopedia of Arda](/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Arda) mentions Crickhollow as a village.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> [Robert Foster](/wiki/Robert_Foster) describes Crickhollow as a "place in Buckland"<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> whereas [Karen Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Fonstad) believes that Crickhollow is just the name of Frodo's house.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
