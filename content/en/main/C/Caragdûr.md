The **Caragdûr** was a black precipice of rock that was located on the north side of the city of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin).

## History {#History}

After killing his wife, [Aredhel](/wiki/Aredhel), [Eöl](/wiki/E%C3%B6l) the [Dark Elf](/wiki/Dark_Elf) was sentenced to be executed by being flung down the Caragdûr. Just before his fall, Eöl cried out to [Maeglin](/wiki/Maeglin), his son, saying that he would die in that same place.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Many years later, during the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin), Maeglin had laid hands upon [Idril](/wiki/Idril) but was forestalled by [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor). Tuor fought with Maeglin and cast him far out so that Maeglin's body struck thrice upon the slopes of [Amon Gwareth](/wiki/Amon_Gwareth). Although Maeglin was not said to have died falling from the Caragdûr, he did share the fate of Eöl in a nearby location.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), a combination of *carag* ("spike") and *[dûr](/wiki/D%C3%BBr "Dûr")* ("dark").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The idea of Maeglin's body striking thrice was included by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) into his *[Silmarillion](/wiki/Silmarillion)* to fulfill Eöl's words, but it was taken from the Lost Tale of "[The Fall of Gondolin](</wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter)> "The Fall of Gondolin (chapter)")", which had no premonition nor Caragdûr.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> That can explain why in the published *Silmarillion* the deaths of father and son happen in different places.

In later revisions of the *Silmarillion*, Tolkien considered and rejected the name *Caragdar*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
