The **Caragdûr** was a black precipice of rock that was located on the north side of the city of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md).

## History {#History}

After killing his wife, [Aredhel](../A/Aredhel.md), [Eöl](../E/Eöl.md) the [Dark Elf](../D/Dark_Elf.md) was sentenced to be executed by being flung down the Caragdûr. Just before his fall, Eöl cried out to [Maeglin](../M/Maeglin.md), his son, saying that he would die in that same place.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Many years later, during the [Fall of Gondolin](../F/Fall_of_Gondolin.md), Maeglin had laid hands upon [Idril](../I/Idril.md) but was forestalled by [Tuor](../T/Tuor.md). Tuor fought with Maeglin and cast him far out so that Maeglin's body struck thrice upon the slopes of [Amon Gwareth](../A/Amon_Gwareth.md). Although Maeglin was not said to have died falling from the Caragdûr, he did share the fate of Eöl in a nearby location.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name is [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md), a combination of *carag* ("spike") and *[dûr](../D/Dûr.md)* ("dark").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The idea of Maeglin's body striking thrice was included by [Christopher Tolkien](Christopher_Tolkien.md) into his *[Silmarillion](../S/Silmarillion.md)* to fulfill Eöl's words, but it was taken from the Lost Tale of "[The Fall of Gondolin](<../T/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter).md>)", which had no premonition nor Caragdûr.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> That can explain why in the published *Silmarillion* the deaths of father and son happen in different places.

In later revisions of the *Silmarillion*, Tolkien considered and rejected the name *Caragdar*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
