**Calaquendi** (the "**Elves of the Light**" or "**Light-elves**"), referred to those [Elves](../E/Elves.md) who had seen the light of the [Two Trees](../T/Two_Trees_of_Valinor.md) in [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md). This group was also often called the **High Elves**.

## History {#History}

The Calaquendi included the [Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md), [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md), and those [Teleri](../T/Teleri.md) (the [Falmari](../F/Falmari.md)) who passed over [Belegaer](../B/Belegaer.md), as well as the [Sindarin](../S/Sindar.md) King [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md) (alone of all his people). Another name for them was the [Amanyar](../A/Amanyar.md) (with the exception of Thingol, who was not counted among them), "Those of Aman". Those kindreds who settled in [Aman](../A/Aman.md) or descended from them were also referred to as **High Elves**, or **Tareldar**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> in the lore of the [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Those of the Elves who refused the summons of the [Valar](../V/Valar.md) or did not complete the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md) to [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md) were called the *Úmanyar*, meaning "Not of Aman" and "[Moriquendi](../M/Moriquendi.md)", or Dark Elves (those who had not seen the light). King [Elu Thingol](../T/Thingol.md) of [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md) held the unique status of being both among the Calaquendi and the Úmanyar, since he originally saw the Light, being one of the three ambassadors brought to Aman by [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md), but did not return to Aman during the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md).

The High Elves returned to [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) because of the [Exile of the Noldor](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md), participated in the [Wars of Beleriand](../W/Wars_of_Beleriand.md) and other events of the [Second](../S/Second_Age.md) and [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).

By the later Third Age, most of the High Elves resided in or had returned to Aman; only a few remained in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), some in [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md). [Wandering Companies](../W/Wandering_Companies.md) of High Elves would traverse [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md) and [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) from time to time; [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) recognised one of those bands as being High Elves, because of chanting the name of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../E/Elbereth.md" title="Elbereth">Elbereth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Calaquendi* consists of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) *[cala](Cala.md)* ("light"), and *[quendi](../Q/Quendi.md)* ("elves"); thus: "Elves of Light". The singular form of the Quenya noun is *Calaquendë*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Cognates of the name *Calaquendi* in other languages are [Telerin](../T/Telerin.md) <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Calapendi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Calapendi (page does not exist)">Calapendi</a></i> and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) *[Calbin](Calben.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*, **Light-elves** was a name given to the [Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-H8_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H8-6">[6]</a></sup> This term was used just once in this application, and never again in any [canonical](Canon.md) source.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In [Norse mythology](../N/Norse_mythology.md), there are the Light Elves (<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_elves" title="wikipedia:Light elves">Ljósálfar</a></i>) and the [Dark Elves](../M/Moriquendi.md) (*Dökkálfar* or <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svart%C3%A1lfar" title="wikipedia:Svartálfar">Svartálfar</a></i>), a tradition which was well known to [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>
