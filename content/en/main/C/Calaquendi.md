**Calaquendi** (the "**Elves of the Light**" or "**Light-elves**"), referred to those [Elves](/wiki/Elves) who had seen the light of the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees_of_Valinor "Two Trees of Valinor") in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor). This group was also often called the **High Elves**.

## History {#History}

The Calaquendi included the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar), [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor), and those [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri) (the [Falmari](/wiki/Falmari)) who passed over [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer), as well as the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindar "Sindar") King [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) (alone of all his people). Another name for them was the [Amanyar](/wiki/Amanyar) (with the exception of Thingol, who was not counted among them), "Those of Aman". Those kindreds who settled in [Aman](/wiki/Aman) or descended from them were also referred to as **High Elves**, or **Tareldar**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> in the lore of the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Those of the Elves who refused the summons of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) or did not complete the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey) to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) were called the *Úmanyar*, meaning "Not of Aman" and "[Moriquendi](/wiki/Moriquendi)", or Dark Elves (those who had not seen the light). King [Elu Thingol](/wiki/Thingol "Thingol") of [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath) held the unique status of being both among the Calaquendi and the Úmanyar, since he originally saw the Light, being one of the three ambassadors brought to Aman by [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB), but did not return to Aman during the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey).

The High Elves returned to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) because of the [Exile of the Noldor](/wiki/Exile_of_the_Noldor), participated in the [Wars of Beleriand](/wiki/Wars_of_Beleriand) and other events of the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).

By the later Third Age, most of the High Elves resided in or had returned to Aman; only a few remained in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), some in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell). [Wandering Companies](/wiki/Wandering_Companies) of High Elves would traverse [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) and [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") from time to time; [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) recognised one of those bands as being High Elves, because of chanting the name of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Elbereth" title="Elbereth">Elbereth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Calaquendi* consists of [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *[cala](/wiki/Cala "Cala")* ("light"), and *[quendi](/wiki/Quendi "Quendi")* ("elves"); thus: "Elves of Light". The singular form of the Quenya noun is *Calaquendë*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Cognates of the name *Calaquendi* in other languages are [Telerin](/wiki/Telerin) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Calapendi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Calapendi (page does not exist)">Calapendi</a></i> and [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[Calbin](/wiki/Calben "Calben")*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, **Light-elves** was a name given to the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar).<sup id="cite_ref-H8_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H8-6">[6]</a></sup> This term was used just once in this application, and never again in any [canonical](/wiki/Canon "Canon") source.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In [Norse mythology](/wiki/Norse_mythology), there are the Light Elves (<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_elves" title="wikipedia:Light elves">Ljósálfar</a></i>) and the [Dark Elves](/wiki/Moriquendi "Moriquendi") (*Dökkálfar* or <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svart%C3%A1lfar" title="wikipedia:Svartálfar">Svartálfar</a></i>), a tradition which was well known to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>
