The **Cracks of Doom** or **Crack of Doom** were a great fissure, a deep chasm filled with fire,<sup id="cite_ref-Doom_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doom-1">[1]</a></sup> in which [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") was forged by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and was destroyed when [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) fell with the One Ring into it.

## Geography {#Geography}

The Cracks of Doom were located in the <i><b>Sammath Naur</b></i>, the Chambers of Fire, a long cave with a high roof high up on the cone of [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom)<sup id="cite_ref-Doom_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doom-1">[1]</a></sup> on the plain of [Gorgoroth](/wiki/Gorgoroth) in the land of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

From a dark entrance in the eastern side of Mount Doom, the door of the *Sammath Naur*, a long cave led inside until the great fissure of the Cracks of Doom cut across the floor and walls of the cave a short way from the door.<sup id="cite_ref-Doom_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doom-1">[1]</a></sup> A [road](/wiki/Sauron%27s_Road "Sauron's Road") led from the west gate of [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr), the Dark Tower of Sauron, to the door of the *Sammath Naur*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Sauron forged the One Ring in the Cracks of Doom in c. [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1600](/wiki/Second_Age_1600 "Second Age 1600"),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and put so much of his own force into the Ring that the only way it could be destroyed would be to throw it back into the Cracks of Doom.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Such an opportunity came in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441")<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> after the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad) but [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) kept the Ring for himself, against the counsel of [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) and [Cirdan](/wiki/Cirdan).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

It was decided in the [Council of Elrond](/wiki/Council_of_Elrond) that the One Ring must be brought to the Cracks of Doom to be destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) reached the Cracks of Doom on [25 March](/wiki/25_March), [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019").<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> It was there that Frodo decided to keep the Ring and put it on his finger and where Gollum bit off the finger with the Ring and fell with it into the fiery chasm destroying the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-Doom_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doom-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

- *Sammath Naur* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name that translates to "Chambers of Fire". *Sammath* is the [collective plural](/wiki/Sindarin#Class_Plural "Sindarin") of the word *sam* believed to mean "room, chamber",<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> cf. [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *sambë*. Note that in the earlier *[Etymologies](/wiki/Etymologies)* the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) reflex of *sambë* was given as *tham*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> The latter word (*[naur](/wiki/Naur "Naur")*) means "fire".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The name *Cracks of Doom* also sometimes just *Crack of Doom* is a wordplay on "cracke of Doome" (*Macbeth*; IV i 117) meaning the crack/peal of thunder or the sudden sound of the last trumpet that announces the Last Day. Here, [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") uses "crack" to mean "fissure". Tolkien stated that he suspected he derived the idea for this sense of the phrase from a book by Algernon Blackwood, which he had read many years prior.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-13">[13]</a></sup>

Although Tolkien could no longer recall the name or specifics of the story, it has been suggested that the book in question is Blackwood's *The Education of Uncle Paul,* in which two children take the titular character to see "a tiny little crack between Yesterday and To-morrow", and slip through it.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
