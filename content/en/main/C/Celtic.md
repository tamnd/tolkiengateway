**Celtic** refers either to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages" title="wikipedia:Celtic languages">Celtic languages</a>, including <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breton_language" title="wikipedia:Breton language">Breton</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_language" title="wikipedia:Cornish language">Cornish</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goidelic_languages" title="wikipedia:Goidelic languages">Gaelic</a>, and [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh), or to the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts" title="wikipedia:Celts">Celts</a>, an historic group of people.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Scholarly interest {#Scholarly_interest}

Tolkien mentioned that Welsh names had a fascination for him since childhood.<sup id="cite_ref-L163_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L163-3">[3]</a></sup> He wrote that he loved Wales and especially the Welsh language,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> that he found Welsh to be especially attractive, <sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and that he found in Welsh an abiding linguistic-aesthetic satisfaction<sup id="cite_ref-L163_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L163-3">[3]</a></sup>. He mentioned that he did not learn [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) until he was an undergraduate,<sup id="cite_ref-L163_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L163-3">[3]</a></sup> that he knew a little Welsh, but that he had always been defeated by Old Irish or its modern descendants,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and that he knew Celtic things, many in their original languages Irish and Welsh<sup id="cite_ref-L19_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L19-7">[7]</a></sup>. He also wrote to his aunt who lived in Wales that he had used prize money (from the Skeat Prize for English) to buy a Welsh grammar written by Sir John Morris Jones.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

Tolkien was thanked for having helped "untiringly with problems of Celtic philology" in preparing the book *[Roman Britain and the English Settlements](/wiki/Roman_Britain_and_the_English_Settlements)* ([1936](/wiki/1936)).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Celtic influences on the legendarium {#Celtic_influences_on_the_legendarium}

### Mythology {#Mythology}

Tolkien claimed knowledge of Celtic languages and legends, but expressed a dislike in Celtic legends and denied that his legendarium is "Celtic".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

However, Celtic concepts are present in some views about the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) (see [Elves - Celtic influence](/wiki/Elves#Celtic_influence "Elves")).

### Other names {#Other_names}

While the [Westron](/wiki/Westron) world displays [Germanic](/wiki/North_Germanic_languages "North Germanic languages") and [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) elements, Tolkien mentioned that the survival of traces of the older language of the [Stoors](/wiki/Stoors) and the Bree-men in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) resembled the survival of Celtic elements in England, that the names of the [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders) had a vaguely 'Celtic' style and that Bree, Combe (Coomb), Archet and Chetwood are modelled on relics of British (i.e. Celtic) names that were chosen according to their sense.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> Tolkien also told the Dutch Translator, [Max Schuchart](/wiki/Max_Schuchart), that there were "'Celtic' elements in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) and [East-farthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing "Eastfarthing") names."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-granddad_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granddad-13">[13]</a></sup> He stated in his guide for translators for the translation of names in *The Lord of the Rings* into other languages that he gave Hobbits from Buckland Welsh names or names of similar style and that Gorhendad meant "great-grandfather" in Welsh<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> as well as that Archet had a Celtic origin and was descended from Welsh argoed<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>.

J.R.R. Tolkien mentioned that he gave names in [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) – such as [Meriadoc](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") or [Gorhendad](/wiki/Gorhendad_Oldbuck "Gorhendad Oldbuck") – a "Celtic cast", and that the relation of Welsh or British to English was somewhat similar to the relation of the older language of the Stoors and Bree-men to Westron. He also noted that many of the actual Buckland and Bree names – such as [Marroc](/wiki/Marroc_Brandybuck "Marroc Brandybuck"), [Madoc](/wiki/Madoc_Brandybuck "Madoc Brandybuck") and [Seredic](/wiki/Seredic_Brandybuck "Seredic Brandybuck") – had something of a Celtic style and the often had -ac, -ic or -oc as their ending.<sup id="cite_ref-granddad_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granddad-13">[13]</a></sup>

These analogies are present mostly relating to [Pre-Númenóreans](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) and the [Stoor](/wiki/Stoors "Stoors") [hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits"), which is evident in place-names such as "[Bree](/wiki/Bree)", and the personal names of the [Bucklanders](/wiki/Bucklanders). According to [Paula Marmor](/wiki/Paula_Marmor), the Celtic elements in [Stoorish](/wiki/Stoorish) names represent [an earlier language](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean "Pre-Númenórean") related to that of the [Bree-landers](/wiki/Bree-landers).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

In *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, the majority of genuine Celtic names is seen in the family trees of the [Brandybucks](/wiki/Brandybuck "Brandybuck"). Names such as [Rorimac](/wiki/Rorimac_Brandybuck "Rorimac Brandybuck"), [Dinodas](/wiki/Dinodas_Brandybuck "Dinodas Brandybuck"), [Gorbadoc](/wiki/Gorbadoc_Brandybuck "Gorbadoc Brandybuck"), [Meriadoc](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck"), and [Marmadoc](/wiki/Marmadoc_Brandybuck "Marmadoc Brandybuck") are Celtic.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

Other names having (or have been suggested as having) a Celtic influence include:

- *[Archet](/wiki/Archet#Etymology)*
- *[Bree](/wiki/Bree#Etymology)*
- *[Carn Dûm](/wiki/Carn_D%C3%BBm#Etymology)*
- *[Carrock](/wiki/Carrock#Etymology)*
- *[Chetwood](/wiki/Chetwood#Etymology)*
- *[Combe](/wiki/Combe#Etymology)*
- *[Crickhollow](/wiki/Crickhollow#Etymology)*
- *[Yale](/wiki/The_Yale#Etymology "The Yale")*

Edward Crankshaw, a reader for [Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin), was assigned to review the current draft of the "[Quenta Silmarillion](/wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion)" in 1937. He had a mostly favourable attitude to it, but he said that the names were "eye-splitting Celtic names". [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) denied Celtic influences in his Elvish names, and he showed distaste for Celtic words.<sup id="cite_ref-L19_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L19-7">[7]</a></sup>

### Sindarin {#Sindarin}

Tolkien explained that he deliberately gave the language [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) a linguistic character like, but not identical with [British-Welsh](/wiki/Welsh "Welsh"),<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> and was constructed to resemble Welsh phonologically<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>. He also mentioned that the lenitions or 'mutations' of Sindarin were deliberately made to resemble those of Welsh in phonetic origin and grammatical use, but are not the same in either of those two aspects.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>
