**Åke Joel Ohlmarks** ([1911](/wiki/1911)-[1984](/wiki/1984)) was a Swedish author, translator and historian of religion. He translated nine of Tolkien's works into Swedish and credited himself with publishing the first biography on Tolkien in the world.

## Ohlmarks and Tolkien {#Ohlmarks_and_Tolkien}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_(Aftonbladet).jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/b/b1/%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_%28Aftonbladet%29.jpg/300px-%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_%28Aftonbladet%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="358" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/b/b1/%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_%28Aftonbladet%29.jpg/450px-%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_%28Aftonbladet%29.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/b/b1/%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_%28Aftonbladet%29.jpg/600px-%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks_%28Aftonbladet%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="804" data-file-height="960"/></a><figcaption>Interview with Ohlmarks in <i>Aftonbladet</i>, entitled &#34;Tolkien was a lousy writer&#34; (early 1980s).</figcaption></figure>

Ohlmarks' translation of *The Lord of the Rings* (*Sagan om ringen*, *Sagan om de två tornen* and *Sagan om konungens återkomst*, collectively known as *[Härskarringen](/wiki/H%C3%A4rskarringen)*) was considered very bad by Tolkien. By [1957](/wiki/1957) Tolkien had seen his draft and was dissatisfied, replying with criticism and a list of changes to be made soon.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien expressed a strong dislike in Letters [228](/wiki/Letter_228 "Letter 228") and [229](/wiki/Letter_229 "Letter 229"). Because of his experience with the Swedish and [Dutch translations](/wiki/Max_Schuchart "Max Schuchart"), Tolkien even wrote *[Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* to prevent further wrongdoing in translations.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Several authors have pointed out that Ohlmarks' translation incorporated badly translated names, injection of adjectives and adverbs, and an archaic and poetic style not found in the original work. Above all else, it has been claimed that there are many errors based on insufficient knowledge of English.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Beregond_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beregond-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Not all comments on Ohlmarks' translation have been critical. Tolkien himself noted Ohlmarks' exemplary translation of *Middle-earth* to *Midgård* (similarly derived from <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/middangeard" title="wiktionary:middangeard">middan-geard</a></i>, the "old 'mythological' name"), and his rendering of *[Marigold](/wiki/Marigold_Gamgee#Etymology "Marigold Gamgee")* into *Majagull Ringblom* (retaining its English sense as both a flower-name referring to the golden colour and personal name).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> [Anders Stenström](/wiki/Anders_Stenstr%C3%B6m) has noted some other cases, where Ohlmarks' knowledge of Old Norse language and literature enabled him able to make a good translation of certain names.<sup id="cite_ref-Beregond_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beregond-4">[4]</a></sup>

However, the negative critique outweighed the positive.<sup id="cite_ref-Beregond_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beregond-4">[4]</a></sup> In the 1970s, Ohlmarks became enraged when [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) wrote that he would only allow a Swedish translation of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* if Ohlmarks was not connected to it.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Two years before his death, Ohlmarks blamed Swedish [Tolkien fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") for a fire to his house, and wrote *[Tolkien och den svarta magin](/wiki/Tolkien_och_den_svarta_magin)*, "Tolkien and the Black Magic". In it, he concocted a conspiracy theory that claimed Tolkien, and [The Tolkien Society](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Society), practised black magic and Nazi occultism.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [2004](/wiki/2004), a new Swedish translation of *The Lord of the Rings*, made by [Erik Andersson](/wiki/Erik_Andersson), was published.<sup id="cite_ref-Beregond_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beregond-4">[4]</a></sup>
