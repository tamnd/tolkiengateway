**"A Far Green Country"** is the thirty-ninth scene of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Return_of_the_King.md)* and the forty-ninth scene of [*The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King* (extended edition)](<../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Return_of_the_King_(extended_edition).md>).

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

[Pippin](../P/Pippin.md) and [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) take respite from battle. As they do so, Pippin says that he *"didn’t think it would end this way"*. Gandalf tells him that death is a path that all must eventually take. He proceeds to give Pippin his take on death, describing it as *"the grey rain curtain of this world rolling back, and all turning to silver glass"*, followed by the coming into view of *"a far green country, under a swift sunrise"*. Pippin smiles, saying that that *"does not sound too bad"*. Gandalf's look of joy swiftly vanishes as they are brought back into their present situation by the sound of hammering on the gates.

## Differences {#Differences}

The description of death given by Gandalf to Pippin in this scene is taken from a dream [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md) has in the books in the house of [Tom Bombadil](../T/Tom_Bombadil.md), in [Fog on the Barrow-downs](../F/Fog_on_the_Barrow-downs.md). There, Frodo heard *"a song that seemed to come like a pale light behind a grey rain-curtain, and growing stronger to turn the veil all to glass and silver, until at last it was rolled back, and a far green country opened before him under a swift sunrise."*<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> At this point in the story, Frodo had no notion of the meaning of this dream, however, after he completed his quest and was sailing into the west, *"it seemed to him that as in his dream in the house of Bombadil, the grey rain-curtain turned all to silver glass and was rolled back, and he beheld white shores and beyond them a far green country under a swift sunrise."*<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> This reinforces the idea that the "death" spoken of by Gandalf in this scene represents a journey into the west, and the coming to [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md).
