**Éowyn** was a member of the [House of Eorl](../H/House_of_Eorl.md) and the niece of King [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md) of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md). She was renowned for slaying the [Witch-king of Angmar](../W/Witch-king_of_Angmar~30551.md). She was the daughter of [Théodwyn](../T/Théodwyn.md), Théoden's sister, and [Éomund](Éomund.md) of [Eastfold](../E/Eastfold.md); her brother was [Éomer](Éomer.md). Following the end of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), she and [Faramir](../F/Faramir.md) were married, and she bore one son, [Elboron](../E/Elboron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Mark_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mark-4">[3]</a></sup>

She was also known as the Lady of Rohan, Lady of the Shield-arm, the White Lady of Rohan,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 2]</a></sup> and Lady of [Ithilien](../I/Ithilien.md).

## History {#History}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>What do you fear, lady?&#34;<br/>&#34;A cage. To stay behind bars, until use and old age accept them, and all chance of doing great deeds is gone beyond recall or desire.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../A/Aragorn.md" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> and Éowyn, <i><a href="../T/The_Return_of_the_King.md" title="The Return of the King">The Return of the King</a></i>, &#34;<a href="../T/The_Passing_of_the_Grey_Company.md" title="The Passing of the Grey Company">The Passing of the Grey Company</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Following the death of her parents in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3002](../T/Third_Age_3002.md), Éowyn and Éomer were brought into Théoden's house and raised there. Éowyn grew to be tall and slender, with a grace and pride that came from her mother.<sup id="cite_ref-Mark_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mark-4">[3]</a></sup>

Prior to the [Battle of the Hornburg](../B/Battle_of_the_Hornburg.md), Éowyn was left to care for [Meduseld](../M/Meduseld.md) when Théoden and Éomer led the remaining [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) to the west. King Théoden named her ruler of Rohan in his and Éomer's absence when [Háma](../H/Háma.md) recommended that one of "The House of Eorl" should rule. At first, Théoden only thought of the men of his house, of which he and Éomer were the last, but Háma reminded the King of Éowyn, who "is fearless," and that "all love her."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[4]</a></sup>

Éowyn revealed her temperament when [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) was to ride into the mountains and take the [Paths of the Dead](../P/Paths_of_the_Dead.md). Unable to dissuade him, she desired to accompany him, declaring herself "weary of skulking in the hills," with a "wish to face peril and battle." However, Aragorn reminded her of her charge to govern the people until the king's return and left without her.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup>

Later, when the forces of Rohan were mustered to go to Gondor, Éowyn disguised herself as a man, and under the alias of **[Dernhelm](../D/Dernhelm.md)**, travelled with the Riders of Rohan, carrying with her [Merry](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md), who was also ordered to remain behind.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup>

During the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../B/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md), she fought by King Théoden, and when he was struck down by the Witch-king of Angmar, she took it upon herself to defend the King of Rohan. Confronting the Witch-king, who boasted that "no living man may hinder me", she removed her helmet, revealing her long golden hair and declaring:

> *"No living man am I! You look upon a woman."*

Though he was filled with doubt at her unveiling, the Witch-king fought her, and broke her shield and arm with his mace. Yet before he could strike her down, Merry, who had not been heeded by the Witch-king, struck him behind the knee with a [Barrow-blade](../D/Daggers_of_Westernesse.md), crippling the wraith, and giving Éowyn the chance to thrust her sword between the crown and mantle, which shattered the blade. Thus Éowyn slew the Witch-king with Merry's aid, and fell upon her fallen foe, seemingly dead.<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-3">[2]</a></sup>

She was granted the title "Lady of the Shield-arm" after the battle in recognition of her triumph, as her shield-arm had been broken in the fight.<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-3">[2]</a></sup>

Shortly afterwards, Éomer found Éowyn beside Théoden, and, believing that both his sister and the King were dead, was filled with grief and fury. Taking the kingship of Rohan, he ordered that Théoden and Éowyn be taken to the Citadel. Éowyn was borne away alongside Théoden in honour, as she had fallen into a death-like sleep from the [Black Breath](../B/Black_Breath.md), and was presumed dead. However, [Prince Imrahil](../I/Imrahil.md) recognised then that she yet lived, and instead ordered that she be taken to the [Houses of Healing](../H/Houses_of_Healing.md), where she would receive help.<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-3">[2]</a></sup>

Éowyn was severely injured, and because of the Black Breath of the Nazgûl, she faced certain death; however, she was treated soon after by Aragorn during his brief sojourn into [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md). After a while she awakened, and since she had not yet recovered fully, she was not permitted to join the [Host of the West](../A/Army_of_the_West.md) that would march on [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md). However, whilst recovering in the Houses of Healing, she met Faramir, with whom she later fell in love.<sup id="cite_ref-Steward_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Steward-9">[7]</a></sup>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/M/Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/75/Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg/200px-Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="284" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/75/Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg/300px-Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/75/Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg/400px-Magdalena_Olechny_-_Eowyn_and_Faramir.jpg 2x" data-file-width="760" data-file-height="1080"/></a><figcaption><i>Eowyn and Faramir</i> by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Magdalena_Olechny.md" title="Category:Images by Magdalena Olechny">Magdalena Olechny</a></figcaption></figure>

After the defeat of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) and the celebrations, on [8 May](8_May.md) she followed her brother, now King of Rohan, back home to [Edoras](../E/Edoras.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup>

Later, Éowyn settled in [Ithilien](../I/Ithilien.md), of which Faramir was made the ruling Prince. She was thereafter known as the *Lady of Ithilien*.<sup id="cite_ref-Steward_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Steward-9">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 3]</a></sup> Éowyn and Faramir were married, and dwelt together in the hills of [Emyn Arnen](../E/Emyn_Arnen.md) in Ithilien.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[9]</a></sup> She kept her friendship with Merry, and when he became [Master of Buckland](../M/Master_of_Buckland.md) in [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [11](../F/Fourth_Age_11.md), she sent him great gifts, as did her brother from Rohan.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[10]</a></sup>

They had at least one son (likely [Elboron](../E/Elboron.md)),<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[11]</a></sup> and their grandson was [Barahir](<../B/Barahir_(grandson_of_Faramir).md>), who wrote *[The Tale of Aragorn and Arwen](../T/The_Tale_of_Aragorn_and_Arwen.md)* in the Fourth Age.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[12]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Éowyn* means "Horse-joy" or "lover of horses" in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) (being a combination of her parents' names: [Éomund](Éomund.md) and [Théodwyn](../T/Théodwyn.md)),<sup id="cite_ref-Intro_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Intro-16">[13]</a></sup> the language [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) adapted to represent [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md).<sup id="cite_ref-L144_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-17">[14]</a></sup>

Éo- sounds like "eh-ah" with the "ah" just barely pronounced, while *y* is the modified *u*,<sup id="cite_ref-Umlaut_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Umlaut-2">[1]</a></sup> which is more or less as *u* in French *lune*.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[15]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The "White Lady of Rohan" title was given by Faramir to Éowyn of Rohan when they were together looking out from the walls of Minas Tirith and saw the huge cloud that rose above Mordor at the fall of Sauron<sup id="cite_ref-Steward_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Steward-9">[7]</a></sup> on [25 March](25_March.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[16]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In an early rough note, Éowyn was originally known as "*Eowyn Elfsheen daughter of Theoden*".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[17]</a></sup> In the first drafts of her appearance, she was split into two characters: Éowyn, sister of Éomer, and [Idis](../I/Idis.md), daughter of Théoden. Tolkien eventually discarded Idis as Éowyn overshadowed her, and planned to have Éowyn wed Aragorn when he became the King of Gondor. However, Tolkien cut out the love story, deciding to make Éowyn "*the twin-sister of Eomund, a stern amazon woman*", and possibly dying to save or avenge Théoden.<sup id="cite_ref-King_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King-21">[18]</a></sup>

## Analysis {#Analysis}

Just as MacDuff disconcerted <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Tragedy_of_Macbeth" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Macbeth</a> by revealing he was not "of woman born", Lady Éowyn found the loophole in the 1,000-year-old prophecy by [Glorfindel](../G/Glorfindel.md), fulfilling that the Witch-king would not be slain by a man. However, the Witch-king actually recited the prophecy incorrectly: he said that "no living man may hinder me," though the prophecy actually said that "not by the hand of *man* will he fall."<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-22">[19]</a></sup> Glorfindel's prophecy, unlike his own version, implies that the Witch-king will not fall to a man. In Tolkien's writing, man and woman applies simply to the gender of any Middle-earth race. Only when he capitalised Man did it denote the race of Men. The Witch-king may not have realised that the prophecy meant only a male, thus, his moment of doubt and hesitation when he faced Éowyn.<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-3">[2]</a></sup>

<b>1955-6: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1955_radio_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1955 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1955 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Olive_Gregg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Olive Gregg (page does not exist)">Olive Gregg</a> provides the voice of Éowyn.</dd></dl>

<b>1978: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Éowyn is briefly seen, but has no lines.</dd></dl>

<b>1979: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1979_radio_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1979 radio series)">The Mind&#39;s Eye&#39;s The Lord of the Rings</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Éowyn is portrayed by <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Karen_Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Karen Hurley (page does not exist)">Karen Hurley</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>1980: <a href="../T/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film).md" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)"><i>The Return of the King</i> (1980 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Éowyn is voiced by actress <a href="../N/Nellie_Bellflower.md" title="Nellie Bellflower">Nellie Bellflower</a>. She appears unintroduced, but Merry fills Pippin (and so the spectator) in on the details. She is not terribly wounded, and appears healthy beside Faramir at the coronation.</dd></dl>

<b>1981: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1981_radio_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1981 radio series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1981 radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="../E/Elin_Jenkins.md" title="Elin Jenkins">Elin Jenkins</a> plays the part of Éowyn. She is introduced much earlier than in the book when <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> escapes from Isengard and borrows a horse from Théoden. Unlike some adaptations her recovery in the Houses of Healing, and the blossoming of her relationship with Faramir are included. She is last encountered in the penultimate episode gifting Merry the horn he would blow later during the scouring of the Shire.</dd></dl>

<b>1992: <a href="../D/Der_Herr_der_Ringe_(1992_German_radio_series).md" title="Der Herr der Ringe (1992 German radio series)"><i>Der Herr der Ringe</i> (1992 German radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Éowyn is played by Donata Höffer.</dd></dl>

<b>2002-3: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Éowyn is portrayed by <a href="../M/Miranda_Otto.md" title="Miranda Otto">Miranda Otto</a>. Jackson&#39;s adaptation shows two different explanations for Éowyn&#39;s injuries after fighting the Witch-king. In the Theatrical Release, her wounds are less severe than in the book; she is conscious but hurt, as opposed to unconscious. In the Extended Edition, she is near death; her brother finds her and grieves, and later she is healed in the Houses of Healing, where she meets Faramir.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a class="mw-redirect" href="../P/Pán_prsteňov_(2001-2003_Slovak_radio_series).md" title="Pán prsteňov (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)"><i>Pán prsteňov</i> (2001-2003 Slovak radio series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The voice of Éowyn is provided by Diana Mórová. Éowyn is alluded to in <i>The Two Towers</i>, but has no lines. She appears fully in <i>The Return of the King</i>. Her role is identical to that in the books.</dd></dl>

<b>2013: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Éowyn is first encountered in Edoras, shortly before the arrival of Gandalf and the Three Hunters. After King Théoden departs with his host, the player stays behind and helps Éowyn with various preparations before moving the citizens to <a href="../D/Dunharrow.md" title="Dunharrow">Dunharrow</a>. As Dernhelm, she travels to Minas Tirith with permission of <a href="../E/Elfhelm.md" title="Elfhelm">Elfhelm</a>, who keeps her secret and gives her the sword of her late cousin <a href="../T/Théodred.md" title="Théodred">Théodred</a>, taken from the <a href="../F/Fords_of_Isen.md" title="Fords of Isen">Fords of Isen</a>. It is the very sword that Eowyn uses to slay the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../W/Witch-King~20091.md" title="Witch-King">Witch-King</a> of <a href="../A/Angmar.md" title="Angmar">Angmar</a>, though it breaks in doing the deed. After healing from her wounds, she attends the coronation of King Elessar, but departs for Edoras prior to Aragorn and Arwen&#39;s wedding.</dd></dl>
