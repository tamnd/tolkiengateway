**"The Battle for Middle-earth is About to Begin"** is the fiftieth scene of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Two_Towers.md)* and the sixty-fifth scene of [*The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers* (extended edition)](<../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Two_Towers_(extended_edition).md>).

## Synopsis {#Synopsis}

After their victory at the battle of [Helm's Deep](../H/Helm's_Deep.md), [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md), [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md), [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md), [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md), [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md), and [Éomer](Éomer.md) ride to a crest which overlooks leagues of land, with the red glow and lightning of [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md) visible in the distance. Gandalf summarises their present situation: the battle for [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) is about to begin, and that their true hope of defeating [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) lies in the quest of [Frodo](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) and [Sam](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md).

As Frodo and Sam follow [Gollum](../G/Gollum.md) through the woods, Sam wonders if a story will ever be made of them.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I wonder if people will ever say, &#34;Let&#39;s hear about Frodo and the Ring.&#34; And they&#39;ll say, &#34;Yes! That&#39;s one of my favourite stories.&#34;
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Samwise Gamgee</cite></p>
</blockquote>

Frodo smiles and tells him he has left out "Samwise the Brave", one of the chief characters. He stops, and says *"Frodo wouldn't have got far without Sam"*. The two hobbits continue on, and Sam sighs *"Samwise the Brave"*.

## Differences {#Differences}

The conversation between Frodo and Sam that occurs in this scene is adapted from the conversation the two hobbits have in the chapter [The Stairs of Cirith Ungol](../T/The_Stairs_of_Cirith_Ungol.md). During their climb of the [stairs](../S/Stairs_of_Cirith_Ungol.md), they take take rest and eat. Here, Sam wonders "what sort of tale" they have fallen into. He muses that they are in the same never-ending tale as [Beren](../B/Beren.md), who never thought he would retrieve a [Silmaril](../S/Silmaril.md), and comments that Beren's danger was worse than theirs. The dialogue which follows in the book very closely resembles that in this scene. A minor difference is that Frodo refers to Sam as "Samwise the stouthearted" (as opposed to "Samwise the Brave") in the book.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
