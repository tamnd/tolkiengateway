The **1952 tape recording** refers to a private [audio recording](../../index/A/Audio_recordings_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md) by [George Sayer](../G/George_Sayer.md) of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) in late August [1952](1952.md).

Tolkien was visiting his friends (George Sayer and his wife) when the recording of Tolkien singing and reading excerpts from *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* and the then unpublished manuscript of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* was performed.<sup id="cite_ref-WH_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WH-1">[1]</a></sup>

When issuing selections from the recording in 1975, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caedmon_Audio" title="wikipedia:Caedmon Audio">Caedmon</a> included the statement: "*Also included is a never-published poem, 'The Mirror of Galadriel,' originally intended for inclusion in the trilogy, yet edited out*". This note was a mistake, but has been reprinted on subsequent releases from Caedmon.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [Wayne G. Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Douglas A. Anderson](../D/Douglas_A._Anderson.md) have given the following account regarding the mistaken note:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Sleeve notes by Ward Botsford and George Sayer. Botsford incorrectly identifies side B, band 16 as an unpublished poem for LR, book 2, ch.7... in fact the verse (<a href="../I/In_Dwimordene,_in_Lórien.md" title="In Dwimordene, in Lórien">In Dwimordene, in Lorien...</a>) was published in LR, bk.3, ch.6
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

According to [George Sayer](../G/George_Sayer.md), selections which were not issued include Tolkien reading the Lord's Prayer in [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md) (and perhaps also in English): he had never seen a tape recorded before "*and said whimsically that he ought to cast out any devil that might be in it by recording a prayer, the Lord’s Prayer in Gothic*". Caedmon also cut out "*conversation which occurred in between his readings from his works*". It is unknown if the original tapes are still extant.<sup id="cite_ref-WH_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WH-1">[1]</a></sup>
