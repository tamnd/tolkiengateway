An **éoherë** was the name used by the [Men](../M/Men.md) of the [Éothéod](Éothéod.md) for a full muster of their armed riders. When [Eorl](../E/Eorl.md) rode from the north to the aid of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), he led an éoherë of some seven thousand horsemen, as well as several hundred mounted archers.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The successors of the Éothéod were the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md), and they continued to use this term. In [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md)'s day, some five centuries after the [Ride of Eorl](../R/Ride_of_Eorl.md), the full éoherë of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md) was reckoned as one hundred [éoreds](Éored.md), or a total of twelve thousand fighting men.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *éoherë* is [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) (<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/eoh#Old_English" title="wiktionary:eoh">eoh</a></i> + <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/here#Old_English" title="wiktionary:here">here</a></i>), and means (approximately) 'horse-host'. Translated into [Númenórean Sindarin](../N/Númenórean_Sindarin.md), this became the name [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md), the much more common term for the Men of Rohan.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
