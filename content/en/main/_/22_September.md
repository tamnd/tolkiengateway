- [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3002](../T/Third_Age_3002.md)

    - [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) overtakes [Shadowfax](../S/Shadowfax.md).
    - Frodo celebrates his birthday with [Fredegar](../F/Fredegar_Bolger.md), [Merry](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md), [Pippin](../P/Peregrin_Took.md) and [Folco](../F/Folco_Boffin.md) during which he finishes the Bagginses' [Old Winyards](../O/Old_Winyards.md).     - [Boromir](../B/Boromir.md) travels towards [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) to ask for counsel.

    - [Bilbo](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) celebrates his 129th birthday in [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) with [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md) and company. [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md) enters [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-chief_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chief-4">[4]</a></sup>

    - Frodo and Sam meet the [Last Riding of the Keepers of the Rings](../L/Last_Riding_of_the_Keepers_of_the_Rings.md) in [Woody End](../W/Woody_End.md).<sup id="cite_ref-chief_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chief-4">[4]</a></sup>
    - [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) becomes 131 years old, the longest-lived Hobbit in history.
- [Fo.A.](../F/Fourth_Age.md) [61](../F/Fourth_Age_61.md):
    - [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) leaves [Bag End](../B/Bag_End.md) for the [Tower Hills](../T/Tower_Hills.md) and to [Grey Havens](../G/Grey_Havens.md), last of the [Ring-bearers](../R/Ring-bearer.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

### Cultural significance {#Cultural_significance}

As this day was the birthday of the [Ring-bearer](../R/Ring-bearer.md) Frodo Baggins it became significant to the [New Reckoning](../N/New_Reckoning.md) after the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md); corresponding to 30 [Yavannië](../Y/Yavannië.md), it was made a festival in honor of Frodo. See also: [Cormare](../C/Cormare.md)

## Notes {#Notes}

As Frodo and Sam ride through the Shire in 3021, the text tells us that "the Moon went westward" above them.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> According to astronomy, on that date of that year, no moon should have been visible.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Tolkien fans celebrate Frodo's birthday as <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Hobbit_Day&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Hobbit Day (page does not exist)">Hobbit Day</a>, after a suggestion by the [American Tolkien Society](../A/American_Tolkien_Society.md); the week containing 22 September each year is called <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tolkien_Week&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tolkien Week (page does not exist)">Tolkien Week</a>.

Despite this, how the [Shire Reckoning](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md), and consequently the date of Bilbo's and Frodo's birthday, corresponds with our calendar is a matter of debate. [Appendix D](../A/Appendix_D.md) says that our New Year's Day ([1 January](1_January.md)) corresponds "more or less" to the Shire's "[9 January](9_January.md)", and in standard years our [14 September](14_September.md) corresponds to the Shire's "22 September" (256 days later). However, Appendix D also says that the Shire calendar's "[Midyear's Day](../M/Midyear's_Day.md)" is "intended to correspond as nearly as possible to the summer solstice." In the Shire calendar, 22 [Halimath](../H/Halimath.md) is 83 days after Midyear's Day. If we take the summer solstice to be our [June 21](../J/June_21.md), then Bilbo's and Frodo's birthday must be 83 days later, which is our [September 12](../S/September_12.md).
