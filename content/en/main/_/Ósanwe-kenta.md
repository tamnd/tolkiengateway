The <i><b><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/%C3%93sanwe" title="Ósanwe">Ósanwe</a>-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Centa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Centa (page does not exist)">kenta</a></b></i>, or "Enquiry into the Communication of Thought", is a summary by an unnamed editor of a longer treatise by [Pengolodh](/wiki/Pengolodh) of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin).

The subject-matter is <i><b><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Sanwe-latya&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sanwe-latya (page does not exist)">sanwe-latya</a></b></i> ([Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) for "thought-opening", but better translated as "telepathy"). Pengolodh included it as an appendix to the *[Lhammas](/wiki/Lhammas)* because of the implications of <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Tengwesta&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tengwesta (page does not exist)">tengwesta</a></i> ("grammar, language") on thought-transmission, i.e. with the development of language, telepathy became more difficult and all but fell out of use among the "[incarnated](/wiki/Incarnate "Incarnate")" (those beings with *[hröa](/wiki/Hr%C3%B6a "Hröa")*).

## Real world history {#Real_world_history}

The essay was written in c. [1959](/wiki/1959)-[60](/wiki/1960 "1960") by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) as a typescript of eight pages, separately paginated, numbered 1-8, and appended to the major work *[Quendi and Eldar](/wiki/Quendi_and_Eldar)*. The existence of this essay was revealed by Christopher Tolkien in [Morgoth's Ring](/wiki/Morgoth%27s_Ring),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> published in [1993](/wiki/1993). It was published and edited by [Carl F. Hostetter](/wiki/Carl_F._Hostetter) for the first time in [*Vinyar Tengwar*, Number 39](/wiki/Vinyar_Tengwar_39 "Vinyar Tengwar 39"), July 1998. From the same editor, the essay was later included as a chapter of *[The Nature of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth)* ([2021](/wiki/2021)).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> A related and short manuscript, "Etymological Notes on the *Ósanwe-kenta*", was published in [*Vinyar Tengwar*, Number 41](/wiki/Vinyar_Tengwar_41 "Vinyar Tengwar 41"), July 2000. This latest essay is mostly a list of linguistic roots.
