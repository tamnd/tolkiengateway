<i><b>inbar</b></i> "a horn"<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup> is an element in the [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md) name *[Barazinbar](../B/Barazinbar.md)*.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word derives from the [Root](../S/Sundocarmë.md) N-B-R,<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup> which was mistyped as [Root](../S/Sundocarmë.md) M-B-R<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> in *[The Treason of Isengard](../T/The_Treason_of_Isengard.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Magnus Åberg speculated that the actual word might be \**nibar* or \**mibar* since words of three radical vowels such this don't begin with a vowel, but the form given in [*Parma Eldalamberon* 17](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_17.md) is merely *inbar*.<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup> The currently-unpublished notes on Khuzdul phonology and root modifications may shed light on this matter.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
