**Íverin** was the name of an island to the west of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) after its breaking during the [Faring Forth](/wiki/Faring_Forth), according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*. The island later became known as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland" title="wikipedia:Ireland">Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Osse_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Osse-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the Faring Forth, a great expedition by the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) of Tol Eressëa for the rescue of their kin in the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands), the island of Tol Eressëa was dragged across the [Ocean](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer") from the West, and anchored near the shores of the Great Lands.

The [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB), angered at the uprooting of the island that he himself has set in its place in the West ages ago, attempted to drag it back to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor). However, the island broke apart, and the western portion of what was once Tol Eressëa became its own island, which the Elves named Íverin, but which would eventually be called *Ireland* by [Men](/wiki/Men).<sup id="cite_ref-Osse_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Osse-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Íverin* is in [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya), however, its meaning is unclear.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Other Qenya names for the island, according to the *[Gnomish Lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon)*, are <i><b>Íwerin</b></i> and <i><b>Iverindor</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ail_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ail-5">[5]</a></sup>

The [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) cognates of *Íwerin* and *Iverindor* are <i><b>Aivrin</b></i> and <i><b>Aîvrien</b></i>, respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-Ail_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ail-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gnomish_Lexicon_Slips&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gnomish Lexicon Slips (page does not exist)">Gnomish Lexicon Slips</a></i>, a compilation of several texts written at least a year after the primary text of the *Gnomish Lexicon* itself, the names <i><b>Iwrien</b></i> and <i><b>Ivrien</b></i> appear as the Gnomish names for the island.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### The story of Íring {#The_story_of_Íring}

In one of the *[Early Runic Documents](/wiki/Early_Runic_Documents)*, contemporary with the *Book of Lost Tales*, there appears a story about a Man called [Íring](/wiki/%C3%8Dring), son of King [Ír](/wiki/%C3%8Dr) from Ireland, who is challenged to a wager by the sons of King [Watol](/wiki/Watol), and ultimately ends up killing them.<sup id="cite_ref-Iri_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iri-1">[1]</a></sup>

### *Ælfwine of England* and associated writings {#Ælfwine_of_England_and_associated_writings}

In a text called *[Ælfwine of England](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine_of_England)* from c. [1920](/wiki/1920), following several similar earlier outlines, the *Book of Lost Tales* was reimagined so that Tol Eressëa was no longer identified with what would eventually become the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles" title="wikipedia:British Isles">British Isles</a>, and the character of [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol)/Ottor Wǽfre from the 5th century northern Germany was replaced by [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), an Anglo-Saxon living in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_century" title="wikipedia:11th century">11th century</a> <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a>.

At the beginning of the text, it is said that the land of England was broken in the "warfare of the Gods", and that the part that was broken was afterwards called *Ireland*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

It is speculated by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) that, since the story of *Ælfwine of England* doesn't include the earlier conception of the dragging of Tol Eressëa across the ocean and its breaking apart by Ossë, the breaking off of Ireland possibly refers to the earliest idea of what would, in the later legendarium, become the cataclysm at the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) and the breaking of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) during the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>

According to one other, contemporary text, it is said that in later ages, most of the [Lost Elves](/wiki/Ilkorindi "Ilkorindi") still left east of Valinor and Tol Eressëa, congregated either in the islands of [Lúthien](/wiki/Luthany "Luthany") or Íverin.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

The island of Ireland is mentioned several times in some of the later stories such as *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)* and *[The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers)*, both in the conversation between the protagonists of the story (especially in reference to the story of [Saint Brendan](/wiki/Saint_Brendan),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> and the poem *[Imram](/wiki/Imram)*<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>),<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> and as a stopping point in Ælfwine's journey to Tol Eressëa.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>
