**Ælfheah** was one of the seven companions of [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) on his voyages, according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in the text called *[Ælfwine of England](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine_of_England)* from *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, and the only one whose name was remembered afterwards.<sup id="cite_ref-Elf_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elf-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After much voyaging and searching for the [Island of the Elves](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa "Tol Eressëa"), Ælfheah was the one who advised Ælfwine to put an end to his quest and return to his home; however, soon after they spotted the lights coming from the West in [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) - but a strong westerly wind pushed them back eastwards, except Ælfwine who jumped out of the ship and swam toward the isle.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Ælfheah's final fate is uncertain, but he most likely died at sea, since it was said of all the seven companions of Ælfwine that eventually "[Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo) Lord of the Sea afterward took them to himself".<sup id="cite_ref-Elf_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elf-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Ælfheah* is in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) and consists of the elements *ælf* ("elf") + *heah* ("high").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

An earlier name for the character was *Gelimer*, possibly being a latinization of a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandalic_language" title="wikipedia:Vandalic language">Vandalic</a> name (exemplified by the King of the Vandals and Alans <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelimer" title="wikipedia:Gelimer">Gelimer</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> deriving from the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language" title="wikipedia:Proto-Germanic language">Proto-Germanic</a> *gailaz* ("merry") + *mērijaz* ("famous, renowned").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The name *Gelimer* was itself a change from an earlier form *Helgor*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
