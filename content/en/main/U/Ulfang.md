**Ulfang the Black** was, along [Bór](/wiki/B%C3%B3r), one of the two greatest chieftains of the [Easterlings](</wiki/Easterlings_(First_Age)> "Easterlings (First Age)") that moved into [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) during the late [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Fin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fin-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After entering Beleriand at [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth)'s bidding, Ulfang and his sons [Uldor](/wiki/Uldor), [Ulfast](/wiki/Ulfast), and [Ulwarth](/wiki/Ulwarth) joined the service of [Caranthir](/wiki/Caranthir). They were given lands to dwell in the [East Beleriand](/wiki/East_Beleriand).<sup id="cite_ref-Fin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fin-2">[2]</a></sup>

However, unbeknownst to the [sons of Fëanor](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor "Sons of Fëanor"), Ulfang was secretly in the employ of Morgoth, and his sons betrayed the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) and the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) during the disastrous [Battle of Unnumbered Tears](/wiki/Battle_of_Unnumbered_Tears).<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-3">[3]</a></sup>

But though Morgoth was victorious in that battle, all three of Ulfang's sons were killed there.<sup id="cite_ref-Battle_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Battle-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology and other names {#Etymology_and_other_names}

The name *Ulfang* was probably not the character's true name, as it seems to be [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "Ugly Beard": <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ul-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ul- (page does not exist)">ul-</a></i> ("ugly") + *[fang](/wiki/Fang "Fang")* ("beard").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

An earlier name for the character was *Ulband*,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup> which was quickly changed to *Ulfand*,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> and then finally to *Ulfang*.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Even though Ulfang was the instigator of the betrayal of the Easterlings, he is not mentioned to have taken part in the battle, and it is noted that his son Uldor was the leader of the treason.

In the appendix of *[The Lost Road and Other Writings](/wiki/The_Lost_Road_and_Other_Writings)* in <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Genealogies&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Genealogies (page does not exist)">The Genealogies</a></i> (dating from the early [1930s](/wiki/1930 "1930")), it is stated that Ulfand the Swart (an earlier name for *Ulfang*) was born in the year 100, and died in 170.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

When comparing the above dates with its associated text *[The Earliest Annals of Beleriand](/wiki/The_Earliest_Annals_of_Beleriand)*, Ulfand died two years before the battle which started in 172.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>

Also, the dates found in *The Earliest Annals of Beleriand* were by and large moved 300 years into the future in *[The Grey Annals](/wiki/The_Grey_Annals)* (c. early [1950s](/wiki/1950 "1950")), such that, for example, an event happening in the year 195 in *The Earliest Annals* now happened in the year [495](/wiki/First_Age_495 "First Age 495").<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>
