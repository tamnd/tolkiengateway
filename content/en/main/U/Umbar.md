**Umbar** was a realm<sup id="cite_ref-Herbs_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herbs-1">[1]</a></sup> on the coast south of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). It included a great cape and a land-locked firth,<sup id="cite_ref-UmbarNote_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UmbarNote-2">[2]</a></sup> which formed a natural haven.<sup id="cite_ref-HM|UI_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HM|UI-3">[3]</a></sup> The name Umbar was used in conjunction with it being made into a great fortress,<sup id="cite_ref-SA2280_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SA2280-4">[4]</a></sup> it being laid siege to<sup id="cite_ref-Earnil_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earnil-5">[5]</a></sup> or it being invested.<sup id="cite_ref-Ciryaher_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ciryaher-6">[6]</a></sup> Since these statements imply a small fortified area, it is probable that Umbar was also the name of a fortified city in this realm.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

Umbar was located south of the mouth of the river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) at the [Bay of Belfalas](/wiki/Bay_of_Belfalas) in a natural haven that was formed by a peninsula that extended west from the coast of the [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer) sea and then bent to the south and almost touched the coast that lay to its south. This form of the coastline left only a narrow entrance to the natural haven. The City of the Corsairs was at the easternmost point of the bay of Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-GeneralMap_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GeneralMap-8">[8]</a></sup>

Umbar was the nearest of the realms of the Southrons to Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Herbs_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herbs-1">[1]</a></sup> It is possible that the realm of Umbar stretched along the coast up to the river [Harnen](/wiki/Harnen) and along the river Harnen as far its source in the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath) before it became a part of Gondor or after it became an independent realm again, because Westron was still the native tongue in this area at the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-F1_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-F1-9">[9]</a></sup>

It is possible that Umbar had a climate that was fluctuating between mild winters and very hot and dry summers.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 1]</a></sup> It is also possible that Umbar had a vegetation of sparse woodlands,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup> because Umbar seems to have had the natural resources to build a fleet of fifty great ships and smaller vessels beyond count that had attacked Pelargir during the War of the Rings<sup id="cite_ref-Debate_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Debate-13">[12]</a></sup> thirtynine years after Aragorn had burnt a great part of the ships of the Corsairs in the haven of Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Thorongil_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thorongil-14">[13]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Foundation and Númenórean rule {#Foundation_and_Númenórean_rule}

It is possible that the bay of Umbar was discovered by [Aldarion](/wiki/Aldarion), the son of the Númenórean king [Tar-Meneldur](/wiki/Tar-Meneldur) when Aldarion scarcely escaped shipwreck in the Harad during a voyage with three ships,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> which lasted from [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [829](/wiki/Second_Age_829 "Second Age 829") to [843](/wiki/Second_Age_843 "Second Age 843").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup> After [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) heard that Aldarion had become a great shipbuilder who sailed his ships to haven far down into the Harad, he chose [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) as a stronghold to counter the threat of landings by the Númenoreans.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup>

After Sauron was defeated by an alliance of the Númenóreans and the Elves of [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) and [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) in the [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron)<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> during the reign of Queen [Tar-Telperiën](/wiki/Tar-Telperi%C3%ABn)<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[18]</a></sup> in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1700](/wiki/Second_Age_1700 "Second Age 1700"),<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> he retreated from the coasts of Middle-earth and did not dare to challenge the Númenóreans for a long time.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup>

Until the end of the reign of the Númenórean king [Tar-Minastir](/wiki/Tar-Minastir) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1869](/wiki/Second_Age_1869 "Second Age 1869"),<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup> the Númenóreans came to Middle-earth as teachers and friends of the local population.<sup id="cite_ref-AfterMinastir_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AfterMinastir-23">[22]</a></sup>

Approximately in [1800](/wiki/Second_Age_1800 "Second Age 1800") the Númenóreans began to establish dominions on the coasts,<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[23]</a></sup> which possibly already included Umbar. It is possible that the son of Tar-Minastir, [Ciryatan](/wiki/Tar-Ciryatan "Tar-Ciryatan") sailed to Umbar, because he voyaged south before he took the sceptre from his father in [1869](/wiki/Second_Age_1869 "Second Age 1869").<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

During the reign of the Númenórean kings [Tar-Ciryatan](/wiki/Tar-Ciryatan) and [Tar-Atanamir](/wiki/Tar-Atanamir), who were both greedy for wealth, the Númenóreans formed two parties, the party of the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful), who remained faithful to the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and maintained their friendship with the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and the party of the [King's Men](/wiki/King%27s_Men), who followed the policy of the King to oppose the [Ban of the Valar](/wiki/Ban_of_the_Valar) that forbade to sail to [Aman](/wiki/Aman) and did not maintain the friendship with the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> The King's Men sailed far away to the south of Middle-earth and established lordships and strongholds<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup> in Umbar, Harad and many other places on the coasts of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-28">[27]</a></sup> The Númenóreans in the service of Tar-Ciryatan and Tar-Atanamir oppressed the men of Middle-earth and levied heavy tribute from the men of the coasts and their ships brought back metals and gems from Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup> It is possible that Umbar was an important point from which tributes and other resources were shipped to Númenor.

After the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Ringwraiths) had appeared around the year [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [2251](/wiki/Second_Age_2251 "Second Age 2251")<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup> and after the terror and mastery of Sauron over Men in Middle-earth had grown, Sauron began to attack the strongholds of the Númenóreans on the coasts of Middle-earth,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup> but he was not yet able to conquer Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-28">[27]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[31]</a></sup>

Before he had married the queen of Númenor [Tar-Míriel](/wiki/Tar-M%C3%ADriel) and ursurped her throne, the Númenórean king [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n) had been a leader in the wars of the Númenóreans on the coasts of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[32]</a></sup> During this time in Middle-earth he became aware of the strength of the realm of Sauron and of Sauron's hatred against Númenor. After his ursurpation of the throne, Ar-Pharazôn received reports that Sauron was attacking with his might, was pressing down on the cities by the coasts and had openly declared to intend to drive the Númenóreans into the sea and to destroy Númenor, Ar-Pharazôn prepared an army and an armada of ships.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup> In [3261](/wiki/Second_Age_3261 "Second Age 3261"),<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup> Ar-Pharazôn landed with his armada at Umbar, marched with his army for seven days, and commanded Sauron to swear fealty to him. Sauron thought that he could not win by force, so he came and humbled himself before Ar-Pharazôn and was taken as a hostage to Númenor.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[35]</a></sup> Umbar was remembered ever after with pride as the site of the landing of the Númenórean army that had humiliated Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[36]</a></sup>

After the Downfall of Númenor, Umbar remained in the hands of the Númenóreans, in essence a realm-in-exile alongside [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). But unlike these others, Umbar had been settled by the King's Men, who were afterwards called the [Black Númenóreans](/wiki/Black_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), who hated the Faithful Númenórean survivors of Arnor and Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-UmbarNote_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UmbarNote-2">[2]</a></sup>

Two Black Númenórean lords, [Herumor](/wiki/Herumor) and [Fuinur](/wiki/Fuinur), rose to power among the Haradrim at the end of [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age). When Sauron prepared to attack Gondor in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), he also gathered servants from the south, including many Númenóreans, because many of them had sailed east from Númenor so settle on the coasts of Middle-earth in the time when Sauron had lived in Númenór had already served him there and continued to serve him in Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-38">[37]</a></sup> Not all the servants of Sauron were killed at the end of the War of the Last Alliance<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-38">[37]</a></sup> in [3429](/wiki/Second_Age_3429 "Second Age 3429").<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[38]</a></sup> Some of them were routed and dispersed and many remembered Sauron in their hearts and hated Arnor and Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_38-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-38">[37]</a></sup> It is possible that Herumor and Fuinor were from Umbar - their fate is unknown, but it is possible that they died during the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance).

The fate of the other settlements of the Númenóreans further south of Umbar is unclear, but it is said that they were 'absorbed', and that no attempt was made to involve them in the wars. This could imply that they were simply too isolated, geographically and politically, and acting on their own behalf. Whatever befell them, they fell out of the picture of the West.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[39]</a></sup>

### Umbar as part of Gondor {#Umbar_as_part_of_Gondor}

Gondor's King [Eärnil I](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnil_I) repaired the haven of [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir), built a great navy and besieged Umbar by land and by sea.<sup id="cite_ref-Earnil_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earnil-5">[5]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [933](/wiki/Third_Age_933 "Third Age 933"), he took Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> Umbar was only taken at great cost, because its Black Númenórean inhabitants as descendants of the King's Men inherited their hatred of Gondor as a realm of the followers of Elendil<sup id="cite_ref-UmbarNote_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UmbarNote-2">[2]</a></sup> and probably defended Umbar vehemently because of that. As a result of the capture of Umbar by Gondor, the Black Númenórean lords of Umbar were driven from Umbar and Umbar became a great harbour and fortress of the power of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Earnil_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earnil-5">[5]</a></sup> Only three years later, in [936](/wiki/Third_Age_936 "Third Age 936"),<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> King Eärnil was lost at sea with many ships and men in a great storm off Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Earnil_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earnil-5">[5]</a></sup>

Eärnil's son [Ciryandil](/wiki/Ciryandil) continued to build ships.<sup id="cite_ref-Earnil_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earnil-5">[5]</a></sup> During his reign the Men of the Harad led by the Black Númenórean lords that had been driven from Umbar came with a large force and besieged Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[41]</a></sup> In [1015](/wiki/Third_Age_1015 "Third Age 1015") Ciryandil was killed in the siege of Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup>

The siege of Umbar lasted for many years, but Umbar could not be taken, because of the sea-power of Gondor,<sup id="cite_ref-Ciryaher_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ciryaher-6">[6]</a></sup> probably because Umbar could be resupplied and reinforced with ships from Gondor by sea. Ciryandil's son [Ciryaher](/wiki/Ciryaher) took enough time to gather an army and a navy, came down by land crossing the river [Harnen](/wiki/Harnen) and by sea and utterly defeated the Men of the Harad<sup id="cite_ref-Ciryaher_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ciryaher-6">[6]</a></sup> in [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050").<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> As a consequence, Ciryaher took the name Hyarmendacil "South-victor" and the kings of the Men of the Harad had to acknowledge the overlordsip of Gondor. During his reign the realm of Gondor extended south to the river Harnen and along the coast to the peninsula and haven of Umbar and the sons of the kings of the Harad lived as hostages in the court of king Ciryaher.<sup id="cite_ref-Ciryaher_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ciryaher-6">[6]</a></sup>

After the capture of Umbar by Gondor, the Gondorians built a great white pillar on the highest hill of the headland above the Haven as a monument of the submission of Sauron to the Númenórean king Ar-Pharazôn. The pillar was crowned with a globe of crystal that reflected the rays of the sun and the moon and shone like a bright star that could be seen in clear weather even on the coasts of Gondor or far out upon the western sea.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[42]</a></sup>

When King [Valacar](/wiki/Valacar) of Gondor grew old there was already a rebellion in the southern provinces. Some of the Dúnedain refused to accept his son <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Eldacar" title="Eldacar">Eldacar</a> as their future King, because Eldacar had been born in a foreign country, had been named Vinitharya in a foreign language in his youth and they feared that Eldacar would have a shorter life, because his mother, who came from a foreign country and who they perceived to be of a "lesser" race had only had a short life compared to the longer life of the Dúnedain.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[43]</a></sup> After the death of King Valacar in [1432](/wiki/Third_Age_1432 "Third Age 1432"), a civil war, called the [Kin-strife](/wiki/Kin-strife), began in Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> The people of the great havens of Umbar and Pelargir and of the coasts supported the Captain of Ships, [Castamir](/wiki/Castamir), who was one of those nearest by blood to the crown.<sup id="cite_ref-Eldacar_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldacar-45">[44]</a></sup> In [1437](/wiki/Third_Age_1437 "Third Age 1437"),<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> Castamir deposed Eldacar, who fled to his kinfolk in [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion).<sup id="cite_ref-Eldacar_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldacar-45">[44]</a></sup> Ten years later, in [1447](/wiki/Third_Age_1447 "Third Age 1447"),<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> Eldacar returned from the north with a great army of Dúnedain from the northern parts of the realm and of Northmen, who had been in the service of Gondor and killed Castamir in a great battle at the crossings of the river [Erui](/wiki/Erui). However the sons of Castamir escaped to Pelargir and Eldacar besieged Pelargir<sup id="cite_ref-Eldacar_45-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldacar-45">[44]</a></sup> in [1447](/wiki/Third_Age_1447 "Third Age 1447").<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1448](/wiki/Third_Age_1448 "Third Age 1448"),<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> after the sons of Castamir had gathered all the forces that they could in Pelargir, they sailed away to Umbar and established an independent lordship and a refuge for all enemies of the King of Gondor in Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-46">[45]</a></sup> It is possible that the rebels took their wives and families with them to Umbar; they had held out long with others of their kin in Pelargir and were able to gather people at Pelargir because Eldacar had no ships to besiege Pelargir by sea. Umbar remained at war with Gondor for many lives of men and was a threat to the coastlands of Gondor and to all traffic on the sea. The region of [South Gondor](/wiki/Harondor "Harondor") became a debatable land between the kings of Gondor and the Corsairs of Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_46-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-46">[45]</a></sup> As a result of the loss of Umbar the control of Gondor over the Men of the Harad decreased.<sup id="cite_ref-Flight_46-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flight-46">[45]</a></sup>

After the Kin-strife often close relatives of the kings of Gondor who were suspected for treason or conspiring against the kings fled to Umbar and joined the rebels.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[46]</a></sup>

In [1540](/wiki/Third_Age_1540 "Third Age 1540"), King [Aldamir](/wiki/Aldamir) was killed in a war with the Harad and the Corsairs of Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup>

In [1551](/wiki/Third_Age_1551 "Third Age 1551"), his son<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[47]</a></sup> [Hyarmendacil II](/wiki/Hyarmendacil_II) defeated the Men of the Harad<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> and crushed Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-49">[48]</a></sup>

The leaders of the Corsairs of Umbar, [Angamaitë](/wiki/Angamait%C3%AB) and [Sangahyando](/wiki/Sangahyando), the great-grandsons of Castamir, learned through spies that [Minardil](/wiki/Minardil), the [King of Gondor](/wiki/King_of_Gondor), was in the [Gondorian](/wiki/Gondor "Gondor") port of [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir) and that he did not suspect any danger, because his father had crushed Harad and Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_49-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-49">[48]</a></sup> In [1634](/wiki/Third_Age_1634 "Third Age 1634"),<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-T4_50-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-T4-50">[49]</a></sup> the Corsairs of Umbar, led by [Angamaitë](/wiki/Angamait%C3%AB) and [Sangahyando](/wiki/Sangahyando), made a raid up the river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), killed Minardil in Pelargir,<sup id="cite_ref-Telemnar_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Telemnar-51">[50]</a></sup> ravaged [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir) and the coasts and escaped with great booty.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_49-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-49">[48]</a></sup>

During the reign of King [Telumehtar](/wiki/Telumehtar), Minardil's great-grand nephew, the Corsairs raided the coasts of Gondor as far as the [Anfalas](/wiki/Anfalas). As a consequence, Telumehtar gathered his forces and took Umbar by storm<sup id="cite_ref-Telumehtar_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Telumehtar-52">[51]</a></sup> and drove out the Corsairs in [1810](/wiki/Third_Age_1810 "Third Age 1810").<sup id="cite_ref-Third_41-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third-41">[40]</a></sup> The last descendants of Castamir died during that war. After the reconquest of Umbar Telumehtar added the title Umbardacil (Umbar-victor) to his name.<sup id="cite_ref-Telumehtar_52-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Telumehtar-52">[51]</a></sup>

## Culture {#Culture}

### Politics and rule {#Politics_and_rule}

It is not known how the Númenórean colony of Umbar was ruled before the Downfall of Númenor when it was a stronghold of the King's Men who were afterwards called the Black Númenóreans. No "King of Umbar" is mentioned in the Annals of the Kings and Rulers after the Down fall of Númenor. It is possible that the Black Númenóreans regarded the Heirs of Elendil as usurpers. It is also possible that they did not decide on a new King as the successor of King Ar-Pharazôn, because they did not know if Ar-Pharazôn had survived the invasion of Aman and had achieved his desire of immortality there or had died in Aman or in the Drowning of Númenor.

It is not known, if [Herumor](/wiki/Herumor) and [Fuinur](/wiki/Fuinur), who rose to power among the Haradrim in the wide lands south of Mordor,<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_38-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-38">[37]</a></sup> were rulers of Umbar.

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1634](/wiki/Third_Age_1634 "Third Age 1634"), Angamaitë and Sangahyando were the leaders of the Corsairs of Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_49-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-49">[48]</a></sup>

At the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) Umbar was occupied by hostile people whose lords were originally rebel Númenóreans and were currently pirates.<sup id="cite_ref-HM|UI_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HM|UI-3">[3]</a></sup> The names and the number of those lords who ruled Umbar during the War of the Rings are not known.

### Language {#Language}

In the course of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) Westron had become the native language of nearly all [Men](/wiki/Men) that lived within the borders of the old kingdom of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), including all along the coasts from Umbar northward and inland as far as the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath). At the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) Westron was still the native tongue in this area.<sup id="cite_ref-F1_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-F1-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">[66]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The origin of the name *Umbar* was forgotten. Umbar already received its name before the ships of the Númenóreans sailed the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">[67]</a></sup> As a consequence, it is a name in one of the [pre-Númenórean](/wiki/Pre-N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean "Pre-Númenórean") languages. Despite the coincidental similarity, the name Umbar is not related to the Quenya word *[umbar](</wiki/Umbar_(word)> "Umbar (word)")*, which means fate.<sup id="cite_ref-Names_69-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Names-69">[68]</a></sup>

<b>2023: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The City of the Corsairs is known properly as &#34;Umbar Baharbêl&#34;, its realm also encompasses the Cape of Umbar and the few settlements upon it. Umbar has an extensive set of inhabited catacombs beneath it, the &#34;Umbar-môkh&#34;, comparable in footprint to the city above. Offshore from the cape is the shipwreck-strewn archipelago called &#34;Zirâr Tarka, the Shield Isles&#34; of Umbar, home to castaways, exiles, and freebooters. The Umbari language is partially inspired by Maltese.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>After the defeat at <a href="/wiki/Pelargir" title="Pelargir">Pelargir</a> and the downfall of <a href="/wiki/Sauron" title="Sauron">Sauron</a>, the Umbari people rebel against those those loyal to Sauron and the <a href="/wiki/Haradrim" title="Haradrim">Haradrim</a> &#34;Empire of Ordâkh&#34; occupying the city. Now a Free City, Umbar is ruled by a council of seven called the &#34;Kindred of the Coins&#34;. At least some of the Kindred have designs against <a href="/wiki/Gondor" title="Gondor">Gondor</a>, and so the players investigate on behalf of <a href="/wiki/King_of_Gondor" title="King of Gondor">King</a> <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Elessar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_Gondor" title="Queen of Gondor">Queen</a> <a href="/wiki/Arwen" title="Arwen">Arwen</a>, while befriending and assisting the citizens.</dd></dl>
