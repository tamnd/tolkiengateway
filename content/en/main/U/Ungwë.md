<i><b>ungwë</b></i> is a noun meaning "spider's web" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya).<sup id="cite_ref-Names_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Names-1">[1]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ungwe.gif"><img src="/w/images/9/9c/Ungwe.gif" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="53" height="65" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="53" data-file-height="65"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

*Ungwë* is also the name in Quenya of the eighth letter of the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) alphabet.<sup id="cite_ref-Names_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Names-1">[1]</a></sup> It is the fourth letter of the second grade or [Tyellë](/wiki/Tyell%C3%AB), which contains "voiced stops" and it is the first letter in the fourth series of consonants. For written Quenya or [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), the fourth series is used for the labialized or *kw*-series, the *[Quessetéma](/wiki/Quesset%C3%A9ma)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Letters_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letters-2">[2]</a></sup> In the standard spelling of Quenya, *Ungwë*represents **NGW**<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> while in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) this letter represents **GW**. In [Westron](/wiki/Westron) the fourth series is used for the *k*-series, the *[Calmatéma](/wiki/Calmat%C3%A9ma)*, and this letter represents **G**.<sup id="cite_ref-Letters_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letters-2">[2]</a></sup> The Westron name for this letter is <i><b>Gá</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word derives from the [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=U%C3%91G&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="UÑG (page does not exist)">UÑG</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

- [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant) = "Gloomy Spider" or "Gloomweaver"
