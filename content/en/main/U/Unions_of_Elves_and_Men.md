Only a few times in the history of [Arda](../A/Arda.md) were there **unions of Elves and Men**. The offspring of such marriages were known as [Half-elven](../H/Half-elven.md).

There were three **unions of the Eldar and the Edain**: [Beren](../B/Beren.md) and [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md), and [Tuor](../T/Tuor.md) and [Idril](../I/Idril.md), in the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md); and [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) and [Arwen](../A/Arwen.md) in the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Additionally, according to a local legend, [Imrazôr](../I/Imrazôr.md), the [Prince of Belfalas](../P/Princes_of_Dol_Amroth.md), married the Elf-maiden [Mithrellas](../M/Mithrellas.md), who gave birth to [a son](../G/Galador.md) and [a daughter](../G/Gilmith.md) before disappearing.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Similar cases {#Similar_cases}

[Finduilas](../F/Finduilas.md), a Noldorin princess of [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md), fell in love with [Túrin](../T/Túrin.md), but he rejected her advances.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Gwindor](../G/Gwindor.md), a lord of Nargothrond, advised her:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Not fitting is it that the Elder Children of Ilúvatar should wed the Younger; nor is it wise, for they are brief, and soon pass, to leave us in widowhood while the world lasts. Neither will fate suffer it, unless it be once or twice only, for some high cause of doom that we do not perceive.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="../T/The_Children_of_Húrin.md" title="The Children of Húrin">The Children of Húrin</a></i>, &#34;<a href="../T/Túrin_in_Nargothrond.md" title="Túrin in Nargothrond">Túrin in Nargothrond</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>

The *[Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth](../A/Athrabeth_Finrod_ah_Andreth.md)*, the debate of [Finrod](../F/Finrod.md) and [Andreth](../A/Andreth.md), contains an in-depth discussion about relationships between Elves and Men. Andreth, a [Wise-woman](../W/Wise-woman.md) of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md), and [Aegnor](../A/Aegnor.md), Finrod's brother, had fallen in love during the [Siege of Angband](../S/Siege_of_Angband.md). However, Aegnor turned away, though neither took another partner for the rest of their lives.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">[5]</a></sup>

Finrod explained to Andreth that the Eldar do not wed during times of war, and that Aegnor knew that the [Siege of Angband would break](../D/Dagor_Bragollach.md) soon. Additionally, while Andreth would grow old and die, Aegnor would carry the memories of Andreth until the [end of Arda](../D/Dagor_Dagorath.md), and he "*would rather have a memory that is fair but unfinished than one that goes on to a grievous end.*"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

As [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) explains, it seems that in the earliest version of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*, the union of a man and an elf was not of much significance, as in the tale of *[Turambar and the Foalókë](../T/Turambar_and_the_Foalókë.md)*, the man [Bethos](../B/Bethos.md) is married to a [Noldo](../G/Gnomes.md)-maiden, and they both have several children. However, in the same phase of these narratives, soon later [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) specified that Eärendel was the only being that is half of both kindreds, and that Tuor and Idril were the first wed between them.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> It should be noted though that the own narrative says that Bethos' wife was a Noldo "as the tale telleth",<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> distancing this fact in a similar way to the case of Imrazôr and Mithrellas.
