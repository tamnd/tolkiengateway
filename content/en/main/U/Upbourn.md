**Upbourn** was a hamlet located in [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan), south of [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras) on the banks of the [Snowbourn](/wiki/Snowbourn) in the valley of [Harrowdale](/wiki/Harrowdale).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name is a modernisation of the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *Upburnan* as would represent [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>

It derives from the fact that the hamlet was some way "up" the river Snowbourn.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>

As [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") noted, *up-* is used in English place-names for river-side villages (as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upavon" title="wikipedia:Upavon">Upavon</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiltshire" title="wikipedia:Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>), especially in contrast to larger places near its mouth (as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upwey,_Dorset" title="wikipedia:Upwey, Dorset">Upwey</a> above <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weymouth,_Dorset" title="wikipedia:Weymouth, Dorset">Weymouth</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>
