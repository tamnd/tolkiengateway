The **University of Leeds** is a centre for learning in the city of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds" title="wikipedia:Leeds">Leeds</a> in Yorkshire, England. [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) taught there between [1920](/wiki/1920) and [1925](/wiki/1925); first as Reader but later a full Professor.

## Tolkien at Leeds {#Tolkien_at_Leeds}

The University was Tolkien's second post-[war](/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") employer. After the *[New English Dictionary](/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary "Oxford English Dictionary")* and a year of tutoring students in [Oxford University](/wiki/Oxford_University), Tolkien decided to apply for a post at the University of Leeds. Tolkien had an interview with <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=George_S._Gordon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="George S. Gordon (page does not exist)">George S. Gordon</a>, the University's Professor of English, in June 1920.<sup id="cite_ref-Interlude_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Interlude-1">[1]</a></sup> It was a fruitful job interview: Tolkien was appointed a Reader in English Language in October of the same year, with a free commission to develop the linguistic side of a large and growing School of English Studies.<sup id="cite_ref-L7_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L7-2">[2]</a></sup>

The start was rough: though Gordon found Tolkien a room in Leeds,<sup id="cite_ref-L46_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L46-3">[3]</a></sup> [Edith](/wiki/Edith_Tolkien "Edith Tolkien") and young [John](/wiki/John_Tolkien "John Tolkien") still lived in [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford). In weekends, Tolkien would go to his family - now expanded with the birth of [Michael](/wiki/Michael_Tolkien "Michael Tolkien"). Not until [1921](/wiki/1921) did Tolkien get full housing for his family, first at <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=5_Holly_Bank&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="5 Holly Bank (page does not exist)">5 Holly Bank</a><sup id="cite_ref-Chronology1921_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chronology1921-4">[4]</a></sup> and then at <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=11_St._Mark%27s_Terrace&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="11 St. Mark&#39;s Terrace (page does not exist)">11 St. Mark&#39;s Terrace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Venture_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venture-5">[5]</a></sup> They later moved to <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=2_Darnley_Road&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="2 Darnley Road (page does not exist)">2 Darnley Road</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Under Gordon, Tolkien began focusing on philology, and taught various courses, such as "[Old English](/wiki/Old_English) heroic verse, the history of English, various Old English and [Middle English](/wiki/Middle_English) texts, Old and Middle English philology, introductory Germanic philology, [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic), [Old Icelandic](/wiki/Norse "Norse") \[...\], and Medieval [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh)". He boasted that he played a role in developing the linguistic side of the School of English Studies which had previously been entirely devoted to literary studies. The formation of the [Viking Club](/wiki/Viking_Club), was a result of his success encouraging the study of philology and especially Old Icelandic amongst his students.<sup id="cite_ref-L7_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L7-2">[2]</a></sup>

His focus on teaching has adversely affected his research output.<sup id="cite_ref-L7_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L7-2">[2]</a></sup> He might have continued to fret on some of his hardest assignments of his time at the *OED*; several notes of his time show his added thoughts on "*walrus*" and "*walnut*" in Leeds notebooks.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The post of Reader was changed into a Professorship,<sup id="cite_ref-L46_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L46-3">[3]</a></sup> and in [1922](/wiki/1922), Tolkien was joined by [E.V. Gordon](/wiki/E.V._Gordon).<sup id="cite_ref-Venture_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venture-5">[5]</a></sup> Together, they started work on *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight](</wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight_(edition)> "Sir Gawain and the Green Knight (edition)")*, and Tolkien continued to work on his *[A Middle English Vocabulary](/wiki/A_Middle_English_Vocabulary)*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> A year later, they would be joined by [Lascelles Abercrombie](/wiki/Lascelles_Abercrombie)<sup id="cite_ref-L46_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L46-3">[3]</a></sup> and [Wilfred Rowland Childe](/wiki/Wilfred_Rowland_Childe).<sup id="cite_ref-Chronology1922_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chronology1922-9">[9]</a></sup>

W. A. Craigie resigned from the post of [Rawlinson and Bosworth Professorship of Anglo-Saxon](/wiki/Rawlinson_and_Bosworth_Professorship_of_Anglo-Saxon) at the Oxford English School, and Tolkien wrote a letter expressing his desire to return there.<sup id="cite_ref-L7_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L7-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien left Leeds [somewhat abruptly](/wiki/Letter_8 "Letter 8") in [1925](/wiki/1925) when he was elected to the Professorship, so that he actually occupied both posts simultaneously for the duration of [1926](/wiki/1926).<sup id="cite_ref-L46_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L46-3">[3]</a></sup> Reflecting on his time at Leeds in a later letter, Tolkien refuted the idea that he did not enjoy his time there:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I was devoted to the University of Leeds, which was very good to me, and to the students, whom I left with regret. The present students are among my most attentive readers...<sup id="cite_ref-L229_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L229-10">[10]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>

## Publications {#Publications}

Tolkien contributed several poems to <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Gryphon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Gryphon (page does not exist)">The Gryphon</a></i>, *[Yorkshire Poetry](/wiki/Yorkshire_Poetry)*, *[A Northern Venture](/wiki/A_Northern_Venture)* and *[Leeds University Verse](/wiki/Leeds_University_Verse_1914-24 "Leeds University Verse 1914-24")*. He also finished stories that would later become *[The Book of Lost Tales Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One)*,<sup id="cite_ref-Venture_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venture-5">[5]</a></sup> and started five poetic works as part of his mythology, but only the *[Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian)* and the *[Lay of the Children of Húrin](/wiki/Lay_of_the_Children_of_H%C3%BArin)* were more than just beginnings, the other three were [abandoned](/wiki/Poems_Early_Abandoned "Poems Early Abandoned").<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

Between [1922](/wiki/1922) and [1928](/wiki/1928) Tolkien tried to work on the "[Clarendon Chaucer](/wiki/Clarendon_Chaucer)" project with <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=George_S._Gordon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="George S. Gordon (page does not exist)">George S. Gordon</a>, in order to produce a volume of selections from the works of Geoffrey Chaucer for Oxford students. However, due to circumstances the project was left unfinished.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
