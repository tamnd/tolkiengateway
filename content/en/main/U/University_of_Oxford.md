The **University of Oxford** is the oldest university in the United Kingdom. [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) studied and taught there.

## Tolkien and Oxford {#Tolkien_and_Oxford}

### Student {#Student}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/Joseph_Wright.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/5/5e/Joseph_Wright.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="205" height="313" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="205" data-file-height="313"/></a><figcaption>Linguist <a href="../J/Joseph_Wright.md" title="Joseph Wright">Joseph Wright</a>, Tolkien&#39;s tutor at Oxford, mentor and later personal friend.</figcaption></figure>

As a student, Tolkien had founded the literary club *[Apolausticks](../A/Apolausticks.md)* ([1912](../_/1912.md)). Many members also joined the *[Chequers Clubbe](../C/Chequers_Clubbe.md)* a dining society at Exeter College founded by Tolkien and Colin Cullis in [1914](../_/1914.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Tolkien (22) was an undergraduate when the [Great War](../G/Great_War.md) broke out ([August](../A/August.md) [1914](../_/1914.md)) but he wanted to complete his studies. He joined the Officers Training Corps at Oxford.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> He had his degree in [June](../J/June.md) [1915](../_/1915.md) and applied for a commission.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxviii</sup>

Years later, Joseph Wright would write Tolkien a letter of recommendation, saying

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I have known Professor Tolkien intimately since the beginning of his undergraduate days at Oxford, and have greatly admired his keen interest in the philological study of Latin, Greek, and more especially the Germanic Languages. He regularly attended my classes and lectures for two years on Comparative Philology in general, and on Latin, Greek, and Gothic, and I formed a high opinion of his attainments in these subjects...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Letter of recommendation on June 25, 1925<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

That time Tolkien was awarded "First Class Honours degree in English Language and Literature".

### Teacher {#Teacher}

After the *[New English Dictionary](../O/Oxford_English_Dictionary.md)* and a year of tutoring students in Oxford, Tolkien decided to apply for a post at the [University of Leeds](University_of_Leeds.md). Tolkien had an interview with <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=George_S._Gordon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="George S. Gordon (page does not exist)">George S. Gordon</a>, the University's Professor of English, in June [1920](../_/1920.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Between [1922](../_/1922.md) and [1928](../_/1928.md) Tolkien tried to work on the "[Clarendon Chaucer](../C/Clarendon_Chaucer.md)" project with Gordon, in order to produce a volume of selections from the works of Geoffrey Chaucer for Oxford students. However, due to circumstances the project was left unfinished.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

After W. A. Craigie resigned from [Rawlinson and Bosworth Professorship of Anglo-Saxon](../R/Rawlinson_and_Bosworth_Professorship_of_Anglo-Saxon.md), Tolkien wrote a letter expressing his desire to return to the University of Oxford English School, giving an account of his time there as an undergraduate, tutor and assistant working on the Dictionary. Though at 33 Tolkien was fairly young for an Oxford Professorship the application was successful.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Tolkien left Leeds somewhat abruptly in [1925](../_/1925.md)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> when he was elected to the Rawlinson and Bosworth Professorship, so that he actually occupied both posts simultaneously for the duration of [1926](../_/1926.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> He continued working on the Chaucer project until he abandoned it in '28.

Tolkien was a busy professor in the Oxford English School, serving on many committees.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxiii</sup> He served in the Rawlinson and Bosworth Professorship and was a Fellow of Pembroke College.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxiv</sup>

During WW2, many of his colleagues were unavailable and he was loaded with more responsibilities, including organizing special courses for Navy and Air Force cadets.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxiii</sup>

In [1945](../_/1945.md), after the death of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cecil_Kennedy_Wyld" title="wikipedia:Henry Cecil Kennedy Wyld">Henry Wyld</a>, he was elected Professor of the Merton chair of English Language and Literature, and developed new series of lectures. Later he became a Fellow of Merton College.<sup id="cite_ref-rc_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rc-4">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:pp. lxxiii-lxxiv</sup>

Upon his retirement, Tolkien [delivered a speech](../V/Valedictory_Address_to_the_University_of_Oxford.md) on [5 June](../_/5_June.md) [1959](../_/1959.md) in the Merton College Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

Oxford kept him connected as an honored member of his college, with the honorary title of Emeritus Professor.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
