**Wilwarin** was one of the [constellations](/wiki/Constellations "Constellations") of [Arda](/wiki/Arda).

Wilwarin was created by the [Valië](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Varda](/wiki/Varda). It was set in the heavens of [Arda](/wiki/Arda) to welcome and give light to the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), who were just waking in [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Wilwarin* (*wilwarind-*, pl. *wilwarindi*) means "[butterfly](/wiki/Butterflies "Butterflies")" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya).<sup id="cite_ref-etym-wil_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-etym-wil-2">[2]</a></sup>

The spelling "Vilvarin" is also attested.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

For cognates and derivatives of the word *wilwarin* referring to the insect, please see: [butterflies#Etymology](/wiki/Butterflies#Etymology "Butterflies").

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) gave no description of the constellation, nor any counterpart in our modern-day constellations, but [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) suggested that it could be the commonly-known constellation <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiopeia_(constellation)" title="wikipedia:Cassiopeia (constellation)">Cassiopeia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Cassiopeia is a plausible candidate for being Wilwarin as its "W"-shape is a reasonable match to that of a butterfly.
