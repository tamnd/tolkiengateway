According to [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins), **were-worms** were creatures dwelling in the [Last Desert](/wiki/Last_Desert) in the [East of East](/wiki/Uttermost_East "Uttermost East").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> They were mentioned only by him. It is not known if they were an actual race or merely a part of Hobbit folklore, which mentions several mythical creatures, such as [dumbledors](/wiki/Dumbledors "Dumbledors"),<sup id="cite_ref-Errantry_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Errantry-2">[2]</a></sup> [hummerhorns](/wiki/Hummerhorns "Hummerhorns")<sup id="cite_ref-Errantry_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Errantry-2">[2]</a></sup> and [turtle-fish](/wiki/Turtle-fish "Turtle-fish").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The element "were-" (as found in "werewolf") derives from a Germanic term denoting male humans.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> A were-worm would therefore be a "man-worm".

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the earliest fragment of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, Bilbo offered to walk to the "Great Desert of Gobi and fight the Wild Wire worm of the Chinese".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Tolkien altered this in a slightly later version to "the last desert in the East" and "the Wild Wireworms of the Chinese",<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> making the word plural. In real life, the term "wireworm" denotes the larva of the click beetle, a pest of crops.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

<b>1982–97: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Were-worms are depicted as a very unusual breed of legless, wingless dragon, capable of altering their shapes. They are divided into two varieties: sand-drakes, animalistic beasts that can mimic the characteristics of objects they touch, and true were-worms, intelligent beings that use sorcery to take on the forms of other things.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Azog" title="Azog">Azog</a>&#39;s forces use giant worms, which Azog calls &#34;earth eaters&#34;, to tunnel underground towards the city of <a href="/wiki/Dale" title="Dale">Dale</a>. When the creatures appear during the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies" title="Battle of Five Armies">Battle of Five Armies</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> identifies them as &#34;were-worms&#34;. In the film, they are depicted as absolutely gigantic, with <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Weta_Digital" title="Weta Digital">Weta Digital</a> stating that they are 400 feet long and 75 feet in diameter. They are apparently used only for tunneling, not fighting, as they do not take part in the ensuing battle.</dd></dl>
