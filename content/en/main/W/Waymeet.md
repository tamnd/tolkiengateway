**Waymeet** or **Waymoot** was a town in the [Westfarthing](/wiki/Westfarthing) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"). The [East Road](/wiki/East_Road), the main road through the Shire, ran through the town on its way to [Michel Delving](/wiki/Michel_Delving). Two smaller roads also led to Waymeet: one from [Little Delving](/wiki/Little_Delving) to the north and one from [Tookland](/wiki/Tookland) and the [Southfarthing](/wiki/Southfarthing) beyond.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Where these roadways came together, a town had grown up, appropriately known as Waymeet.

## History {#History}

In the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), the ruffians who took over the Shire had a staging post or barracks in Waymeet,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and it was from here that [Sharkey's Men](/wiki/Sharkey%27s_Men) set out for the [Battle of Bywater](/wiki/Battle_of_Bywater), in which they were soundly defeated by the [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name refers to the village being at the meeting of three ways.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-3">[3]</a></sup>

The name *Waymeet* is a modernised spelling of the older form, [Waymoot](/wiki/Waymoot), which was the spelling preferred by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") in early drafts of *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King)*. Though he ultimately modernised the name within the book itself, the map of the Shire in the prologue of *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* was never corrected, and still carries the older spelling of the name.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-4">[4]</a></sup>
