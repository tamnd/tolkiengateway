**Windbeam** was the name of the great **Horn of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil)** blown only in extreme circumstances, according to one of the outlines of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* as published in *[The Treason of Isengard](/wiki/The_Treason_of_Isengard)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind-3">[3]</a></sup> Other than this outline, the horn is not mentioned in any other writing on [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup> It was, however, mentioned in the *[Letters from Father Christmas](/wiki/Letters_from_Father_Christmas)*<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup>, stating that [Father Christmas](/wiki/Father_Christmas) had not had to blow it for over four hundred years.<sup id="cite_ref-first_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When Windbeam was blown in the [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon)<sup id="cite_ref-chronology_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronology-2">[2]</a></sup>, even [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) faintly heard its sound and looked "North-west"<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind-3">[3]</a></sup> at "the dust of battle"<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind-3">[3]</a></sup> as he climbed [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) alongside [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) towards the [Sammath Naur](/wiki/Sammath_Naur)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-sauron_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sauron-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other writings {#Other_writings}

In the *[Letters from Father Christmas](/wiki/Letters_from_Father_Christmas)*, Windbeam, the great "[golden](/wiki/Gold "Gold") trumpet"<sup id="cite_ref-first_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-1">[1]</a></sup> or **Horn**, hung "over the fireplace in the hall" of [Father Christmas](/wiki/Father_Christmas)'s [Cliff House](/wiki/Cliff_House), where it was gathering dust<sup id="cite_ref-third_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-third-7">[7]</a></sup> due to not having been used for over four hundred years. The sound of Windbeam was apparently capable of traveling "as far as the North Wind" blew.<sup id="cite_ref-second_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-8">[8]</a></sup>

In early [October](/wiki/October) in the autumn of [1941](/wiki/1941), the [Goblins](/wiki/Goblins), taking advantage of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II" title="wikipedia:World War II">war</a>, sought to take over the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=North_Pole&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="North Pole (page does not exist)">North Pole</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-first_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-1">[1]</a></sup> By [November](/wiki/November), they even besieged, and had nearly taken, the Cliff House.<sup id="cite_ref-second_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-8">[8]</a></sup> As a consequence, Father Christmas blew "three blasts on" Windbeam to summon reinforcements from [snowboys](/wiki/Snow-men "Snow-men"), polar bears, and [Elves](/wiki/Elves), who all arrived three days later.<sup id="cite_ref-second_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-8">[8]</a></sup> A year after the battle, the Goblins were still recovering from their defeat and Windbeam was gathering dust again "over the mantlepiece" of Father Christmas's Cliff House.<sup id="cite_ref-third_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-third-7">[7]</a></sup>

In his commentary, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes that in both the *[Letters from Father Christmas](/wiki/Letters_from_Father_Christmas)* and the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Windbeam, called the **Great Horn** in the note, is only blown in extreme scenarios and can be heard at a great distance.<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup> He also notes that "beam" in Windbeam is related to *bēam* in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), which is derived from *bēme* ("trumpet").<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup>
