**Windbeam** was the name of the great **Horn of [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md)** blown only in extreme circumstances, according to one of the outlines of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* as published in *[The Treason of Isengard](../T/The_Treason_of_Isengard.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind-3">[3]</a></sup> Other than this outline, the horn is not mentioned in any other writing on [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup> It was, however, mentioned in the *[Letters from Father Christmas](../L/Letters_from_Father_Christmas.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup>, stating that [Father Christmas](../F/Father_Christmas.md) had not had to blow it for over four hundred years.<sup id="cite_ref-first_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When Windbeam was blown in the [Battle of the Morannon](../B/Battle_of_the_Morannon.md)<sup id="cite_ref-chronology_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronology-2">[2]</a></sup>, even [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md) faintly heard its sound and looked "North-west"<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind-3">[3]</a></sup> at "the dust of battle"<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind-3">[3]</a></sup> as he climbed [Mount Doom](../M/Mount_Doom.md) alongside [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md) towards the [Sammath Naur](../S/Sammath_Naur.md)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-sauron_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sauron-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other writings {#Other_writings}

In the *[Letters from Father Christmas](../L/Letters_from_Father_Christmas.md)*, Windbeam, the great "[golden](../G/Gold.md) trumpet"<sup id="cite_ref-first_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-1">[1]</a></sup> or **Horn**, hung "over the fireplace in the hall" of [Father Christmas](../F/Father_Christmas.md)'s [Cliff House](../C/Cliff_House.md), where it was gathering dust<sup id="cite_ref-third_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-third-7">[7]</a></sup> due to not having been used for over four hundred years. The sound of Windbeam was apparently capable of traveling "as far as the North Wind" blew.<sup id="cite_ref-second_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-8">[8]</a></sup>

In early [October](../O/October.md) in the autumn of [1941](../_/1941.md), the [Goblins](../G/Goblins.md), taking advantage of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II" title="wikipedia:World War II">war</a>, sought to take over the <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=North_Pole&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="North Pole (page does not exist)">North Pole</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-first_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-1">[1]</a></sup> By [November](../N/November.md), they even besieged, and had nearly taken, the Cliff House.<sup id="cite_ref-second_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-8">[8]</a></sup> As a consequence, Father Christmas blew "three blasts on" Windbeam to summon reinforcements from [snowboys](../S/Snow-men.md), polar bears, and [Elves](../E/Elves.md), who all arrived three days later.<sup id="cite_ref-second_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-8">[8]</a></sup> A year after the battle, the Goblins were still recovering from their defeat and Windbeam was gathering dust again "over the mantlepiece" of Father Christmas's Cliff House.<sup id="cite_ref-third_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-third-7">[7]</a></sup>

In his commentary, [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) notes that in both the *[Letters from Father Christmas](../L/Letters_from_Father_Christmas.md)* and the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), Windbeam, called the **Great Horn** in the note, is only blown in extreme scenarios and can be heard at a great distance.<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup> He also notes that "beam" in Windbeam is related to *bēam* in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md), which is derived from *bēme* ("trumpet").<sup id="cite_ref-note_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-4">[4]</a></sup>
