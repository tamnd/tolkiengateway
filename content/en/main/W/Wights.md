**Wights** was a name used for [undead](../U/Undead.md) beings,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> especially as a short form for the [Barrow-wights](../B/Barrow-wights.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*wight* is derived from [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *wiht*, meaning "living being, creature". The related <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Saxon" title="wikipedia:Old Saxon">Old Saxon</a> *wiht* means "thing, demon".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> It has been suggested that [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) had this later "*connection to the underworld in mind when he chose to refer to the grave-spirits of the Barrow-downs as 'wights'*".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Many works of fantasy fiction, role-playing games and computer and video games use the term *wight* as the name of spectral creatures very similar to Tolkien's Barrow-wights: [Dungeons and Dragons](../D/Dungeons_and_Dragons.md) has created a monster called "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wight_(Dungeons_%26_Dragons)" title="wikipedia:Wight (Dungeons &amp; Dragons)">Wight</a>", a kind of undead, and the new terminology is also exemplified in the <i><a class="external text" href="http://awoiaf.westeros.org/index.php/Wights" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">wights</a></i> of *A Song of Ice and Fire* series.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/W/Wights_(LOTRO).jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/51/Wights_%28LOTRO%29.jpg/300px-Wights_%28LOTRO%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="251" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/5/51/Wights_%28LOTRO%29.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="334"/></a><figcaption><i>Wights</i> in <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online"><i>LOTRO</i></a></figcaption></figure>

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Wights inhabit the graves of Dúnedan nobles, taking their power from a buried lord. Gaming statistics are given for minor, lesser, and major Wights.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2002-5: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Wights (not to be confused with the Barrow-wights appearing in the same game) are one of the three types of <a href="../G/Ghosts.md" title="Ghosts">ghosts</a>. Wights refer to cursed beings, who remain &#34;<i>bound to the will and goals of the one who cursed them</i>&#34; in their after-life. Unlike wraiths and phantoms, wights can possess the remains of other dead people.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007-: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Wights (crypt-wights) are categorized among the creatures called the Dead, &#34;<i>designed to be reminiscent of the wights of the great Barrow-downs in Eriador</i>&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup></dd></dl>
