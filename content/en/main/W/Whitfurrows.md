**Whitfurrows** was a village or small town that laid in the [Eastfarthing](../E/Eastfarthing.md), on the [East Road](../E/East_Road.md) through [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md), approximately midway between [Frogmorton](../F/Frogmorton.md) (about ten miles to the west) and the [Brandywine Bridge](../B/Brandywine_Bridge.md) (a similar distance to the east). It laid at the western edge of the [Bridgefields](../B/Bridgefields.md) region of the [Eastfarthing](../E/Eastfarthing.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Whitfurrows appears to have been a centre or staging-post of some kind for the [Shirriffs](../S/Shirriffs.md) during the [War of the Ring](War_of_the_Ring.md).<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="../../help/R/References.md" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Whitfurrows* contains the element *whit-*, a short form of white in personal (Whitlock) and local names (Whitley), or similarly Whitwell in the Shire (an actual English place-name). In English place-names, the colour usually refers to the colour of the soil.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

[David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) suggests an etymology from possible [Old Hobbitish](../O/Old_English.md) *Hwitfure* meaning "white furrows".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the first edition of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, the name of a place from which one of the special runners came with a "secret message" in the chapter "[The Scouring of the Shire](../T/The_Scouring_of_the_Shire.md)" was [Bamfurlong](../B/Bamfurlong.md). In the second edition of *The Lord of the Rings*, the name of this place was changed to Whitfurrows<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>, and *Bamfurlong* was inserted as the name of Farmer Maggot's land, which was unnamed in the first edition<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.
