**Wolfgang Krege** ([1 February](/wiki/1_February) [1939](/wiki/1939) — [13 April](/wiki/13_April) [2005](/wiki/2005))<sup id="cite_ref-LK_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LK-1">[1]</a></sup> was a German author and translator. He translated several of [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s works to German.

Wolfgang Krege grew up in Berlin, where he began his philosophy studies at the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin" title="wikipedia:Free University of Berlin">Free University of Berlin</a> during the early 1960s. He worked as a lexicon editor, copywriter and reader. Since [1970](/wiki/1970) he worked as a translator for numerous publishing groups, too.<sup id="cite_ref-LK_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LK-1">[1]</a></sup>

The first of Tolkien’s books Krege translated, was *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* (*Das Silmarillion* [1978](/wiki/1978)). His new translation of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* was published [1997](/wiki/1997) (*Der Hobbit oder Hin und zurück*). With his knowledge of J.R.R. Tolkien and his works, he wrote the encyclopaedia *Handbuch der Weisen von Mittelerde* in [1996](/wiki/1996) and an [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) dictionary, called *Elbisches Wörterbuch*, in [2003](/wiki/2003).

Beside his translations of Tolkien’s works, he was the translator of books by authors like <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Burgess" title="wikipedia:Anthony Burgess">Anthony Burgess</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.Annie_Proulx" title="wikipedia:E.Annie Proulx">E. Annie Proulx</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9lie_Nothomb" title="wikipedia:Amélie Nothomb">Amélie Nothomb</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Conrad" title="wikipedia:Joseph Conrad">Joseph Conrad</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Goldman" title="wikipedia:William Goldman">William Goldman</a> and many others.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In [2000](/wiki/2000) his most discussed translation was published, a new German rendering of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* (*Der Herr der Ringe*), to replace [Margaret Carroux](/wiki/Margaret_Carroux)’ earlier translation. Krege tried to render the original language styles into German equivalents and to make the translated text, in his view, more vivid and easier to understand for younger readers. This worked mostly, but major points of critism of his translation were that he used the different language styles in a too inconsistent way and that he chose the German of the early 1990s, including various slang words of this time.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Wolfgang Krege was married to the German editor and translator Roswith Krege-Mayer.<sup id="cite_ref-LK_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LK-1">[1]</a></sup>
