**William Morris** was an English artist and author, who wrote and published poetry, fiction, and translations of ancient and medieval texts throughout his life. [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) was influenced by both the artistic and literary work of Morris.

## Literary influence {#Literary_influence}

Literary works by Morris, which Tolkien explicitly stated to have had an impact on his writing, are his translation of the *[Völsunga Saga](/wiki/List_of_books_in_Tolkien%27s_library#Völsunga_Saga "List of books in Tolkien's library")*,<sup id="cite_ref-CGMorris_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGMorris-1">[1]</a></sup> and his novels *[The House of the Wolfings](/wiki/The_House_of_the_Wolfings)* and *[The Roots of the Mountains](/wiki/The_Roots_of_the_Mountains)*. Tolkien also said in an early letter to [Edith](/wiki/Edith_Tolkien "Edith Tolkien") that he tried to use some of Morris's literary techniques when writing "[The Story of Kullervo](/wiki/The_Story_of_Kullervo)".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Furthermore, scholars have deduced possible implicit influences from these and several others of Morris's works: *The Earthly Paradise* (for *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*),<sup id="cite_ref-CGMorris_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGMorris-1">[1]</a></sup> *The Roots of the Mountains* (for [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum)),<sup id="cite_ref-TS_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS-4">[4]</a></sup> *The Wood beyond the World* (for [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) and [Fangorn](/wiki/Fangorn_Forest "Fangorn Forest")),<sup id="cite_ref-TS_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TS-4">[4]</a></sup> and his translation of *The Saga of Gunnlaug the Worm-tongue* (for [Gríma Wormtongue](/wiki/Gr%C3%ADma "Gríma")).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Artistic influence {#Artistic_influence}

In several illustrations, Tolkien was clearly inspired by the decorative style found in the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_and_Crafts_Movement" title="wikipedia:Arts and Crafts Movement">Arts and Crafts Movement</a> (of which Morris was the central figure and one of the founders) and the related <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau" title="wikipedia:Art Nouveau">Art Nouveau</a>. The design philosophy of Morris was to re-introduce traditional craftsmanship by using simple forms and patterns and often medieval, romantic or folk styles of decoration (in reaction to early to the early modern industrial design), a theme which can be seen in some of Tolkien's illustrations from the late 1920s (see, e.g., '[Tol Sirion](</wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tol_Sirion_(Colored_by_H.E._Riddett).jpg> "File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tol Sirion (Colored by H.E. Riddett).jpg")'), some of his paintings for *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, the ornamental patterns drawn in later years,<sup id="cite_ref-CGMorris_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CGMorris-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> and his hand-drawn maps of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> It is known that Tolkien owned a copy of Morris' lecture *Some Hints on Pattern Designing* (1899).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
