The **Wainriders** were a people, or confederacy of [Men](../M/Men.md) from the [East](../E/East.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-1">[1]</a></sup>

They traveled in great wagons (wains) and their chieftains rode [chariots](../C/Chariots.md) in battle. In addition, they were better armed and stronger than previous [invaders from the East](../E/Easterlings.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-1">[1]</a></sup> They camped in fortified camps of wagons. Their young women were also trained in arms, and they, along with old men and youths, stayed behind, able to defend their homes from attackers.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

The Wainriders were stirred by [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) to descend upon [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and its allies, some two centuries after the time of the [Great Plague](../G/Great_Plague.md)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and remained a dire threat for nearly a hundred years.

## History {#History}

The Wainriders entered history in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1851](../T/Third_Age_1851.md) during the reign of King [Narmacil II](../N/Narmacil_II.md) of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> when they began their attack on [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> They quickly overran and scattered the [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md),<sup id="cite_ref-fourth_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fourth-6">[6]</a></sup> who were living in the plains between the forest of [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md) and the [River Running](../R/River_Running.md) with permanent homes on the eaves of the forest, especially in the [East Bight](../E/East_Bight.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1856](../T/Third_Age_1856.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-1">[1]</a></sup> King Narmacil went north to the plains south of Mirkwood with a large army and assembled as many of the scattered remnants of the Northmen as he was able to. The Wainriders defeated the army of Gondor and the Northmen and killed King Narmacil in the [Battle of the Plains](../B/Battle_of_the_Plains.md). The defeated army of Gondor retreated over the [Dagorlad](../D/Dagorlad.md) to Ithilien. Gondor had to retreat its borders to the Anduin and the [Emyn Muil](../E/Emyn_Muil.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-1">[1]</a></sup> and abandoned all its possessions east<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> of the [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) except [Ithilien](../I/Ithilien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-fourth_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fourth-6">[6]</a></sup> It is believed that the [Ringwraiths](../N/Nazgûl.md) entered [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md) at this time.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-1">[1]</a></sup> The Wainriders occupied the lands of the Northmen and enslaved most of the survivors who stayed behind; a few Northmen fled over the River Running and joined the people of [Dale](../D/Dale.md) near the [Lonely Mountain](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md), others to Gondor and some followed [Marhwini](../M/Marhwini.md) to the [Vales of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

The enslaved Northmen revolted against the Wainriders, and Narmacil's son [Calimehtar](<../C/Calimehtar_(King_of_Gondor).md>) took advantage; in [1899](../T/Third_Age_1899.md)<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> he defeated the Wainriders on the plain of [Dagorlad](../D/Dagorlad.md) winning a temporary reprieve.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

After that defeat, the Wainriders allied themselves with the [Men](../H/Haradrim.md) of [Near Harad](../N/Near_Harad.md) and [Khand](../K/Khand.md) and made a simultaneous assault on Gondor from the north and the south. In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1944](../T/Third_Age_1944.md)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> King [Ondoher](../O/Ondoher.md) was lost in the [northern battle](../D/Disaster_of_the_Morannon.md), but his general [Eärnil](../E/Eärnil_II.md) defeated the southern invaders. The victorious Wainrider army in the north, celebrating their victory over Ondoher, suddenly found themselves set upon by Eärnil. In an encounter that became known as the [Battle of the Camp](../B/Battle_of_the_Camp.md), the Wainriders were put to rout out of Ithilien, and a great part of those who fled perished in the [Dead Marshes](../D/Dead_Marshes.md).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

It was later seen that the invasions of the Wainriders, as so many of Gondor's perils, were engineered by the emissaries of Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Miryam Librán-Moreno compares the Wainriders with the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns" title="wikipedia:Huns">Huns</a> and states that the Huns were a nomadic people from Central Asia who rode in chariots providing chapter 40 (i.e. XL in roman numbers) paragraph 210 of Jordanes' *De origine actibusque Getarum* (abbreviated as *Get.* by her) as a reference for the latter statement. She also mentions that the Huns invaded the steppes north of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea" title="wikipedia:Black Sea">Black Sea</a> and defeated and enslaved the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths" title="wikipedia:Goths">Goths</a> who lived there.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes" title="wikipedia:Jordanes">Jordanes</a> was an Eastern Roman writer from the sixth century who is believed to be of Gothic descent who wrote about the History of the Goths in latin. However, Jordanes only mentioned that <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila_the_Hun" title="wikipedia:Attila the Hun">Attila the Hun</a> had the camp to which he fled during the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Catalaunian_Plains" title="wikipedia:Battle of the Catalaunian Plains">Battle of the Catalaunian Planes</a> fortified with wagons and also mentioned that the Huns fought together with various other allied peoples, including the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amali_dynasty" title="wikipedia:Amali dynasty">Ostrogoths</a> and the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gepids" title="wikipedia:Gepids">Gepidae</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) was familiar with [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md)<sup id="cite_ref-L272_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L272-16">[16]</a></sup> and latin<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> and the history of the Goths.<sup id="cite_ref-L272_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L272-16">[16]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> He also mentioned that the name [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md) (in very early German *mirkiwidu*) was used for the boundary between the Goths and the Huns<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> and used latinized Gothic names for persons from the early [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md) of [Rhovanion](../R/Rhovanion.md), such as [Vidugavia](../V/Vidugavia.md) and [Vidumavi](../V/Vidumavi.md).<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

[Andreas Moehn](../A/Andreas_Moehn.md) notes that some ancient peoples traveled in wagons, such as the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamaxobii" title="wikipedia:Hamaxobii">Hamaxobioi</a></i> from Greek sources, or the <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians" title="wikipedia:Scythians">Scythians</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>
