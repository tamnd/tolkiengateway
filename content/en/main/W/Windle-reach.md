**Windle-reach** was a lower part of the [Withywindle](/wiki/Withywindle) river beginning at the [Withy-weir](/wiki/Withy-weir) straight up to the [Grindwall](/wiki/Grindwall).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reach_(geography)" title="wikipedia:Reach (geography)">reach</a>" can refer to any length of a river or to a level stretch between locks in a canal, but especially to "a straight portion of a stream or river, as from one turn to another"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> which is here the most probable explanation, as it gives a word-play combining *windle* 'winding' and *reach* 'straight'.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
