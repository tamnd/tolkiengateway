**Weathertop**, known in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) as **Amon Sûl**, was the southernmost top of the [Weather Hills](/wiki/Weather_Hills). It stood a little away from the others, and its conical top was flattened.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-1">[1]</a></sup>

Of old, it formed the boundary between [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain) and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur).

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

The Weather Hills lay at the centre of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor), near the [East Road](/wiki/East_Road) that split [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

A tower had stood on the hill since the days of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil), and he had stored the *[palantír](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri "Palantíri")* in its chambers. It is said that before the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), Elendil stood at the hill watching the hosts of [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) coming to join his forces.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-1">[1]</a></sup>

After King [Eärendur](</wiki/E%C3%A4rendur_(King_of_Arnor)> "Eärendur (King of Arnor)") passed and the kingdom was split in three, all of his sons coveted the hill, or rather the [Stone](/wiki/Amon_S%C3%BBl-stone "Amon Sûl-stone") inside. It lay on the exact tri-state point, but eventually passed to [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain), the realm of [Amlaith](/wiki/Amlaith), the eldest son of Eärendur, although it already possessed the [other](/wiki/Elostirion-stone "Elostirion-stone") [two](/wiki/Ann%C3%BAminas-stone "Annúminas-stone") Stones.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Destruction {#Destruction}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/da/Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png/300px-Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="171" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/da/Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png/450px-Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/da/Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png/600px-Fall_of_Amon_Sul_RotWK.png 2x" data-file-width="615" data-file-height="350"/></a><figcaption>The Fall of Amon Sûl in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i></figcaption></figure>

In the days of [Argeleb](/wiki/Argeleb_I "Argeleb I"), the royal lines of [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur) had failed. Argeleb therefore claimed both realms as his own, hoping to reunite [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor), but the claim was resisted by Rhudaur, where the Dúnedain had dwindled and the land was now largely ruled by hillmen, who were driven by the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar).

As tensions with Angmar increased, Argeleb fortified his border. A great bastion arose on Weathertop, and the East Road and lower Hoarwell were fortified also. For nigh half a century, the line held, though Argeleb lost his life in battle with the Hillmen. The Witch-king did not trouble the Men, and had sent his troops further East, to the [Second Siege of Imladris](/wiki/Second_Siege_of_Imladris). But in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1409](/wiki/Third_Age_1409 "Third Age 1409"), he came forth with such a force from Angmar that no army could stop him. He [razed](/wiki/Fall_of_Amon_S%C3%BBl "Fall of Amon Sûl") Amon Sûl and brought its mighty walls down.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup> [Arveleg](/wiki/Arveleg_I "Arveleg I"), son of Argeleb, was slain,<sup id="cite_ref-TA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-4">[4]</a></sup> as well as the [last prince of Cardolan](/wiki/Last_prince_of_Cardolan "Last prince of Cardolan"),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> but the Lord of the Nazgûl failed to retrieve the *palantír*. It was secured and brought back to [Fornost Erain](/wiki/Fornost_Erain).<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup>

### In ruins {#In_ruins}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/8/89/Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg/300px-Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="207" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/8/89/Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg/450px-Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/8/89/Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg/600px-Alan_Lee_-_Weathertop.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1103"/></a><figcaption><i>Weathertop</i> by <a href="/wiki/Alan_Lee" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

For many centuries, the once great fortress stood in ruins on the hill. The locals paid little heed to it. It faded back into obscurity, and it was not until [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018") the hill reappeared in the chronicles and annals. In early October, it was the scene of two fights involving the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl); one with [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) on [October 3](/wiki/3_October "3 October") and one with [the Ring-bearer](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") [three days later](/wiki/6_October "6 October").<sup id="cite_ref-Chief_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chief-6">[6]</a></sup> Gandalf on [Shadowfax](/wiki/Shadowfax) was secretly chased by the Ringwraiths until the night of October 3, when six<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> or nine<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Black Riders, led by the [Witch King](/wiki/Witch_King), attacked him. They were repulsed after a violent fight which burnt the hilltop, and [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo) and [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) saw the lights of that battle from their camp at the [Midgewater Marshes](/wiki/Midgewater_Marshes).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> The Witch King and [Khamûl](/wiki/Kham%C3%BBl) remained watching Weathertop (waiting for the Ring Bearer to come) for two days thereafter, along with three other Wraiths.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> After fleeing from Bree, [Strider](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") and the [Hobbits](/wiki/Travellers "Travellers") avoided the main road and approached Weathertop from the north. At the top they discovered a cairn with a message from Gandalf, and spotted the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl") approaching in the distance along the road. That night, the Ringwraiths attacked their camp in a dell below the summit, stabbing [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") with a [Morgul-knife](/wiki/Morgul-knife), but were driven off by the others.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-1">[1]</a></sup>

<b>1978: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Aragorn and the hobbits do not actually enter the ruins of Weathertop. However, moments before the attack of the Ringwraiths, a tall tower can be seen in the background.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The fight with the Nazgûl has been moved from the camp near the hill to the ruins of the tower.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Weathertop is the fifth level. Aragorn has to take the four Hobbits to the hilltop, battling wolves and Orcs on the way there. At the top, they notice Gandalf had been there, and dropped a rune stone. As he explains it to Frodo, a troll attacks, and serves as the level&#39;s boss.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Weathertop is the second level. Aragorn must drive the Nazgûl away before they kill Frodo.</dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II">The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Destroying Amon Sûl is the fourth level in the <i>Angmar</i> campaign in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II:_The_Rise_of_the_Witch-king" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king">The Rise of the Witch-king</a></i>. The bastion is protected by five mystical Mallorn trees: one generates a solar flare that incinerates enemies, one heals damaged buildings, one creates lush Elvish pastures (that boost the Men&#39;s armor and damage), one stuns targeted enemies and one summons a battalion of archers. The mission is completed when either all five mallorns are destroyed, or the tower falls.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Additionally, two maps of Amon Sûl are available in Skirmish mode: one with the tower, which is identical to the mission save for the Mallorn trees, and one with the tower in ruins and all other buildings removed.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Weathertop is the most prominent landmark of the <a href="/wiki/Lone-lands" title="Lone-lands">Lone-lands</a> region. After the passing of <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a> and the Hobbits, the Ranger Candaith sets up a camp at its foothold, the player later helps him defend the summit of Weathertop against two enemy assaults.</dd></dl>

<b>2012: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Weathertop is the meeting place between <a href="/wiki/Azog" title="Azog">Azog</a> and his lieutenants. Azog kills <a href="/wiki/Yazneg" title="Yazneg">Yazneg</a> for his failue to capture or kill <a href="/wiki/Thorin_and_Company" title="Thorin and Company">Thorin and Company</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game)" title="The One Ring (role-playing game)">The One Ring</a> — <a href="/wiki/Ruins_of_the_Lost_Realm" title="Ruins of the Lost Realm">Ruins of the Lost Realm</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Before the arrival of the Númenóreans, the hill was a religious site of the Middle Men, who left offerings there. Just south of the hill were the ruined pillars of a massive stone arch featuring worn carvings of Elendil and Isildur. This arch was the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/omphalos" title="wikipedia:omphalos">omphalos</a> of Arnor, the centre point from which all distances were measured.<sup id="cite_ref-Weathertop_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weathertop-12">[12]</a></sup></dd></dl>
