**Sir William Alexander Craigie** ([1867](/wiki/1867) – [1957](/wiki/1957)) was a philologist, lexicographer and [Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") professor. He was [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s immediate predecessor as [Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon](/wiki/Rawlinson_and_Bosworth_Professor_of_Anglo-Saxon) from [1916](/wiki/1916) to [1925](/wiki/1925).

## Biography {#Biography}

William Craigie was born in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee" title="wikipedia:Dundee">Dundee, Scotland</a> and as a child developed a love for languages, particularly Scottish Gaelic. He enrolled at the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews" title="wikipedia:University of St Andrews">University of St Andrews</a> where he studied German, French, Danish and Icelandic. He was awarded a scholarship to study at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford" title="wikipedia:Balliol College, Oxford">Balliol College, Oxford</a> where he began learning Celtic.<sup id="cite_ref-musa_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-musa-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [1897](/wiki/1897), Craigie was the third editor hired to work on the *[Oxford English Dictionary](/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary)*.<sup id="cite_ref-musa_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-musa-1">[1]</a></sup> He worked on N, Q, R, U, and V, Si–Sq, and Wo–Wy;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[note 1]</a></sup> in total his work accounting for one-fifth of the original dictionary. He was Taylorian lecturer in the Scandinavian languages in [1904](/wiki/1904) before becoming Rawlinson and Bosworth professor of Anglo-Saxon in 1916 until his resignation in 1925.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> After Craigie's resignation Tolkien applied for,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup> and held the position until [1945](/wiki/1945).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>
