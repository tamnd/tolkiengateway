**Westron** (<i><b>Adûni</b></i> in itself) was the [language](../L/Languages.md) of the [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md) of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md). By the end of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) it was more or less a universal language spoken throughout the [races](../R/Races.md) and peoples of the [Westlands](Westlands.md), thus it was also known as the **Common Speech**.

Westron, through its ancestor [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md), was distantly related to other native languages of the [Middle Men](../M/Middle_Men.md) of the Westlands, like [Northern Mannish](../N/Northern_Mannish.md) of [Rhovanion](../R/Rhovanion.md), and [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md); all descended from the [ancient languages](../T/Taliska.md) of the [Edain](../E/Edain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/300px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/450px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/600px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4371" data-file-height="2126"/></a><figcaption>Genealogy of Middle-Earth&#39;s human languages</figcaption></figure>

The Westron speech is derived from the [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) tongue of [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-L144_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Númenoreans](../N/Númenoreans.md) brought it to the western coastlands of the continent of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) when they established trade outposts and forts in the third millennium of the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md). Westron originated as a creole language spoken between the Númenoreans and the native [Middle Men](../M/Middle_Men.md), and from those Númenorean colonies it spread to most of the westlands.

After the [Downfall of Númenor](../D/Downfall_of_Númenor.md), the [Faithful](../F/Faithful.md) Númenoreans neglected their 'unfaithful' language in favour of [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md), allowing Adûnaic as spoken in Middle-earth to change and evolve chaotically among the Middle Men. But later it was enriched and softened under Elvish influence.

### Distribution {#Distribution}

In the course of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) Westron was adopted by nearly all [Men](../M/Men.md) and [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) that lived within the borders of the old kingdoms of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) and [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) all along the coasts from [Umbar](../U/Umbar.md) northward to the Bay of Forochel, and inland to the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) and the [Ephel Dúath](../E/Ephel_Dúath.md) and north up the river [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) to the [Gladden Fields](../G/Gladden_Fields.md) between the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) and the Anduin. At the time of the [War of the Ring](War_of_the_Ring.md) Westron was still the native tongue in this area.<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) itself was a rustic dialect of Westron.

For this, Westron was also known as the **Common Speech**. [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) used Westron as a public language, as [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md) was private. Some [Elves](../E/Elves.md) also spoke Westron, although some, like [Haldir](../H/Haldir.md)'s brothers, didn't leave their lands much and had not learned the Common Speech.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Even [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) spoke Westron, which was the base of [Orkish](../O/Orkish.md) tongues.<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

The [Northmen](../N/Northmen.md) also spoke Westron, such as [Beorn](../B/Beorn.md) and the [Lake-men](../L/Lake-men.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The [Woses](Woses.md),<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> the [Dunlendings](../D/Dunlendings.md)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) were examples of Men whose language was not Westron, but they spoke it in interlingual circumstances.<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup>

Under King [Thengel](../T/Thengel.md) of Rohan (who had lived in Gondor for many years before taking the throne), the Common Speech began to be used as the language of the court instead of their native [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md) (thus [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md), [Éomer](../_/Éomer.md), and [Éowyn](../_/Éowyn.md) were all functionally bilingual in Rohirric and Common Speech) "though not all thought this a good thing..."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Grammar {#Grammar}

Westron had both <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-V_distinction" title="wikipedia:T-V distinction">&#34;deferential&#34; and &#34;familiar&#34; pronouns</a> for the second (and sometimes third) person, but the deferential pronouns had fallen out of use in the [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) dialect. Interestingly, this is the opposite of what happened in English, where the familiar "thee/thou" fell out of favour and the deferential "you" became universal.

This lack of a deferential pronoun and universal use of the familiar pronoun is what [Gondorians](../G/Gondorians.md) were referring to when they repeatedly remarked that Hobbit-speech sounded strange.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Etymology and names {#Etymology_and_names}

Westron is a translation of the original name <i><b>Adûni</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> (cf. [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) *[Adûn](../A/Adûn.md)* ("west")), and "Common Speech" translates the Westron term <b><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=S%C3%B4val&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sôval (page does not exist)">Sôval</a> <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ph%C3%A2r%C3%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Phârë (page does not exist)">Phârë</a></b><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>, of identical meaning. In [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) the language was called **[Annúnaid](../A/Annúnaid.md)** (Westron), or **[Falathren](../F/Falathren.md)** (Shore-language).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

According to Tolkien's fiction, Westron was the language spoken and understood by the protagonists of *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-L144_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-3">[3]</a></sup> Many names of characters and places, in the book's "reality", were in Westron.

However, Tolkien mentions that Westron was presented as having been completely replaced by [Modern English](../M/Modern_English.md) in the text. This had certain important implications: first of all, proper names with derivations understandable or evident to speakers of Westron had been translated, to preserve the effect to the English reader. Thus, names like *Baggins*, *Bagshot Row*, *Peregrin*, *Rivendell* et cetera, are not the *actual* names as spoken by the characters but are presented as *translations*.

Of course, outside the fictional context of the story, it is clear that there was no such "translation": the English names came first and the "original" forms in Westron or other languages were devised by Tolkien later.

[Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md) ("cloven valley") was actually called *[Karningul](../R/Rivendell.md#Etymology)*, and [Bag End](../B/Bag_End.md) was actually called <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Labin-nec&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Labin-nec (page does not exist)">Labin-nec</a></i>, after *[Labingi](../B/Baggins_Family~66343.md)*, the real form of [Baggins](../B/Baggins_Family~66343.md). In some cases the explanations became quite involved, such as the river Brandywine (Sindarin [Baranduin](../B/Baranduin.md), "golden-brown river") being actually called *[Branda](../B/Branda.md)-[nîn](../N/Nîn.md)*, a punning Westron name meaning "border-water", which was later punned again as <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Bralda&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Bralda (page does not exist)">Bralda</a>-<a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=H%C3%AEm&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Hîm (page does not exist)">hîm</a></i> meaning "heady ale".

This logic went one step further by also presenting all [Mannish](../M/Mannish.md) languages akin to Westron in languages related to English, so that their "understandability" by the protagonists would be simulated to the English reader.<sup id="cite_ref-L144_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-3">[3]</a></sup>

This utter replacement of Westron by English was taken so far that some sources that should give actual Westron have been turned to English too. For instance, in [Moria](../M/Moria.md), an illustration of the runic text on [Balin](../B/Balin.md)'s gravestone is given. The text is said to be written in both Khuzdul and Westron. But while the first part of the inscription seems to really be a bit of Khuzdul, the second part is actually plain English, just written in [cirth](../C/Cirth.md).

## Corpus {#Corpus}

The corpus of Westron is small; several of the Westron forms given above were not published in Tolkien's lifetime. Tolkien never worked out Westron to the same extent as [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) or even [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md).

Many words come from [Appendix F](../A/Appendix_F.md) and the creation of it in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](../T/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth.md)*. A detailed Westron phonology and additional grammar notes can be found in the manuscripts that Tolkien sold to the archives of [Marquette University](../M/Marquette_University.md) (they were initially written for the [Appendix F](../A/Appendix_F.md) of *The Lord of the Rings*)<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>. These documents include declensions of animate and inanimate nouns and the present tense conjugation of the verb *pūt-* “to blow”<sup id="cite_ref-:0_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-16">[16]</a></sup>; their content was partially published in [*Tyalië Tyelelliéva* 17](../T/Tyalië_Tyelelliéva_17.md) in [2001](../_/2001.md). Even now, the corpus is small (roughly 150 words).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>
