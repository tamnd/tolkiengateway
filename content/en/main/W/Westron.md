**Westron** (<i><b>Adûni</b></i> in itself) was the [language](/wiki/Languages "Languages") of the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). By the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) it was more or less a universal language spoken throughout the [races](/wiki/Races "Races") and peoples of the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands), thus it was also known as the **Common Speech**.

Westron, through its ancestor [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), was distantly related to other native languages of the [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men) of the Westlands, like [Northern Mannish](/wiki/Northern_Mannish) of [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion), and [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese); all descended from the [ancient languages](/wiki/Taliska "Taliska") of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain).<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/300px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/450px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/3/39/Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg/600px-Tree_of_human_tongues_expanded.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4371" data-file-height="2126"/></a><figcaption>Genealogy of Middle-Earth&#39;s human languages</figcaption></figure>

The Westron speech is derived from the [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) tongue of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-L144_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Númenoreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenoreans) brought it to the western coastlands of the continent of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) when they established trade outposts and forts in the third millennium of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age). Westron originated as a creole language spoken between the Númenoreans and the native [Middle Men](/wiki/Middle_Men), and from those Númenorean colonies it spread to most of the westlands.

After the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor), the [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful) Númenoreans neglected their 'unfaithful' language in favour of [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish), allowing Adûnaic as spoken in Middle-earth to change and evolve chaotically among the Middle Men. But later it was enriched and softened under Elvish influence.

### Distribution {#Distribution}

In the course of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) Westron was adopted by nearly all [Men](/wiki/Men) and [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) that lived within the borders of the old kingdoms of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) all along the coasts from [Umbar](/wiki/Umbar) northward to the Bay of Forochel, and inland to the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath) and north up the river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) to the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields) between the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and the Anduin. At the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) Westron was still the native tongue in this area.<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) itself was a rustic dialect of Westron.

For this, Westron was also known as the **Common Speech**. [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) used Westron as a public language, as [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) was private. Some [Elves](/wiki/Elves) also spoke Westron, although some, like [Haldir](/wiki/Haldir)'s brothers, didn't leave their lands much and had not learned the Common Speech.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Even [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) spoke Westron, which was the base of [Orkish](/wiki/Orkish) tongues.<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

The [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen) also spoke Westron, such as [Beorn](/wiki/Beorn) and the [Lake-men](/wiki/Lake-men).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The [Woses](/wiki/Woses),<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> the [Dunlendings](/wiki/Dunlendings)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) were examples of Men whose language was not Westron, but they spoke it in interlingual circumstances.<sup id="cite_ref-lang_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lang-1">[1]</a></sup>

Under King [Thengel](/wiki/Thengel) of Rohan (who had lived in Gondor for many years before taking the throne), the Common Speech began to be used as the language of the court instead of their native [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) (thus [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden), [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer), and [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn) were all functionally bilingual in Rohirric and Common Speech) "though not all thought this a good thing..."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Grammar {#Grammar}

Westron had both <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-V_distinction" title="wikipedia:T-V distinction">&#34;deferential&#34; and &#34;familiar&#34; pronouns</a> for the second (and sometimes third) person, but the deferential pronouns had fallen out of use in the [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) dialect. Interestingly, this is the opposite of what happened in English, where the familiar "thee/thou" fell out of favour and the deferential "you" became universal.

This lack of a deferential pronoun and universal use of the familiar pronoun is what [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) were referring to when they repeatedly remarked that Hobbit-speech sounded strange.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Etymology and names {#Etymology_and_names}

Westron is a translation of the original name <i><b>Adûni</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> (cf. [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) *[Adûn](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBn)* ("west")), and "Common Speech" translates the Westron term <b><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=S%C3%B4val&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sôval (page does not exist)">Sôval</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ph%C3%A2r%C3%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Phârë (page does not exist)">Phârë</a></b><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>, of identical meaning. In [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) the language was called **[Annúnaid](/wiki/Ann%C3%BAnaid)** (Westron), or **[Falathren](/wiki/Falathren)** (Shore-language).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

According to Tolkien's fiction, Westron was the language spoken and understood by the protagonists of *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-L144_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-3">[3]</a></sup> Many names of characters and places, in the book's "reality", were in Westron.

However, Tolkien mentions that Westron was presented as having been completely replaced by [Modern English](/wiki/Modern_English) in the text. This had certain important implications: first of all, proper names with derivations understandable or evident to speakers of Westron had been translated, to preserve the effect to the English reader. Thus, names like *Baggins*, *Bagshot Row*, *Peregrin*, *Rivendell* et cetera, are not the *actual* names as spoken by the characters but are presented as *translations*.

Of course, outside the fictional context of the story, it is clear that there was no such "translation": the English names came first and the "original" forms in Westron or other languages were devised by Tolkien later.

[Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) ("cloven valley") was actually called *[Karningul](/wiki/Rivendell#Etymology "Rivendell")*, and [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) was actually called <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Labin-nec&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Labin-nec (page does not exist)">Labin-nec</a></i>, after *[Labingi](/wiki/Baggins_Family "Baggins Family")*, the real form of [Baggins](/wiki/Baggins_Family "Baggins Family"). In some cases the explanations became quite involved, such as the river Brandywine (Sindarin [Baranduin](/wiki/Baranduin), "golden-brown river") being actually called *[Branda](/wiki/Branda)-[nîn](/wiki/N%C3%AEn "Nîn")*, a punning Westron name meaning "border-water", which was later punned again as <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Bralda&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Bralda (page does not exist)">Bralda</a>-<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=H%C3%AEm&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Hîm (page does not exist)">hîm</a></i> meaning "heady ale".

This logic went one step further by also presenting all [Mannish](/wiki/Mannish) languages akin to Westron in languages related to English, so that their "understandability" by the protagonists would be simulated to the English reader.<sup id="cite_ref-L144_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-3">[3]</a></sup>

This utter replacement of Westron by English was taken so far that some sources that should give actual Westron have been turned to English too. For instance, in [Moria](/wiki/Moria), an illustration of the runic text on [Balin](/wiki/Balin)'s gravestone is given. The text is said to be written in both Khuzdul and Westron. But while the first part of the inscription seems to really be a bit of Khuzdul, the second part is actually plain English, just written in [cirth](/wiki/Cirth "Cirth").

## Corpus {#Corpus}

The corpus of Westron is small; several of the Westron forms given above were not published in Tolkien's lifetime. Tolkien never worked out Westron to the same extent as [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) or even [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic).

Many words come from [Appendix F](/wiki/Appendix_F) and the creation of it in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*. A detailed Westron phonology and additional grammar notes can be found in the manuscripts that Tolkien sold to the archives of [Marquette University](/wiki/Marquette_University) (they were initially written for the [Appendix F](/wiki/Appendix_F) of *The Lord of the Rings*)<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>. These documents include declensions of animate and inanimate nouns and the present tense conjugation of the verb *pūt-* “to blow”<sup id="cite_ref-:0_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-16">[16]</a></sup>; their content was partially published in [*Tyalië Tyelelliéva* 17](/wiki/Tyali%C3%AB_Tyelelli%C3%A9va_17 "Tyalië Tyelelliéva 17") in [2001](/wiki/2001). Even now, the corpus is small (roughly 150 words).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>
