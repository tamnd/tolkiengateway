**Water-lilies** were water flowers known for their round, flat leaves floating on the surfaces of ponds and quiet rivers. Their flowers can be of many colours, but [Goldberry](/wiki/Goldberry) the [River-daughter](/wiki/River-daughter) seems to have had a particular interest in white lilies. [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil) travelled to the lower reaches of the [Withywindle](/wiki/Withywindle) to gather white water-lilies for her, and it was while returning from a lily-gathering expedition that he discovered [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and his companions, and rescued them from [Old Man Willow](/wiki/Old_Man_Willow).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Goldberry seems to have used her lilies to recreate her original home in the river: when Tom brought the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) back to his house, they found a seated Goldberry surrounded by water-lilies floating in pots of earthenware.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Like many other types of plant and flower, water-lilies were also known to grow in the verdant lands of [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) by the River [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin). Long after their adventure with Tom and Goldberry, Frodo and [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") found their broad leaves floating in a quiet stream running down to the Great River.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the poem *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](</wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil_(poem)> "The Adventures of Tom Bombadil (poem)")* it appears that Tom first met Goldberry when she pulled him by his beard under water-lilies in the Withywindle River.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
