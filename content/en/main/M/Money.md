**Money**, specifically minted coin money, was used in the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands) as early as the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).

## History {#History}

In [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), the chief currency was the [Castar](/wiki/Castar); a small silver coin known as the [Tharni](/wiki/Tharni) was worth one-fourth of a Castar.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Azog](/wiki/Azog) threw a money purse at [Nár](/wiki/N%C3%A1r) containing "few coins of little worth" (which the Dwarves later stuffed in the mouth of Azog's decapitated head after his defeat).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

When [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) rushed from his door to meet [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin) and his party of dwarves at the [Green Dragon Inn](/wiki/Green_Dragon "Green Dragon"), he found himself outside without "a hat, a walking stick, or any *money*, or anything that he usually took with him when he went out".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

After the death of [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) and the destruction of [Esgaroth](/wiki/Esgaroth) the people were angry at the [Master of Lake-town](/wiki/Master_of_Lake-town) and declared, "We have had enough of the old men and the *money-counters*!" Others took up the cry of "Up with the Bowman (meaning [Bard](/wiki/Bard)) and down with *Moneybags*".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Much of the gold and silver wealth of Dwarf-horde of Smaug appears to have been minted. [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) took home two chests from the horde, one containing gold and the other silver,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and as a result (in addition to his family's pre-existing wealth) lived very comfortably for the next 50 years and was considered quite rich. In addition to the [Hobbits of Bree](/wiki/Hobbits_of_Bree), the [Hobbits of the Shire](/wiki/Hobbits_of_the_Shire) apparently dealt in coin money as well, otherwise they might not have been so eager to search [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) after Bilbo left, looking for secret treasure rooms.

Money was mentioned in relation to the trading town of [Bree](/wiki/Bree). At the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), a pony was considered to be worth about four [silver pennies](/wiki/Silver_pennies "Silver pennies"). The well-to-do owner of the [Prancing Pony](/wiki/Prancing_Pony), [Barliman Butterbur](/wiki/Barliman_Butterbur), considered a loss of 30 silver pennies to be a considerable blow.<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-6">[6]</a></sup> A gold piece was regarded as a particularly extravagant reward for good news.

When [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) and the four hobbits were camped on the side of [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop), [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee") wandered to the edge of the dell. Soon he came running back in fear and said, "I durstn't go outside this dell for any *money*."<sup id="cite_ref-Knife_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knife-6">[6]</a></sup>

The above phrase suggests that (in the early days of [Tolkien fandom](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom)) there was some belief that coin money used as actual "currency" did not exist in Middle-earth; references to money are scarce, not highlighted and obviously forgettable among the narrative.
