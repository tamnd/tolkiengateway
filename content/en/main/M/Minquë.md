**minquë** is the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word for the number eleven.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The origin of the element "-quë", seen in *[enquë](/wiki/Enqu%C3%AB "Enquë")* (six), *minquë* (eleven), and *[yunquë](/wiki/Yunqu%C3%AB "Yunquë")* (twelve) puzzled early investigators. [Pengolodh](/wiki/Pengolodh), however, pointed out that *minquë* was the key; it evidently an early word for the start (first) of a new decade and therefore meant something like "*min* again".<sup id="cite_ref-vt48_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt48-2">[2]</a></sup>

It was further proposed by Pengolodh that it based on the [Common Eldarin](/wiki/Common_Eldarin) stem <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=KEWE&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="KEWE (page does not exist)">KEWE</a> ("new, fresh"; "anew, repeated"), which had the forms \[<i>*kwē</i> &gt;\] *(e)kwē̆* ("afresh, anew") or *keu-* (cf. Q. *keure* ("renewed")). The CE *min(i)k(e)we* was likely the common ancestor of [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *minib*, [Telerin](/wiki/Telerin) *minipe*, and the Quenya \[*minkwe* &gt;\] *minquë*.<sup id="cite_ref-vt48_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt48-2">[2]</a></sup>

*Minquë* then influenced its neighbour *yū(e)neke*, which originally meant "double six" (*yū-* ("both", "twice") + *eneke* ("six")),<sup id="cite_ref-vt48_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt48-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:10</sup> but adopted the element *-que* and became *yunquë*.<sup id="cite_ref-vt48_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt48-2">[2]</a></sup> This system was so strong that it ended up changed the word for six as well (*eneke*, *enke* &gt; *enquë*).<sup id="cite_ref-vt48_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt48-2">[2]</a></sup>
