The **Menelmacar** ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin). "Menelvagor"), meaning the **Swordsman of the Sky**,<sup id="cite_ref-FR|I3_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FR|I3-1">[1]</a></sup> is one of the [constellations](/wiki/Constellations "Constellations") set in the heavens by [Varda](/wiki/Varda) to enlighten the awakening of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves). It was gathered by Varda from among the ancient stars, set as a foreboding of the [Last Battle](/wiki/Last_Battle).<sup id="cite_ref-silm_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silm-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

[Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") and his companions were spending their night with the Elves in the woods of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"); the Elves burst into song as Menelvagor, the [Swordsman of the Sky](/wiki/Swordsman_of_the_Sky), climbed in the sky, and started the time for dinner, speech and merriment.<sup id="cite_ref-FR|I3_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FR|I3-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The elements of the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name are: *[menel](/wiki/Menel "Menel")*, "the heavens" and *macar* "swordsman", therefore "Heaven-Swordsman".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The name Menelvagor is a direct calque of the constellation's [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name Menelmacar, "Swordsman of the Sky".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

Menelmacar is called **Menelmakil** in a note to Tolkien’s essay [Quendi and Eldar](/wiki/Quendi_and_Eldar)<sup id="cite_ref-qu_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-qu-6">[6]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:359–424</sup> on the origin of the Elvish names for Elves: ' *Telumehtar* 'warrior of the sky', an older name for *Menelmakil*, Orion'<sup id="cite_ref-qu_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-qu-6">[6]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:411, n.15</sup>

In [The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> the word *makil* is translated as 'sword', whereas *mahtar* is translated as 'warrior' ( &gt; -*mehtar* in *Telumehtar*; -*makar* in *Menelmacar* ). Therefore, the full word means "Heaven-sword". It is possible that the spelling of *Menelmakil* may be just an error.

**Telumehtar** ([Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) "Warrior of the Sky") is another name of the constellation Menelmacar. Behind this name lies an early story about a [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") named [Telimektar](/wiki/Telimektar), son of [Tulkas](/wiki/Tulkas), who apparently was converted into the constellation of Orion.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Apart from the story of Telimektar there are two mentions of Telumehtar the Constellation:

1. The name is quoted as an example of the pronunciation of the consonant /H/ with a reference to Orion, explained in a footnote: "usually called in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) Menelvagor, --- Q\[uenya\] Menelmacar".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
2. With the translation "*Warrior of the Sky*", as an older name for Menelmakil, Orion.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

The name Menelvagor meaning "Swordsman of the Sky" is also used.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The constellation Menelvagor entered into the story only in the latest phases of its composition. In earlier versions of the manuscript of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* the Elves begun to sing as 'the yellow moon rose',<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> and in a later revision 'in the East the thin silver rind of the New Moon appeared'<sup id="cite_ref-HM|RS_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HM|RS-12">[12]</a></sup> due to Tolkien's calculations considering the phases of the Moon;<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> still there was a fault: the Moon would not have risen from the East at that time of the year;<sup id="cite_ref-HM|RS_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HM|RS-12">[12]</a></sup> thus the Moon was finally changed to the apparition of Menelvagor and other [stars](/wiki/Stars "Stars").

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Menelmacar is identified in our world as the constellation <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_(constellation)" title="wikipedia:Orion (constellation)">Orion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
