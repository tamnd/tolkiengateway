**Meduseld**, also known as the **Golden Hall** was the great<sup id="cite_ref-King1_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King1-1">[1]</a></sup> royal hall of the [Kings of Rohan](../K/King_of_Rohan.md)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> that stood on a terrace on the top of the hill<sup id="cite_ref-King2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King2-3">[3]</a></sup> on which [Edoras](../E/Edoras.md), the capital city of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md) was built.<sup id="cite_ref-King1_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King1-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Description {#Description}

On the top of the hill broad high stairs led up from a green terrace to a wide paved terrace on which Meduseld stood. On both sides of the topmost step of the stair were seats made of stone on which guards set.<sup id="cite_ref-King2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King2-3">[3]</a></sup>

At the nothern end of Meduseld were doors on great hinges, which were locked with heavy bars. The hall was a long and wide hall with great painted carved pillars that supported its high roof,<sup id="cite_ref-King2_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King2-3">[3]</a></sup> which appeared to be thatched with gold when seen from far off.<sup id="cite_ref-King1_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King1-1">[1]</a></sup> A louver in the roof let in light and allowed smoke get out. There were windows high under the eaves on the eastern side of the hall. The floor of the hall was paved with a mosaic of stones in many colours that contained intertwined branching runes and strange devices. Its walls were richly decorated with woven tapestries depicting figures from the history and legends of the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md), including [Eorl](../E/Eorl.md) on his horse riding to the [Battle of the Field of Celebrant](../B/Battle_of_the_Field_of_Celebrant.md). In the middle of the hall was a long hearth with a wood-fire. At the southern end of the hall three steps lep up to a raised dais with a great golden chair on which the King of Rohan sat facing the doors.<sup id="cite_ref-King2_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King2-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After the [Éothéod](../_/Éothéod.md) had settled in [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md), the [Second King of Rohan](../K/Kings_of_Rohan.md), [Brego](../B/Brego.md), the son of [Eorl](../E/Eorl.md), built Meduseld on top of the hill of Edoras. The hall was completed in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2569](../T/Third_Age_2569.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> During the feast that was held by Brego to hallow the newly built hall [Baldor](../B/Baldor.md),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> his first son,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> vowed to walk the [Paths of the Dead](../P/Paths_of_the_Dead.md). Baldor entered the Paths of the Dead in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2570](../T/Third_Age_2570.md),<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> but never returned and his father died of grief in the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2758](../T/Third_Age_2758.md)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> the [Dunlending](../D/Dunlendings.md) leader, [Wulf](../W/Wulf.md), took Edoras and Meduseld and proclaimed himself king. [Helm Hammerhand](../H/Helm_Hammerhand.md)'s son [Haleth](<../H/Haleth_(son_of_Helm).md>) fell defending the doors of the great hall. Wulf was eventually overthrown by [Fréaláf](../F/Fréaláf.md), son of [Hild](../H/Hild.md), in [2759](../T/Third_Age_2759.md).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In the late [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), Meduseld was the home of King [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

On [20 September](../_/20_September.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> the wizard [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) came to Meduseld and warned king Théoden that the wizard [Saruman](../S/Saruman.md) had gathered wolves and [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) in [Isengard](../I/Isengard.md) and was planning to make an alliance with [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md). However Théoden sent Gandalf away under the influence of his advisor [Gríma](../G/Gríma.md) who was secretly in the service of Saruman.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

Gandalf came back to Meduseld with [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md), [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md) and [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md) on [2 March](../_/2_March.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md) and freed Théoden from the influence of Gríma and Théoden decided to lead his forces to defend Rohan against the invading forces of Saruman.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

After King Théoden died in the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../B/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md) his body was brought back to Edoras on [7 August](../_/7_August.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> where he lay until his funeral three days later.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> After his funeral Meduseld was decorated with beautiful hangings and filled with light and the greatest feast since the time of its construction was held in the Golden Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Meduseld* is an [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) name, which means "mead-house" or a "house in which feasting takes place" (i.e. a Hall of feasts).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-19">[19]</a></sup> The first component *medu* means "mead",<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> but as a word it has connotations of "joy". The second component *seld* means "seat", "residence", "mansion" or "hall".<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

A manuscript version of what would later become the chapter [The King of the Golden Hall](../T/The_King_of_the_Golden_Hall.md) contained a description of the doors and of the portico before the doors. The portico had pillars made from great trees, which were carved with interlacing figures and were gilded and painted. The doors were made of wood and carved with many beasts and birds with jewelled eyes and golden claws. [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) thinks that the description of the exterior of the hall may have got lost during the redrafting of the material.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In many ways Meduseld is inspired by [Anglo-Saxon](../O/Old_English.md) poetry, particularly *[Beowulf](<../B/Beowulf_(poem).md>)*, which contains the mead-hall <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heorot" title="wikipedia:Heorot">Heorot</a> — the "golden hall" of King <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrothgar" title="wikipedia:Hrothgar">Hrothgar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> The name *meduseld* is used in *Beowulf* in line 3065.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-19">[19]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Road_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Road-25">[25]</a></sup> On first seeing Meduseld, [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md) says of it "The light of it shines far across the land"<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> This is a translation of line 311 of *Beowulf*, *líxte se léoma ofer landa fela*,<sup id="cite_ref-Road_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Road-25">[25]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> meaning "The light of it shines over many lands."
