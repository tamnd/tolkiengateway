<i><b>Morgoth&#39;s Ring</b></i>, published in [1993](/wiki/1993), is the tenth volume of [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien)'s 12-volume book series, *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)*, in which he analyzes the unpublished manuscripts of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

This volume, along with the subsequent *[The War of the Jewels](/wiki/The_War_of_the_Jewels)*, provides detailed writings and editorial commentary pertaining to Tolkien's cosmology that eventually would become the published *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. This volume mentions a few characters excluded elsewhere, including [Findis](/wiki/Findis) and [Irimë](/wiki/Irim%C3%AB), the daughters of [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB).

The title of this volume comes from a statement in one of Tolkien's essays:

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>Just as Sauron concentrated his power in the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/One_Ring" title="One Ring">One Ring</a>, Morgoth dispersed his power into the very matter of <a href="/wiki/Arda" title="Arda">Arda</a>, thus the whole of Middle-earth was Morgoth&#39;s Ring.</i>&#34;</dd></dl>

## Inscriptions {#Inscriptions}

There is an inscription in the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) characters in the first pages of every *History of Middle-earth* volume, written by Christopher Tolkien and describing the contents of the book. The inscription in Book X reads:

<dl><dd>&#34;<i>In this book are given many of the later writings of John Ronald Reuel Tolkien concerning the history of the <a href="/wiki/Elder_Days" title="Elder Days">Elder Days</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Music_of_the_Ainur" title="Music of the Ainur">Music of the Ainur</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Nurtal%C3%AB_Valin%C3%B3reva" title="Nurtalë Valinóreva">Hiding of Valinor</a>; here much is told of the <a href="/wiki/Sun" title="Sun">Sun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Moon" title="Moon">Moon</a>; of the immortal <a href="/wiki/Eldar" title="Eldar">Eldar</a> and the death of the <a href="/wiki/Atani" title="Atani">Atani</a>; of the beginning of the Orcs and of the evil power of Melkor, the Morgoth, the Black Foe of the World.</i>&#34;</dd></dl>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The first of two companion volumes which documents the later writing of <i>The Silmarillion</i>, Tolkien&#39;s epic tale of war.<br/>
After the <i>The Lord of the Rings</i> was at last achieved, J.R.R. Tolkien turned his attention once again to &#34;the Matter of the Elder Days&#34;. The text of <a href="/wiki/The_Annals_of_Aman" title="The Annals of Aman">The Annals of Aman</a>, the &#34;Blessed Land&#34; in the far West, is given in full; while in writings hitherto unknown is seen the nature of the problems that Tolkien explored in his later years, as new and radical ideas, portending upheaval in the old narratives, emerged at the heart of the mythology, and as the destinies of Men and Elves, mortals and immortals, became of central significance, together with a vastly enlarged perception of the evil of Melkor, the Shadow upon Arda.<br/>
The second part of this history of the later <i>Silmarillion</i> is concerned with developments in the legends of Beleriand after the completion of <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>.
</p>
</blockquote>
