<i><b>Multi-Users in Middle-earth</b></i> (**MUME**) is a free and unlicensed <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUD#Player_versus_player_MUDs" title="wikipedia:MUD">player versus player</a> text-based online role-playing game, set in [J. R. R. Tolkien](../J/J._R._R._Tolkien.md)'s fictional world of [Middle-earth](Middle-earth.md), as described in *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-netgames2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames2-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-pramod-nayar_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pramod-nayar-6">[6]</a></sup> The game was released in 1992<sup id="cite_ref-robb-simpson_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robb-simpson-3">[3]</a></sup> and has been continually enhanced since its inception in the fall of [1991](../_/1991.md).<sup id="cite_ref-MUME_About_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MUME_About-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Game characteristics {#Game_characteristics}

The game is set during [Middle-earth](Middle-earth.md)'s late Third Age, taking place nearly 100 years prior to *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* and running through the entire events narrated within *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-tolkien-games_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkien-games-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-netgames2_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames2-5">[5]</a></sup> In *MUME* players can explore and live in [Middle-earth](Middle-earth.md), and choose to join the epic war between the forces of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) and the armies of the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md).<sup id="cite_ref-MUME_About_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MUME_About-7">[7]</a></sup> The action takes place between [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2850](../T/Third_Age_2850.md) (when [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) finds the key to [Erebor](../L/Lonely_Mountain.md) right before *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*) and [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md) (just after to the events of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*).<sup id="cite_ref-tolkien-games_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkien-games-2">[2]</a></sup> The world includes many locations across Middle-earth such as [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md), the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md), the [Grey Havens](../G/Grey_Havens.md), the [Shire](../S/Shire.md), [Bree](../B/Bree.md), [Fornost](../F/Fornost.md), [Tharbad](../T/Tharbad.md), [Rivendell](../R/Rivendell.md),<sup id="cite_ref-mark-wolf_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mark-wolf-8">[8]</a></sup> [Goblin-town](../G/Goblin-town.md), and the [Mines of Moria](Mines_of_Moria.md)<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup> The world design is based on maps from *[Middle-earth Role Playing](Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md)* using a gridding process<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup> to accurately follow the scale of Tolkien's maps.<sup id="cite_ref-tolkien-games_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkien-games-2">[2]</a></sup>

*Multi-Users in Middle-earth* is known for its <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUD#Player_versus_player_MUDs" title="wikipedia:MUD">player versus player</a><sup id="cite_ref-robb-simpson_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robb-simpson-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-raph-koster_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raph-koster-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-totalbiscuit-youtube_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-totalbiscuit-youtube-11">[11]</a></sup> where players choose avatars to role-play the conflict and war between the armies of the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md) and the forces of the [Dark Lord](../S/Sauron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup> Players are able to play as an [elf](../E/Elf.md), [dwarf](../D/Dwarf.md), [human](../H/Human.md), [hobbit](../H/Hobbit.md), [orc](../O/Orc.md), or [troll](../T/Troll.md) to fight both players and non-player characters.<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup> These adventures reward players with the experience and travel points necessary to level their character and attain additional power.<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Development {#Development}

*Multi-Users in Middle-earth* was started in 1991<sup id="cite_ref-mark-wolf_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mark-wolf-8">[8]</a></sup> by Philippe Rochat at the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Institute_of_Technology" title="wikipedia:Swiss Federal Institute of Technology">Swiss Federal Institute of Technology</a> in Lausanne, who was soon joined by Claude Indermitte, Pier Donini, and David Gay.<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup>

*Multi-Users in Middle-earth* uses the engine of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DikuMUD" title="wikipedia:DikuMUD">DikuMUD</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-last-outpost_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-outpost-12">[12]</a></sup> while the mudlib is Mudlle, a custom scripting language developed by David Gay and Gustav Hållberg.<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Reception {#Reception}

In April 1998, *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* was selected as The Mud Connector's Mud of the Month.<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup> In a September 2000 interview with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raph_Koster" title="wikipedia:Raph Koster">Raph Koster</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-raph-koster_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raph-koster-10">[10]</a></sup> the lead designer of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_Online" title="wikipedia:Ultima Online">Ultima Online</a></i> and the chief creative officer of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EverQuest_II" title="wikipedia:EverQuest II">EverQuest II</a></i>, lists *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* as one of the games that influenced him as a game designer by "doing such interesting things with player conflict". *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* was regarded by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TotalBiscuit" title="wikipedia:TotalBiscuit">TotalBiscuit</a> as his favorite MUD<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> and harder than <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls" title="wikipedia:Dark Souls">Dark Souls</a></i> with "crazy \[...\] Absolutely insane" PvP. <sup id="cite_ref-totalbiscuit-youtube_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-totalbiscuit-youtube-11">[11]</a></sup> In November 2017, *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* creator Pier Donini was selected by the podcast *Roguelike Radio* to join the discussion panel on MUDs.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
