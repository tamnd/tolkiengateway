<i><b>Multi-Users in Middle-earth</b></i> (**MUME**) is a free and unlicensed <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUD#Player_versus_player_MUDs" title="wikipedia:MUD">player versus player</a> text-based online role-playing game, set in [J. R. R. Tolkien](/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien)'s fictional world of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), as described in *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-netgames2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames2-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-pramod-nayar_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pramod-nayar-6">[6]</a></sup> The game was released in 1992<sup id="cite_ref-robb-simpson_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robb-simpson-3">[3]</a></sup> and has been continually enhanced since its inception in the fall of [1991](/wiki/1991).<sup id="cite_ref-MUME_About_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MUME_About-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Game characteristics {#Game_characteristics}

The game is set during [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth)'s late Third Age, taking place nearly 100 years prior to *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and running through the entire events narrated within *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-tolkien-games_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkien-games-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-netgames2_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames2-5">[5]</a></sup> In *MUME* players can explore and live in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), and choose to join the epic war between the forces of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and the armies of the [Free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples).<sup id="cite_ref-MUME_About_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MUME_About-7">[7]</a></sup> The action takes place between [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2850](/wiki/Third_Age_2850 "Third Age 2850") (when [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) finds the key to [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain") right before *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*) and [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019") (just after to the events of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*).<sup id="cite_ref-tolkien-games_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkien-games-2">[2]</a></sup> The world includes many locations across Middle-earth such as [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains), the [Grey Havens](/wiki/Grey_Havens), the [Shire](/wiki/Shire), [Bree](/wiki/Bree), [Fornost](/wiki/Fornost), [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad), [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell),<sup id="cite_ref-mark-wolf_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mark-wolf-8">[8]</a></sup> [Goblin-town](/wiki/Goblin-town), and the [Mines of Moria](/wiki/Mines_of_Moria)<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup> The world design is based on maps from *[Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing)* using a gridding process<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup> to accurately follow the scale of Tolkien's maps.<sup id="cite_ref-tolkien-games_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tolkien-games-2">[2]</a></sup>

*Multi-Users in Middle-earth* is known for its <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUD#Player_versus_player_MUDs" title="wikipedia:MUD">player versus player</a><sup id="cite_ref-robb-simpson_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robb-simpson-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-raph-koster_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raph-koster-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-totalbiscuit-youtube_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-totalbiscuit-youtube-11">[11]</a></sup> where players choose avatars to role-play the conflict and war between the armies of the [Free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples) and the forces of the [Dark Lord](/wiki/Sauron "Sauron").<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup> Players are able to play as an [elf](/wiki/Elf "Elf"), [dwarf](/wiki/Dwarf "Dwarf"), [human](/wiki/Human "Human"), [hobbit](/wiki/Hobbit "Hobbit"), [orc](/wiki/Orc "Orc"), or [troll](/wiki/Troll "Troll") to fight both players and non-player characters.<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup> These adventures reward players with the experience and travel points necessary to level their character and attain additional power.<sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Development {#Development}

*Multi-Users in Middle-earth* was started in 1991<sup id="cite_ref-mark-wolf_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mark-wolf-8">[8]</a></sup> by Philippe Rochat at the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Institute_of_Technology" title="wikipedia:Swiss Federal Institute of Technology">Swiss Federal Institute of Technology</a> in Lausanne, who was soon joined by Claude Indermitte, Pier Donini, and David Gay.<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup>

*Multi-Users in Middle-earth* uses the engine of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DikuMUD" title="wikipedia:DikuMUD">DikuMUD</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-netgames_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-netgames-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-last-outpost_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-last-outpost-12">[12]</a></sup> while the mudlib is Mudlle, a custom scripting language developed by David Gay and Gustav Hållberg.<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Reception {#Reception}

In April 1998, *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* was selected as The Mud Connector's Mud of the Month.<sup id="cite_ref-motm_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-motm-9">[9]</a></sup> In a September 2000 interview with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raph_Koster" title="wikipedia:Raph Koster">Raph Koster</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-raph-koster_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raph-koster-10">[10]</a></sup> the lead designer of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_Online" title="wikipedia:Ultima Online">Ultima Online</a></i> and the chief creative officer of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EverQuest_II" title="wikipedia:EverQuest II">EverQuest II</a></i>, lists *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* as one of the games that influenced him as a game designer by "doing such interesting things with player conflict". *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* was regarded by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TotalBiscuit" title="wikipedia:TotalBiscuit">TotalBiscuit</a> as his favorite MUD<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> and harder than <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls" title="wikipedia:Dark Souls">Dark Souls</a></i> with "crazy \[...\] Absolutely insane" PvP. <sup id="cite_ref-totalbiscuit-youtube_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-totalbiscuit-youtube-11">[11]</a></sup> In November 2017, *Multi-Users in Middle-earth* creator Pier Donini was selected by the podcast *Roguelike Radio* to join the discussion panel on MUDs.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
