**Mithril Miniatures** is an Irish manufacturer of collectable metal miniatures based on the works of J.R.R. Tolkien. It operates as a brand and trading name of The Edman Group Ltd, the same parent company behind the company Prince August from which Mithril originated in [1987](../_/1987.md).<sup id="cite_ref-briefhistory_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-briefhistory-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lostminiswiki_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostminiswiki-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Following success with their own "Fantasy Armies" line of ready-cast figures in the 1980s, Prince August sought to acquire a high-profile licensing agreement and successfully negotiated a worldwide license with the Tolkien Estate. To differentiate the new product line, the company branded itself as “[Mithril](Mithril.md)” when marketing it.<sup id="cite_ref-briefhistory_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-briefhistory-1">[1]</a></sup>

Mithril maintained a close working relationship with [Iron Crown Enterprises](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md), the publisher of *[Middle-earth Role Playing](Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md)*. They collaborated with ICE president [Pete Fenlon](../P/Pete_Fenlon.md) to ensure their miniature designs were consistent with the aesthetic established in ICE's sourcebooks.<sup id="cite_ref-briefhistory_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-briefhistory-1">[1]</a></sup> This partnership ended with ICE's loss of the license and subsequent bankruptcy.<sup id="cite_ref-lostminiswiki_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostminiswiki-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Design {#Design}

The visual identity of the range was defined by sculptor Chris Tubb, who joined Prince August in 1979. The initial high-quality casting standards were established by Tom Wilkinson, while his successor Dan Kelleher innovated on those for mass production. All figures are factory-primed with a durable base coat, making them suitable for display even when unpainted.<sup id="cite_ref-design_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-design-3">[3]</a></sup>
