**MOR** is a [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") signifying "black, dark".<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SApp_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SApp-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Derivatives {#Derivatives}

- [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya): *[morna](/wiki/Morna "Morna")* ("black", adjective);<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-2">[2]</a></sup> *[mornie](/wiki/Mornie "Mornie")* ("blackness", noun);<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-2">[2]</a></sup> *[more](/wiki/More "More")*<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup>

- [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin): *[morn](/wiki/Morn "Morn")*; *[môr](/wiki/M%C3%B4r "Môr")*<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions {#Other_versions}

In the [*Etymologies*](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies") appears the root MOR-, yielding [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) *mori* ("black"). From this root derives such words as:<sup id="cite_ref-LR_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-4">[4]</a></sup>

- Quenya: *[morna](/wiki/Morna "Morna")* ("gloomy, sombre"); *[mordo](/wiki/Mordo "Mordo")* ("shadow, obscurity, stain, smear, dimness")<sup id="cite_ref-VT45a_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT45a-5">[5]</a></sup>

- [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin): *maur* ("gloom"); *[morn](/wiki/Morn#Other_versions "Morn")* ("black")<sup id="cite_ref-VT45a_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT45a-5">[5]</a></sup>

- [Ilkorin](/wiki/Ilkorin): *môr* ("night")

## Examples {#Examples}

The element *mor* appears in a large number of compounds. [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes in the *[Etymologies](/wiki/Etymologies)* that the entry MOR- is "*extremely confused through changes and afterthought additions*" and that it is not clear if "*all the forms given were intended to stand*".<sup id="cite_ref-LR_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-4">[4]</a></sup> It can therefore be hard to know if the element *mor* in a compound is supposed to consist of a certain derivative of MOR.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Based on manuscripts held at the [Cushing Memorial Library and Archives collection](/wiki/Cushing_Memorial_Library_and_Archives_collection), Madeline J. Keyser has suggested that [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") was inspired by a real-world word *mora*, which "*seems to be of North (Scandinavian) and West Germanic origin — the Latin root is found in Modern English words such as murky, and in other Germanic words associated with darkness*". A manuscript note appears to consist of the glosses *mora* "dark", *moranya* "darker", and *moranta* "darkest".<sup id="cite_ref-Sixteen_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sixteen-6">[6]</a></sup>
