**MOR** is a [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) [root](../S/Sundocarme.md) signifying "black, dark".<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SApp_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SApp-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Derivatives {#Derivatives}

- [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md): *[morna](Morna.md)* ("black", adjective);<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE17_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-2">[2]</a></sup> *[mornie](Mornie.md)* ("blackness", noun);<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-2">[2]</a></sup> *[more](More.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup>

- [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md): *[morn](Morn.md)*; *[môr](Môr.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-L297_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L297-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions {#Other_versions}

In the [*Etymologies*](../T/The_Etymologies.md) appears the root MOR-, yielding [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) *mori* ("black"). From this root derives such words as:<sup id="cite_ref-LR_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-4">[4]</a></sup>

- Quenya: *[morna](Morna.md)* ("gloomy, sombre"); *[mordo](Mordo.md)* ("shadow, obscurity, stain, smear, dimness")<sup id="cite_ref-VT45a_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT45a-5">[5]</a></sup>

- [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md): *maur* ("gloom"); *[morn](Morn.md#Other_versions)* ("black")<sup id="cite_ref-VT45a_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT45a-5">[5]</a></sup>

- [Ilkorin](../I/Ilkorin.md): *môr* ("night")

## Examples {#Examples}

The element *mor* appears in a large number of compounds. [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) notes in the *[Etymologies](../E/Etymologies.md)* that the entry MOR- is "*extremely confused through changes and afterthought additions*" and that it is not clear if "*all the forms given were intended to stand*".<sup id="cite_ref-LR_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-4">[4]</a></sup> It can therefore be hard to know if the element *mor* in a compound is supposed to consist of a certain derivative of MOR.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Based on manuscripts held at the [Cushing Memorial Library and Archives collection](../C/Cushing_Memorial_Library_and_Archives_collection.md), Madeline J. Keyser has suggested that [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) was inspired by a real-world word *mora*, which "*seems to be of North (Scandinavian) and West Germanic origin — the Latin root is found in Modern English words such as murky, and in other Germanic words associated with darkness*". A manuscript note appears to consist of the glosses *mora* "dark", *moranya* "darker", and *moranta* "darkest".<sup id="cite_ref-Sixteen_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sixteen-6">[6]</a></sup>
