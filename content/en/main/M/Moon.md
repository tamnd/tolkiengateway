**Ithil**, the **Moon**, is a celestial object seen in the skies of [Arda](/wiki/Arda) at night.

## History {#History}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Elena_Kukanova_-_Varda_and_creation_of_Isil.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/1/11/Elena_Kukanova_-_Varda_and_creation_of_Isil.jpg/300px-Elena_Kukanova_-_Varda_and_creation_of_Isil.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="444" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/1/11/Elena_Kukanova_-_Varda_and_creation_of_Isil.jpg/450px-Elena_Kukanova_-_Varda_and_creation_of_Isil.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/1/11/Elena_Kukanova_-_Varda_and_creation_of_Isil.jpg 2x" data-file-width="507" data-file-height="750"/></a><figcaption><i>Varda and creation of Isil</i> by Elena Kukanova</figcaption></figure>

After the [Darkening of Valinor](/wiki/Darkening_of_Valinor) and the destruction of the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees_of_Valinor "Two Trees of Valinor"), [Telperion](/wiki/Telperion), the White Tree, bore one last Flower of Silver before its end. According to the lore of the [Elder Days](/wiki/Elder_Days), [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) and his people made a vessel to carry to the silver flower aloft, and [Tilion](/wiki/Tilion), one of the hunters of [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB), was granted the task of steering the new Moon through the sky.<sup id="cite_ref-Sil_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sil-1">[1]</a></sup>

Tilion guided his charge up into the western skies just as [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin) and his people were crosssing [Helcaraxë](/wiki/Helcarax%C3%AB), helping them to illuminate their hard way to [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), and so the end of the [Long Night](/wiki/Darkening_of_Valinor "Darkening of Valinor") and the ages of the [Stars](/wiki/Stars) was marked. After seven lunar "days" the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) also rose. The Moon first rose above [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) in the far West of the World, but [Varda](/wiki/Varda) came to change this arrangement, so that the Moon would pass beneath the World, and arise in the east instead, as it does to this day. Even as the Moon rose above the darkness in the west, Fingolfin's host blew silver trumpets and began its march into Middle-earth, and the shadows of the Noldor went long and black before them.<sup id="cite_ref-Sil_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sil-1">[1]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Darryl_Elliott_-_Moon.jpg"><img src="/w/images/c/c6/Darryl_Elliott_-_Moon.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="288" height="244" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="288" data-file-height="244"/></a><figcaption><i>Moon</i> by Darryl Elliott</figcaption></figure>

According to the legends of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), Tilion was an unsteady steersman, sometimes dwelling overlong beneath the Earth, or appearing in the sky at the same time as the Sun. He was drawn to the bright new Sun, launched from Valinor shortly after his own vessel,<sup id="cite_ref-Sil_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sil-1">[1]</a></sup> and his coming too close to his fiery companion was said to account for the darkening of the Moon's face.

According to a tradition,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Melkor will discover how to break the [Door of Night](/wiki/Door_of_Night), and will destroy both the Sun and the Moon.

## Lore of the Moon {#Lore_of_the_Moon}

### Númenóreans {#Númenóreans}

For the Númenóreans, the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) and the **Moon** - *Ûri* and *Nîlû*, called collectively also *Ûriyat* ("two suns") or *Ûrinîl(uw)at* ("two sun-moon") - were personified entities, the [Man in the Moon](/wiki/Man_in_the_Moon) and the [Lady of the Sun](/wiki/Lady_of_the_Sun).<sup id="cite_ref-Drowning_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Drowning-4">[4]</a></sup> They were the chief heavenly lights, and the enemies of the eternal Dark.<sup id="cite_ref-L347_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L347-5">[5]</a></sup>

This notion was carried over to [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), where the sons of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) each had either of the lights in his name: [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) and [Anárion](/wiki/An%C3%A1rion), and with them their cities [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil) and [Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Anor), and the lands that lay about them, [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) and [Anórien](/wiki/An%C3%B3rien).<sup id="cite_ref-L347_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L347-5">[5]</a></sup>

### Hobbits {#Hobbits}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Gail_McIntosh_-_New_Moon.jpg"><img src="/w/images/9/93/Gail_McIntosh_-_New_Moon.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="294" height="250" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="294" data-file-height="250"/></a><figcaption><i>New Moon</i> by <a href="/wiki/Category:Images_by_Gail_McIntosh" title="Category:Images by Gail McIntosh">Gail McIntosh</a></figcaption></figure>

When the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) were still a [wandering](/wiki/Wandering_Days "Wandering Days") people, their calendric unit was not a "week", but a "month", governed more or less by the Moon. However, through contact with alien peoples (perhaps the [Dúnedain of Arnor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Arnor)), they adopted the notion of weeks which formed the [Shire Reckoning](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

In Hobbit folklore, the moon was imagined to be inhabited by the [Man in the Moon](/wiki/Man_in_the_Moon).<sup id="cite_ref-Man_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Man-7">[7]</a></sup>

### Dwarves {#Dwarves}

The [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) based their calendar on the Moon. The Dwarven year began with the first day of the last moon of Autumn on the threshold of Winter.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> However it seems that by the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) the Dwarves adopted the [Stewards' Reckoning](/wiki/Stewards%27_Reckoning),<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and few had the skill to calculate the [Durin's Day](/wiki/Durin%27s_Day).

## Other names {#Other_names}

- <i><b>Isil</b></i>: a ([Q](/wiki/Quenya "Quenya"), pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈisil]</span>) or **Iþil** ([VQ](/wiki/Vanyarin "Vanyarin"), pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈiθil]</span>) was a name given to the Moon in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) by the [Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar). It is said to mean "the Sheen".<sup id="cite_ref-Sil_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sil-1">[1]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Rána</b></i>: although the name given to the Moon in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) was Isil, the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) preferred a name of their own, *Rána*, the Wayward.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> *Rána* (or *Rana*), meaning "The Wanderer"<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> or "the wayward",<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word derived from the [Common Eldarin](/wiki/Common_Eldarin) [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [RAN](/wiki/RAN) ("wander, stray, meander").<sup id="cite_ref-VT_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT-13">[13]</a></sup> In the *[Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies")* appears the [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) form *Rana*, and [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) *Rhân*. Both are descendant forms of [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) *Ranā* ("Moon").<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Ithil</b></i>: a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word meaning "the moon" or "(full) moon".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> The word can be found in *[Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil)*, *[Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien#Etymology)*, and *[ithildin](/wiki/Ithildin#Etymology "Ithildin")*. In the *[Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies "The Etymologies")*, [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) *Ithil* ("(poetic name of the) Moon") derives from the [roots](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") THIL/SIL.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Elfaron</b></i>: Meaning "Star-hunter" being a coumpound of [EL](/wiki/EL) and PHAR.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Phanaikelūth</b></i>: The oldest recorded name of the Moon was its [Valarin](/wiki/Valarin) name, *Phanaikelūth*. This was said to mean "bright mirror";<sup id="cite_ref-QaE_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-QaE-19">[19]</a></sup> making it the only name to accurately describe the origin of moonlight.
- [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) referred to the Moon as *The White Face*.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>
- The full moon of mid-[November](/wiki/November) was referred as the <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter%27s_moon" title="wikipedia:Hunter&#39;s moon">Hunter&#39;s Moon</a></b>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Early concepts {#Early_concepts}

In the early versions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") as described in *[The Book of Lost Tales Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One)*, the Moon was described in great detail as an immense island of crystal (*vírin*).<sup id="cite_ref-LT1VIII_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1VIII-22">[22]</a></sup> The spots on the Moon were also explained by telling that the Rose of [Silpion](/wiki/Silpion) fell because [Lórien](/wiki/Irmo "Irmo") did not want to pick it from the Tree.<sup id="cite_ref-LT1VIII_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1VIII-22">[22]</a></sup>

The phases of the Moon were explained that: first, like the earlier Silpion, the Rose needed to be watered with its shining dew and created it in greater quantities, and the heavy dew had to be removed; second, the Moon had to stay within the lower layers of [Ilwë](/wiki/Ilw%C3%AB) and the winds made it wobble.<sup id="cite_ref-LT1VIII_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1VIII-22">[22]</a></sup>

[Silmo](/wiki/Silmo) wanted to steer the Moon but could not do it, so [Manwë](/wiki/Manw%C3%AB) appointed [Ilinsor](/wiki/Ilinsor), who was joined by other air spirits. The [Man in the Moon](/wiki/Man_in_the_Moon) is described in those writings as being an old Elf who secretly hid on the island of the Moon, and built his minaret there.<sup id="cite_ref-LT1VIII_22-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1VIII-22">[22]</a></sup> This is alluded to further in Tolkien's *[Roverandom](/wiki/Roverandom)*, where the Man in the Moon also lives in a Minaret.

Other Elvish names in this fase were:

- <i><b>Celebron</b></i>: [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) name for "Moon".<sup id="cite_ref-Telimpe_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Telimpe-23">[23]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Crithosceleg</b></i>: Gnomish name meaning "disc of glass".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-24">[24]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Gilthalont</b></i>: unclear [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) name with the elemnent *giltha* ("white metal").<sup id="cite_ref-Ilsalunte_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ilsalunte-25">[25]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Ilsaluntë</b></i>: [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) name meaning "silver boat".<sup id="cite_ref-Ilsalunte_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ilsalunte-25">[25]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Minethlos</b></i>: Gnomish name meaning "argent isle".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-24">[24]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Rána</b></i>: Qenya name for "Moon" used by the Gods.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-24">[24]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Sil</b></i>: Qenya name for "Moon" used by the fairies, literally "Rose".<sup id="cite_ref-Silindril_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silindril-26">[26]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Silindril</b></i>: Qenya name meaning "Moon-cauldron".<sup id="cite_ref-Silindril_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silindril-26">[26]</a></sup>

### Later concepts {#Later_concepts}

In the [Round World](/wiki/Round_World_version_of_the_Silmarillion "Round World version of the Silmarillion") version of the legendarium, the Sun and the Moon were not the fruit of the Two Trees, but actually preceded the creation of the Trees. Instead, the Trees preserved the light of the Sun before it was tainted by Melkor when he ravished [Arien](/wiki/Arien).

## Inspirations {#Inspirations}

### Gender {#Gender}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Mani_and_Sol.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/3/37/Mani_and_Sol.jpg/300px-Mani_and_Sol.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="332" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/3/37/Mani_and_Sol.jpg/450px-Mani_and_Sol.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/3/37/Mani_and_Sol.jpg 2x" data-file-width="542" data-file-height="600"/></a><figcaption>A depiction of the Norse god Máni and the goddess Sól (Lorenz Frølich, 1895) who could be the inspirations of Tilion and <a href="/wiki/Arien" title="Arien">Arien</a></figcaption></figure>

The fact that the Moon in Tolkien's legendarium is connected to a male entity or person (like Tilion or the Man in the Moon), is obviously derived from the Germanic mythology of which Tolkien was an expert; for example the Old Norse <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1ni" title="wikipedia:Máni">Máni</a> is a male lunar god and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B3l" title="wikipedia:Sól">Sól</a> is a female solar goddess.

On the contrary, other mythologies of the world had connected the Moon with the feminine, like the Græco-Roman deities <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna" title="wikipedia:Luna">Luna</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selene" title="wikipedia:Selene">Selene</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecate" title="wikipedia:Hecate">Hecate</a> and others.

The gender of the celestial deity is mirrored also in the gender of the noun for "Moon" in these languages (eg. French *la Lune* but German *der Mond*). This was also perhaps the case with some of the [languages](/wiki/Languages "Languages") of [Arda](/wiki/Arda) ([Ghân-buri-ghân](/wiki/Gh%C3%A2n-buri-gh%C3%A2n) refers to the Sun as a woman).

### Phases {#Phases}

<p>...my moons in the crucial days between Frodo&#39;s flight and the present situation (arrival at Minas Morghul) were doing impossible things, rising in one part of the country and setting simultaneously in another.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>&#39;s letter to <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien</a>, <a href="/wiki/24_April" title="24 April">24 April</a> <a href="/wiki/1944" title="1944">1944</a><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Tolkien was very careful in the moon phases described in his works, so that they fit the passing of days realistically. Tolkien was remodelling the phases while reviewing the book in [1944](/wiki/1944) so that he preserves the consistency. The moon phases described in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* correspond to the phases of years [1941](/wiki/1941)-[2](/wiki/1942 "1942") and it is probable that he used a recent calendar for reference.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> Unfortunately, some minor errors (that can be detected only after thorough measurement and astronomical knowledge) slipped his attention (for example cf. [September 22](/wiki/22_September "22 September"))

### Calendar {#Calendar}

The mention that Hobbits and Dwarves based their year and months on the Moon, mirrors the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/lunar_calendar" title="wikipedia:lunar calendar">lunar calendar</a> used by several ancient cultures through history, such as the Hebrews. The observation of the Moon phases formed the months; the words are even related etymologically in some languages (cf. the English words "Moon" and "month").

## In other stories {#In_other_stories}

The moon is an important location in [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s children's story *[Roverandom](/wiki/Roverandom)*. After being turned into a toy dog, the wizard [Psamathos Psamathides](/wiki/Psamathos_Psamathides) summons his postman [Mew](/wiki/Mew) (a seagull) to take [Rover](/wiki/Rover) to the moon to see the [Man-in-the-Moon](/wiki/Man-in-the-Moon).<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> One side of the moon is white with a dark sky and tall mountains, whilst the dark side of the moon is dark with a light sky and deep valleys.<sup id="cite_ref-Roverandom_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roverandom-34">[34]</a></sup> Although home to spiders, the main enemy on the moon is the White Dragon (later renamed the Mottled Monster).<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup>
