**Middle-earth Enterprises**, formerly known as *Tolkien Enterprises* (sometimes abbreviated to *TE*), is the subsidiary of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Fellowship_Entertainment&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Fellowship Entertainment (page does not exist)">Fellowship Entertainment</a>, itself owned by the [Embracer Group](/wiki/Embracer_Group), used to manage it's licenses for *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* and *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*.

## History {#History}

In [1969](/wiki/1969), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) sold certain rights to [United Artists](/wiki/United_Artists), for US$250,000.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> This was then resold to independent producer [Saul Zaentz](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz) in [1976](/wiki/1976), who in turn formed *Tolkien Enterprises* in [1977](/wiki/1977).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [Ralph Bakshi](/wiki/Ralph_Bakshi), who had tried to argue with United Artists over an animated feature film for years, found more success with Zaentz. With a budget of US$7 million, it became quite an endeavour.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d2/Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg/250px-Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="187" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d2/Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg/375px-Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d2/Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg/500px-Tolkien_Enterprises.jpg 2x" data-file-width="802" data-file-height="601"/></a><figcaption>Tolkien Enterprises logo</figcaption></figure>

After Bakshi's box office failure, and the non-success of the [Rankin/Bass](/wiki/Rankin/Bass) productions, Zaentz was reluctant to market another adaptation, but would grant license to [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson) if he could find a production studio. After being turned down by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Miramax&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Miramax (page does not exist)">Miramax</a>, [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema) went ahead with the film [The Lord of the Rings](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [2010](/wiki/2010), the company changed it's name to Middle-earth Enterprises, which is it's current name.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [2022](/wiki/2022), the Swedish holding company [Embracer Group](/wiki/Embracer_Group) bought Middle-earth Enterprises, along with the rights for adaptations of The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

In [2025](/wiki/2025), after a failed investment deal, Embracer Group split up many of its holdings. In particular among these [Asmodee](/wiki/Asmodee), a massive French board game publisher that was one of Embracer's subsidiaries, was spun out into its own separate company: Asmodee Group AB. Later that year, Middle-earth Enterprises announced that Asmodee had become the exclusive category manager for all MEE-licensed tabletop games.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [2026](/wiki/2026), Embracer spun out a new company, <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Fellowship_Entertainment&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Fellowship Entertainment (page does not exist)">Fellowship Entertainment</a> to be the new owner of Middle-earth Enterprises and ultimate steward for the *Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* properties.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Copyright {#Copyright}

Middle-earth Enterprises holds marketing and licensing rights for the titles *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*, and many proper names and short phrases from the books. As a consequence, they also own all stage and cinema rights to these two books, and the related merchandising rights.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> However they do not have the television rights for the books (the rights to adapt both The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings in a television series in whatever form longer than eight episodes).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
