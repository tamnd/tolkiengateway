**Margaret Carroux (née Bister)** ([31 May](../_/31_May.md) [1912](../_/1912.md) — [22 July](../_/22_July.md) [1991](../_/1991.md))<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was a German translator. She translated many French and English literary works into German.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Margaret Bister was born as the daughter of a German-born Frenchman in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin" title="wikipedia:Berlin">Berlin</a>. In her hometown she began to study economics, English and French. In [1936](../_/1936.md), Margaret Bister broke off her studies for personal reasons and began to work as a commercial clerk and foreign language correspondent. After <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II" title="wikipedia:World War II">World War II</a>, she worked for the military government in Berlin and translated newspaper articles. In [1948](../_/1948.md), she left her hometown and moved to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt" title="wikipedia:Frankfurt">Frankfurt am Main</a>. She had a short marriage to a civil engineer, with whom Margaret Carroux had two children.<sup id="cite_ref-EM_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EM-3">[3]</a></sup>

Margaret Carroux began in the early 1960s to translate fiction and non-fiction books into German. She translated books by authors like <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_O%27Brien" title="wikipedia:Edna O&#39;Brien">Edna O&#39;Brien</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Potok" title="wikipedia:Chaim Potok">Chaim Potok</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7oise_Sagan" title="wikipedia:Françoise Sagan">Françoise Sagan</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadine_Gordimer" title="wikipedia:Nadine Gordimer">Nadine Gordimer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Her most famous translation is that of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* (published as *Der Herr der Ringe*, [1969](../_/1969.md)/[1970](../_/1970.md)), which she translated with the poetess [Ebba-Margareta von Freymann](../E/Ebba-Margareta_von_Freymann.md).<sup id="cite_ref-UL_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UL-5">[5]</a></sup>

The *"Carroux-Translation"* of *The Lord of the Rings* was the first German translation of the book. A second was made by [Wolfgang Krege](../W/Wolfgang_Krege.md), published in the year [2000](../_/2000.md).<sup id="cite_ref-UL_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UL-5">[5]</a></sup>

Margaret Carroux tried to capture the literary style of Tolkien's original work, to create the same atmosphere in the German version. During her translation work, she used the *[Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings](../G/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*. In the original books, all characters differ from each other through different levels of language, from the formal archaic speech to the everyday speech. Some critics of Carroux accused her of standardising this linguistic feature too much, for a nearly constant old language-style. But many fans like her *old-fashioned* rendering, because it follows the original text's flow and style very closely.<sup id="cite_ref-RN_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RN-6">[6]</a></sup> In an interview in [1977](../_/1977.md), Margaret Carroux emphasized, that she had some disputes with the editor while translating *The Lord of the Rings*. The editor wanted, against Carroux’s intention, a purely literal translation of the books and therefore, revised the unpublished translation several times.<sup id="cite_ref-EM_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EM-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RN_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RN-6">[6]</a></sup>

In 1991 and [2008](../_/2008.md) revised editions of the *"Carroux-Translation"* were published. The first one, revised and improved by Roswith Krege-Mayer, contains for the first time the first part of [Appendix E](../A/Appendix_E.md) (*Pronunciation of Words and Names*), translated by [Helmut W. Pesch](../H/Helmut_W._Pesch.md).<sup id="cite_ref-UL_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UL-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the second one, Stephan Askani, editor of publisher Klett-Cotta, and translator Lisa Kuppler corrected some place-names (some were retranslated) — especialy of villages in [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) — and errors in the spelling of Elvish or [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) words and names in the text and the poems. The corrections are based on the *Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings* and the actual [HarperCollins](../H/HarperCollins.md) edition of *The Lord of the Rings* of [2004](../_/2004.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Carroux and Tolkien {#Carroux_and_Tolkien}

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/L/LotR_first_German_edition.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/38/LotR_first_German_edition.jpg/350px-LotR_first_German_edition.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="350" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/3/38/LotR_first_German_edition.jpg/525px-LotR_first_German_edition.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/3/38/LotR_first_German_edition.jpg 2x" data-file-width="700" data-file-height="300"/></a><figcaption>The first German <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>-Edition, 1969/70</figcaption></figure>

In [September](../S/September.md) [1967](../_/1967.md) Margaret Carroux contacted [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) through publisher [Rayner Unwin](../R/Rayner_Unwin.md), about her work on translating *The Lord of the Rings*. Carroux sent to Tolkien her translation of *[Leaf by Niggle](../L/Leaf_by_Niggle.md)* to allow him the opportunity to evaluate her work as a translator of his literary works. She asked Tolkien for a meeting, too. But Tolkien was ill at that time and so the meeting took place three months later on [December 13](../_/13_December.md), [1967](../_/1967.md) in [Oxford](../O/Oxford.md). In early December Carroux had already sent the part of *The Lord of the Rings* she had translated so far (about a hundred pages) to Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-8">[8]</a></sup>

After their meeting in Oxford, they corresponded with each other to discuss some problems Carroux experienced while translating, especially with the poems. Carroux sent him some scripts and Tolkien praised and elated her.

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I should certainly not have taken the trouble that I took with your speci­mens, if I had not felt that you had the sympathy and understanding required, and only needed a little help and some encouragement to per­severe in what is a very difficult task.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Fragment from Tolkien&#39;s letter to Carroux, <a href="../_/29_September.md" title="29 September">September 29</a>,<a href="../_/1968.md" title="1968">1968</a></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

In the same letter Tolkien talks about his scrupulosity, if a co-translator, for example a lyricist, would read the text intensely enough, to understand the exact meaning or importance of the poems. Because of this, Tolkien extended to Carroux more help with the poems.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Works by J.R.R. Tolkien translated by Carroux {#Works_by_J.R.R._Tolkien_translated_by_Carroux}

- [1964](../_/1964.md): *[Leaf by Niggle](../L/Leaf_by_Niggle.md)* (*Blatt von Tüftler*, published in the book *Fabelhafte Geschichten*)
- [1969](../_/1969.md)-[1970](../_/1970.md):*[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* (*Der Herr der Ringe*)
    - 1969: *[The Fellowship of the Ring](../T/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)* (*Die Gefährten*)
    - 1970: *[The Two Towers](../T/The_Two_Towers.md)* (*Die Zwei Türme*)
    - 1970: *[The Return of the King](../T/The_Return_of_the_King.md)* (*Die Rückkehr des Königs*)
    - 1970: *[Appendices](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Appendices.md)* (*Anhänge und Register* (incomplete))
