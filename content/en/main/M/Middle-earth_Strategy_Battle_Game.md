The **Middle-earth Strategy Battle Game** is a tabletop miniature wargame produced by [Games Workshop](/wiki/Games_Workshop) and based on [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema)'s [*Middle-earth* series](</wiki/Middle-earth_(New_Line_Cinema_series)> "Middle-earth (New Line Cinema series)"), though at times it has adapted characters and scenarios from the books.

It was formerly known as **The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game** when it released in [2001](/wiki/2001) to coincide the release of the [*The Lord of the Rings* films](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)"). Another set of miniatures was produced for [*The Hobbit* films](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)> "The Hobbit (film series)") in [2012](/wiki/2012) known as **The Hobbit Strategy Battle Game**.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Both games were merged together under a new name along with a new rules manual.

## Development {#Development}

### Background {#Background}

In the 1980s, Games Workshop produced a range of miniatures for *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, using original character designs based on fantasy art popular of the time.<sup id="cite_ref-Citadel_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Citadel-2">[2]</a></sup> This was the first range of Lord of the Rings miniatures that Citadel created, taking over from [Grenadier Miniatures](/wiki/Grenadier_Models_Inc. "Grenadier Models Inc.") in 1985, before the license passed to [Mithril Miniatures](/wiki/Mithril_Miniatures) around 1987. The earliest releases were semi-solid base, having a small solid base; later releases were slot based.

### Release {#Release}

The game was initially released in [2001](/wiki/2001) to coincide in with the film *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring")*. New box sets with updated rules were also released for *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers "The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers")* and *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King "The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King")* films. Later, beginning with the *[Shadow &amp; Flame](/wiki/Shadow_and_Flame "Shadow and Flame")* supplement, Games Workshop began to add content that was featured in the original book but not in the film adaptations: eg. [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil), [Radagast](/wiki/Radagast) and [Glorfindel](/wiki/Glorfindel). Games Workshop has also expanded its license with original material on areas such as [Harad](/wiki/Harad) and [Khand](/wiki/Khand), with mixed reactions. The most recent complete edition of the rules, *[The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game: Rulebook](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Strategy_Battle_Game:_Rulebook)*, was released by Games Workshop in September 2005, while a compact edition of the rules came with the *[Mines of Moria Boxed Set](</wiki/Mines_of_Moria_(2005_boxed_set)> "Mines of Moria (2005 boxed set)")*.

In early [2009](/wiki/2009) Games Workshop also released an expansion to the original game called *[War of the Ring](</wiki/War_of_the_Ring_(2009_book)> "War of the Ring (2009 book)")* which, according to the company, allows players to emulate the large battles included in [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* by streamlining the game system.

In addition to gaming, The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game includes other common elements of the miniature wargaming hobby. These include the collecting, painting and conversion of miniature figures used in play, as well as the modelling of gaming terrain from scratch. These aspects of the hobby are covered in Games Workshop's monthly *[White Dwarf](/wiki/White_Dwarf)*, as well as formerly in the fortnightly *[Battle Games in Middle-earth](/wiki/Battle_Games_in_Middle-earth)*.

### Current licensing {#Current_licensing}

The current Lord of the Rings range stems from Games Workshop's rights from [Middle-earth Enterprises](/wiki/Middle-earth_Enterprises) to produce a skirmish war game based on the films, and also on *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* and *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* books, in the 25mm miniature scale.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup> (The rights to produce a role playing game version of the films were sold to another firm, [Decipher, Inc.](/wiki/Decipher,_Inc.)) They also have the rights to produce a board game based on The Hobbit, called *[The Battle of Five Armies](</wiki/The_Battle_of_Five_Armies_(2005_board_game)> "The Battle of Five Armies (2005 board game)")*, using smaller miniatures to enact larger battles.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

Games Workshop has not acquired the rights to *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*, which is still the exclusive property of the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate), but has the right to develop its own derivative intellectual property to fill in the gaps in Tolkien's [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"). This is particularly true of [Harad](/wiki/Harad), which has a range of invented places (such as "Kârna", "Badharkân", "Hidâr", "Nâfarat", "Abrakân", and "Dhâran-sar"<sup id="cite_ref-BOPF_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BOPF-5">[4]</a></sup>) and characters (such as the Hasharin<sup id="cite_ref-BOPF_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BOPF-5">[4]</a></sup> and "Dalamyr, Fleetmaster of Umbar"<sup id="cite_ref-LOME_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LOME-6">[5]</a></sup>).
