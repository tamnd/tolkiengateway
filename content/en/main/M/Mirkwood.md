**Mirkwood** ([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"): *Taur e-Ndaedelos*<sup id="cite_ref-LotRAppF_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LotRAppF-1">[1]</a></sup>) was a great forest east of the river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) in [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion). It was previously called Greenwood the Great ([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"): *Eryn Galen*), until the Shadow of the influence of Sauron(known as the [Necromancer](/wiki/Necromancer) at the time) came into it. It was named the [Wood of Greenleaves](/wiki/Eryn_Lasgalen "Eryn Lasgalen") ([S](/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"): *[Eryn Lasgalen](/wiki/Eryn_Lasgalen)*) when the shadow was lifted after the defeat of Sauron.

## Geography {#Geography}

Mirkwood seems to have had much the same boundaries as it did at the end of the Third Age for most of its history. It was roughly rectangular in shape, stretching from the foothills of the [Grey Mountains](/wiki/Grey_Mountains) in the north to the [North Undeep](/wiki/North_Undeep) in the south, and from the east edge of the vale of [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) to the [Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain).

It was bisected by the ancient [Old Forest Road](/wiki/Old_Forest_Road). Later, when this road became unusable, a second path through the forest was made to the north. Between the two paths lay the [Mountains of Mirkwood](/wiki/Mountains_of_Mirkwood). The [Forest River](/wiki/Forest_River) cut through the forest's northern end from its source in the western Grey Mountains, joined in the centre by the [Enchanted River](/wiki/Enchanted_River) which flowed north from the Mountains of Mirkwood.

South of the Old Forest Road the [East Bight](/wiki/East_Bight) created the *Narrows of the Forest*, only one hundred miles across. South and west of the narrows was the a hill called [Amon Lanc](/wiki/Amon_Lanc).

## History {#History}

### First Age {#First_Age}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/67/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg/300px-L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="260" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/67/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg/450px-L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/6/67/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg/600px-L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1_-_Mirkwood_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="888"/></a><figcaption>Nandor in Mirkwood (art by <a href="/wiki/L%C3%ADda_Holubov%C3%A1" title="Lída Holubová">Lída Holubová</a></figcaption></figure>

Originally called Greenwood the Great, the forest may have once formed part of the vast primeval woodland which covered most of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) during the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees) in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), likely linked to the wood that would become [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) across the Anduin.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) passed through the area on their [journey](/wiki/Great_Journey "Great Journey") to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) and it was first populated at this time by the [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor). Unwilling to cross the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains), these Elves settled also in the wooded valleys of the river Anduin. They multiplied and were joined by wandering [Avari](/wiki/Avari), and became known as the Silvan or Wood-elves.

The Old Forest Road was constructed very early in Greenwood's history by the [Longbeard](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk "Durin's Folk") [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) to carry traffic between their territory in the Misty Mountains to [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"), and the [Iron Hills](/wiki/Iron_Hills).This road would be later abandoned as the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) and [Grey Mountains](/wiki/Grey_Mountains) took [Gundabad](/wiki/Mount_Gundabad "Mount Gundabad") and the surrounding region, cutting off communication between Khazad-dûm and the Iron Hills and Erebor.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

Around the year [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [750](/wiki/Second_Age_750 "Second Age 750"), [Oropher](/wiki/Oropher), a [Sinda](/wiki/Sindar "Sindar") prince and a refugee from fallen [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), removed from [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon), with a handful of other Sindar, to the Greenwood, where he was taken by the Silvan Elves as their lord. This forested region to the east of the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) is where [Silvan Elves](/wiki/Silvan_Elves), of [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor) descent and the [Avari](/wiki/Avari), lived.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Oropher built his [Halls](/wiki/Elvenking%27s_Halls "Elvenking's Halls") at [Amon Lanc](/wiki/Amon_Lanc) and was accepted as the leader of the Wood-elves of Greenwood, later the [Elves of Mirkwood](/wiki/Elves_of_Mirkwood), forming the [Woodland Realm](/wiki/Woodland_Realm).

During the First and Second Age, the [Northmen](/wiki/Northmen), such as the ancestors of the [Woodmen](/wiki/Woodmen), had been allies of the [Longbeards](/wiki/Longbeards) and the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) of the hills surrounding the Greenwood, and were enemies of the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) and [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The Woodmen maintained friendly relations with the Elves of the Greenwood.<sup id="cite_ref-UT_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT-6">[6]</a></sup>

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

The first millennium of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) probably saw the creation of the East Bight by men living in the eastern eaves of the forest. These men may have formed part of the [Kingdom of Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion "Rhovanion") led by [Vidugavia](/wiki/Vidugavia). Men, such as the [Éothéod](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od), and [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) also lived in the vale of Anduin and were likely responsible for the retreat of the forest's western border.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/f/f6/Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg/300px-Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/f/f6/Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg/450px-Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/f/f6/Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg/600px-Marya_Filatova_-_Thranduil_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="752"/></a><figcaption>&#34;<a href="/wiki/Thranduil" title="Thranduil">Thranduil</a>&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Marya_Filatova" title="Marya Filatova">Marya Filatova</a></figcaption></figure>

At the beginning of the Third Age, after the Battle of Dagorlad, [Thranduil](/wiki/Thranduil) replaced [Oropher](/wiki/Oropher) (who had fallen during the Battle) as king of the Woodland Realm. Probably as a result of massive losses at the [Battle of Dagorlad,](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad "Battle of Dagorlad") the Silvan population of Greenwood was diminished and became mainly concentrated in the hills then known as [Emyn Duir](/wiki/Mountains_of_Mirkwood "Mountains of Mirkwood"). At the beginning of the second millennium of the Third Age, a mysterious being came to Amon Lanc in the south of Greenwood the Great, and there built the stronghold known as [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur); "hill of sorcery." This was the magician known as the Necromancer, who was later revealed to be none other than Sauron himself, and from his fortress tower a shadow of nightshade began to spread across the woodlands. The story of Sauron's darkening of Greenwood the Great mirrors events in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), when he occupied the highland forests of [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion), north of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand). That region, too, fell under his shadow, and was also known as [Taur-nu-Fuin](</wiki/Taur-nu-Fuin_(Dorthonion)> "Taur-nu-Fuin (Dorthonion)"). From that time on, the Elves came to refer to the Greenwood as Taur-nu-Fuin, the forest under nightshade, rendered into the [Mannish](/wiki/Mannish) tongue as 'Mirkwood'.

Around the [same time](/wiki/Third_Age_1636 "Third Age 1636") as the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague) devastated [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) and [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), the [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) grew deep in Greenwood and evil things reappeared. Later the wise noted this coincidence and considered it a sign of Sauron's return.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Indeed, by the time of [Cirion](/wiki/Cirion) (c. T.A. 2500), [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur) controlled the [Balchoth](/wiki/Balchoth), a tribe of [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) who dwelt east of Mirkwood; often they made raids through the forest up to the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin), until they were all but deserted, until [defeated](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Field_of_Celebrant "Battle of the Field of Celebrant").<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

Sauron's arrival caused a darkening of Greenwood, and at this point it became known as Mirkwood. The children of [Shelob](/wiki/Shelob), giant [spiders](/wiki/Spiders "Spiders"), as well as bats and orcs in Dol Guldur's service occupied the forest and it became thicker, darker and covered in cobwebs.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

This caused Thranduil to lead the Silvan population of Mirkwood to migrate north for the last time, north of the forest river. The Wood-elves specifically dwell apparently exclusively in the [Elvenking's Halls](/wiki/Elvenking%27s_Halls) at the eastern end of the Forest River. The ancient Old Forest Road was abandoned by men and Dwarves alike, with a new but seldom used path being made further from Dol Guldur and the Hobbits near the forest's eastern border migrated away.

Mirkwood remained a place of fear throughout the Third Age, though the kingdoms of [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain") and [Dale](/wiki/Dale) flourished briefly in the time of the [Kings under the Mountain](/wiki/King_under_the_Mountain "King under the Mountain"). This prosperity was ended by the arrival of the [Dragon](/wiki/Dragons "Dragons") [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) who brought yet further desolation to the area north-eastern Mirkwood. Small homesteads of 'Woodmen' are also recorded as living in the western edge of the forest south of the old road in [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941").<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

In [2850](/wiki/Third_Age_2850 "Third Age 2850"), [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) visited Mirkwood and entered Dol Guldur, this time in secret. The Grey Pilgrim discovered that the Necromancer was none other than Sauron, who had regained his powers, millennia after the Battle of Dagorlad.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:BFME2_-_Galadriel_throws_down_the_walls_of_Dul_Guldur.JPG"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/BFME2_-_Galadriel_throws_down_the_walls_of_Dul_Guldur.JPG/300px-BFME2_-_Galadriel_throws_down_the_walls_of_Dul_Guldur.JPG" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="181" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/0/0d/BFME2_-_Galadriel_throws_down_the_walls_of_Dul_Guldur.JPG 1.5x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="272"/></a><figcaption>Galadriel casts down the walls of Dol Guldur</figcaption></figure>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/2/2a/Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg/300px-Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="515" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/2/2a/Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg/450px-Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/2/2a/Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg/600px-Peter_Pracownik_-_Five_of_Coins.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="769" data-file-height="1319"/></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Thorin_and_Company" title="Thorin and Company">Thorin&#39;s Company</a> in Mirkwood</figcaption></figure>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), as [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) were going on the [Quest of Erebor](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor), the shadow over Mirkwood was lifted, albeit temporarily. While Thorin and Company were journeying to Erebor, the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council), prompted by the [Wizard](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards") [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)'s discovery of the true identity of Sauron drove him from Dol Guldur. Gandalf also planned the successful [Quest of Erebor](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor), which resulted in the slaying of Smaug in the same year. The combination of these two events allowed the re-established kingdoms of Erebor and Dale and the flourishing of the Woodland Realm and of a confederacy of Woodmen led by the [Beornings](/wiki/Beornings). The Darkness of Mirkwood was lifted for a while.

Only ten years after these events in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2951](/wiki/Third_Age_2951 "Third Age 2951") Sauron, now based in [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), sent [Khamûl](/wiki/Kham%C3%BBl) and two other [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) to reoccupy Dol Guldur.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

On 21 March [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) who had captured [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) in the [Dead Marshes](/wiki/Dead_Marshes) arrived with Gollum in Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

Two days later, on 23 March [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), Gandalf arrived in Mirkwood<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-14">[14]</a></sup> and began to interrogate Gollum.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

On 29 March [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), Gandalf left Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-Note6_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Note6-14">[14]</a></sup>

On 20 June, Orcs attacked the Elves of Mirkwood and Gollum was able to escape from his captivity.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> Afterwards Legolas leaves Mirkwood to travel to Rivendell to attend the Council of Elrond and to report that Gollum has escaped.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

On 11, 15 and 22 March [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> forces from Dol Guldur assaulted the realm of Lórien, but they were repulsed each time because of the valor of the Elves of Lórien and by the power of Galadriel, but the woods on the borders of Lórien were seriously damaged.<sup id="cite_ref-Great_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-19">[19]</a></sup>

On 15 March [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"), the day of the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields) in front of Minas Tirith,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> forces from Dol Guldur invaded the realm of Thranduil in Mirkwood. Thranduil defeated the forces of Dol Guldur in a long battle under the trees during which there was great damage through by fire.<sup id="cite_ref-Great_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-19">[19]</a></sup>

On 28 March [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> the host of the Elves of [Lórien](/wiki/L%C3%B3rien) led by [Celeborn](/wiki/Celeborn) crossed the Anduin in boats and conquered Dol Guldur. Galadriel destroyed the walls of Dol Guldur and uncovered its pits and the forest was cleansed.<sup id="cite_ref-Great_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-19">[19]</a></sup>

On 6 April [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> Celeborn and Thranduil met in the middle of Mirkwood and gave it the new name *Eryn Lasgalen*, The Wood of Greenleaves. They divided the forest between them so that the realm of Thranduil encompassed the northern part of the wood down to the [Mountains of Mirkwood](/wiki/Mountains_of_Mirkwood) and that the realm of Celeborn encompassed the southern part of the wood south of the [Narrows of the Forest](/wiki/Narrows_of_the_Forest), which was named East Lórien by Celeborn. Thranduil and Celeborn gave all the wide forest between the Mountains of Mirkwood and the Narrows of the Forest to the [Beornings](/wiki/Beornings) and the [Woodmen](/wiki/Woodmen).<sup id="cite_ref-Great_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-19">[19]</a></sup>

### Fourth Age {#Fourth_Age}

Though initially they prospered as the darkness was lifted, the elves of the Wood of Greenleaves were destined either to depart for Valinor or fade into rustic forest spirits. The forest probably then ultimately fell under the dominion of Men, the descendants of the Beornings and the men of Dale.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Mirkwood* is an English name, which means "gloomy wood". It is a combination of "mirk" and "wood".<sup id="cite_ref-L289_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L289-23">[23]</a></sup>

The name is also inspired by the ancient name *Myrkviðr.*

## Other names {#Other_names}

*Eryn Galen* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, which means "Greenwood". Paul Strack suggests that it is a combination of *[eryn](/wiki/Eryn "Eryn")* ("wood") and the [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") form *galen* of *[calen](/wiki/Calen "Calen")* ("green").<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup>

*Taur e-Ndaedelos* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, which literally means "forest of the great fear".<sup id="cite_ref-LotRAppF_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LotRAppF-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that it is a combination of *[taur](/wiki/Taur "Taur")* ("forest"), *[en](/wiki/En "En")* ("of the") and *daedelos* ("horrible fear").<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> In another point, the latter word was given the form <i>Dae<b>dh</b>elos</i> (with [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") *-delos*) and glossed as "Shadow of Abomination".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>

*Taur-nu-Fuin* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, which means "Mirkwood" or "Forest under Night(shade)". Paul Strack suggests that it is a combination of *[taur](/wiki/Taur "Taur")* ("forest"), *[nu](/wiki/Nu "Nu")* ("under") and *[fuin](/wiki/Fuin "Fuin")* ("night" or "nightshade").<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> It is a namesake of [Taur-nu-Fuin](</wiki/Taur-nu-Fuin_(Dorthonion)> "Taur-nu-Fuin (Dorthonion)") in [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion), which existed before the First Age ended and [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) was drowned.

*Eryn Lasgalen* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name,<sup id="cite_ref-Eryn_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eryn-29">[29]</a></sup> which means "Wood of Greenleaves".<sup id="cite_ref-Great_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Great-19">[19]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that it is a combination of *[eryn](/wiki/Eryn "Eryn")* ("wood"), *[lass](/wiki/Lass "Lass")* ("leaf") and the [lenited](/wiki/Lenition "Lenition") form *galen* of *[calen](/wiki/Calen "Calen")* ("green").<sup id="cite_ref-Eryn_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eryn-29">[29]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, the [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) names *Ladwen-na-Dhaideloth*, *Ladwen Daideloth* and *Dor-na-Dhaideloth* appear, referring to a land of [Tol Eressea](/wiki/Tol_Eressea) known as [Heath of the Sky-roof](/wiki/Heath_of_the_Sky-roof). It was the site of a great battle, and afterwards was known as [Withered Heath](/wiki/Withered_Heath).<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

*Mirkwood* is the Anglicized form of the Norse name <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrkvi%C3%B0r" title="wikipedia:Myrkviðr">Myrkviðr</a></i> or *mirkiwidu*, originally hailing from [Eddaic poems](/wiki/Poetic_Edda "Poetic Edda"). *Myrkviðr* was the name of a "dark boundary-forest ... the great forest that divided the land of the Goths from the land of the Huns".<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> In a letter to his grandson Michael, Tolkien wrote that the name "*Mirkwood* was not his invention but was probably the Primitive Germanic name of the great mountainous forest regions that anciently formed a barrier to the south of the lands of Germanic expansions and in some traditions became used especially for the boundary between Goths and Huns. He then continues to discuss the origin of the name in very early German *mirkiwidu* writings.<sup id="cite_ref-L289_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L289-23">[23]</a></sup>

Projected into Old English, the term appears as *Myrcwudu* in Tolkien's *[The Lost Road](/wiki/The_Lost_Road)*, as a poem sung by [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine):<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup>

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Sea-danes and Goths, Swedes and Northmen,<br/>
Franks and Frisians, folk of the islands,<br/>
Swordmen and Saxons, Swabes and English,<br/>
and the Langobards who long ago<br/>
beyond Myrcwudu a mighty realm<br/>
and wealth won them in the Welsh countries<br/>
where Ælfwine Eadwine&#39;s heir<br/>
in Italy was king. All that has passed.
</p>
</div>

The name *Mirkwood* was also used by [William Morris](/wiki/William_Morris) in his novel *[The House of the Wolfings](/wiki/The_House_of_the_Wolfings)* ([1888](/wiki/1888)).<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup>
