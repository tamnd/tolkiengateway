**Miruvor** and **miruvórë** were types of cordial used by the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves).

## History {#History}

*Miruvórë* was a mead or nectar of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) of which the Elves did not know the ingredients or making, though thought it was made of the honey/nectar of certain flowers in Valinor.<sup id="cite_ref-pe17_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:64</sup> It was poured during their festivals in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

*Miruvor* (the same word, in a reduced form) refers to a reviving cordial made in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), probably made basically of honey of scented flowers.<sup id="cite_ref-pe17_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:64</sup> [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) gave a flask of the "Cordial of Imladris" to [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) before the [Company of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring "Fellowship of the Ring") departed on their southward journey. Gandalf used this drink to revitalize themselves on [Caradhras](/wiki/Caradhras).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Other [races](/wiki/Races "Races"), such as the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) and even the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) possessed drinks with similar properties, but [ent-draught](/wiki/Ent-draught "Ent-draught") is the most potent of these cordials.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*miruvor* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), taken by borrowed [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word *miruvórë*. The word was thought of as "precious juice", with a false etymology from [MIR](/wiki/MIR) ("precious") + [WOR](/wiki/WOR) ("exude"); the root "WOR" did not, however, exist in either Quenya or Sindarin.<sup id="cite_ref-pe17_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:37–38</sup>

The actual origin was [Valarin](/wiki/Valarin), from the word *[mirubhōze](/wiki/Mirubh%C5%8Dze "Mirubhōze")*, a honey wine.<sup id="cite_ref-pe17_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:38</sup>

The Quenya form also appears as *miruvórë*, explained as "a special wine or cordial".<sup id="cite_ref-WJ_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Quenya word *lisse-miruvóreva* occurs in the "Lament" sung by [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) in "Farewell to Lórien"; being translated as "of the sweet mead<i>&#34;.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></i>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The invented word goes back to at least [1915](/wiki/1915). In that early phase of the legendarium, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) envisioned it as derived from real-life [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic) elements \**midu* ("mead") + *wopeis* ("sweet") &gt; miduwopi &gt; miduwodi &gt; misuwosi &gt; Q. miruvore.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
