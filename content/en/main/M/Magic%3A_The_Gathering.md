**Magic: The Gathering**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> is a collectible card game created and designed by Richard Garfield and produced by [Wizards of the Coast](/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast) starting in [1993](/wiki/1993). It is the predecessor for all contemporary collectable card games, including [Middle-earth Collectible Card Game](/wiki/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game) and [The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game).

While originally purely featuring its own fantasy settings, in 2021 *Universes Beyond* was introduced, a sub-brand that instead featured licensed properties.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup> On 23 June 2023, Wizards released *[The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Tales_of_Middle-earth)*, a Universes Beyond set with 451 cards that were new renditions of various characters and events from the Lord of the Rings trilogy.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> A second Tolkien-related Universes Beyond set, *[The Hobbit](/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_-_The_Hobbit "Magic: The Gathering - The Hobbit")*, followed in August 2026.

## Overview {#Overview}

Magic: the Gathering is a card game that positions the players as "Planeswalkers", powerful wizards who battle each other for dominance. The goal of the game is reduce your opponent's life to zero by casting spells, wielding rare artefacts and summoning creatures to ultimately deal enough damage to your opponent before they do the same to you.

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/4b/MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png/300px-MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="419" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/4/4b/MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png/450px-MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/4/4b/MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png/600px-MTG_Goblin_Assailant_LTR_295.png 2x" data-file-width="745" data-file-height="1040"/></a><figcaption><i>An example of a creature card from Magic. Goblin Assailant, a card from the Tales of Middle-earth expansion, costs one red mana and one of any colour. It has both the &#34;Goblin&#34; and &#34;Warrior&#34; creature types. It has no abilities (only flavour text), a power of 2 and a toughness of 2.</i></figcaption></figure>

Each player draws from a deck of cards they have assembled (typically between 40 and 100 cards, depending on the format) that is randomized before play. Each card will typically represent one entity or concept, such as a fireball spell or a dragon you can summon. Most cards have a cost which must be paid with by generating "mana", a resource (generally produced by land cards) that can be one of five colours: White, Blue, Black, Red or Green. Each colour of mana typically has a theme: white cards represent divinity and healing, blue cards reflect intellect and disrupting magic, black cards include undead horrors and poison, red cards embrace fire and and aggressive attacks, and green evokes nature and wild beasts.

Magic has a set of core rules that dictate how a player's turn proceeds and how cards can be played, as well as "keywords" that are shorthand. For example the "Flying" keyword indicates that a creature can't be stopped except by other creatures with Flying. Also, many cards contain unique text that may bend (or break) the core rules, and/or synergize with other cards based on their type, colour or keywords.

New cards are typically released in a "set" of a few hundred cards that share a particular theme or story, with each set having an associated "booster pack" that will contain over a dozen randomized cards. Each card has a rarity that will indicate how often it appears in a booster pack: Common, Uncommon, Rare and Mythic. In recent years, Magic sets have also released "Collector Boosters", packs that only contain rarer cards with foil treatment, alternate art or serialization (such as The One Ring in the Tales of Middle-earth expansion).

Magic supports a wide variety of methods of play, with the most popular being one-versus-one duels and the Commander multiplayer format. There are also many different formats where the allowed card pool is limited, such as to a specific time frame (Modern, where only more recent cards are allowed) or a specific rarity (Pauper, where only common cards can be utilized).
