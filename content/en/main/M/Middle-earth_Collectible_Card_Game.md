<i><b>Middle-earth Collectible Card Game</b></i> is a collectible card game released by [Iron Crown Enterprises](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md) in late [1995](../_/1995.md). It is the first CCG based on [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s [Middle-earth](Middle-earth.md), with added content from [ICE](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md)'s [*Middle-earth Role Playing* game](Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md).

The cards used in the game feature original artwork by a multitude of artists, many of them longtime Tolkien illustrators such as [John Howe](../J/John_Howe.md), [Ted Nasmith](../T/Ted_Nasmith.md), [Angus McBride](../A/Angus_McBride.md), et al.

The game's production ended in [1999](../_/1999.md), when ICE lost the license that allowed them to use the content of Tolkien's *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* and *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The license ended up with [Decipher](../D/Decipher.md), who would publish their own unrelated [collectible card game](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game.md) in [2001](../_/2001.md) to coincide with the release of the [film adaptations](<../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md>), bringing along *Middle-earth CCG'*s line editor [Mike Reynolds](Mike_Reynolds.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Overview {#Overview}

In its basic form, it is a game for one to five players, each choosing one of the [five wizards](../I/Istari.md) to represent themselves. A *turn* in the game consists of one player's wandering around Middle-earth with the help of famous characters of Middle-earth, trying to gather influence and power to aid in the battle against [The Dark Lord](../S/Sauron.md), while another player tries to harass, and ultimately kill his *characters* with specific *hazard* cards. Then, for each player's turn, the roles change clockwise around the table (the player who was moving his characters then throws hazards at the player to his or her right). Any player may also win by acquiring and destroying [The One Ring](../T/The_One_Ring.md) after a complicated set of events.

Later expansions have added the possibility to take the role of a [Ringwraith](../N/Nazgûl.md) of [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) (*[The Lidless Eye](Middle-earth%253A_The_Lidless_Eye.md)* set), a corrupted wizard playing by his own rules (*[The White Hand](Middle-earth%253A_The_White_Hand.md)* expansion), the malevolent [Balrog](../B/Balrogs.md) (*[The Balrog](Middle-earth%253A_The_Balrog.md)* expansion), or even Sauron himself. The game is somewhat distinguished from most other CCG's with the use of two six-sided dice for a random factor and also by the actual map of Middle-earth, including *regions* your companies travel through, and *site cards* they visit.

### Rarity {#Rarity}

Like the majority of collectible card games, individual cards in *Middle-earth CCG* occurred more or less often in a booster or starter deck (except in *[The Balrog](Middle-earth%253A_The_Balrog.md)* expansion). To describe the rarity of a card, [ICE](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md) used a code system:

- **R** = Rare.
- **U** = Uncommon.
- **C** = Common.
- **F#** or **Fixed#** = Part of a fixed set of cards (of a starter deck).

These abbreviations were often combined with second abbreviations (e.g., "CB2"). For more information on the second abbreviations, which varied in the different expansion and basic sets, see the Card list sections for the individual card sets below.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Production {#Production}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/M/MECCG_-_Card_Back.png.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/f/fd/MECCG_-_Card_Back.png/300px-MECCG_-_Card_Back.png" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="412" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/f/fd/MECCG_-_Card_Back.png/450px-MECCG_-_Card_Back.png 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/f/fd/MECCG_-_Card_Back.png/600px-MECCG_-_Card_Back.png 2x" data-file-width="1390" data-file-height="1907"/></a><figcaption>Card back of <i>Middle-earth CCG</i></figcaption></figure>

After the increasing success of [Magic: The Gathering](Magic%253A_The_Gathering.md), the arrangement [Iron Crown Enterprises](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md) had to jointly produce a Lord of the Rings card game with [Wizards of the Coast](../W/Wizards_of_the_Coast.md) fell through. ICE then decided to do it by itself. Looking at the existing options on the market, they quickly agreed on two core ideas: characters would be at the forefront, and the game would need a “sense of movement”. From this they settled on centring the game around the [Wizards](../W/Wizards.md). [Coleman Charlton](../C/Coleman_Charlton.md) then spent “a month or a month and a half” on designing the basic gameplay, leading towards the game's alpha phase in which test games were played with printed-out prototype decks. Felon said that of all the playtesters, the foreign groups provided more “bang for the buck”, bringing in different perspectives and being forward-thinking when it came to eventually having to translate card texts.<sup id="cite_ref-inquestscenes_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inquestscenes-5">[5]</a></sup>

### Card design and artwork {#Card_design_and_artwork}

During the playtests, [Pete Fenlon](../P/Pete_Fenlon.md) went looking for artwork. According to Fenlon: “Because of the nature of the setting, word of mouth sort of spread around, and people started coming to us.” Overseas playtesting groups led to many artist suggestions. Germans led them to artist [Jo Hartwig](../J/Jo_Hartwig.md), who ended up creating a giant map of Middle-earth, as well as 52 region cards. Italians suggested [Stefano Baldo](../S/Stefano_Baldo.md) and [Angelo Montanini](../A/Angelo_Montanini.md). Meanwhile, from their own United of America, artist [Donato Giancola](../D/Donato_Giancola.md) was hired based on an old portfolio. [Liz Danforth](../L/Liz_Danforth.md) had already done illustrations for [Middle-earth Role Playing](Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md), and was also eager to join the project. It was up to art director [Jessica M. Ney-Grimm](../J/Jessica_M._Ney-Grimm.md) to coordinate the massive effort. So many pieces were sent in that they had a wealth of choice. “We actually rejected pieces by a lot of good artists, having them redo them, or even shifting the pieces to another artist“, said Fenlon. Rejections were usually related to details that didn't match, which led to Ney-Grimm sending out explicit text descriptions.<sup id="cite_ref-inquestscenes_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inquestscenes-5">[5]</a></sup>

Artists sent their original works to Iron Crown Enterprises, and after photographing the works were returned to the artists. Because digital cameras in the 1990s lacked the necessary resolution and quality, the artwork was photographed on film. This could, for example, be <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_film" title="wikipedia:Sheet film">4 in × 5 in format film</a>. To ensure colour accuracy, a control bar with known colour values was included in the photograph. <sup id="cite_ref-processing_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-processing-6">[6]</a></sup>

The visual designs of the cards themselves was handled by graphic designers <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Derek_Carbonneau&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Derek Carbonneau (page does not exist)">Derek Carbonneau</a> and <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Nick_Morawitz&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Nick Morawitz (page does not exist)">Nick Morawitz</a>. They decided to give each card type a distinct border texture: resources had copper, hazards had iron, sites and regions had parchment, while characters had stone. Site cards were given a backside taken from Hartwig's map, while the regular cards had the [Eye of Sauron](../E/Eye_of_Sauron.md), though both shared the same border.<sup id="cite_ref-inquestscenes_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inquestscenes-5">[5]</a></sup>

Its with those designs that, after colour corrections of the artwork, the final card would be composited with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QuarkXPress" title="wikipedia:QuarkXPress">QuarkXPress</a>, which was the software used for all graphic design, resulting in a high-quality digital print. This was then compared to the original artwork, a process which was repeated until the digital print was a satisfactory match to the painting.<sup id="cite_ref-processing_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-processing-6">[6]</a></sup>

A final digital version of the card image would then be sent to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartamundi" title="wikipedia:Cartamundi">Cartamundi</a> for printing.<sup id="cite_ref-processing_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-processing-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Products {#Products}

### Card sets {#Card_sets}

- [1995](../_/1995.md): <i><b><a href="Middle-earth%253A_The_Wizards.md" title="Middle-earth: The Wizards">The Wizards</a></b></i> (METW) - first basic set
    - [1996](../_/1996.md): *[The Dragons](Middle-earth%253A_The_Dragons.md)* (METD) - expansion set
    - [1996](../_/1996.md): *[Dark Minions](Middle-earth%253A_Dark_Minions.md)* (MEDM) - expansion set
- [1997](../_/1997.md): <i><b><a href="Middle-earth%253A_The_Lidless_Eye.md" title="Middle-earth: The Lidless Eye">The Lidless Eye</a></b></i> (MELE) - second basic set
    - [1997](../_/1997.md): *[Against the Shadow](Middle-earth%253A_Against_the_Shadow.md)* (MEAS) - expansion set
- [1997](../_/1997.md): <i><b><a href="Middle-earth%253A_The_White_Hand.md" title="Middle-earth: The White Hand">The White Hand</a></b></i> (MEWH)
- [1998](../_/1998.md): <i><b><a href="Middle-earth%253A_The_Balrog.md" title="Middle-earth: The Balrog">The Balrog</a></b></i> (MEBA)

Two more expansion sets were designed, but never reached publication: *The Dwarf-lords* and *The Elf-lords*. In addition, a complete revision of the MECCG was planned to be released in 1999, which was to be named *Lord of the Rings Expandable Card Game* (LORE CG).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Before the card game's release, a set titled *War Hosts* was announced as the set following *The Dragons*.<sup id="cite_ref-inquestscenes_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-inquestscenes-5">[5]</a></sup>
