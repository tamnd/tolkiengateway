**Mithe** was a body of water in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), the outflow of the [Shirebourn](/wiki/Shirebourn) river.

At the Mithe there was a landing-stage called [Mithe Steps](/wiki/Mithe_Steps),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> from which a lane ran to [Deephallow](/wiki/Deephallow) and so on to the [Causeway](/wiki/Causeway) road that went through [Rushey](/wiki/Rushey) and [Stock](/wiki/Stock).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Andreas Möhn](/wiki/Andreas_M%C3%B6hn) has suggested that *Mithe* means "Place where two streams meet", derived from [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mu%C3%BE" title="wiktionary:muþ">mūþ</a></i> or <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gemy%C3%BE" title="wiktionary:gemyþ">ġemȳþ</a></i> "river-mouth, meeting of streams". Möhn adds that *Mithe* "is evidently related to 'mouth' and probably a derivative surviving in English place-names".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The name is probably unrelated to the [Middle English](/wiki/Middle_English) verb <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mithen" title="wiktionary:mithen">mithen</a></i> which meant to hide, conceal or avoid.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>
