<i><b>Mallorn</b></i> is a literary and academic journal published annually by [The Tolkien Society](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Society). It is the sister publication of *[Amon Hen](</wiki/Amon_Hen_(journal)> "Amon Hen (journal)")*.

## History {#History}

*Mallorn* was founded in [October](/wiki/October) [1970](/wiki/1970) as the successor to [Vera Chapman](/wiki/Vera_Chapman)'s *Belladonna's Broadsheet* to be the magazine of [The Tolkien Society](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Society). However, due to the difficulty in producing *Mallorn* regularly, its functions as a bulletin to Society members were moved to the new publication of *[Amon Hen](</wiki/Amon_Hen_(journal)> "Amon Hen (journal)")* (the first issue was known as *[Henneth Annûn](/wiki/Henneth_Ann%C3%BBn_1 "Henneth Annûn 1")*); this enabled *Mallorn* to fashion itself into the more scholarly publication that exists today.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Content {#Content}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>[Mallorn] considers reviews, comment, scholarly articles, original poetry, artwork and original fiction (excluding fan fiction). Unsolicited material is welcome
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i>Mallorn</i>, no. <a href="/wiki/Mallorn_46" title="Mallorn 46">46</a>, Autumn <a href="/wiki/2008" title="2008">2008</a>, p. 3</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Although the description of potential articles has little changed over the years, *Mallorn'*s focus has traditionally been on scholarly articles. There has been recent criticism from Society members that both poetry<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and academic articles are being "ostracised" under the new editor.<sup id="cite_ref-AH213_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AH213-3">[3]</a></sup> Gee does not refute this by saying that he is reluctant to publish poetry because he does not see himself as a competent judge, therefore the publishing of poetry is "extremely unlikely",<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> however, he has since "relented" and publishes poetry based on whether he likes it or not.<sup id="cite_ref-Mallorn_47_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mallorn_47-5">[5]</a></sup>

Due to publishing limitations, Gee also recommends that scholarly articles should be "shorter and more accessible" (unless they are "truly stellar pieces of scholarship") in order to fit in more articles which appeal to a broader range of membership.<sup id="cite_ref-AH213_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AH213-3">[3]</a></sup> In his third issue as editor, Gee re-iterated that articles should be shorter (maximum of 4,000 words for a commentary piece; 1,000 words for a review), and that he would not be publishing serialisations.<sup id="cite_ref-Mallorn_47_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mallorn_47-5">[5]</a></sup> Gee's rationale is:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The publication landscape has been changed by the very welcome appearance of <i><a href="/wiki/Tolkien_Studies_(journal)" title="Tolkien Studies (journal)">Tolkien Studies</a></i>. This has set a new and generally high standard for Tolkien scholarship, challenging <i>Mallorn</i> to raise its game. However, <i>Tolkien Studies</i> means that <i>Mallorn</i> doesn&#39;t have to take the entire world of Tolkien scholarship on its shoulders, and can discover for itself a new freedom to broaden its scope, and become a more entertaining and accessible publication of which Society members can be justifiably proud.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Henry Gee in <i><a href="/wiki/Amon_Hen_(journal)" title="Amon Hen (journal)">Amon Hen</a></i>, no. <a href="/wiki/Amon_Hen_211" title="Amon Hen 211">211</a>, <a href="/wiki/May" title="May">May</a> <a href="/wiki/2008" title="2008">2008</a>, pp. 28-9</cite></p>
</blockquote>
