**Minas Morgul** (originally called **Minas Ithil**) was the twin city of [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith) before its fall to the forces of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). It was situated some way within the narrow [Morgul Valley](/wiki/Morgul_Vale "Morgul Vale"), guarding the pass between [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) and [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor).

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

In its prime, Minas Ithil was a beautiful place. It sat high on a rocky seat "upon the black knees" of the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath), overlooking the valley. Moonlight reflected off the marble walls of the city so that it seemed to shine. The tall tower of the city had many windows, and the topmost part rotated slowly from one side to the other. A white bridge crossed the nearby [river](/wiki/Morgulduin "Morgulduin"), with large meadows on both banks of the stream.

Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a></figcaption></figure>

After being taken by the forces of evil, the city became terrible, and the valley was filled with decay. The city was lit by a pale white light, and the gate was shaped like an open mouth with gleaming teeth and eyes. The meadows were filled with deadly<sup id="cite_ref-Council2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council2-1">[1]</a></sup> white flowers emitting a sickly odour. At the head of the bridge crossing the [Morgulduin](/wiki/Morgulduin) were corrupted human and bestial statues. The river steamed a poisonous, deadly cold vapour, and the water was undrinkable.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The city was accessed by the [Morgul-road](/wiki/Morgul-road), which ran from Osgiliath to the crossroads and along the north side of the [Morgulduin](/wiki/Morgulduin) until across from the city, where it crossed the valley on a white bridge before wounding up to the city gate in the northern wall.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">[5]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

After the destruction of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) and [Anárion](/wiki/An%C3%A1rion), the sons of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil), landed in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). Isildur built Minas Ithil near the mountainous border of [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) from where he would rule the fief of [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien); while Anárion built [Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Anor) to rule over the fief of [Anórien](/wiki/An%C3%B3rien). Minas Ithil housed the [Ithil-stone](/wiki/Ithil-stone), a *[palantír](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri "Palantíri").*<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

When [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) returned after escaping [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor)'s destruction, he attacked the exiles of Númenor, and his forces [took Minas Ithil](/wiki/First_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil "First Fall of Minas Ithil") by storm. When the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) defeated Sauron in the year [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3429](/wiki/Second_Age_3429 "Second Age 3429"), Minas Ithil was restored as a watchtower.

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

After the defeat of the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) in the north, he returned to Mordor in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1980](/wiki/Third_Age_1980 "Third Age 1980"), summoning the other [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) with him to prepare the return of their master. A few years later ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2000](/wiki/Third_Age_2000 "Third Age 2000")) they laid siege to Minas Ithil and [two years later](/wiki/Third_Age_2002 "Third Age 2002") they managed to [take the city](/wiki/Second_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil "Second Fall of Minas Ithil").<sup id="cite_ref-ta_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ta-7">[7]</a></sup> The [Ithil-stone](/wiki/Ithil-stone) was also captured and later used by Sauron to influence [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman) and [Denethor II](/wiki/Denethor "Denethor").<sup id="cite_ref-ta_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ta-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

Minas Ithil was occupied by fell creatures, and it changed into a foul, evil place. As a result, it came to be called Minas Morgul, which in the [tongue of Gondor](/wiki/Gondor_Sindarin "Gondor Sindarin") means "Tower of Dark Sorcery". Many [Gondorians](/wiki/Gondorians) fled from [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien).<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[9]</a></sup>

From Minas Morgul the Lord of the Nazgûl twice challenged his old enemy, King [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur). Finally Eärnur rode with his knights against Morgul. He never returned, ending the line of the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-ta_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ta-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-9">[9]</a></sup>

During the [Watchful Peace](/wiki/Watchful_Peace), the lords of Morgul had secretly bred the [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai), and in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2475](/wiki/Third_Age_2475 "Third Age 2475") these creatures assailed and overran Ithilien.

### War of the Ring {#War_of_the_Ring}

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), a host from Minas Morgul set forth toward [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith). The host was witnessed by Frodo, Sam and Gollum. After some [opposition in Osgiliath](/wiki/Faramir%27s_defence_of_Osgiliath "Faramir's defence of Osgiliath"), the Morgul-host proceeded to the [Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Pelennor_Fields) and laid [siege](/wiki/Siege_of_Gondor "Siege of Gondor") to Minas Tirith. However the host, including the city's garrison was devastated during the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields).

Following the battle, the [Army of the West](/wiki/Army_of_the_West) pulled down the bridge leading to [Morgul Vale](/wiki/Morgul_Vale) and set its fields aflame. They met no opposition as the entire city's garrison had been killed at the Battle.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

After the War of the Ring, [Faramir](/wiki/Faramir) was made [Prince of Ithilien](/wiki/Prince_of_Ithilien) by King [Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn"), who advised him to make his abode in the [Emyn Arnen](/wiki/Emyn_Arnen) south-east of Minas Tirith for "Minas Ithil in Morgul Vale shall be utterly destroyed, and though it may in time to come be made clean, no man may dwell there for many long years".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> It is not known if the city was ever rebuilt.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

**Minas Morgul** is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name. It means "Tower of Sorcery".<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Council_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-13">[13]</a></sup> It is a compound of *[minas](/wiki/Minas "Minas")* ("tower") and *[morgul](/wiki/Morgul "Morgul")* ("black sorcery"), *Morgul* is a compound of *[mor(n)](/wiki/Mor "Mor")* ("black") and *[gul](/wiki/Gul "Gul")* ("sorcery, black arts").<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

**Minas Ithil** is another Sindarin name. It means "Tower of the Moon"<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> or "Tower of the Rising Moon".<sup id="cite_ref-Council_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-13">[13]</a></sup> It is a compound of *[minas](/wiki/Minas "Minas")* ("tower") and *[ithil](/wiki/Moon#Other_names "Moon")* ("moon").

In [Orkish](/wiki/Orkish), Minas Morgul was known as **Dushgoi**.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In earlier drafts, Minas Morgul was called Minas Morghul,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> and guarded [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol) (then called Kirith Ungol) instead of the [Tower of Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Tower_of_Cirith_Ungol).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>The <i><a href="/wiki/Minas_Ithil_(book)" title="Minas Ithil (book)">Minas Ithil</a></i> city module contains detailed information about Minas Ithil in T.A. 1640 and three adventures that take place in Minas Ithil.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><i><a href="/wiki/The_Kin-strife" title="The Kin-strife">The Kin-strife</a> campaign module contains detailed information about Minas Ithil during the time of the <a href="/wiki/Kin-strife" title="Kin-strife">Kin-strife</a> and three adventures that take place in Minas Ithil.</i></dd></dl>

<b>2001: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Nazgûl are shown exiting the fortress, beginning their search for <a href="/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins" title="Bilbo Baggins">Bilbo Baggins</a> who is in possession of <a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring" title="The One Ring">The One Ring</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King">The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="/wiki/Frodo_Baggins" title="Frodo Baggins">Frodo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee" title="Samwise Gamgee">Sam</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gollum" title="Gollum">Gollum</a> pass the fortress during their journey to <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Mordor</a>. It is portrayed as dark with sharp walls, illuminated by a green, hazy light. Frodo, influenced by the power of the Ring, approaches the entrance to the city, but is stopped by Sam and Gollum. A large army and the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Witch_King" title="Witch King">Witch King</a> mounted on a flying <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Fell_beast" title="Fell beast">fell beast</a> are shown exiting the city, signifying the start of the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields" title="Battle of the Pelennor Fields">Battle of the Pelennor Fields</a>.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="/wiki/2017" title="2017">2017</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_War" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of War">Middle-earth: Shadow of War</a></i>:</b>

<b><a href="/wiki/2019" title="2019">2019</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>During the march of the Host of the West towards the <a href="/wiki/Black_Gate" title="Black Gate">Black Gate</a> <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> destroys the bridge leading out of Minas Morgul to prevent <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a>&#39;s armies from being flanked. After the downfall of Sauron this action has an unintentional effect of protecting the Dead City itself from any attack from the west. Following Aragorn&#39;s coronation, King Elessar charges steward <a href="/wiki/Faramir" title="Faramir">Faramir</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Rangers_of_Ithilien" title="Rangers of Ithilien">Rangers of Ithilien</a> with reclaiming the <a href="/wiki/Morgul_Vale" title="Morgul Vale">Morgul Vale</a> and cleansing the Dead City.</dd></dl>
