**Mermaids**, or water spirits, were creatures only mentioned fleetingly in the earliest versions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") and linguistic writings of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE12_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE12-2">[2]</a></sup> Other names for these creatures were **foam-maidens**, **foam-fays**, **foam-riders**,<sup id="cite_ref-MC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC-3">[3]</a></sup> and **nymphs**.<sup id="cite_ref-PE12_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE12-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Mermaids apparently lived in caverns in the sea, that where down to the path of the Sun.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Mermaids are mentioned in the incomplete drafts of *[The Tale of Eärendel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_E%C3%A4rendel)*. Young [Eärendel](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel) befriends the Oarni at the [Mouths of Sirion](/wiki/Mouths_of_Sirion), who love him. Later however he is shipwrecked at the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Fiord_of_the_Mermaid&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Fiord of the Mermaid (page does not exist)">Fiord of the Mermaid</a> by the treachery of [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB) and another kind of mermaids enchant his sailors; Tolkien noted that they were possibly "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Earthlings&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Earthlings (page does not exist)">earthlings</a> or [fays](/wiki/Fays "Fays")? - or both".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup> Later the Oarni save Eärendel at [Falasquil](/wiki/Falasquil) when Ossë wrecked him again. Finally, it is mentioned that the Oarni helped him build [Wingilot](/wiki/Wingilot) at Falasquil, with which he sailed for [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor).

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The mermaids were part of what apparently was a three-fold division of the lesser [Ainu](/wiki/Ainur "Ainur") spirits: [sylphs](/wiki/Sylphs "Sylphs") (spirits of the air), [sprites](/wiki/Sprites "Sprites") (spirits of the earth), and water spirits.<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-6">[5]</a></sup>

In poems, the foam-fays are described as having "blossom-white hair" shining "in the light of the sun", "pale arms on the sea's bosom", and to be "chanting wild songs".<sup id="cite_ref-MC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC-3">[3]</a></sup>

Ui (the precursor of [Uinen](/wiki/Uinen)) was given the title *Oarista* ("Queen of Mermaids").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

## Names and divisions {#Names_and_divisions}

A basic division of two groups of water spirits, with their [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) names, are given as follow:

- <i><b>Oarni</b></i> (troop of [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB)): "spirits of the sea"; also <i><b>Oaritsi</b></i> (from *oaris*, "mermaid")<sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-9">[8]</a></sup>
- <i><b>Falmaríni</b></i> and <i><b>Wingildi</b></i> (troop of [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB)): "spirits of the sea-foam"<sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-9">[8]</a></sup> (also <i><b>wingildin</b></i>, "foam-maidens, foam-fays";<sup id="cite_ref-MC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC-3">[3]</a></sup> <i><b>wingild-</b></i> "nymph",<sup id="cite_ref-PE12_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE12-2">[2]</a></sup> or "foam-maiden";<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> *wingi* "mermaid, foam-maid";<sup id="cite_ref-PE21_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE21-11">[10]</a></sup> and *wingil* "sea-nymph"<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>)

Also mentioned are *flathwin* or *-nir* ("foam fay"), and *nendil*, *nennil* ("waterfay").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup> A [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) gloss is given as *nindari* ("river-maid, nymph").<sup id="cite_ref-PE21_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE21-11">[10]</a></sup>

## Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

Mermaids are not mentioned in the later versions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"). Many of the classes of beings references in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)* were superseded by the concept of the [Maiar](/wiki/Maiar), which were not introduced until the reworking of the [Annals of Valinor](/wiki/Annals_of_Valinor) into the [Annals of Aman](/wiki/Annals_of_Aman).<sup id="cite_ref-MRP2b_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MRP2b-14">[13]</a></sup>

## Inspiration and other mentions {#Inspiration_and_other_mentions}

[Patrick H. Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_H._Wynne) and [Christopher Gilson](/wiki/Christopher_Gilson) have noted that Tolkien's use of nymphs (and related spirits) is likely influenced by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus" title="wikipedia:Paracelsus">Paracelsus</a>'s chart of four elementals: *sylphs* (air), *pygmies* or *gnomes* (earth), *nymphs* (water), and *salamanders* (fire).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

According to [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis), Tolkien once told him that historical people in a sense did not falsely claim to see "nymphs in the fountains and woods and <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Dryads" title="Dryads">dryads</a> in the wood", since the production of food was closely connected to one's own land and territory: people came to internalise the nature surrounding them.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup>
