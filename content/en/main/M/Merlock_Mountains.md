The **Merlock Mountains** are mentioned in the [Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")-poem *[The Mewlips](/wiki/The_Mewlips)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is unknown if the Merlock Mountains refer to an actual mountain range in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), or if they were just an invention by the poet. As the poem describes a location similar to [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> it is possible that they were a fictitious version of the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains), or what the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) knew about them.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom") have speculated that the name *Merlock* was inspired by the name *Morlock* appearing in "Knocking at the Door", an earlier version of the poem (which was perhaps itself inspired by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells" title="wikipedia:H. G. Wells">H.G. Wells</a>'s <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morlock" title="wikipedia:Morlock">Morlock</a></i>) <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
