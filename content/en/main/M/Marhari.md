**Marhari** was a [Northman](../N/Northmen.md) descended from the [Kingdom of Rhovanion](../R/Rhovanion.md) and a member of the royal house. His distant ancestor was King [Vidugavia](../V/Vidugavia.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1851](../T/Third_Age_1851.md),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> a tribe of [Easterlings](../E/Easterlings.md) called the [Wainriders](../W/Wainriders.md) began invading from the [East](../E/East.md). Marhari led the soldiers of Rhovanion, together with the armies of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) under King [Narmacil II](../N/Narmacil_II.md), against the invaders at the [Battle of the Plains](../B/Battle_of_the_Plains.md)<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-3">[3]</a></sup> in [1856](../T/Third_Age_1856.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The battle was a disaster, as Narmacil was killed and Marhari fell in his rearguard. The Wainriders then overran Rhovanion and enslaved its people.<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-3">[3]</a></sup>

Marhari's son [Marhwini](Marhwini.md) led a group of refugees west to the [Vales of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md). They would become the Éothéod, the ancestors of the mighty [Riders of Rohan](../R/Riders_of_Rohan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) suggests that the name *Marhari* is "[Gothic](../G/Gothic.md) in form" and that it contains the Gothic element *marh* ("horse"), which corresponds to [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *mearh* ("horse") whose plural is *mearas*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

[David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) and [Arden R. Smith](../A/Arden_R._Smith.md) suggest that the name is early [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) in form.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

It is also possible that it is an Old High German (OHG) name, which means "horse-soldier" (literally "horse-army(man)") and consists of the element *marh*, which corresponds to Gothic <i>*marhi</i> ("horse"), and the element *hari* ("army"), which corresponds to Gothic *harjis* ("army").<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) the element *hari* became *here* and is seen in the later [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md) name [Dúnhere](../D/Dúnhere.md), which translates as "hill warrior".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

David Salo suggests that it could mean "He who has an army of horses" or "cavalry general". [Tom Shippey](../T/Tom_Shippey.md) suggests that it means "horse army" and that it is not Gothic, but in a hypothetic reconstructed language, in proto-germanic.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
