**Marhari** was a [Northman](/wiki/Northmen "Northmen") descended from the [Kingdom of Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion "Rhovanion") and a member of the royal house. His distant ancestor was King [Vidugavia](/wiki/Vidugavia).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1851](/wiki/Third_Age_1851 "Third Age 1851"),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> a tribe of [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) called the [Wainriders](/wiki/Wainriders) began invading from the [East](/wiki/East). Marhari led the soldiers of Rhovanion, together with the armies of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) under King [Narmacil II](/wiki/Narmacil_II), against the invaders at the [Battle of the Plains](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Plains)<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-3">[3]</a></sup> in [1856](/wiki/Third_Age_1856 "Third Age 1856").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The battle was a disaster, as Narmacil was killed and Marhari fell in his rearguard. The Wainriders then overran Rhovanion and enslaved its people.<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-3">[3]</a></sup>

Marhari's son [Marhwini](/wiki/Marhwini) led a group of refugees west to the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin). They would become the Éothéod, the ancestors of the mighty [Riders of Rohan](/wiki/Riders_of_Rohan).<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) suggests that the name *Marhari* is "[Gothic](/wiki/Gothic) in form" and that it contains the Gothic element *marh* ("horse"), which corresponds to [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *mearh* ("horse") whose plural is *mearas*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

[David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) and [Arden R. Smith](/wiki/Arden_R._Smith) suggest that the name is early [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) in form.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

It is also possible that it is an Old High German (OHG) name, which means "horse-soldier" (literally "horse-army(man)") and consists of the element *marh*, which corresponds to Gothic <i>*marhi</i> ("horse"), and the element *hari* ("army"), which corresponds to Gothic *harjis* ("army").<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) the element *hari* became *here* and is seen in the later [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) name [Dúnhere](/wiki/D%C3%BAnhere), which translates as "hill warrior".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

David Salo suggests that it could mean "He who has an army of horses" or "cavalry general". [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) suggests that it means "horse army" and that it is not Gothic, but in a hypothetic reconstructed language, in proto-germanic.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
