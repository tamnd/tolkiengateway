<i><b>már</b></i> (pl. <i><b>mardi</b></i>) means "dwelling" in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:64</sup>

It was used with a defining genitive or in a genitival compound. If added to a personal name, it referred to the residence of the named head of a family and included the adjacent lands attached to permanent buildings and dwellings (ex. [Ingwemar](/wiki/Ingwemar)). When added to the name of a people, it referred to the whole area occupied by them (ex. [Valimar](/wiki/Valimar), [Eldamar](/wiki/Eldamar)). In many cases it thus became synonymous with [nórë](/wiki/N%C3%B3r%C3%AB "Nórë").<sup id="cite_ref-dwell_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dwell-2">[2]</a></sup>

There are several specifically defined meanings where it did *not* apply. It was not used in the context of a single person.<sup id="cite_ref-dwell_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dwell-2">[2]</a></sup> It was not used to mean a small separate building with a specific function, such as a bake-house or wood-house, which was usually called "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=K%C3%B6a&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Köa (page does not exist)">köa</a>". It was also not used to mean "house" in the sense of a family of power or authority, which was instead represented by words meaning "kindred".<sup id="cite_ref-dwell_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dwell-2">[2]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:fn3</sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

From [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [MBAR](/wiki/MBAR) "dwelling, habitation".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Notes {#Notes}
