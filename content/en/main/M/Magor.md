**Magor** was a son of [Malach Aradan](/wiki/Malach_Aradan), and the third head of the [House of Marach](/wiki/House_of_Marach).

## History {#History}

Magor led a part of his [father's people](/wiki/Folk_of_Marach "Folk of Marach") away from [Hithlum](/wiki/Hithlum) and southward down the [River Sirion](/wiki/Sirion "Sirion"). They settled among the southern foothills of the [Ered Wethrin](/wiki/Ered_Wethrin),<sup id="cite_ref-West_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-West-3">[3]</a></sup> near the sources of river [Teiglin](/wiki/Teiglin).<sup id="cite_ref-New_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-New-1">[1]</a></sup>

Magor's grandson [Hador](/wiki/Hador) and his people returned over the mountains and entered the service of the Elven [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) High-king [Fingolfin](/wiki/Fingolfin), and Magor's grandson was soon made [Lord of Dor-lómin](/wiki/Lord_of_Dor-l%C3%B3min). From Magor's famous grandson his people took Hador's name and re-named the House of Marach to the [House of Hador](/wiki/House_of_Hador).<sup id="cite_ref-West_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-West-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

<i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Magor_(word)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Magor (word) (page does not exist)">magor</a></i> is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "the Swordsman".<sup id="cite_ref-Wise_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wise-4">[4]</a></sup>

His epithet <i><b>Dagorlind</b></i> means "Singer in Battle",<sup id="cite_ref-Wise_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wise-4">[4]</a></sup> clearly a combination of *[dagor](/wiki/Dagor "Dagor")* ("battle") + *[lind](/wiki/Lind "Lind")* ("song, music; singer").

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In later writings, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) intended to switch places between Magor and [Hador](/wiki/Hador), so that Hador was to be the son of Malach Aradan, and Magor (nicknamed *Dagorlind*) the father of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Galdor" title="Galdor">Galdor</a>, [Gundor](/wiki/Gundor) and [Glóredhel](/wiki/Gl%C3%B3redhel). In that conception, the establishment of the lordship of [Dor-lómin](/wiki/Dor-l%C3%B3min) was moved two generations back. However, that change was never properly incorporated in the texts.<sup id="cite_ref-Wise_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wise-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
