**Milton Waldman** ([1895](/wiki/1895)-[1976](/wiki/1976)) was born in the United States and was an adviser and senior editor at the London publisher <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Collins (page does not exist)">Collins</a>.

Waldman was introduced to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) by Father [Gervase Mathew](/wiki/Gervase_Mathew) in autumn [1949](/wiki/1949). Due to the success of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, Waldman expressed interest in both *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* and *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. Tolkien showed parts of *The Silmarillion* to Waldman who offered to publish it if Tolkien would complete it. He would also publish *The Lord of the Rings* if Tolkien felt no commitment to [George Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_and_Unwin "Allen and Unwin").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In April of [1950](/wiki/1950), Tolkien urged [Stanley Unwin](/wiki/Stanley_Unwin) to agree to publish both books, and when Unwin refused,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Tolkien took them to Waldman, hoping to begin typesetting the following autumn. But there were delays, largely caused by Waldman's frequent absences in [Italy](/wiki/Italy) and his bad health; his colleagues back in England were much less enthusiastic about Tolkien's works. To Tolkien's disappointment, Waldman had told him that *The Lord of the Rings* was too long, and although Tolkien said he would try to make cuts, there is no evidence he entered such a process.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> By the latter part of [1951](/wiki/1951) no definite arrangements for publication had yet been made, and Collins were becoming anxious about the combined length of both books. At Waldman's request to know better his [Secondary world](/wiki/Secondary_world), Tolkien wrote a 10,000 words-long [Letter to Milton Waldman](/wiki/Letter_to_Milton_Waldman) with the intention of demonstrating that *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Silmarillion* were interdependent and indivisible.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Waldman was interested so much that he had a typed copy made<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> but eventually still refused.

In [1956](/wiki/1956), after *The Lord of the Rings* has been published, Waldman unsuccessfully attempted to publish *The Hobbit* in Collin's Fontana paperback series.
