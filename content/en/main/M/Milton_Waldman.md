**Milton Waldman** ([1895](../_/1895.md)-[1976](../_/1976.md)) was born in the United States and was an adviser and senior editor at the London publisher <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Collins (page does not exist)">Collins</a>.

Waldman was introduced to [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) by Father [Gervase Mathew](../G/Gervase_Mathew.md) in autumn [1949](../_/1949.md). Due to the success of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)*, Waldman expressed interest in both *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* and *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*. Tolkien showed parts of *The Silmarillion* to Waldman who offered to publish it if Tolkien would complete it. He would also publish *The Lord of the Rings* if Tolkien felt no commitment to [George Allen &amp; Unwin](../A/Allen_and_Unwin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In April of [1950](../_/1950.md), Tolkien urged [Stanley Unwin](../S/Stanley_Unwin.md) to agree to publish both books, and when Unwin refused,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Tolkien took them to Waldman, hoping to begin typesetting the following autumn. But there were delays, largely caused by Waldman's frequent absences in [Italy](../I/Italy.md) and his bad health; his colleagues back in England were much less enthusiastic about Tolkien's works. To Tolkien's disappointment, Waldman had told him that *The Lord of the Rings* was too long, and although Tolkien said he would try to make cuts, there is no evidence he entered such a process.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> By the latter part of [1951](../_/1951.md) no definite arrangements for publication had yet been made, and Collins were becoming anxious about the combined length of both books. At Waldman's request to know better his [Secondary world](../S/Secondary_world.md), Tolkien wrote a 10,000 words-long [Letter to Milton Waldman](../L/Letter_to_Milton_Waldman.md) with the intention of demonstrating that *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Silmarillion* were interdependent and indivisible.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Waldman was interested so much that he had a typed copy made<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> but eventually still refused.

In [1956](../_/1956.md), after *The Lord of the Rings* has been published, Waldman unsuccessfully attempted to publish *The Hobbit* in Collin's Fontana paperback series.
