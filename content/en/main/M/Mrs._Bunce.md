**Mrs. Bunce** was an old [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") inhabitant of the town of [Delving](/wiki/Delving) who appeared in *[Perry-the-Winkle](/wiki/Perry-the-Winkle)*, a nursery rhyme<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> by [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee)<sup id="cite_ref-preface_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-preface-3">[3]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the poem, Mrs. Bunce was "walking the street" in the early morning when she was greeted by the [Lonely Troll](/wiki/Lonely_Troll) who came into town to make a friend. As a consequence, she dropped her umbrella and basket, and "yelled a frightful yell" before running home to hide "beneath her bed". Her yell was heard by [Pott the Mayor](/wiki/Pott_the_Mayor), who dived underground in fear.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

Later, Mrs. Bunce was among the [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits) who travelled to <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Faraway&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Faraway (page does not exist)">Faraway</a> in order to demand food from the Lonely Troll. Mrs. Bunce was among four Hobbits who the Troll never wanted to see again.<sup id="cite_ref-Perry_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Perry-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In the first version of the poem in early [1928](/wiki/1928),<sup id="cite_ref-commentary_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-commentary-4">[4]</a></sup> which was entitled *The Bumpus*, Mrs. Bunce was called **Mrs. Thomas** and had the same role as in the latest version. However, in the first version, when the people all line up at the Bumpus's door, Mrs. Thomas is described as being "late and last" with "her bonnet" being askew.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> No changes to the character occurred in the second version on [29 March](/wiki/29_March),<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> in which Tolkien revised the poem as part<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> of his *[Tales and Songs of Bimble Bay](/wiki/Tales_and_Songs_of_Bimble_Bay)* series. The third version also retained the same details in relation to Mrs. Thomas.<sup id="cite_ref-commentary_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-commentary-4">[4]</a></sup>

In early [1961](/wiki/1961),<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> when Tolkien revised the poem for inclusion within *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil)*, Mrs. Thomas was given the name Mrs. Bunce and her role was very slightly reduced.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2025: <i><a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring_(role-playing_game)" title="The One Ring (role-playing game)">The One Ring</a> — <a href="/wiki/Hobbit_Tales" title="Hobbit Tales">Hobbit Tales</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2960" title="Third Age 2960">2960</a>, Thomas and Gilda Bunce were the proprietors of the Stone&#39;s Throw, an inn near the <a href="/wiki/Three-Farthing_Stone" title="Three-Farthing Stone">Three-Farthing Stone</a>. They have spread rumours about the Stone being haunted or cursed, even dressing up as ghosts, to boost their business.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></dd></dl>
