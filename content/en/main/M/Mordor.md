**Mordor** was a land in the south-east of the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands) east of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). During most of the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and [Third Ages](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") it was ruled by [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and it was his dwelling and base from which he attempted to conquer [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).

Mordor was surrounded by three enormous mountain ridges from the North, from the West and from the South, protecting it from an unexpected invasion by any of the [Free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples).

Only a few times in history did the free peoples have anything to do with Mordor; the [Last Alliance](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men "Last Alliance of Elves and Men") broke into the land to fight Sauron, and millennia later [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) infiltrated that land to destroy [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring").

## Geography {#Geography}

Mordor was protected from three sides by mountain ranges, arranged roughly rectangularly: the [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui) in the north, and the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath) in the west until they turned to the east, forming a southern range.<sup id="cite_ref-FRMap_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRMap-1">[1]</a></sup> The [Morgul Pass](/wiki/Morgul_Pass) led from the [Morgul Vale](/wiki/Morgul_Vale) in [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien), which was guarded by the city of [Minas Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul) (earlier [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil)) through the Ephel Dúath into Mordor; [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol), a higher more difficult pass was guarded by the giant spider [Shelob](/wiki/Shelob) and by the [Tower of Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Tower_of_Cirith_Ungol). Another known fortress was [Durthang](/wiki/Durthang) in the northern Ephel Dúath.<sup id="cite_ref-RKMap_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKMap-2">[2]</a></sup>

In the north-west corner of Mordor the deep valley of [Udûn](</wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)> "Udûn (valley)") was the only entrance for large armies, and that is where Sauron built the [Black Gate](/wiki/Black_Gate) of Mordor. In front of the Black Gate lay the [Dagorlad](/wiki/Dagorlad). Sauron's main fortress [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr) was at the foothills of [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui). To south-west of Barad-dûr lay the arid [Plateau of Gorgoroth](/wiki/Plateau_of_Gorgoroth) and the volcanic [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) (also called [Orodruin](/wiki/Orodruin));<sup id="cite_ref-RKMap_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKMap-2">[2]</a></sup> to the east lay the plain of [Lithlad](/wiki/Lithlad). The land in the western parts of Mordor were largely infertile, producing only sparse [brambles](/wiki/Brambles_of_Mordor "Brambles of Mordor").

The southern part of Mordor, [Nurn](/wiki/Nurn),<sup id="cite_ref-FRMap_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRMap-1">[1]</a></sup> was slighly more fertile, and moist enough to carry the inland [sea of Núrnen](/wiki/Sea_of_N%C3%BArnen "Sea of Núrnen"). Nurn was made somewhat fertile because the ash blown from Mount Doom left its soil nutrient rich, thus allowing dry-land farming. Unfortunately, the inland sea of Núrn was salty, not freshwater.

To the west of Mordor was the narrow land of Ithilien with the great river [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), to the east [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn), and to the south-east, [Khand](/wiki/Khand).<sup id="cite_ref-FRMap_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRMap-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

[Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) created Mount Doom in the long [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), and the name "Mordor" may have been given to the land before Sauron settled there because of its eruptions.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Also in the First Age, the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) migrated westward, and historians in Gondor believed that they came through lands south of Mordor and, after turning northward into Ithilien, became the first Men to cross the Anduin.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The first being known to occupy the mountains of Mordor was Shelob, fleeing from the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) in [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) at the end of the First Age. She fed herself on [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and [Men](/wiki/Men) living or passing nearby until these became scarce.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Sauron settled in Mordor around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1000](/wiki/Second_Age_1000 "Second Age 1000"). In the north-western corner of this land stood Mount Doom, in which [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") would be forged. Near Orodruin he built his stronghold [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr). After this time, Sauron was known as the [Dark Lord of Mordor](/wiki/Dark_Lord "Dark Lord").

For two and a half thousand years, Sauron ruled Mordor uninterruptedly. It was from Mordor that he [made war against](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron "War of the Elves and Sauron") the [Elves of Eregion](/wiki/Elves_of_Eregion) and came to dominate most of Eriador in a period known as the [Dark Years](/wiki/Dark_Years). That was until he was repelled by the [High Men](/wiki/High_Men) of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor). Retreating to Mordor, Sauron then directed his power over the far south and east of Mordor conquering and dominating the savage tribes of the [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) and the [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Almost a thousand years later, Sauron was captured by the Númenóreans and brought to their island kingdom, eventually causing its [destruction](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor#Destruction "Númenor"). Sauron returned to Mordor as a spirit and resumed his rule.

### The Last Alliance and Third Age {#The_Last_Alliance_and_Third_Age}

Sauron's rule was interrupted yet again when his efforts to overthrow the surviving Men and Elves failed, and they fought their way back to their foe's domain. After several months of siege in the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad), forces of the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men) came into Mordor. Sauron was defeated in a final battle and the Dark Tower was leveled to the ground, but Mordor was not settled by Men because of the dreadful memory of Sauron, and Orodruin.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-8">[8]</a></sup>

The [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor) built watchtowers and fortresses around Mordor, such as the mountain fortress of [Durthang](/wiki/Durthang), the [Towers of the Teeth](/wiki/Towers_of_the_Teeth) at the [Morannon](/wiki/Morannon), and the [Tower of Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Tower_of_Cirith_Ungol), in order to prevent Sauron's servants from returning to Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-Tower_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tower-9">[9]</a></sup>

However, Gondor had failed in the long run; during the [Great Plague](/wiki/Great_Plague), the population was so diminished that troops were recalled and the fortresses abandoned.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> Deprived of guard, Mordor began to fill with evil things again, and it is said the first shadow was the [Lord of the Nazgûl](/wiki/Lord_of_the_Nazg%C3%BBl) ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1980](/wiki/Third_Age_1980 "Third Age 1980")) who summoned the other [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl"), first appearing since the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), to prepare the return of Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-rings_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rings-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ta_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ta-12">[12]</a></sup> [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil) was conquered by the Ringwraiths in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2002](/wiki/Third_Age_2002 "Third Age 2002"); other fortifications that were supposed to defend Gondor from the menace inside Mordor were captured and turned into a means of shielding Mordor. Sauron resided in [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur), until the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) [attacked](/wiki/Attack_on_Dol_Guldur "Attack on Dol Guldur") it in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), forcing Sauron to flee.

Meanwhile Mordor had been long prepared for him, and declaring himself openly he returned to Mordor in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2951](/wiki/Third_Age_2951 "Third Age 2951"), where he finished reconstructing his Dark Tower.<sup id="cite_ref-ta_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ta-12">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> By that time Mordor was protected too well to be captured by any military might that was available to the Free Peoples; in the north of Mordor during the War of the Ring were the great garrisons and forges of war, while surrounding the bitter inland [Sea of Núrnen](/wiki/Sea_of_N%C3%BArnen) to the south lay the vast fields tended for the provision of the armies by hordes of slaves brought in from lands to the east and south.

### War of the Ring {#War_of_the_Ring}

During the War of the Ring, Sauron gathered all his forces to Mordor. After the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields), a Host of the West went to the Black Gate. Sauron sent his army to destroy the Men of Gondor and Rohan, but then [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) destroyed the One Ring and Mordor fell. The Dark Tower, the Black Gate and the Towers of Teeth collapsed to ruin. Mount Doom exploded. Both Sauron and his Ringwraiths were apparently destroyed.

After the ultimate defeat of Sauron, Mordor became mostly empty again as the [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) inside it fled or were killed. Crippled by thousands of years of abuse and neglect, but capable of sustaining life, the land of Mordor was given to the defeated foes of Gondor as a consolation, as well as to the freed slaves of Nurn who were formerly forced to farm there to feed the armies of Mordor.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

<i><b>Mordor</b></i> is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name. It means "Black Land". It is a compound of *[MOR-](/wiki/Mor "Mor")* ("black, dark") and *[dôr](/wiki/D%C3%B4r "Dôr")* ("land").<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

Its name in [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) was <i><b>Nargûn</b></i>, which contains the element *narg* ("black").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

The first ever reference to Sauron's land is mentioned without a name, in the story of the Fall, written in the 1930s, as an inner land far from the sea, over the mountains. There [Thû](/wiki/Th%C3%BB) had a fortress, and "[Amroth](/wiki/Amroth)" assailed him.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> The name Mordor, the Black Country, appears first time in the second version of that text, along with Elendil and Gil-galad (both of them mentioned first time).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

It is not uncommon for names in Tolkien's fiction to have relevant meanings in several languages, both those invented by Tolkien, and "real" ones, but this of course happens with any two languages.

- A proposed etymology is [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *morðor*, which means "mortal sin" and later "murder".
- *Mordor* is also a name cited in some Nordic mythologies referring to a land where its citizens practice evil without knowing it, imposed on themselves by the society long created for that purpose.

Tolkien is reported to have identified Mordor with the volcano of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromboli" title="wikipedia:Stromboli">Stromboli</a> off Sicily.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> Graham Tayar, a classmate of Tolkien's at King Edwards School, claimed that Tolkien told him once "that the physical setting \[of Mordor\] derived directly from the trenches of World War One, the wasteland of shell-cratered battlefields where he had fought in 1916".<sup id="cite_ref-RC_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-20">[20]</a></sup>
