**Minas Tirith** ([S.](../S/Sindarin.md) 'Tower of the Guard') was a city of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), originally called **Minas Anor**. From [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1640](../T/Third_Age_1640.md)<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup> onwards it was the capital of the South-kingdom and the seat of its [Kings](../K/Kings_of_Gondor.md) and ruling [Stewards](../S/Stewards_of_Gondor.md).

## History {#History}

### Minas Anor {#Minas_Anor}

The city of Minas Tirith was originally a fortress, Minas Anor ([S.](../S/Sindarin.md) 'Tower of the Sun'), which was probably built after the founding of the realm of Gondor in [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [3320](../S/Second_Age_3320.md) before [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [3429](../S/Second_Age_3429.md).<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-2A_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-2A-1">[1]</a></sup> It was the western counterpart to [Minas Ithil](Minas_Ithil.md) ([S.](../S/Sindarin.md) 'Tower of the Moon'): though was originally of less importance, and was built as a small fort on the summit of Amon Anor mostly to guard [Rath Dínen](../R/Rath_Dínen.md), the Tombs of the Kings.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> From Osgiliath the sons of [Elendil](../E/Elendil.md) jointly ruled the newly-founded [South-kingdom](../G/Gondor.md), but Minas Anor was home to [Anárion](../A/Anárion.md)'s House and Minas Ithil to [Isildur](../I/Isildur.md)'s.<sup id="cite_ref-S-Rings_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-Rings-4">[4]</a></sup> Therefore when the seven *[palantíri](../P/Palantíri.md)* were divided amongst the [Realms in Exile](../R/Realms_in_Exile.md) one was placed in Minas Anor.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TT-Palantir_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TT-Palantir-6">[6]</a></sup>

[Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) attacked Gondor in [S.A.](../S/Second_Age.md) [3429](../S/Second_Age_3429.md), [taking](../F/First_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil.md) [Minas Ithil](Minas_Ithil.md) and forcing Isildur to flee north to his father in [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md). Anárion meanwhile was besieged in Osgiliath and Minas Anor,<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-2A_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-2A-1">[1]</a></sup> until he was relieved by the [Last Alliance of Elves and Men](../L/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men.md). In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2](../T/Third_Age_2.md) Isildur planted the second [White Tree of Gondor](../W/White_Tree_of_Gondor.md) in Minas Anor in memory of his brother, who had perished during the [Siege of Barad-dûr](../S/Siege_of_Barad-dûr.md). From this point the Kingship of Gondor belonged solely to the heirs of Anárion, who continued to rule from Osgiliath.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PM-Heirs_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-Heirs-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [420](../T/Third_Age_420.md)<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup> Minas Anor was rebuilt by [Ostoher](../O/Ostoher.md), the seventh [King of Gondor](../K/Kings_of_Gondor.md), and from his reign onwards the Kings removed there from Osgiliath in the summer.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup> It remained the second city of Gondor for the next thousand years as the Kingdom reached the height of its power under the [Ship-kings](../S/Ship-kings.md), and then fell into its long decline. Osgiliath was burned and its *palantír* lost during the [Kin-strife](../K/Kin-strife~3650.md), but the army of [Castamir](../C/Castamir.md) did not breach the walls of Minas Anor by force.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 1]</a></sup> Its people resented Castamir's rule and were angered by his cruel treatment of Osgiliath, and it is likely that they sided with the forces of [Eldacar](<../E/Eldacar_(King_of_Gondor).md>) when he returned from the north in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1447](../T/Third_Age_1447.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Gondor_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gondor-9">[8]</a></sup> After the defeat of Castamir, Minas Anor gradually grew more prominent.

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1636](../T/Third_Age_1636.md) Gondor was devastated by the [Great Plague](../G/Great_Plague.md), leaving Osgiliath depopulated and falling into ruin. [Telemnar](../T/Telemnar.md) was killed along with his children and many others of the [Dúnedain](../D/Dúnedain.md), and the second White Tree died. Soon afterwards, his nephew [Tarondor](<../T/Tarondor_(King_of_Gondor).md>) permanently moved the [King's House](../K/King's_House.md) to Minas Anor and planted the third White Tree ([T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1640](../T/Third_Age_1640.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup>

For a short time under Tarandor and his heirs the decline of Gondor was slowed, although constant wars with various groups of [Easterlings](../E/Easterlings.md) took their toll. In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1900](../T/Third_Age_1900.md) [Calimehtar](<../C/Calimehtar_(King_of_Gondor).md>) built the first [White Tower](../T/Tower_of_Ecthelion.md) in the Citadel of Minas Anor to house the city's *palantír*.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup> Just over a century later, however, the kingdom was dealt a harsh blow. In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2002](../T/Third_Age_2002.md) Minas Ithil, where the guard on Mordor had long since slackened, was [captured](../S/Second_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil.md) by the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md). It became known as Minas Morgul, and in turn Minas Anor was renamed Minas Tirith, 'Tower of the Guard' or the 'Guarded City'.<sup id="cite_ref-RK-MinasTirith_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-MinasTirith-10">[9]</a></sup> Soon after [Eärnur](../E/Eärnur.md), the last King of Gondor, was killed in the Morgul Vale, and the lordship of the South-kingdom passed to the [Stewards](../S/Stewards.md).<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup>

### Minas Tirith {#Minas_Tirith}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/R/Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/d8/Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg/300px-Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="199" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/d8/Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg/450px-Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/d/d8/Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg/600px-Ray_Minn_-_Minas_Tirith_over_the_Pelennor_Fields.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="1274"/></a><figcaption>Minas Tirith over the Pelennor Fields by <a href="../../category/I/Images_by_Ray_Minn.md" title="Category:Images by Ray Minn">Ray Minn</a></figcaption></figure>

Following a brief respite in the [Watchful Peace](../W/Watchful_Peace.md), Gondor under the Stewards became increasingly beset by enemies: control of [Ithilien](../I/Ithilien.md) and the ruined bridges of [Osgiliath](../O/Osgiliath.md) passed back and forth between Minas Tirith and Minas Morgul, the coastlands were raided by the [Corsairs of Umbar](../C/Corsairs_of_Umbar.md), and Easterlings assailed them from the north.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup> The Citadel was improved under [Ecthelion I](../E/Ecthelion_I.md) ([T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2685](../T/Third_Age_2685.md)–[2698](../T/Third_Age_2698.md)),<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup> who rebuilt the [White Tower](../T/Tower_of_Ecthelion.md) which afterwards bore his name.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup> However, the third White Tree died at the same time as the twenty-first Steward, [Belecthor II](../B/Belecthor_II.md), and this time a new seedling could not be found to replace it. The dead tree was left standing in the Citadel.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup>

In the reign of [Ecthelion II](../E/Ecthelion_II.md) ([T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2953](../T/Third_Age_2953.md)–[T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2984](../T/Third_Age_2984.md))<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup> Minas Tirith was strengthened against Mordor, where Sauron had now declared himself openly. It was at this time that [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) the future King first came to the city under the name [Thorongil](../T/Thorongil.md), and did great deeds.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA-5">[5]</a></sup>

### War of the Ring {#War_of_the_Ring}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/9/9c/CdxIconArticle.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content"><i>Main article:</i> <b><a href="../B/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md" title="Battle of the Pelennor Fields">Battle of the Pelennor Fields</a></b></div></div>

During the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md), the brunt of [Mordor](Mordor.md)'s assault on the [Free peoples](../F/Free_peoples~19934.md) was directed at Gondor and Minas Tirith. [Cair Andros](../C/Cair_Andros.md) fell on [10 March](../_/10_March.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md) and on [12 March](../_/12_March.md) a company led by [Faramir](../F/Faramir.md) was forced to retreat from its defence of Osgiliath.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup> An effort was made to repair the Rammas Echor, but this came too late.<sup id="cite_ref-RK-MinasTirith_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-MinasTirith-10">[9]</a></sup> With the crossings of Anduin taken the Pelennor was overrun, and Minas Tirith was besieged by a great army of Morgul orcs and Easterlings led by the [Witch-king](../W/Witch-king.md). The city was under-manned, and its defenders had little hope; on [15 March](../_/15_March.md) the Great Gate was breached and the last ruling Steward, [Denethor II](../D/Denethor.md), burned himself in despair.<sup id="cite_ref-RK-Siege_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-Siege-11">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RK-Pyre_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-Pyre-12">[11]</a></sup> But [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) was also present, and rescuing Faramir from his father he took charge of the defence of Minas Tirith. The [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) under [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md) also came unlooked for to the city's aid, and [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) led a force up the river from [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md). Seeing this [Imrahil](../I/Imrahil.md), [Prince of Dol Amroth](../P/Prince_of_Dol_Amroth.md), led a sally from the city, and the three armies were able to break the siege in the decisive [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../B/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md).<sup id="cite_ref-RK-Pelennor_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-Pelennor-13">[12]</a></sup>

On [1 May](../_/1_May.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md), returning with the victorious from the [Battle of the Morannon](../B/Battle_of_the_Morannon.md), Aragorn was crowned on the plain outside Minas Tirith, and he entered the city as King Elessar. On [25 June](../_/25_June.md) he discovered a sapling of the line of [Nimloth](../N/Nimloth_of_Númenor.md) in a hidden hallow of Mount Mindolluin. This was planted in the Court of the Fountain – the fourth White Tree of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-AppB-3A_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB-3A-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RK-StewardKing_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-StewardKing-14">[13]</a></sup>

### Fourth Age {#Fourth_Age}

Under King Elessar, Minas Tirith was restored and made more beautiful than ever before: the Great Gate was reforged of *[mithril](Mithril.md)* and steel by the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) of the [Glittering Caves](../G/Glittering_Caves.md),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup> led by [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md), the streets were paved with white marble and the city was filled with trees and fountains. Dwarves came to work in the city and [Elves](../E/Elves.md) enjoyed coming to it.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Minas_Tirith.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/b/b2/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Minas_Tirith.jpg/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Minas_Tirith.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="342" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/b/b2/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Minas_Tirith.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="431" data-file-height="492"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Minas Tirith&#34; (unfinished drawing) by <a href="../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a></figcaption></figure>

At some point in the past, the city was considered to be part of the region of [Anórien](../A/Anórien.md) but was considered separate by the time of the War of the Ring.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-17">[16]</a></sup>

The city was situated on the [Hill of Guard](../H/Hill_of_Guard.md) – the "out-thrust knee" of [Mount Mindolluin](Mount_Mindolluin.md), connected to the main mass of the mountain by a narrow 'shoulder'.<sup id="cite_ref-RK-MinasTirith_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-MinasTirith-10">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WR-MinasTirith_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-MinasTirith-18">[17]</a></sup> It faced eastward towards Osgiliath, over the [Pelennor Fields](../P/Pelennor_Fields.md) surrounding the city, fertile townlands stretching from the walls of the city proper to the [Rammas Echor](../R/Rammas_Echor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-RK-MinasTirith_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-MinasTirith-10">[9]</a></sup>

The city was built on the hill with seven concentric tiers cut on the hill culminating in the [Citadel](../C/Citadel_of_Gondor.md) at the summit, 700 feet above the plain below. The outer wall was called the [City Wall](../C/City_Wall.md) and was black, of the same material used in [Orthanc](../O/Orthanc.md). The City Wall was vulnerable only to earthquakes capable of rending the ground where it stood.<sup id="cite_ref-RK-MinasTirith_10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-MinasTirith-10">[9]</a></sup>

Each level was walled and held a gate with each gate faced a different direction: only the great gate and that of the seventh level faced east; the gate to the second level faced southeast, and that to the third faced northeast; so altering between the two such that the path up through the levels wound to and fro rather than following a straight line. An outcropping of rock as high as the seventh level bisected all the lower levels except the lowest on the line of the [Great Gate](../G/Great_Gate_of_Minas_Tirith.md). The winding path through the city therefore passed through tunnels in this 'keel' five times.

The **First Level** included the [Rath Celerdain](../R/Rath_Celerdain.md), a white paved street with an inn, the [Old Guesthouse](../O/Old_Guesthouse.md).

The **Sixth Level** contained stables for riders, and the [Houses of Healing](../H/Houses_of_Healing.md). There was also [Fen Hollen](../F/Fen_Hollen.md), a door which was almost always closed, leading to [Rath Dínen](../R/Rath_Dínen.md),<sup id="cite_ref-RK-MinasTirith_10-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK-MinasTirith-10">[9]</a></sup> the 'shoulder' of rock that joined the Hill to the main mass of Mount Mindolluin rose to the level of the fifth wall and was fortified with large ramparts, where the tombs of the Kings of Gondor and their Stewards lay. The uppermost tunnel was delved into the spur of rock that jutted out of the eastern face of the Hill; The keystone of its archway was carved with the head of a crowned King. [Guards of the Citadel](../G/Guards_of_the_Citadel.md) manned the Seventh Gate which faced eastward in line with the Great Gate 700 feet below and emerged into the Citadel, the city's strongest point, surrounded by high walls and battlements on the 'keel'.

The **Citadel** housed the [Court of the Fountain](<../C/Court_of_the_Fountain_(Minas_Tirith).md>) and the [Tower of Ecthelion](../T/Tower_of_Ecthelion.md), which brought the total height of the city to 1000 feet.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[note 2]</a></sup> Before the Tower grew the [White Tree](../W/White_Tree_of_Gondor.md) in a court. There were also the [King's House](../K/King's_House.md), lodgings for the Steward, [Merethrond](Merethrond.md), barracks for the Guard of the Citadel, and other buildings for guests and other workers.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Minas Tirith* is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name,<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-20">[18]</a></sup> which means "Tower of Guard"<sup id="cite_ref-Guard_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guard-21">[19]</a></sup> or "Tower of Watch".<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-20">[18]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that the name is a combination of *[minas](Minas.md)* ("tower", "fort", "city") and *[tirith](../T/Tirith.md)* ("watch", "ward", "guard").<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[20]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

*Minas Anor* is a Sindarin name,<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-23">[21]</a></sup> which means "Tower of the Sun"<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[22]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[23]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[24]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[25]</a></sup> or "Tower of the Setting Sun".<sup id="cite_ref-S-Rings_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-Rings-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[26]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that the name is a combination of *[minas](Minas.md)* ("tower", "fort", "city") and *[anor](../S/Sun.md#Other_names)* ("sun").<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-23">[21]</a></sup>

The city is named *Mundburg* by the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md), which means "Guardian-fortress" in [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md).<sup id="cite_ref-RC_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-29">[27]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[28]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[29]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[30]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[31]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[32]</a></sup> Like many names in [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md), it is probably derived from [Old English](../O/Old_English.md), likely consisting of *mund* ("protection")<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[33]</a></sup> and *burg* ("fortress").<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[34]</a></sup>

Minas Tirith was also referred to as the Tower of Guard,<sup id="cite_ref-Guard_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guard-21">[19]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[35]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[36]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[37]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[38]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RK|Steward_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK|Steward-41">[39]</a></sup> the Guarded City,<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[40]</a></sup> and the Tower of Anor.<sup id="cite_ref-RK|Steward_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RK|Steward-41">[39]</a></sup>

The names Stone-city and Stone-houses were used by the [Drúedain](../D/Drúedain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[41]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[42]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In manuscripts Tolkien used the Old English names **Gemenburg**, **Heatorras**, **Giemen**, *Minas Tirith*, and **Mundbeorg**. *Gemen* means "care", "heed" or "watch". *Heatorras* means "high towers" and *Mundbeorg* means "protection-hill".<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[43]</a></sup> The Old English variants *berg* and *beorg* mean "hill" or "mountain".<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[44]</a></sup>

On an unfinished sketch, Tolkien has written the names *Stanburg* ([Old English](../O/Old_English.md)) and *Steinborg* ([Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md)) both meaning "Stone City".<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[45]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Minas Tirith's most obvious historical parallel is the capital of the Byzantine Empire, Byzantium or Constantinople. Founded by the Roman emperor Constantine, Constantinople would eventually be one of the largest cities of its day and be a fortress under constant attack by its enemies. Similarly, Minas Tirith would become the chief fortification of Gondor in the latter days of its decline.

Like Minas Tirith, the Byzantine capital Constantinople sat near a strategic waterway (the Anduin to the Bay of Tolfalas in the case of Minas Tirith, the Bosporus Strait which divides the Mediterranean and Black Seas in the case of Constantinople) and was protected by massive walls that were virtually impregnable until the rise of effective gunpowder weapons in the real-world 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[46]</a></sup>

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) summarized the story of *The Silmarillion* and of *The Lord of the Rings* in a long [letter to Milton Waldman](../L/Letter_to_Milton_Waldman.md), that was probably written in late 1951.<sup id="cite_ref-L131_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-49">[47]</a></sup> In this letter he stated that in the Third Age Gondor rises to a peak of power and then "fades slowly to decayed Middle-Age, a kind of proud, venerable, but increasingly impotent Byzantium"<sup id="cite_ref-L131_49-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-49">[47]</a></sup> and that the story in Book Five of *The Lord of the Rings* comes to the "half-ruinous Byzantine City of Minas Tirith"<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[48]</a></sup>.
