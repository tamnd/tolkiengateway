The **Minlamad thent/estent** was one of the three Elvish [Verse Modes](../V/Verse_Modes.md). The other two being the *[Ann-thennath](../A/Ann-thennath.md)*, and the *[Linnod](../L/Linnod.md)*. The Minlamad thent/estent is typically used in narns, intending to be spoken rather than sung.

## History {#History}

The verse form was used by the poet, [Dírhaval](../D/Dírhaval.md), in his sole work, the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../N/Narn_i_Chîn_Húrin.md" title="Narn i Chîn Húrin">Narn i Chîn Húrin</a> (The Tale of the Children of Húrin)

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The *minlamad thent/estent* can be broken up as follows: [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Minlamed&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Minlamed (page does not exist)">minlamed</a></i> = *first-echoing*, referring to alliteration. <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Thent&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Thent (page does not exist)">thent</a></i> = *short*. The ending <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Estent&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Estent (page does not exist)">estent</a></i> is likely derived from *thent*, possibly meaning *very short*. In poetic notation, a */* indicates a stressed syllable. *minlamed* could be related to the Elvish root for sound, in which case, the meaning changes to *one-sound-stop*. In this case, the *ann-thennath* could mean *long lines*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other Versions of the Legendarium {#Other_Versions_of_the_Legendarium}

In ‘’Unfinished Tales *(Page 146), Christopher Tolkien gave the term the name of* Minlamed thent/estent*. In* The War of the Jewels *(page 311/315, note #3), he revealed his mistake and that the term’s name actually was just* Minlamad thent/estent*.*
