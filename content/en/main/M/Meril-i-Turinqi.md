**Meril-i-Turinqi** was the **Lady of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa)**, according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

Meril dwelt in a white house inside a great *[korin](/wiki/Korin "Korin")* of ancient [elms](/wiki/Elms "Elms"), near [Ingil's tower](/wiki/Tirin_na_Gilweth "Tirin na Gilweth").<sup id="cite_ref-Tur_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tur-3">[3]</a></sup> She was of great beauty and accompanied by [Elven](/wiki/Elves "Elves") maidens.<sup id="cite_ref-Mer_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mer-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

Meril was one of the [Inwir](/wiki/Inwir), the royal clan of the [Teleri](/wiki/Vanyar#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium "Vanyar"),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> being a great-granddaughter of [Inwë](/wiki/Inw%C3%AB), the King of all the Eldar. However, she was also akin to the [Solosimpi](/wiki/Solosimpi), and had long ago seen the [Bay of Faëry](/wiki/Bay_of_Eldamar "Bay of Eldamar").<sup id="cite_ref-Ing_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ing-6">[6]</a></sup>

Following the [March of Liberation](/wiki/March_of_Liberation) and the war with [Melko](/wiki/Melko), [Ingil](/wiki/Ingil) son of Inwë, her grandfather, led the remaining Elves and [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes) to [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa), where they built the city of [Koromas](/wiki/Koromas), later known as *Kortirion* due to the [great tower](/wiki/Tirin_na_Gilweth "Tirin na Gilweth") that Ingil built.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Later, Ingil returned to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor),<sup id="cite_ref-Ing_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ing-6">[6]</a></sup> and eventually Meril became the Lady of the Isle.

At some point after the March of Liberation, when the [Olórë Mallë](/wiki/Ol%C3%B3r%C3%AB_Mall%C3%AB) was blocked and the children of [Men](/wiki/Men) could no longer visit Valinor in their sleep, one [man](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel "Eärendel") nonetheless managed to find his way to the West and sought the Elves' help, to which Meril responded by asking [Lindo](/wiki/Lindo), an Elf of Tol Eressëa, to devise a plan.

Afterwards, Lindo and his wife [Vairë](</wiki/Vair%C3%AB_(wife_of_Lindo)> "Vairë (wife of Lindo)") built the [Cottage of Lost Play](/wiki/Cottage_of_Lost_Play) in Kortirion, from which the remaining children of Men that stayed with the Elves went to the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands) and comforted the Mannish children living there.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Arrival of Eriol {#Arrival_of_Eriol}

Around the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_century" title="wikipedia:5th century">5th century AD</a>, [Eriol](/wiki/Eriol), a Mannish mariner, arrived to Tol Eressëa. After visiting many places on the island, including the Cottage of Lost Play, [Littleheart](/wiki/Littleheart) son of [Voronwë](/wiki/Voronw%C3%AB) brought him to Meril's house, where Eriol requested to taste *[limpë](/wiki/Limp%C3%AB "Limpë")*, a magic Elven drink, seeking kinship and fellowship with the Elves.

Meril denied, telling him that it is dangerous for a <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Mortal" title="Mortal">mortal</a> [Man](/wiki/Man) to do so, as [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) made his [Children](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar "Children of Ilúvatar") different, and drinking *limpë* would erase his old desires but awake new ones; she also warned him that he would one day long for his lands again.<sup id="cite_ref-Mer_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mer-4">[4]</a></sup> To explain better she told him the stories of *[the Chaining of Melko](/wiki/The_Chaining_of_Melko "The Chaining of Melko")*<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and *[the Coming of the Elves and the Making of Kôr](/wiki/The_Coming_of_the_Elves_and_the_Making_of_K%C3%B4r "The Coming of the Elves and the Making of Kôr")*.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

Some time after, Meril visited the Cottage of Lost Play during a festival, before Eriol went to [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel), where Littleheart told the tale of *[the Fall of Gondolin](</wiki/The_Fall_of_Gondolin_(chapter)> "The Fall of Gondolin (chapter)")*.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

### The Faring Forth {#The_Faring_Forth}

According to one outline for the continuation of the *Lost Tales*, the disastrous [Faring Forth](/wiki/Faring_Forth), the great expedition to the Great Lands for the rescue of the [Lost Elves](/wiki/Ilkorindi "Ilkorindi"), was instigated prematurely by Eriol himself against Meril's command, who, longing for his home, returned to the Great Lands and "preached" of the Faring Forth. Thus, due to Eriol's impatience and recklessness, the Faring Forth was doomed to fail.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

Nothing else is known about Meril, or her fate after the Faring Forth.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Meril-i-Turinqi* is [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) for "Queen of Flowers", being a combination of <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Meril" title="Meril">meril</a></i> ("flowers") + *[i](/wiki/I "I")* ("the") + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Turinqi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Turinqi (page does not exist)">turinqi</a></i> ("queen").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

An alternative spelling of the name was *Meril-i-Turinqui*.<sup id="cite_ref-Tur_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tur-3">[3]</a></sup>

An earlier Qenya form of the name was *Veril-i-Turinqi*.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) cognate for *Meril-i-Turinqi* is <i><b>Gwidhil-i-Durinthi</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> (changed from *Gwedhil*) or <i><b>Gwithil-i-Durinthi</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Gwi_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwi-16">[16]</a></sup>

Earlier Gnomish versions of the name include: *Miril-i-durwin*,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> *Gwedhil Turlin*,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[note 1]</a></sup> and *Gwidhil (Gwedda) Durinthir*<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> (with *Gwedda* being a diminutive of *Gwidhil*, later changed to *Gwetha*).<sup id="cite_ref-Gwi_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gwi-16">[16]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### Early legendarium {#Early_legendarium}

In an earlier text in the *[Qenya Lexicon](/wiki/Qenya_Lexicon)*, the role of Meril-i-Turinqi was instead given to [Erinti](/wiki/Erinti), at that time imagined as the [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") of love, music, beauty and purity who, like Meril in the later texts, lived in a *korin* of elms in the center of Tol Eressëa.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup>

### Later legendarium {#Later_legendarium}

According to [John Garth](/wiki/John_Garth), the character of [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) from *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* might have been inspired by Meril-i-Turinqi.<sup id="cite_ref-Wit_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wit-22">[21]</a></sup>

In one text from the later [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), the name *[Meril](/wiki/Amari%C3%AB#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium "Amarië")* appears as the name of the wife of [Felagund](/wiki/Felagund) and the mother of [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad).<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup>
