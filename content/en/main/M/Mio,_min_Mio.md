<i><b>Mio, min Mio</b></i> ("Mio, my Mio"; published in English as *Mio, my Son*) is a Swedish language children's book by [Astrid Lindgren](../A/Astrid_Lindgren.md). The book has been described as a work in the genre <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_fantasy" title="wikipedia:High fantasy">High fantasy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

As both *Mio, min Mio* and the first two volumes of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* were published in 1954 and can be said to carry some apparent similarities, a Swedish humorous newspaper article concocted a fictitious conspiracy theory that Tolkien and Lindgren must have secretly met some time in the 1930s and agreed upon writing *"fairy-tales of their own but with a common theme"* ("var sin saga med gemensamt tema"). The joke aside, the similarities are quite abound:<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

- *cloaks granting invisibility*
- *energy bread* (Tolkien: [lembas](../L/Lembas.md); Lindgren: Mio eats a magic bread which takes away hunger)
- *apparently dead trees*
- *intelligent horses* (Tolkien: [Shadowfax](../S/Shadowfax.md); Lindgren: Miramis)
- *black scouts* (Tolkien: the [Ringwraiths](../N/Nazgûl.md) of Sauron; Lindgren: the black scouts of Kato)
- *an evil tyrant who is defeated by two "halflings" – an involuntary hero and his loyal squire*
- *after the death of the tyrant, a tree who was believed to be dead is flowering again*
