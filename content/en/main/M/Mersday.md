**Mersday** was the sixth day of the week in the [Shire Calendar](/wiki/Shire_Calendar). It represented the [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) day *[Oraearon](/wiki/Oraearon)*, dedicated to the [Sea](/wiki/Sea).<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the reform of [Isengrim Took II](/wiki/Isengrim_Took_II),<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup> dates such as [1 March](/wiki/1_March) and [1 September](/wiki/1_September) always fell on Mersday.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Mersday* represents an archaic [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) form "Meresdei", a word preserved in the [Yellowskin](/wiki/Yellowskin).<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup> It contains the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) element <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mere#Old_English" title="wiktionary:mere">mere, mer-</a></i> "lake, pool; sea".

*Mersday* is translated as Thursday in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, however as it was one day before the chief day of the week, it rather corresponded to our Saturday. For this reason there was one exception, and in the poem *[The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late](/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Stayed_Up_Too_Late)*, Mersday was translated as "Saturday" instead of Thursday.<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
