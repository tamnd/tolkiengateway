**Mersday** was the sixth day of the week in the [Shire Calendar](../S/Shire_Calendar.md). It represented the [Númenórean](../N/Númenórean.md) day *[Oraearon](../O/Oraearon.md)*, dedicated to the [Sea](../S/Sea.md).<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup>

After the reform of [Isengrim Took II](../I/Isengrim_Took_II.md),<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup> dates such as [1 March](../_/1_March.md) and [1 September](../_/1_September.md) always fell on Mersday.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Mersday* represents an archaic [Hobbitish](../H/Hobbitish.md) form "Meresdei", a word preserved in the [Yellowskin](../Y/Yellowskin.md).<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup> It contains the [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) element <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mere#Old_English" title="wiktionary:mere">mere, mer-</a></i> "lake, pool; sea".

*Mersday* is translated as Thursday in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, however as it was one day before the chief day of the week, it rather corresponded to our Saturday. For this reason there was one exception, and in the poem *[The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late](../T/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Stayed_Up_Too_Late.md)*, Mersday was translated as "Saturday" instead of Thursday.<sup id="cite_ref-cal_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cal-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
