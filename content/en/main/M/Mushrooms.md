**Mushrooms** were an edible [fungus](/wiki/Funguses "Funguses"). It is said that Hobbits have a passion for mushrooms, surpassing even the greediest likings of [Big People](/wiki/Men "Men").<sup id="cite_ref-Cut_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cut-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Mushrooms were a particular delight of young [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins), who would often steal them from [Farmer Maggot](/wiki/Farmer_Maggot)'s fields. Old Maggot still remembered it when Frodo came to his [farm](/wiki/Bamfurlong "Bamfurlong") years later, and as a parting gift, [Mrs. Maggot](/wiki/Mrs._Maggot) gave Frodo a basket of the prized mushrooms.<sup id="cite_ref-Cut_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cut-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) described himself as a Hobbit in all but size, noting that he was very fond of mushrooms.<sup id="cite_ref-L213_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L213-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In a note discussing the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain), Tolkien made mention about the fungi in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age): they were generally considered ugly and dangerous by the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and most of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain), and the less wise called them "ork-plants", believing them cursed and blighted by [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth). The Eldar did not eat them and only a few Edain knew how to distinguish the edible ones and could eat them because of great hunger when lost in the wild. The Drúedain had a lore of plants and ate fungi with pleasure (to the astonishment of the other peoples), and they cultured their favorite ones. They passed this knowledge to the [Haladin](/wiki/Haladin) who ate them when guests out of courtesy, and resolved to them at need. Tolkien later discarded the note believing that this trait made the Drúedain "too like Hobbits".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

<b>1984-1997: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>The various books released for the game describe <a href="/wiki/Mushrooms_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Mushrooms in Middle-earth Role Playing">many different kinds of mushrooms</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring">The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Though the scenes in Maggot&#39;s house have been omitted, a nod to mushrooms is given when the four Hobbits escape Farmer Maggot. <a href="/wiki/Peregrin_Took" title="Peregrin Took">Pippin</a> spots them after a comment on a &#34;short cut&#34;, making the full phrase sound as &#34;A short cut to... mushrooms!&#34;, the chapter title.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game)" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Mushrooms are collectible items found throughout the Shire. When eaten, they restore 10 points of the player&#39;s health.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game)" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (2003 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Mushrooms serve as power-ups throughout the game. One mushroom - green and blue of colour - restores one health bubble.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Later in the game, larger, clustered mushrooms serve as a full healing item.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2005: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game</a>: <a href="/wiki/Paths_of_the_Wise:_The_Guide_to_Magicians_and_Loremasters" title="Paths of the Wise: The Guide to Magicians and Loremasters">Paths of the Wise</a></i></b>

<dl><dd><b>Carnestad</b> (<a href="/wiki/Sindarin" title="Sindarin">S.</a> “red healing”) was described in the book as a squat mushroom with a bright red cap, native to <a href="/wiki/Mirkwood" title="Mirkwood">Mirkwood</a>. It neutralized disease, broke fevers, and alleviated symptoms of sickness. Elves guarded its patches carefully, while Men sought to sell them and Orcs tried to deny others their benefits.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Mushrooms can be grown by players who are farmers by profession. Players who are cooks can make these into mushroom pie, fried mushroom and fried dace with mushrooms. These are foods for low leveled characters that can increase different traits.</dd></dl>

<b>2012: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>During the <a href="/wiki/White_Council" title="White Council">White Council</a> <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a>, in order to dismiss <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a>&#39;s fears about the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Necromancer" title="Necromancer">Necromancer</a>, mentions that <a href="/wiki/Radagast" title="Radagast">Radagast</a> consumes mushrooms excessively, which have blurred his cognition and judgement.</dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor" title="Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor">Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>Several mushrooms can be foraged in the game:<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Blue Milk</b> was easily distinguished by the blue colour of its stalk and cap, and when cut it oozed a milky blue liquid which gave it its name. While the liquid and the dried parts could be used for medicinal purposes (often for treating sores), eating the mushroom whole could stave off hunger for half a day while also improving mental clarity.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Carandôl</b> was a small red mushroom that was used in all sorts of cooking to enhance flavour. For healers, it strengthened the effects of mixtures and salves.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd><b>Naugrimbas</b> was a foul-tasting yet edible mushroom. It was derisively labeled &#34;Dwarves&#39; bread&#34;, an insult to both the mushroom and Dwarves, since Dwarves were known to use it for rations as a last resort. It had some medicinal purposes, being used to treat burn wounds.</dd></dl>
