**Michael George Ramer** (born in <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Seventh_Age_1929&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Seventh Age 1929 (page does not exist)">1929</a>) was a fictional member of the [Notion Club](/wiki/Notion_Club).

## History {#History}

Ramer was born in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary" title="wikipedia:Hungary">Hungary</a> in 1929, and was educated at Jesus College. His parents returned to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England" title="wikipedia:England">England</a> when he was four years old, where he lived by [Gunthorpe Park](/wiki/Gunthorpe_Park) in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matfield" title="wikipedia:Matfield">Matfield</a> during his childhood.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

He spent a good deal of time in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland" title="wikipedia:Finland">Finland</a> and Hungary between <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Seventh_Age_1956&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Seventh Age 1956 (page does not exist)">1956</a> and <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Seventh_Age_1968&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Seventh Age 1968 (page does not exist)">1968</a>. He was a professor of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finno-Ugric_languages" title="wikipedia:Finno-Ugric languages">Finno-Ugric</a> philology, but was better known as a writer of romances. Among his other interests were <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages" title="wikipedia:Celtic languages">Celtic languages</a> and antiquities.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

He had written a book called *[The Stone-eaters](/wiki/The_Stone-eaters)* that has a Glacier in it.<sup id="cite_ref-Space_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Space-3">[3]</a></sup>

During a meeting of the Club at [20 February](/wiki/20_February), <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Seventh_Age_1987&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Seventh Age 1987 (page does not exist)">1987</a>, Ramer read a story he had written about space travel. He was criticized by [Guildford](/wiki/Nicholas_Guildford "Nicholas Guildford") for not contriving convincing means of traversing great distances in space.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Afterwards he thought of an idea of using dreams as means of traveling from one point in space to another, and he perfected the method of recollecting bits of information from the dreams which would have been otherwise forgotten.<sup id="cite_ref-Space_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Space-3">[3]</a></sup>

He claimed to have visited many worlds in his vivid dreams, which he described in great detail, and many words in alien languages he had remembered. At the end of the retelling of his dreams to the Club, he said that he had landed on a planet very similar to the Earth, and he saw what he described as buildings and structures, indicating a presence of sentient beings.<sup id="cite_ref-Space_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Space-3">[3]</a></sup>

In one of the subsequent meetings, when [Lowdham](/wiki/Alwin_Arundel_Lowdham "Alwin Arundel Lowdham") mentions the name of *[Nūmenōr](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor "Númenor")*, Ramer seems to be intrigued, saying that it was his name for [Atlantis](/wiki/Atlantis-haunting "Atlantis-haunting");<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and during the *Night 66* he notes a peculiar sequence of names: <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Albuin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Albuin (page does not exist)">Albuin</a> son of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Auduin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Auduin (page does not exist)">Auduin</a>, [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine) son of [Éadwine](/wiki/%C3%89adwine) and Alwin son of [Edwin](/wiki/Edwin_Lowdham "Edwin Lowdham"), all names bearing the same two meanings.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Afterwards, he asked [Professor Rashbold](/wiki/Professor_Rashbold) to transliterate and translate, if he can, a manuscript which was written by Edwin Lowdham, Arundel's father in an unknown script (which turned out to be [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar)), and he succeeded, finding that the language in which the text was written was [Old English](/wiki/Old_English). The text described an unknown cataclysmic event, which turned out to be the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In one of the manuscripts, beneath *Ramer* [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote *Self*, indicating that originally he intended Ramer to serve as a sort of an alter-ego for himself inside the text. Later, he wrote *CSL* (indicating [C.S. Lewis](/wiki/C.S._Lewis)) and finally he wrote *To*. However, according to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), it would be useless to seek any great similarity between the characters from the text and the real people.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
