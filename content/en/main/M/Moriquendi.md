The **Moriquendi** ([Q](/wiki/Quenya "Quenya"): "Elves of Darkness"; singular *Moriquende*) were all Elves who did not behold the [Light](/wiki/Light_of_Valinor "Light of Valinor") of the [Two Trees of Valinor](/wiki/Two_Trees_of_Valinor).

## History {#History}

When the Vala [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB) came to invite the Elves of [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen) to come live in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor), 56 of 144 chose to remain, becoming [Avari](/wiki/Avari), while another 26 of 144 stopped along the way, becoming [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor) and [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup> All those Elves who never reached Valinor are "Dark Elves."

In the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), the Sindar viewed the term "Moriquendi" as offensive, so the Noldorin [Exiles](/wiki/Exiles) gave the name [Úmanyar](/wiki/%C3%9Amanyar) ("Those not of [Aman](/wiki/Aman)") to those who started the journey but did not finish it. This specifically included the Sindar and the [Laiquendi](/wiki/Laiquendi), and, broadly, also the other Nandor and their [Silvan](/wiki/Silvan) and [Galadhrim](/wiki/Galadhrim) descendants.

King [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) was a special exception to these categories: he had been to Valinor as an ambassador of the Elves and saw the Light of the Trees, but he did not complete the Journey. As such he was one of the *Úmanyar* but not a Dark Elf, and was reckoned among the [Calaquendi](/wiki/Calaquendi).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup> Nonetheless, the [Sons of Fëanor](/wiki/Sons_of_F%C3%ABanor) called Thingol "Dark Elf" as an insult.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

The special title "**Dark Elf**" given to [Eöl](/wiki/E%C3%B6l) of [Nan Elmoth](/wiki/Nan_Elmoth) seems to refer to his personal aversion to the light of the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) rather than his heritage:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>But now the trees of Nan Elmoth were the tallest and darkest in all Beleriand, and there the sun never came; and there Eöl dwelt, who was named the Dark Elf. [...] There he lived in deep shadow, loving the night and the twilight under the stars.
</p>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Tolkien said that in his earliest conception, the Dark Elves were "imagined as wandering about, and often ill-disposed towards the 'Light-Elves'". Later he considered an additional use of the term: "sometimes applied to Elves captured by [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) and enslaved and then released to do mischief among the Elves." He thought that this latter idea should be taken up for Eöl.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In Norse mythology, there are the [Light Elves](/wiki/Light-elves "Light-elves") (<i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_elves" title="wikipedia:Light elves">Ljósálfar</a></i>) and the Dark Elves (*Dökkálfar* or <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svart%C3%A1lfar" title="wikipedia:Svartálfar">Svartálfar</a></i>), a tradition which was well known to [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup>
