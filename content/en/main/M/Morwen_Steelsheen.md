**Morwen** <i><b>Steelsheen</b></i> was the queen to King [Thengel](/wiki/Thengel) of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) and the mother of his renowned heir, [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden).<sup id="cite_ref-Eorl_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eorl-4">[4]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Although Morwen dwelt in [Lossarnach](/wiki/Lossarnach), she did not belong to the people of that land. Her father was originally from [Belfalas](/wiki/Belfalas), having relocated to Lossarnach for its flowering vales. He was a descendant of a former [Prince of Dol Amroth](/wiki/Prince_of_Dol_Amroth), making him and Morwen kinsfolk of [Prince Imrahil](/wiki/Imrahil "Imrahil").<sup id="cite_ref-linear_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-linear-3">[3]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2943](/wiki/Third_Age_2943 "Third Age 2943"), Morwen married [Thengel](/wiki/Thengel) of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), though she was seventeen years the younger. While living in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) she bore three children, of whom [Théoden](/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden), the second, was her only son. Following the death of his father, [Fengel](/wiki/Fengel), Thengel inherited the [Kingship of Rohan](/wiki/King_of_Rohan "King of Rohan") and returned to Rohan with Morwen. There, she bore him two more daughters.<sup id="cite_ref-Eorl_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eorl-4">[4]</a></sup> The youngest, [Théodwyn](/wiki/Th%C3%A9odwyn), was the fairest<sup id="cite_ref-Eorl_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eorl-4">[4]</a></sup> and would become the mother of [Éomer](/wiki/%C3%89omer) and [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn)<sup id="cite_ref-Third_Line_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third_Line-5">[5]</a></sup>.

## Description {#Description}

The [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) bestowed upon Morwen the epithet *Steelsheen*.<sup id="cite_ref-Third_Line_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third_Line-5">[5]</a></sup>

As one of the [Dúnedain of the South](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_the_South) with high [Númenórean](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean) lineage, Morwen passed on traits of stature<sup id="cite_ref-linear_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-linear-3">[3]</a></sup> and bearing<sup id="cite_ref-Third_Line_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third_Line-5">[5]</a></sup>. Her descendants - particularly Éomer - were said to be considerably taller than most of the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim), and some inherited her dark hair.<sup id="cite_ref-linear_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-linear-3">[3]</a></sup> Her granddaughter [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn), was also slender and tall, and bore the same graceful and proud-bearing manner of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-Third_Line_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Third_Line-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Morwen* has earlier origins in [Tolkien's](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") [Legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium), notably belonging to a prominent member of the [House of Bëor](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor) in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age). In [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) it means "Dark Maiden", from the root [MOR](/wiki/MOR)- ("black, dark, darkness") + the female suffix *[-wen](/wiki/-wen)* ("maiden").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

Didier Salamon and David Giraudeau suggest that her epithet *Steelsheen* is a modernized form of an Old English name that consists of the elements *stíle* ("steel")<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and *scíne* ("beautiful")<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>. Mark Fisher alternatively suggests that the epithet could refer to a glint like steel in her eyes, drawing a parallel to her First Age namesake, [Morwen](/wiki/Morwen), who was given the epithet *Elfsheen* because of the light of her glance (and the beauty of her face).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
