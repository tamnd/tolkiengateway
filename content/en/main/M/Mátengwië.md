**Mátengwië** refers to the "language of the hands" among the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar).<sup id="cite_ref-Palta_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palta-1">[1]</a></sup> It was also known as <i><b>hwermë</b></i>, and it was not a sign [language](/wiki/Languages "Languages") of its own like [Iglishmêk](/wiki/Iglishm%C3%AAk) among the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) (although both shared surprising analogies), but part of the corporal expression of the Eldar, which was the first thing they paid attention to when speaking.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> [Men](/wiki/Men) had similar gestures, but with different or opposite meaning. [Elves](/wiki/Elves) put great attention to the details, so their gestures could change depending even the fingers.<sup id="cite_ref-Palta_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palta-1">[1]</a></sup> Early [loremasters](/wiki/Loremasters "Loremasters") did not give much importance to *hwermë*, but it is said that [Pengolodh](/wiki/Pengolodh) collected much material concerning gesture-systems before departing from [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Examples {#Examples}

- "Asking for a gift" = one hand palm upwards (opening all fingers indicates great need).<sup id="cite_ref-Palta_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palta-1">[1]</a></sup>
- "Be at the service or command of someone" = both hand palms upwards.<sup id="cite_ref-Palta_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palta-1">[1]</a></sup>
- "Prohibition, silence, demanding stop, rejection" = one hand palm forwards (opening all fingers indicates more hostility).<sup id="cite_ref-Palta_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palta-1">[1]</a></sup>
- "Greeting or welcome" = one hand palm backwards (waving of the fingers towards the signaller indicates emphasis). In casual greeting, the hand was held edge forward.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
- "Peace, completely unarmed" = both arms opened wide below shoulder-level with palms outwards.<sup id="cite_ref-Palta_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Palta-1">[1]</a></sup>
- "Receiving guests" = stand up if seated, even if the host is a mighty king.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In making the one-handed gestures, either hand could be used without change in significance. Using both hands was more emphatic, indicating a command from a community or party, or from an authority via a subordinate.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Mátengwië* is [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), being a combination of *[má](/wiki/M%C3%A1 "Má")* ("hand") + <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Tengwi%C3%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tengwië (page does not exist)">tengwië</a></i> ("language"). At first, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote the term as *málambë*, but as [Patrick H. Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_H._Wynne) explains, *[lambë](/wiki/Lamb%C3%AB "Lambë")* refers to "tongue movement", so the term was corrected to a word coming from the [TEÑ](/wiki/TE%C3%91) stem ("indicate, signify").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
