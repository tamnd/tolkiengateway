**Middle-earth: The Dragons**, released in [1996](/wiki/1996), was the first expansion set for *[Middle-earth: The Wizards](/wiki/Middle-earth:_The_Wizards)*, the first basic set of [Middle-earth Collectible Card Game](/wiki/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game). As the name implies, it deals mostly with [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons), their treasure and the northern regions of Middle-earth.

Five new dragons were introduced: [Bairanax](/wiki/Bairanax), [Eärcaraxë](/wiki/E%C3%A4rcarax%C3%AB), [Itangast](/wiki/Itangast), [Scatha](/wiki/Scatha), and [Scorba](/wiki/Scorba).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> New cards supporting the existing dragons [Agburanar](/wiki/Agburanar), [Daelomin](/wiki/Daelomin), [Leucaruth](/wiki/Leucaruth), and [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) were also released.

*The Dragons* was sold in booster packs of 15 cards (36 boosters per display), featuring 1 rare, 4 uncommons and 10 commons per booster pack. A [corresponding player guide](/wiki/Middle-earth:_The_Dragons_Player_Guide "Middle-earth: The Dragons Player Guide") was also released.

## Reception {#Reception}

*The Dragons* won the Origins Award for *Best Graphic Presentation of a Card Game or Expansion of 1996*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In a review for *The Duelist*, Rick Swan praised the new rulesbook's clarity. He considered the manifestation system, which saw dragons appearing as a creature, a permanent event, and a long event, to be “the game’s best feature”. *The Dragons*, according to Swan, broadened “the scope of the game rather than merely increasing the number of cards”.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Andy Butcher, writing for *Arcane*, positively reviewed *The Dragons* as well, concluding that it managed “to emphasise the positive aspects of the game, without adding greatly to the already weighty rules.”<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
