**Farewell We Call to Hearth and Hall!** is a song that was sung by [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck") and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") at the house in [Crickhollow](/wiki/Crickhollow) after the "conspiracy" of [Frodo's](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins") friends was revealed. Merry and Pippin had composed it for the occasion, modeling it and setting it to the same tune as the [dwarf-song](/wiki/Far_over_the_misty_mountains_cold "Far over the misty mountains cold") that [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") had heard in [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) at the start of his adventure. After it was sung Frodo praised the song and took the line "We must away ere break of day" literally, to Pippin's dismay.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Text {#Text}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><audio src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/c/cc/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Farewell_We_Call_to_Hearth_and_Hall%21.mp3" width="300" class="mw-file-element" style="max-width: 100%; width: 300px;" controls=""><a href="https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Farewell_We_Call_to_Hearth_and_Hall!.mp3">https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Farewell_We_Call_to_Hearth_and_Hall!.mp3</a></audio><figcaption><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. <div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Farewell we call to hearth and hall!<br/>
Though wind may blow and rain may fall,<br/>
We must away ere break of day<br/>
Far over wood and mountain tall.<br/>
<br/>
To <a href="/wiki/Rivendell" title="Rivendell">Rivendell</a>, where Elves yet dwell<br/>
In glades beneath the misty fell,<br/>
Through moor and waste we ride in haste,<br/>
And whither then we cannot tell. <br/>
<br/>
With foes ahead, behind us dread,<br/>
Beneath the sky shall be our bed,<br/>
Until at last our toil be passed,<br/>
Our journey done, our errand sped.<br/>
<br/>
We must away! We must away!<br/>
We ride before the break of day!
</p>
</div>
