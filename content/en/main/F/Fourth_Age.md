The **Fourth Age** began after [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) was finally defeated, when his [Ruling Ring](../T/The_One_Ring.md) was destroyed, and the [Keepers of the Three Rings](../K/Keepers_of_the_Three_Rings.md) left Middle-earth for the [Uttermost West](../A/Aman.md).

## History {#History}

The [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) was held to have ended when [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md) left Middle-earth on [29 September](../_/29_September.md) of [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3021](../T/Third_Age_3021.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-2">[2]</a></sup>

This age was marked by the recovery of the [Númenorean](../N/Númenor.md) kingdoms of [Arnor](../A/Arnor.md) and [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), while the [House of Durin](../H/House_of_Durin.md) retook [Moria](../M/Moria.md) until the race of [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) failed.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Last Ship](../L/Last_Ship.md) of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), carrying [Círdan](../C/Círdan.md), [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md), and all remaining Elves of the [First Age](First_Age.md) left Middle-earth some time after <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Fo.A.</a> [171](Fourth_Age_171.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Records_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-4">[4]</a></sup> Eventually that would lead to the final [Dominion of Men](../D/Dominion_of_Men.md) over [Arda](../A/Arda.md), coming together with the total waning of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) and other dwindling races, such as the [Ents](../E/Ents.md) and probably the Dwarves.

[Eldarion](../E/Eldarion.md)'s reign would have lasted for about 100 years after the death of [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-L338_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L338-5">[5]</a></sup>

There is no concrete information on more than the first few centuries of this Age, so it is not known when it ended, although it was probably shorter than 3 millennia,<sup id="cite_ref-Letter211_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter211-6">[6]</a></sup> around c. 2,430 years or more,<sup id="cite_ref-Sixty_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sixty-7">[7]</a></sup> and was followed by the [Fifth Age](../L/Later_Ages.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Letter211_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter211-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Sixty_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sixty-7">[7]</a></sup>

Additionally, according to one of the drafts of the *[Appendices](../A/Appendices.md)*, it is said:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Of <a href="../E/Eldarion.md" title="Eldarion">Eldarion</a> son of <a href="../A/Aragorn.md" title="Aragorn">Elessar</a> it was foretold that he should rule a great <a href="../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md" title="Reunited Kingdom">realm</a>, and <b>that it should endure for a hundred generations of <a href="../M/Men.md" title="Men">Men</a> after him, that is until a new age brought in again new things</b>; and from him should come the kings of many realms in long days after.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="../T/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth.md" title="The Peoples of Middle-earth">The Peoples of Middle-earth</a></i>, &#34;<a href="../T/The_Tale_of_Years_of_the_Third_Age.md" title="The Tale of Years of the Third Age">The Tale of Years of the Third Age</a>&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup></cite></p>

## Reckoning of years {#Reckoning_of_years}

Not all calendars in Middle-earth reset their count of years to <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Fo.A.</a> [1](Fourth_Age_1.md) in the same year, on the same date, or indeed at all. As such, years given for certain events in the Fourth Age differ according to the calendar used.

The [Shire-reckoning](../S/Shire-reckoning.md) is the calendar system used for all dates in the [Red Book](../R/Red_Book.md), with [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1](../T/Third_Age_1601.md) corresponding to [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1601](../T/Third_Age_1601.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-9">[9]</a></sup> Years in Shire-reckoning did not restart at the end of the Third Age; they continued uninterrupted from [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1421](../T/Third_Age_3021.md) to [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1422](Fourth_Age_1.md), and "in so far as the Hobbits took any account of the change of Age, they maintained that it began with [2 Yule](../_/2_Yule.md) 1422."<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-2">[2]</a></sup> This means that [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1422](Fourth_Age_1.md) is also [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3022](../T/Third_Age_3022.md) and <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Fo.A.</a> [1](Fourth_Age_1.md) as far as dates in the Red Book are concerned.

However, in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and the rest of the [Reunited Kingdom](../R/Reunited_Kingdom.md) outside the Shire, the [New Reckoning](../N/New_Reckoning.md) calendar entered use starting in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md). Under this calendar, [25 March](../_/25_March.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3021](../T/Third_Age_3021.md) is the first day of <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Fo.A.</a> [1](Fourth_Age_1.md), some 9 months earlier than the new year in the Shire.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendars_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendars-2">[2]</a></sup> The [Shire Calendar](../S/Shire_Calendar.md) and the [New Reckoning](../N/New_Reckoning.md) begin their years on different days: 2 Yule for the Shire and 25 March for the rest of the Reunited Kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup>

There are few dates given in the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) that require the reader to account for this difference, because most dates of the Fourth Age are already explicitly expressed in terms of the Shire Reckoning. For example, [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md) and [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md)'s departure from Middle-earth is given as [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1541](Fourth_Age_120.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Later_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Later-11">[10]</a></sup> The exceptions are both from the [Note on the Shire Records](../N/Note_on_the_Shire_Records.md) and are as follows:

- Tolkien explains that the text of *The Lord of the Rings* descends from a copy of the Red Book made in Gondor that bore the note "Findegil, King’s Writer, finished this work in IV 172."<sup id="cite_ref-Records_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-4">[4]</a></sup> The accompanying text notes the equivalent year [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1592](Fourth_Age_171.md), which is <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Fo.A.</a> [171](Fourth_Age_171.md) in the Shire Reckoning, so this note must have been written with regard to the New Reckoning calendar before 7 Narvinyë.
- In the same paragraph, Tolkien refers to [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md)'s retirement to Gondor in "IV 64."<sup id="cite_ref-Records_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Records-4">[4]</a></sup> We are told elsewhere that Peregrin and his companion [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) arrived in Gondor sometime between the autumn and the ending of [S.R.](../S/Shire_Reckoning.md) [1484](Fourth_Age_63.md), which is <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Fo.A.</a> [63](Fourth_Age_63.md) in the Shire Reckoning.<sup id="cite_ref-Later_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Later-11">[10]</a></sup> As such, the stated "IV 64" must be in reference to the New Reckoning calendar, again, prior to 7 Narvinyë.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The Prologue of the first edition of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* mistakenly mentions that the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md) "ended with the great years [1418](../T/Third_Age_3018.md) and [1419](../T/Third_Age_3019.md) of the Shire."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup> whereas the Age ended in [1421](../T/Third_Age_3021.md) and [1422](Fourth_Age_1.md). This reference was removed in the second edition.
