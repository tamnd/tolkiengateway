<i><b>fae</b></i> is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "spirit".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Cognates {#Cognates}

- [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) *[fëa](/wiki/F%C3%ABa "Fëa")*

<dl><dd>Tolkien mentions that the name <i><a href="/wiki/F%C3%ABanor" title="Fëanor">Fëanor</a></i> is a Sindarinized version of the Quenya <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/F%C3%ABan%C3%A1ro" title="Fëanáro">Fëanáro</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> so is not proper Sindarin. His genuine Sindarin name would be <i>Faenor</i> instead.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup></dd></dl>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fae" title="wiktionary:fae">Fae</a></b> is an archaic English word referring to "magic" or "[fairies](/wiki/Fairies "Fairies")". This word was used by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") in the early version of his [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") (*[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*) to refer to the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) and the [Elves](/wiki/Elves). <span></span>
