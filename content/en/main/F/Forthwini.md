**Forthwini** was the second [Lord of the Éothéod](../L/Lord_of_the_Éothéod.md) while they lived in the [Vales of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md). He was the son of [Marhwini](../M/Marhwini.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

During Forthwini's rule his spies learned that the [Wainriders](../W/Wainriders.md), a tribe of [Easterlings](../E/Easterlings.md) who had invaded decades earlier, had recovered and were allying themselves with other forces in the [East](../E/East.md). He alerted King [Ondoher](../O/Ondoher.md) of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) of the growing threat, and later the [Éothéod](../_/Éothéod.md) joined the armies of Gondor in battle against the invaders. Though Ondoher and both his sons were slain, his successor [Eärnil II](../E/Eärnil_II.md) eventually destroyed the Wainriders in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1944](../T/Third_Age_1944.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Cirion_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cirion-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> There is no record of Forthwini's fate.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The [leader of the Éothéod](../L/Leader_of_the_Éothéod.md) whose name stared with *Marh*, who rode to battle in the campaign against the Wainriders, may have been Forthwini's successor<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and/or son, but this is not stated.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> The next definitively known chieftain of the Éothéod is [Frumgar](Frumgar.md), who was chieftain<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1977](../T/Third_Age_1977.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> The specific relationship between Forthwini and Frumgar is unknown.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) suggests that the name *Forthwini* is [Gothic](../G/Gothic.md) in form and that the second element *wini* ("friend") is an archaic form of [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *wine* seen in the [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md) names of several Kings of Rohan ( [Fréawine](Fréawine.md), [Goldwine](../G/Goldwine.md) and [Folcwine](Folcwine.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

[David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) and [Arden R. Smith](../A/Arden_R._Smith.md) suggest that the name is early [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) in form.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

It is also possible that it is a hybrid [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) and Old High German (OHG) name. The first element is Old English (Anglo-Saxon \[AS\]) *forth*<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> and the second element is Old High German (OHG) *wini* ("friend"), which corresponds to Anglo-Saxon (AS) *wine*.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>

David Salo suggests that it means "friend of the front" or "friend, who is at the front", so a companion-in-arms who is at the front line of the tribal army. [Tom Shippey](../T/Tom_Shippey.md) suggests that it is Old Mercian English and means "forefriend". Since forth correspons to *faran* ("go", "go away") it could mean "travel friend".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>
