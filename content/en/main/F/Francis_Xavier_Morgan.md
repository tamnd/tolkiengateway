**Father Francis Xavier Morgan** ([18 January](/wiki/18_January) [1857](/wiki/1857), <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Puerto_de_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa" title="wikipedia:El Puerto de Santa María">El Puerto de Santa María</a> - [11 June](/wiki/11_June) [1935](/wiki/1935), [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham)) was a Roman Catholic priest at the [Birmingham Oratory](/wiki/Birmingham_Oratory) in [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham). He was half Welsh and half Anglo-Spanish.

He took care of [Mabel Tolkien](/wiki/Mabel_Suffield "Mabel Suffield") during her illness, and took over custody of [Ronald](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") and [Hilary Tolkien](/wiki/Hilary_Tolkien) after her death. Father Francis was a [pipe-smoker](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_inspirations#pipe-smoking "J.R.R. Tolkien's inspirations") and Tolkien derived his own custom from this.

Father Francis Xavier Morgan, forbade Tolkien to see [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Tolkien "Edith Tolkien") until he was twenty-one because he saw her as a distraction from his studies. With one exception, Tolkien obeyed this instruction to the letter while Father Morgan's guardianship lasted.

Tolkien named his first son, [John Francis Reuel](/wiki/John_Tolkien "John Tolkien") after him.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In 2013, [José Manuel Ferrández Bru](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Ferr%C3%A1ndez_Bru) published *[La Conexion Española de J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/La_Conexion_Espa%C3%B1ola_de_J.R.R._Tolkien)*, a Spanish-language biography about Francis Xavier Morgan.<sup id="cite_ref-bru_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bru-2">[2]</a></sup> The book was later translated into English.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the biographical drama film [Tolkien](</wiki/Tolkien_(film)> "Tolkien (film)"), he was portrayed by actor [Colm Meaney](/wiki/Colm_Meaney).

<h2><span id="In_Tolkien.27s_works"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Tolkien&#39;s_works">In Tolkien&#39;s works</span></h2>

In the *[Gnomish Lexicon](/wiki/Gnomish_Lexicon)*, Tolkien translates the name "Francis" into [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish): *Faidron* or *Faithron*. These names are clearly related with other words of the same lexicon: *fair* ("free, unconstrained"), *faidwen/faith* ("liberty"), *faithir* ("liberator, Saviour").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The translation is based on etymology as the name <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_(given_name)#Derivation" title="wikipedia:Francis (given name)">Francis</a> (from Italian Francesco) derives from the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks#Etymology" title="wikipedia:Franks">Franks</a>, who in turn gave the word <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/frank" title="wiktionary:frank">frank</a> ("free of servitude; candid, outspoken, unreserved").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Ferrández Bru considered that Tolkien honored Father Francis this way.

Ferrández Bru suggests that [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) was partly inspired from Father Francis, considering his opposition to the courtship of [Beren](/wiki/Beren) to [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien); with whom Tolkien identified himself and Edith.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
