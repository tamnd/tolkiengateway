**Father Francis Xavier Morgan** ([18 January](../_/18_January.md) [1857](../_/1857.md), <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Puerto_de_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa" title="wikipedia:El Puerto de Santa María">El Puerto de Santa María</a> - [11 June](../_/11_June.md) [1935](../_/1935.md), [Birmingham](../B/Birmingham.md)) was a Roman Catholic priest at the [Birmingham Oratory](../B/Birmingham_Oratory.md) in [Birmingham](../B/Birmingham.md). He was half Welsh and half Anglo-Spanish.

He took care of [Mabel Tolkien](../M/Mabel_Suffield.md) during her illness, and took over custody of [Ronald](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) and [Hilary Tolkien](../H/Hilary_Tolkien.md) after her death. Father Francis was a [pipe-smoker](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien's_inspirations.md#pipe-smoking) and Tolkien derived his own custom from this.

Father Francis Xavier Morgan, forbade Tolkien to see [Edith Bratt](../E/Edith_Tolkien.md) until he was twenty-one because he saw her as a distraction from his studies. With one exception, Tolkien obeyed this instruction to the letter while Father Morgan's guardianship lasted.

Tolkien named his first son, [John Francis Reuel](../J/John_Tolkien.md) after him.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In 2013, [José Manuel Ferrández Bru](../J/José_Manuel_Ferrández_Bru.md) published *[La Conexion Española de J.R.R. Tolkien](../L/La_Conexion_Española_de_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)*, a Spanish-language biography about Francis Xavier Morgan.<sup id="cite_ref-bru_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bru-2">[2]</a></sup> The book was later translated into English.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the biographical drama film [Tolkien](<../T/Tolkien_(film).md>), he was portrayed by actor [Colm Meaney](../C/Colm_Meaney.md).

<h2><span id="In_Tolkien.27s_works"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Tolkien&#39;s_works">In Tolkien&#39;s works</span></h2>

In the *[Gnomish Lexicon](../G/Gnomish_Lexicon.md)*, Tolkien translates the name "Francis" into [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md): *Faidron* or *Faithron*. These names are clearly related with other words of the same lexicon: *fair* ("free, unconstrained"), *faidwen/faith* ("liberty"), *faithir* ("liberator, Saviour").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The translation is based on etymology as the name <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_(given_name)#Derivation" title="wikipedia:Francis (given name)">Francis</a> (from Italian Francesco) derives from the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks#Etymology" title="wikipedia:Franks">Franks</a>, who in turn gave the word <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/frank" title="wiktionary:frank">frank</a> ("free of servitude; candid, outspoken, unreserved").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Ferrández Bru considered that Tolkien honored Father Francis this way.

Ferrández Bru suggests that [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md) was partly inspired from Father Francis, considering his opposition to the courtship of [Beren](../B/Beren.md) to [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md); with whom Tolkien identified himself and Edith.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
