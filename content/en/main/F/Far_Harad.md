**Far Harad**, a part of the larger region known as [Harad](/wiki/Harad), was a name used in Northwestern [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) for a distant, unknown land far to the south of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-RKV6_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKV6-1">[1]</a></sup> The name simply means the Far South ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[harad](/wiki/Harad#Etymology "Harad")*, "south"),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> as opposed to [Near Harad](/wiki/Near_Harad), the Near South.

Some men from Far Harad travelled north at the time of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), and fought on the side of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) at the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields). These warriors were described as black men with white eyes and red tongues like [half-trolls](/wiki/Half-trolls "Half-trolls").<sup id="cite_ref-RKV6_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKV6-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") stated that [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) might have made (although unlikely) a visit to Far Harad during one of his travels, but that it was too short a visit for there having "*acquired a special name*".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-97: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The land is detailed in the <a href="/wiki/Far_Harad:_The_Scorched_Land" title="Far Harad: The Scorched Land">Far Harad: The Scorched Land</a> supplement, where <b>Bozisha-Miraz</b> (“Dowry of the Goddess”) is given as the local name. Its only small fertile region was called <b>Raj</b> (“Paradise”), and as such this name was also commonly used for the entire land. The river Rijesha flowed through the region. Bozisha-Dar, known as Harshport in the <a href="/wiki/Westron" title="Westron">Common Speech</a>, was a major port city located at the place where the river met the sea. Its old cultural capital was Tresti, where the local cult of a moon goddess was centred around a well.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Far Harad was already a strong kingdom by the time the Númenóreans landed, though after its king was assassinated on the orders of <a href="/wiki/Fifth_of_the_Nazg%C3%BBl" title="Fifth of the Nazgûl">Akhôrahil</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ciryatanor" title="Ciryatanor">Ciryatanor</a> it was ruled by a council of regents for thousands of years. For a long time, the region resisted Sauron, but by <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_2000" title="Third Age 2000">2000</a> a new cult arose that replaced traditional moon goddess worship with a new Sun God whose symbol was a <a href="/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron" title="Eye of Sauron">burning, lidless eye</a>. The well of Tresti was sabotaged, and the city was abandoned in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_2178&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 2178 (page does not exist)">2178</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Third_Age_2194&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Third Age 2194 (page does not exist)">2194</a>, Far Harad became a kingdom again under the cruel Sun Lords. Only after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon" title="Battle of the Morannon">defeat of Sauron</a> was Tresti rebuilt and a new king crowned.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup></dd></dl>
