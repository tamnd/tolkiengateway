**Fengel** ([T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2870](../T/Third_Age_2870.md) - [2953](../T/Third_Age_2953.md)) was the fifteenth [King of Rohan](../K/King_of_Rohan.md).

## History {#History}

Fengel was the third and only surviving son of [Folcwine](Folcwine.md), and became king after the death of his father in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2903](../T/Third_Age_2903.md).

Fengel's two older brothers, [Folcred](Folcred.md) and [Fastred](<Fastred_(son_of_Folcwine).md>), were killed in the fief [South Gondor](../S/South_Gondor.md) while fighting for [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), and as a result Fengel grew up rather spoiled. As a grown man he became known for his greed and love of gold.

His son [Thengel](../T/Thengel.md) left Rohan because of disagreements with his father, living with [his wife](../M/Morwen_Steelsheen.md)'s family in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) instead.

After a rule of over 50 years Fengel died in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2953](../T/Third_Age_2953.md), and Thengel reluctantly returned to Rohan.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Fengel* means "prince" in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup> The word appears in three places in *Beowulf.* The word is from a Germanic root meaning "to grasp".

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2012: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the last winter of his reign, King Fengel was hunting north of the <a href="../E/Entwade.md" title="Entwade">Entwade</a> when an elderly woman named Wyrgende stumbled into his camp. Fengel sent her away into the dark night and the next day she was found frozen to death at the foot of a tall boulder, in which she had carved a curse against him. King Fengel was furious and ordered Wyrgende&#39;s body burned and the stone destroyed, but no man of his company could more her, and every hammer and mattock they set to the stone shattered on contact. Fengel himself assailed the stone with his sword until the blade shattered, and a shard pierced his thigh. The wound festered, and King Fengel died of poisoned blood on the first day of Spring.</dd></dl>
