**Far Easton**<sup id="cite_ref-Smith_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-1">[1]</a></sup> was the easternmost "village of people" related to the villagers of [Wootton Major](/wiki/Wootton_Major).<sup id="cite_ref-Essay_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Essay-2">[2]</a></sup> Though the people of Wootton Major had many "eastern customers", such people were resented when they came and were expected to act normal.<sup id="cite_ref-Essay_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Essay-2">[2]</a></sup> When [Smith Smithson](/wiki/Smith_Smithson) became a smith like <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Joe_Smith_of_West-side&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Joe Smith of West-side (page does not exist)">his father</a>, his skill became very "well known in his country".<sup id="cite_ref-Smith_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-1">[1]</a></sup> By the time his father passed away, Smith was the best in the craft "between Far Easton and the [Westwood](/wiki/Westwood)".<sup id="cite_ref-Smith_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Background {#Background}

In an essay entitled "[Smith of Wootton Major](/wiki/Smith_of_Wootton_Major_essay "Smith of Wootton Major essay")", [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) explained that the expression "From Far Easton to Westwood" represents the "bounds of the world to the villagers" of Wootton Major.<sup id="cite_ref-Essay_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Essay-2">[2]</a></sup> He also explains that if Wootton Major lost connection to [Faery](/wiki/Faery), then not even trade with "eastern customers" would save its prosperity.<sup id="cite_ref-Essay_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Essay-2">[2]</a></sup> In an essay on *Literary Hub*, [John Garth](/wiki/John_Garth) noted that the way that Tolkien used the expression "from Far Easton to the Westwood" is a pun "on *eastern* and *westward*".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In an article for *[Leaves from the Tree: J.R.R. Tolkien's Shorter Fiction](/wiki/Leaves_from_the_Tree:_J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_Shorter_Fiction)* in [1991](/wiki/1991), [Alex Lewis](/wiki/Alex_Lewis) noted that "Far Easton and Westwood are names that could have come from the Midlands" of England.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In the United Kingdom there are three Eastons: One is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easton,_Bristol" title="wikipedia:Easton, Bristol">council ward</a> within the city of Bristol. In Essex there are two – "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Easton" title="wikipedia:Little Easton">Little Easton</a>", a village that dates to the twelfth century, and "<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Easton,_Essex" title="wikipedia:Great Easton, Essex">Great Easton</a>", a village one mile north of Little Easton.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>
