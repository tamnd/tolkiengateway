The four **Farthings** were four of the five (later six) divisions of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md). They were known simply as [Northfarthing](../N/Northfarthing.md), [Westfarthing](../W/Westfarthing.md), [Southfarthing](../S/Southfarthing.md) and [Eastfarthing](../E/Eastfarthing.md).<sup id="cite_ref-shire_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shire-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The [Westron](../W/Westron.md) word was *[tharni](../T/Tharni.md)*, the [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) word (presumably) <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Canath&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Canath (page does not exist)">canath</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Farthings were further divided into <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/folkland" title="wiktionary:folkland">folklands</a></i>, that bore the names of the land's leading family, such as [Tookland](../T/Tookland.md) of the Westfarthing, named after the [Took Family](../T/Took_Family.md); although many other families had migrated to other regions by the late [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md).<sup id="cite_ref-shire_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shire-1">[1]</a></sup>

The [Watch](../W/Watch.md) assigned three [Shirriffs](../S/Shirriffs.md) to each Farthing.<sup id="cite_ref-shire_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shire-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Buckland](../B/Buckland.md) (the East March) was not a part of the Shire, and the [Westmarch](../W/Westmarch.md) was not a part of any Farthing.<sup id="cite_ref-shire_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shire-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In English a farthing ([Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *feorðing* "fourth part, quarter" &gt; *ferthing* "a negligible amount") is a quarter of a penny.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-4">[4]</a></sup>

In Shire geography used in the original sense "fourth part". [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) noted that the application to the divisions of the Shire to English ears has a perhaps comical effect.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The four Farthings of the Shire were based on the three traditional Ridings (from the Old English *thridings*) of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire" title="wikipedia:Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, which were formerly used as administrative divisions of the county (<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="wikipedia:North Riding of Yorkshire">North Riding</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="wikipedia:West Riding of Yorkshire">West Riding</a> and <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="wikipedia:East Riding of Yorkshire">East Riding</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

"Farthing" was used in Gloucestershire. Tolkien did not mention this in *[Nomenclature](../G/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, but it is likely he knew of its existence as his wife [Edith Bratt](../E/Edith_Tolkien.md) was born in Gloucestershire.
