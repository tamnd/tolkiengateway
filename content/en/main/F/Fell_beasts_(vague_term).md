**Fell beasts** and **fell creatures** were vague terms used to describe different monstrous, evil, and dangerous [animals](/wiki/Kelvar "Kelvar") and other [creatures](/wiki/Creatures "Creatures") that existed in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) (but not in [Aman](/wiki/Aman))<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> from the [Elder Days](/wiki/Elder_Days) until the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). The [winged steeds](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl-birds "Nazgûl-birds") of the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> in addition to [Werewolves](/wiki/Werewolves),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> were described as fell beasts. The Nazgûl themselves<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and [hill-trolls](/wiki/Hill-trolls "Hill-trolls")<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> were sometimes called fell creatures. In some cases [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs), [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls), and [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons) were distinguished from fell beasts.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

When the [Spring of Arda](/wiki/Spring_of_Arda) was marred, some beasts became monstrous creatures with horns and tusks.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> In [Utumno](/wiki/Utumno), [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth") bred monsters of different shapes and kinds.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> At these times, [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB) came to Middle-earth as a hunter of monsters and fell creatures<sup id="cite_ref-:1_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-10">[10]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

While Melkor was [captured](/wiki/Chaining_of_Melkor "Chaining of Melkor") by the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), not all of his evil creatures were destroyed. They multiplied in the darkness, and even before the return of Morgoth, fell beasts of the North were roaming far and wide in the lands east of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains),<sup id="cite_ref-:2_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-12">[12]</a></sup> so the [Nandor](/wiki/Nandor) who inhabited woods of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) were frightened<sup id="cite_ref-:3_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-13">[13]</a></sup> and fled from the plains to the hills<sup id="cite_ref-:2_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-12">[12]</a></sup> or migrated to [Ossiriand](/wiki/Ossiriand).<sup id="cite_ref-:3_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-13">[13]</a></sup> Soon fell creatures came even to [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) over passes in the mountains and from the [dark forests](/wiki/Taur-im-Duinath "Taur-im-Duinath") of the south.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-14">[14]</a></sup> When Morgoth returned, he gathered countless hosts of beasts, [demons](/wiki/Demons "Demons"), and Orcs in [Angband](/wiki/Angband),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> while the wilderness of [Nan Dungortheb](/wiki/Nan_Dungortheb) was inhabited by fell creatures of [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> including [spiders](/wiki/Spiders "Spiders") and other monsters.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

Fell beasts continued to trouble Middle-earth during the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) and the Third Age.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">[7]</a></sup> In the Third Age, many fell beasts and evil creatures began hunting in the [Greenwood](/wiki/Mirkwood "Mirkwood") when the forest became haunted by darkness.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> The [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) gathered many fell creatures as well as Orcs and evil [Men](/wiki/Men) in his realm of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) in order to destroy the [Dúnedain of Arnor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Arnor).<sup id="cite_ref-appendixa_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendixa-19">[19]</a></sup> The [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_the_South "Dúnedain of the South") of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) also faced threats from monsters in addition to Orcs and evil Men.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) had a vision at the [Seat of Seeing](/wiki/Seat_of_Seeing) atop [Amon Hen](/wiki/Amon_Hen) wherein he saw [Wood-elves](/wiki/Wood-elves) and [Woodmen](/wiki/Woodmen) [battling](/wiki/Battle_under_the_trees "Battle under the trees") against fell beasts in Mirkwood.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In relation to the expression "fell things" in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> <i><a class="external text" href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fell#Etymology_4" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">fell</a></i> means "dreadful, terrible, fierce, cruel" as an archaic English word.

## Similar terms {#Similar_terms}

**Monsters**,<sup id="cite_ref-:1_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-10">[10]</a></sup> **fell beings**<sup id="cite_ref-:4_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-14">[14]</a></sup>, **foul creatures**<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> and **evil creatures**<sup id="cite_ref-:1_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-10">[10]</a></sup> were similarly vague and perhaps even more broad terms. Both monstrous spiders<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> and Orcs<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> were sometimes called "foul creatures"<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>. [Oliphaunts](/wiki/Oliphaunts) were sometimes called "monsters"<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup> and "great monsters"<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> as well as occasionally "[great beasts](/wiki/Great_beasts "Great beasts")".

**Beasts of [Angband](/wiki/Angband)** was a term to describe all beasts in the hosts of Morgoth,<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup> such as [wolves](/wiki/Wolves "Wolves") and maybe Dragons as well.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>

**Misshapen beasts**, alongside demons, Dragons, and Orcs, were among evil things devised by Morgoth in the days of his dominion that continued to trouble [Men](/wiki/Men) during the [Dark Years](/wiki/Dark_Years) of the Second Age.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>

**[Spell](/wiki/Magic "Magic")-enslaved beasts**, alongside Orcs, Trolls, [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings), and [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim), were a part of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron)'s army in the [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon)<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> The exact nature of these beasts is unknown.
