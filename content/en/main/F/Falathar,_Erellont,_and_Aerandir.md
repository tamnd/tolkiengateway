**Falathar**, **Erellont**, and **Aerandir**, were the three faithful companions that were noted as having sailed all the seas besides [Eärendil](../E/Eärendil.md). They were with him when the ship [Vingilot](../V/Vingilot.md) made its epic journey into the distant [West](../A/Aman.md), to reach the [Blessed Realm](../B/Blessed_Realm.md) itself. While Eärendil and [Elwing](../E/Elwing.md) journeyed ashore, they remained aboard the ship. After Eärendil had delivered his embassy to the [Valar](../V/Valar.md), their emissary [Eönwë](../E/Eönwë.md) came to the three mariners and set them in another boat. The Valar sent a wind out of the west to blow them back across the [Great Sea](../B/Belegaer.md) and, presumably, they landed again somewhere in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), though their ultimate fate is not recorded.<sup id="cite_ref-lostroad_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostroad-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Falathar* contains [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) *[falas](Falas.md)* ("shore, line of surf").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The ending is not explained, but a relationship to <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Thar-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Thar- (page does not exist)">thar-</a></i> "across, over, beyond" is not inconceivable.

*Aerandir* is [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md), meaning "Sea-wanderer" (*[aer](../A/Aer.md)* + *[randir](../R/Randir.md)*).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The name originally appeared in an early manuscript as **Airandir**,<sup id="cite_ref-lostroad_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostroad-1">[1]</a></sup> a form later rejected by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The meaning of *Erellont* is unknown. It has been speculated that the name means "lonely (star) ship" in [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 1]</a></sup> or that the name is "possibly a dialectal Sindarin of the Falas" or even a "combination of \[Mannish\] and Elvish".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Quenta](../T/The_Quenta.md)* of 1930, his only named companion was [Voronwë](../V/Voronwë.md).<sup id="cite_ref-SM_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-8">[7]</a></sup> In the later 1930s *[Quenta Silmarillion](<../Q/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road).md>)*, his three companions were Falathar, Aerandir, and Erellont.<sup id="cite_ref-lostroad_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostroad-1">[1]</a></sup> It was this version that [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) chose to use in *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)*. In a later note, [Gelmir and Arminas](../G/Gelmir_and_Arminas.md) were among his companions.<sup id="cite_ref-ga495n11_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ga495n11-9">[8]</a></sup>

## Speculation {#Speculation}

[Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) never specified the race of the three mariners: while it has been suggested that they were [Men](../M/Men.md),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> others argue that they were [Elves](../E/Elves.md).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>
