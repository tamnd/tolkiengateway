**Falathar**, **Erellont**, and **Aerandir**, were the three faithful companions that were noted as having sailed all the seas besides [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil). They were with him when the ship [Vingilot](/wiki/Vingilot) made its epic journey into the distant [West](/wiki/Aman "Aman"), to reach the [Blessed Realm](/wiki/Blessed_Realm) itself. While Eärendil and [Elwing](/wiki/Elwing) journeyed ashore, they remained aboard the ship. After Eärendil had delivered his embassy to the [Valar](/wiki/Valar), their emissary [Eönwë](/wiki/E%C3%B6nw%C3%AB) came to the three mariners and set them in another boat. The Valar sent a wind out of the west to blow them back across the [Great Sea](/wiki/Belegaer "Belegaer") and, presumably, they landed again somewhere in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), though their ultimate fate is not recorded.<sup id="cite_ref-lostroad_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostroad-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Falathar* contains [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[falas](/wiki/Falas "Falas")* ("shore, line of surf").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The ending is not explained, but a relationship to <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Thar-&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Thar- (page does not exist)">thar-</a></i> "across, over, beyond" is not inconceivable.

*Aerandir* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), meaning "Sea-wanderer" (*[aer](/wiki/Aer "Aer")* + *[randir](/wiki/Randir "Randir")*).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The name originally appeared in an early manuscript as **Airandir**,<sup id="cite_ref-lostroad_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostroad-1">[1]</a></sup> a form later rejected by Tolkien.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

The meaning of *Erellont* is unknown. It has been speculated that the name means "lonely (star) ship" in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 1]</a></sup> or that the name is "possibly a dialectal Sindarin of the Falas" or even a "combination of \[Mannish\] and Elvish".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[The Quenta](/wiki/The_Quenta)* of 1930, his only named companion was [Voronwë](/wiki/Voronw%C3%AB).<sup id="cite_ref-SM_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-8">[7]</a></sup> In the later 1930s *[Quenta Silmarillion](</wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion_(Lost_Road)> "Quenta Silmarillion (Lost Road)")*, his three companions were Falathar, Aerandir, and Erellont.<sup id="cite_ref-lostroad_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lostroad-1">[1]</a></sup> It was this version that [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) chose to use in *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. In a later note, [Gelmir and Arminas](/wiki/Gelmir_and_Arminas) were among his companions.<sup id="cite_ref-ga495n11_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ga495n11-9">[8]</a></sup>

## Speculation {#Speculation}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") never specified the race of the three mariners: while it has been suggested that they were [Men](/wiki/Men),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> others argue that they were [Elves](/wiki/Elves).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>
