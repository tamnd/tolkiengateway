**[Fell beasts](<Fell_beasts_(vague_term).md>)**<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>, also called **Nazgûl-birds**<sup id="cite_ref-name_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-4">[4]</a></sup>, **Black Wings**<sup id="cite_ref-blackwing_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blackwing-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>, and **hell-hawks**<sup id="cite_ref-siege_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>, were huge pterodactylic<sup id="cite_ref-letter211_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter211-11">[11]</a></sup> flying creatures that served as steeds for the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md).

## Description {#Description}

Fell beasts were great winged creatures with a beak and claws<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> and were described as similar to great carrion birds<sup id="cite_ref-blackwing_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blackwing-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>, but monstrous looking<sup id="cite_ref-harrowdale_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-harrowdale-14">[14]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> and greater than all other birds<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>, including [Eagles](../E/Eagles.md)<sup id="cite_ref-siege_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>. They were dark and appeared shadow-like even at night.<sup id="cite_ref-dark_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dark-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> Their body was naked, lacking any quills or feathers. They had a long neck as well as vast pinions consisting of a web-like "hide between horned fingers".<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> They possessed a "fell speed" which allowed them to fly faster than the wind.<sup id="cite_ref-deadmarshes_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deadmarshes-21">[21]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> Furthermore, fell beasts also gave off a stench. In battle they swooped upon prey with their feet to drop foes from the sky,<sup id="cite_ref-siege_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-9">[9]</a></sup> crushed enemies under their sheer weight,<sup id="cite_ref-siege_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-9">[9]</a></sup> and used their vast wings to blow foul gusts of wind.<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>A creature of an older world maybe it was, whose kind, lingering in forgotten mountains cold beneath the Moon, outstayed their day, and in hideous eyrie bred this last untimely brood, apt to evil. And the Dark Lord took it, and nursed it with fell meats, until it grew beyond the measure of all other things that fly; and he gave it to his servant to be his steed.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="../T/The_Return_of_the_King.md" title="The Return of the King">The Return of the King</a></i>, &#34;<a href="../T/The_Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md" title="The Battle of the Pelennor Fields">The Battle of the Pelennor Fields</a>&#34; <sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

While their origins are uncertain, the fell beasts may have hailed from an earlier age of the world,<sup id="cite_ref-letter211_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter211-11">[11]</a></sup> a diminishing number of which had clung to survival in the remote reaches of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup> Under Sauron's care, these creatures were prepared for the use of his Nazgûl, who employed them in travel and in the uses of war.<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup>

Following the destruction of the black horses used by the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md) in their pursuit of [Frodo Baggins](Frodo_Baggins.md), Sauron appointed his brood of fell beasts to serve as the [Ringwraiths'](../R/Ringwraiths.md) new steeds.<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup> These were glimpsed on several occasions before the [Siege of Gondor](../S/Siege_of_Gondor.md), where they bore the [Nazgûl](../N/Nazgûl.md) to war and plagued the armies of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) and [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-siege_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup>

As the [Fellowship of the Ring](Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md) travelled down the [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md), a winged creature briefly attempted to assail the company before being driven off by an arrow from [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md)' bow.<sup id="cite_ref-dark_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dark-18">[18]</a></sup>

On Thursday [1 March](../_/1_March.md), while Frodo, [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md), and [Gollum](../G/Gollum.md) were crossing the [Dead Marshes,](../D/Dead_Marshes.md) a Nazgûl borne on a winged creature passed overhead at midnight at "the speed of the wrath of Sauron."<sup id="cite_ref-deadmarshes_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deadmarshes-21">[21]</a></sup> On Sunday [4 March](../_/4_March.md),<sup id="cite_ref-date_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-date-24">[24]</a></sup> the party witnessed another Nazgûl, possibly "on some swift errand from Barad-dûr", pass overhead on a winged creature. On Monday [5 March](../_/5_March.md),<sup id="cite_ref-date_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-date-24">[24]</a></sup> an hour after midnight, a third winged creature flew overhead in the direction of Mordor.

Around dawn on Tuesday<sup id="cite_ref-tuesday_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tuesday-25">[25]</a></sup> [6 March](../_/6_March.md), "a flying darkness in the shape of a monstrous bird" swooped over the hall of [Meduseld](../M/Meduseld.md) in [Edoras.](../E/Edoras.md) To deter an attack, [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) counselled the [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) to assemble at [Dunharrow](../D/Dunharrow.md), in the valley under the mountains, rather than in the fields outside Edoras. [Théoden](../T/Théoden.md) was told of the sighting after returning from the [Hornburg](../H/Hornburg.md) three days later.<sup id="cite_ref-harrowdale_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-harrowdale-14">[14]</a></sup>

Five Nazgûl, aloft on winged creatures, assailed [Faramir](Faramir.md) and his company as they retreated from [Osgiliath](../O/Osgiliath.md). They were driven off by Gandalf, who caused a white light to be emitted from his hand, which the Nazgûl could not face.<sup id="cite_ref-siege_9-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-siege-9">[9]</a></sup>

After the arrival of the Rohirrim to the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](../B/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields.md), the [Witch-king](../W/Witch-king.md) of [Angmar](../A/Angmar.md) abandoned his horse for a fell beast. Upon this steed he confronted the host of the Rohirrim, and slew Théoden's [horse](../S/Snowmane.md) from underneath him. [Éowyn](../_/Éowyn.md), casting away the guise of Dernhelm, leapt to the king's defense and killed the beast, and with the help of [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) slew its rider.<sup id="cite_ref-pelennor_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelennor-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

As in the expression "fell things" occurring earlier in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> the word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fell#Etymology_4" title="wiktionary:fell">fell</a></i> in this sense is an archaic English word meaning "dreadful, terrible".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>

The term "[fell beasts](<Fell_beasts_(vague_term).md>)" is not used solely for the winged steeds of the Nazgûl: it was also used as a descriptor for various evil creatures of [Mirkwood](../M/Mirkwood.md),<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup> and also a descriptor of a type of creature hunted by [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md) in addition to monsters.<sup id="cite_ref-valar_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-valar-29">[29]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In a reply to a letter, [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) wrote that he did not intend for the Nazgûl-bird<sup id="cite_ref-name_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-4">[4]</a></sup> to be a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterosaur" title="wikipedia:Pterosaur">pterodactyl</a>, though he acknowledged that it was "*pterodactylic*" and "owes much to" the "mythology of the 'Prehistoric'". He considered that its description left open the possibility for it to be "a last survivor of older geological eras".<sup id="cite_ref-letter211_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter211-11">[11]</a></sup>

<b><a href="../_/1978.md" title="1978">1978</a>: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)">The Lord of the Rings</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><a href="../W/Witch-king.md" title="Witch-king">The Witch-king</a><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>, carrying a mace, is shown riding on a winged creature. However, Bakshi&#39;s film only covers events up to the <a href="../B/Battle_of_the_Hornburg.md" title="Battle of the Hornburg">Battle of the Hornburg</a>, so that is the only appearance of a Nazgûl-bird.<sup id="cite_ref-name_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-4">[4]</a></sup>.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/1980.md" title="1980">1980</a>: <i><a href="../T/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film).md" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)">The Return of the King</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Nazgûl ride winged horses. In the <a class="external text" href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hWjt6LGhHsI" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">confrontation</a> between Éowyn and the Witch-king, the latter is seen riding a plump black-grey dragon-like animal. Gandalf had called it a <b>Carrion-fowl</b>.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/1982.md" title="1982">1982</a>-<a href="../_/1997.md" title="1997">9</a>7: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Fell Beasts are said to likely be &#34;distant relatives of ancient <a href="../C/Cold-drakes.md" title="Cold-drakes">Cold-drakes</a>&#34;, and &#34;grow to lengths of 30 feet (with 30-35-foot wingspans)&#34;. In the middle Third Age they live in <a href="../M/Mountains_of_Mirkwood.md" title="Mountains of Mirkwood">mountainous</a> areas of Mirkwood, presumably because of the <a href="../S/Sauron.md#Sauron&#39;s_Return" title="Sauron">Necromancer</a>&#39;s presence at <a href="../D/Dol_Guldur.md" title="Dol Guldur">Dol Guldur</a>. They are used as steeds by both the Nazgûl and a few <a href="../O/Olog-hai.md" title="Olog-hai">Olog-hai</a> warlords.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[note 1]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/1995.md" title="1995">1995</a>-<a href="../_/1998.md" title="1998">8</a>: <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game.md" title="Middle-earth Collectible Card Game">Middle-earth Collectible Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><b>Fell Beast</b> increases the number of strikes of one Nazgûl hazard-creature. <b>Wild Fell Beast</b> is a Drake which attacks with three strikes. With the card &#34;Fell Rider&#34; the Ringwraith may move to a non-Darkhaven site, but without allies and followers.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2001.md" title="2001">2001</a>-<a href="../_/2003.md" title="2003">3</a>: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series).md" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The fell beasts are depicted as more <a href="../D/Dragons.md" title="Dragons">Dragon</a>-like and serpentine. As opposed to having beaks, they have lipless mouths full of jagged teeth, fins running along their spine, long whip-like tails and dark gray scales. This depiction is heavily influenced by <a href="../J/John_Howe.md" title="John Howe">John Howe</a>, who was one of the concept artists for the films.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>In the films, the fell beasts are used for attack much more often than in the books, with the Nazgûl usually swooping down and screeching, making the defenders at the gate of Minas Tirith run away, leaving <a href="../G/Gandalf.md" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> alone to face the Witch-king as he enters.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Although on screen the films never make this mistake, sometimes cast or crew members (<a href="../L/Lawrence_Makoare.md" title="Lawrence Makoare">Lawrence Makoare</a> and <a href="../R/Richard_Taylor.md" title="Richard Taylor">Richard Taylor</a> most notably) on the commentary tracks and the documentaries refer to the fell beasts as Nazgûl; this is incorrect. The fell beasts are the creature that the nine Nazgûl ride, and the mistake probably arose because fell beasts are always seen with a Nazgûl atop them.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2001.md" title="2001">2001</a>-: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Strategy_Battle_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game">The Lord of the Rings Strategy Battle Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Fell Beasts are portrayed without beaks, reminiscent of the depiction in <i>The Lord of the Rings: The Motion Picture Trilogy</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[32]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2002.md" title="2002">2002</a>: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game).md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>A Nazgûl mounted on a fell beast (depicted as a dragon-like creature) appears in the <a href="../A/Amon_Hen.md" title="Amon Hen">Amon Hen</a> stage of the PC and console versions of the game, where it serves as the final boss. In the first phase of the battle, the beast crawls on the ground and fights Aragorn (armed with his sword). During the second and final phase, it flies into the sky and shoots green fireballs from its mouth down at Aragorn (who shoots back at it with his bow), until it is weakened and finally shot down by Legolas in the game&#39;s ending cutscene.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2002.md" title="2002">2002</a>-<a href="../_/2005.md" title="2005">5</a>: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Roleplaying_Game.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game">The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Hell-hawks, employed as mounts by the Ringwraiths and found wild in southern Mirkwood and (after the War of the Ring) in Gondor, resemble a &#34;cross between lizards and featherless birds&#34;. They were bred by Sauron in mockery of the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Eagles.md" title="Eagles">Great Eagles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2011.md" title="2011">2011</a>: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_War_in_the_North.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Agandaûr has several Fell beasts under his control. At the end of Chapter 1, Agandaûr flees on the back of one of them.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[34]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b><a href="../_/2023.md" title="2023">2023</a>: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_Return_to_Moria.md" title="The Lord of the Rings: Return to Moria">The Lord of the Rings: Return to Moria</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Fell beasts are among the monsters infesting Moria. The female dragon Narag-Shazon claims that Sauron forcibly employed her to breed the creatures.</dd></dl>
