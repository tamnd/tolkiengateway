**Finnish** is a Finnic language of the Uralic family of languages of northern Europe and one of the few non-Indo-European languages spoken in Europe. It is spoken primarily in Finland with many speakers in Sweden, Norway, and Russia. Other Finnic languages include Estonian, Veps, and Karelian.

The phonotactics of the Finnish language excited [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) which he likened to "bottles of an amazing wine of a kind and flavour never tasted before" that "quite intoxicated" him.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

It became was the inspiration for the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) language which Tolkien named [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) (later [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya)), a language designed to emulate the beauty evoked by Finnish. Other than in the field of phonotactics, a small part of the Quenya vocabulary was probably directly inspired by Finnish words, cf. Q. <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Laps%C3%AB&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lapsë (page does not exist)">lapsë</a></i> "babe",<sup id="cite_ref-ParfEdhellen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ParfEdhellen-2">[2]</a></sup> perhaps from F. <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/lapsi#Finnish" title="wiktionary:lapsi">lapsi</a></i> "child".

Tolkien used genuine Finnish for the names of [Paksu](/wiki/Paksu) and [Valkotukka](/wiki/Valkotukka), the two polar bears in *[The Father Christmas Letters](/wiki/The_Father_Christmas_Letters)*. <span></span>
