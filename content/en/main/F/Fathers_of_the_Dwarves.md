The **Seven Fathers of the Dwarves**, also called the **Seven Fathers**<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup> and the **Dwarf-fathers**<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>, were the first of their race.<sup id="cite_ref-Aule_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aule-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

The [Vala](../V/Valar.md) [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) created the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) because he was impatient for the arising of the [Children of Ilúvatar](../C/Children_of_Ilúvatar.md) ([Elves](../E/Elves.md) and [Men](../M/Men.md)) and he wished for children to love and instruct. It was the period known as the [Sleep of Yavanna](../S/Sleep_of_Yavanna.md) when [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) was dark and silent and was roamed by monsters from [Utumno](../U/Utumno.md). For this, Aulë made them strong and resistant and able to endure hardships; but as he had only a vague impression of the Children of Ilúvatar, his creations were structured differently, shorter and stunted.<sup id="cite_ref-Aule_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aule-3">[3]</a></sup>

He created seven [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md), and was teaching them the language he had devised for them ([Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md)), but Aulë was not Ilúvatar who had the [Flame Imperishable](../S/Secret_Fire.md) and his children were dumb, able to move and speak only if he wished so, and would remain motionless whenever Aule would think elsewhere.<sup id="cite_ref-Aule_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aule-3">[3]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aulë_the_Destroyer.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5d/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_the_Destroyer.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_the_Destroyer.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="461" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5d/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_the_Destroyer.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_the_Destroyer.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/5/5d/Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_the_Destroyer.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Aul%C3%AB_the_Destroyer.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1041" data-file-height="1600"/></a><figcaption><i>Aulë Prepares to Destroy His Children</i> by <a href="../T/Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

Ilúvatar confronted him for his impatience. Aulë offered his creations to Ilúvatar and was ready to break them in repentance. But Ilúvatar accepted his offer and gave them life of their own, and the Dwarves started cowering and pleading for mercy despite Aule's will.<sup id="cite_ref-Aule_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aule-3">[3]</a></sup>

However, the Seven Fathers of the Dwarves had to wait until the Elves first arrived, and Aulë laid them to rest in various places in Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-Aule_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aule-3">[3]</a></sup> The eldest of all, <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../D/Durin.md" title="Durin">Durin</a>, "lay alone" at [Mount Gundabad](../G/Gundabad.md) in the north of the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Dwarves_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dwarves-4">[4]</a></sup> He later founded the line of the **[Longbeards](../D/Durin's_Folk~15256.md)** (or *Sigin-tarâg* in Khuzdul), the Dwarves which were most friendly to the Elves and Men, mostly referred to as [Durin's Folk](../D/Durin's_Folk~15256.md). His city was [Khazad-dûm](../M/Moria.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

Two others were laid to rest in the [Ered Luin](../E/Ered_Luin.md) or *Blue Mountains*, and they founded the lines of the **[Broadbeams](../B/Broadbeams.md)** and the **[Firebeards](Firebeards.md)** who later lived in [Nogrod](../N/Nogrod.md) and [Belegost](../B/Belegost.md). The other four Dwarf-fathers were laid to rest in two pairs in [Rhûn](../R/Rhûn.md); lands at least as far east of Mount Gundabad as it lay east of the Blue Mountains. They founded the lines of the **[Ironfists](../I/Ironfists.md)**, **[Stiffbeards](../S/Stiffbeards.md)**, **[Blacklocks](../B/Blacklocks.md)**, and **[Stonefoots](../S/Stonefoots.md)**.<sup id="cite_ref-Dwarves_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dwarves-4">[4]</a></sup>

It was said that the Seven Fathers of the Dwarves would return to life after generations among their own folk.<sup id="cite_ref-Aule_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aule-3">[3]</a></sup> This did not mean reincarnation, however. Rather, the bodies of the Dwarf-fathers would be preserved, and their spirits would return to the bodies and live once more.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Durin was known to return to life <a class="mw-disambig" href="../D/Durin_(disambiguation).md" title="Durin (disambiguation)">six more times</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

Of the Seven Fathers of the Dwarves, only Durin is said to have "lain alone".<sup id="cite_ref-Durin_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Durin-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The reference to Durin being alone can be interpreted as referring to the fact that, in older versions of the story, Durin alone had no female companion (references are made by Tolkien to the "Thirteen Dwarves" created by Aulë, Durin and the six pairs<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>) but later it meant that Durin was indeed laid down to rest alone while the other Dwarf-fathers were laid to rest as three pairs. In that same conception, Tolkien considered that there were other Dwarves made that were put to sleep beside their Dwarf-fathers; Durin would be again an exception, as he created his Folk by gathering Dwarves from other clans during his wanders.<sup id="cite_ref-PM|XNotes_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM|XNotes-8">[8]</a></sup>

A last version suggested that the legend of the Making of the Dwarves should be altered (indeed very radically altered) to a form in which other Dwarves were laid to sleep near to the Dwarf-fathers, excluing probably Durin.<sup id="cite_ref-PM|XNotes_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM|XNotes-8">[8]</a></sup>

In *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)* there were only two groups of Dwarves, the Nauglath of [Nogrod](../N/Nogrod.md) under their king [Naugladur](../N/Naugladur.md) and the Indrafangs, the [Longbeards](../L/Longbeards.md) of [Belegost](../B/Belegost.md) under their king [Bodruith](../B/Bodruith.md).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> They live south of Artanor ([Doriath](../D/Doriath.md)).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

According to the *Tolkien Encyclopedia*, Aulë's creation of the Dwarves is [sub-creation](../S/Sub-creation.md) which aims to honor the wider Creation of God/Eru, a concept expressed in *[Mythopoeia](../M/Mythopoeia.md)*; "and may indicate anxieties about the independent value of art."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
