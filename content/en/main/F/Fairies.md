**Fairies** were creatures of [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) legend, only mentioned fleetingly.<sup id="cite_ref-H1_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H1-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In early writings of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md), *Fairies* was a name for the [Elves](../E/Elves.md#Fairies).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

### Other names {#Other_names}

Faërie, Faëry, Faery, Fairyland \[...\]

## Inspiration and etymology {#Inspiration_and_etymology}

[J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s concept of *Faërie* uses a deliberate variant spelling of *fairy*. While both spellings derive from [Middle English](../M/Middle_English.md) *faie* ("possessing magical powers"), the name *fairy* carries connotations of 'prettiness'. Tolkien wanted to distance himself from this modern sense, and by using *Faërie* (variants included *Faery* and *Fayery*) he sought "*connotations older and considerably darker*".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
