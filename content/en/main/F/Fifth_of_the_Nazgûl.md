The **Fifth of the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl)** was never differentiated in Tolkien's works, but several adaptations did attribute individual traits to this specific Nazgûl.

[Iron Crown Enterprises](/wiki/Iron_Crown_Enterprises) constructed an elaborate non-[canonical](/wiki/Canon "Canon") backstory for [Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing), identifying the character as *Akhôrahil*, a backstory which was also used in the [Middle-earth Collectible Card Game](/wiki/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game).<sup id="cite_ref-ICE_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICE-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

[Decipher](/wiki/Decipher), for their [Trading Card Game](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game "The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game"), instead limited this individualization to naming the character *[Úlairë](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl#Other_names "Nazgûl") Lemenya* ([Q.](/wiki/Quenya "Quenya") Fifth Nazgûl), and assigning the epithet of *Black Enemy*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

While some other adaptations also individualized the Nine, none are known to have assigned specific numbers.

## Backstory in Middle-earth Role Playing {#Backstory_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing}

**Akhôrahil** was born in [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1888&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1888 (page does not exist)">1888</a> to a wealthy family. He was a cousin of Prince Mûrazôr (the later [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king)), and was noted as brilliant but spoiled.

In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1904&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1904 (page does not exist)">1904</a>, his family moved to Middle-earth to found the kingdom of [Ciryatanor](/wiki/Ciryatanor), with his father, Ciryamir, as its client-king. Akhôrahil enjoyed the authority his family held, but in his greed he sought to usurp his father's throne. In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1918&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1918 (page does not exist)">1918</a>, he made a pact with a [Haradan](/wiki/Haradrim "Haradrim") priest, exchanging his eyes for two magical sapphire gems known as the *Eyes of the Well*. Though now physically blind, these gems granted him magical powers. Using his new abilities, he cast a spell on his father, who was overcome with despair and took his own life.

Akhôrahil ascended to the throne of Ciryatanor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_1919&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 1919 (page does not exist)">1919</a> and proclaimed himself the Storm King. He married his sister, Akhôraphil, and began a campaign of conquest, expanding his kingdom into the lands of the Haradrim and along the Bay of Ormal.

His campaigns and growing power caught the attention of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron). In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_2000&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 2000 (page does not exist)">2000</a>, an emissary offered Akhôrahil a [Ring of Power](/wiki/Nine_Rings "Nine Rings"), and thus he became the fifth King to fall under Sauron's sway.

Already eccentric before his transformation, the Storm King now became a recluse. His wife fled with his children, after which he purged his court of both those too loyal to the [King of Númenor](/wiki/King_of_N%C3%BAmenor) as well as those who acted too independently. Still, he maintained a nominal allegiance to Númenor and kept sending tributes for 250 years.

As [Tar-Ancalimon](/wiki/Tar-Ancalimon) ascended the throne, he request all colonies to reaffirm their loyalty. His envoy arrived in Ciryatanor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Second_Age_2250&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Second Age 2250 (page does not exist)">2250</a>. Realizing that Númenor would seek to expand again, he held the emissary as a hostage and officially allied himself with [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). Tar-Ancalimon, after ransoming his envoy, responded by launching an invasion, which led to a crushing defeat for the Storm King in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [2280](/wiki/Second_Age_2280 "Second Age 2280").

Having fled to Mordor, he would spend the next ten centuries directing the slaves of [Nurn](/wiki/Nurn) to supply food for Sauron's armies. He fled after Sauron's surrender to [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n), though after the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor) he returned to Nurn, later leading its host in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance). He was defeated with his master and passed into the shadows.

Akhôrahil reappeared around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050"), residing in the Far South and reestablishing his [host](/wiki/Army_of_the_Southern_Dragon "Army of the Southern Dragon") before being summoned back to Mordor by Sauron in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1640](/wiki/Third_Age_1640 "Third Age 1640"). He helped rebuild Sauron's strongholds and became a close companion and trusted lieutenant to the Witch-king, sharing a special rapport due to their similar backgrounds as powerful sorcerers. He stayed with the Witch-king at [Minas Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul) after the fortress's capture in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2002](/wiki/Third_Age_2002 "Third Age 2002").

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), Akhôrahil rode with the Black Riders in search of the One Ring. He fought in the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields), later joining the remaining wraiths at the [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon), where he perished with Sauron's defeat.<sup id="cite_ref-ICE_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICE-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Weaponry in Middle-earth Role Playing {#Weaponry_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing}

Beyond a [Morgul-knife](/wiki/Morgul-knife), the Ringwraith is noted to have wielded the **Yellow Hammer**, a gold steel mace inlaid with copper.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> <span></span>
