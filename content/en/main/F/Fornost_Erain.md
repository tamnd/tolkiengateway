**Fornost Erain** was the second capital of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and later the capital of [Arthedain](/wiki/Arthedain). It was located at the south end of the [North Downs](/wiki/North_Downs), about 100 miles north of [Bree](/wiki/Bree), at the northern end of the [North-South Road](/wiki/North-South_Road).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

It is possible that Fornost was founded around or after the establishment of the realm of Arnor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320"),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> because there is no record of the Númenóreans penetrating so far north before the Downfall of Númenor. After the fall of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) in the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance), around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1](/wiki/Third_Age_1 "Third Age 1"), the larger part of the Army of Arnor marched west on the North-South Road<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> through the [Fords of Isen](/wiki/Fords_of_Isen) and continued north to Fornost.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The number of [Dúnedain of Arnor](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain_of_Arnor) had decreased because of the War of the Last Alliance and the [Disaster of the Gladden Fields](/wiki/Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields) and the city of [Annúminas](/wiki/Ann%C3%BAminas) turned into ruins. As a consequence the heirs of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Valandil" title="Valandil">Valandil</a> moved the capital of Arnor to Fornost.<sup id="cite_ref-Council_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [861](/wiki/Third_Age_861 "Third Age 861"), when King [Eärendur](</wiki/E%C3%A4rendur_(King_of_Arnor)> "Eärendur (King of Arnor)") died, Arnor was divided<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> into the kingdoms of Arthedain, [Cardolan](/wiki/Cardolan) and [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur), because of dissensions among his sons.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> As a consequence, Fornost became the capital of the new realm of Arthedain.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1409](/wiki/Third_Age_1409 "Third Age 1409"), when the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) took the tower of [Amon Sûl](/wiki/Weathertop "Weathertop"), the *[palantír](/wiki/Amon_S%C3%BBl-stone "Amon Sûl-stone")* that was located in the tower was saved and carried back to Fornost during the retreat. The new young King [Araphor](/wiki/Araphor) repelled the enemy from Fornost and the North Downs.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

### Fall {#Fall}

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1974](/wiki/Third_Age_1974 "Third Age 1974"),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> the forces of the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) invaded Arthedain before the end of the winter and [captured](/wiki/Fall_of_Fornost "Fall of Fornost") Fornost<sup id="cite_ref-Arvedui_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arvedui-11">[11]</a></sup>. King [Arvedui](/wiki/Arvedui) first resisted on the North Downs until the last, then fled with some of his guard to [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") mines at the northern end of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) and drowned in the [Icebay of Forochel](/wiki/Icebay_of_Forochel) with the two [Palantíri](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADri) he had saved from the forces of Angmar in March of the following<sup id="cite_ref-TA1975_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1975-12">[12]</a></sup> year<sup id="cite_ref-Arvedui_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arvedui-11">[11]</a></sup>. Meanwhile, Fornost became the dwelling of the Witch-king and his evil folk, who ursurped the house of the old kings.<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-13">[13]</a></sup>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1975](/wiki/Third_Age_1975 "Third Age 1975"),<sup id="cite_ref-TA1975_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1975-12">[12]</a></sup> a fleet of ships from [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), led by prince [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur) of Gondor, landed at [Mithlond](/wiki/Grey_Havens "Grey Havens")<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-13">[13]</a></sup>. The [Elves of Lindon](/wiki/Elves_of_Lindon) led by [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan), the remaining [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain) of the North<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-13">[13]</a></sup>, and some [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) from the [Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")<sup id="cite_ref-Arvedui_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arvedui-11">[11]</a></sup> joined the [Host of the West](</wiki/Host_of_the_West_(Arnor)> "Host of the West (Arnor)")<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-13">[13]</a></sup>. Eärnur fought the Witch-king of Angmar in the plains west of Fornost and [defeated](/wiki/Battle_of_Fornost "Battle of Fornost") the armies of Angmar, but the Witch-king himself escaped.<sup id="cite_ref-Eärnil_13-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eärnil-13">[13]</a></sup> After the death of King Arvedui and the defeat of the army of Angmar, the Kingdom of Arthedain ended, because few Dúnedain of the North were left and the population of all the other peoples of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) had decreased. The Dúnedain of the North turned into a secret and wandering people that passed into the shadows.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> As a consequence, it is possible that Fornost was abandoned after the defeat of the army of Angmar.

### Legacy {#Legacy}

After Fornost Erain was abandoned, [Men](/wiki/Men) referred to it as **Deadmen's Dike** and feared to go there.<sup id="cite_ref-Council_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-5">[5]</a></sup> According to [Barliman Butterbur](/wiki/Barliman_Butterbur), the innkeeper of [The Prancing Pony](/wiki/The_Prancing_Pony), Fornost was said to be a haunted land. However, the [Rangers](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North "Rangers of the North") went there,<sup id="cite_ref-Homeward_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Homeward-15">[15]</a></sup> but it is not known what they did there and how long they stayed there.

It is possible that Fornost was rebuilt and resettled after [King Elessar](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") [reunited Gondor and Arnor](/wiki/Reunited_Kingdom "Reunited Kingdom"), as Gandalf had announced that King Elessar would go there again one day and fair folk would be riding through [Bree](/wiki/Bree) and that many folk used to live at the end of the Greenway on the North Downs.<sup id="cite_ref-Homeward_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Homeward-15">[15]</a></sup>

*Fornost Erain* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "Northern Fortress (of the) Kings", being a compound of *forn* ("north") + *[ost](/wiki/Ost "Ost")* ("fortress") and *erain*, the plural of *[aran](/wiki/Aran "Aran")* ("king").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

The [Westron](/wiki/Westron) names of *Fornost Erain* and *Fornost* were "Norbury of the Kings"<sup id="cite_ref-Homeward_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Homeward-15">[15]</a></sup> and "Norbury",<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> respectively. Norbury is a modernised form of [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *north-burg*, which means "north (fortified) town".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In an earlier manuscript version of *The Tale of Years of the Second Age* it is mentioned that the shards of Narsil were brought to Valandil in Imladris, that he became King of Arnor and lived in Fornost.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

In an earlier version of what would later become [Appendix A](/wiki/Appendix_A) it is mentioned that Annúminas became deserted due to the decrease of the people and that the city of the realm of Arthedain of the oldest son of Earendur was Fornost.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup> An even earlier version contained a note that Valandil removed to Fornost and that Annuminas was deserted, but this was later struck out and Annuminas became deserted was added to the note for king Earendil whose name was later changed to Earendur.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

In an earlier manuscript version of *The Tale of Years of the Third Age* it is mentioned that Valandil became King of Arnor and lived in Annuminas in T.A. 10, that Amlaith of Fornost ruled the realm of Arthedain, Annuminas is deserted after the death of Earendur in T.A. 861 and the names of the kings at Fornost were no longer given in High-elven form.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>

In an earlier version of the Prologue of *The Lord of the Rings*<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> and in an earlier manuscript version of *The Tale of Years of the Third Age*<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> the Westron name of Fornost was *Northworthy*.

Earlier names of Fornost are *Osforod, the Northburg*,<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup> or *Fornobel, the North Burg*.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup> On the [The First Map of The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings) (and on the subsequent [1943 map](/wiki/The_1943_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The 1943 Map of The Lord of the Rings")), Fornost is called *Fornobel*.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>

<b>1997: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a> — <a href="/wiki/Arnor:_The_Land" title="Arnor: The Land">Arnor: The Land</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Fornost Erain was the heavily fortified capital of <a href="/wiki/Arthedain" title="Arthedain">Arthedain</a> during its final centuries, its militaristic citadel a stark contrast to the open scholarly city of Annúminas. It housed the <i>Dagorwaith Aran</i> (S. “Royal Army”) and the <i>Ostirith</i> (S. “Citadel Guard”). <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a>, <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Elladan" title="Elladan">Elladan</a>, and <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Elrohir" title="Elrohir">Elrohir</a> were known to regularly visit the city. The citadel was built on limestone bedrock, the cracks of which were used to drain sewage, though its deep caves led into the <a href="/wiki/Under-deeps" title="Under-deeps">Under-deeps</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2006: <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II:_The_Rise_of_the_Witch-king" title="The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king">The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the expansion pack, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II:_The_Rise_of_the_Witch-king" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II: The Rise of the Witch-king">The Rise of the Witch-king</a></i>, Fornost is besieged by the forces of the Witch-king.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Fornost Erain, simply called Fornost, was located in the northern end of the <a href="/wiki/North_Downs" title="North Downs">North Downs</a>. Its ruins were overrun with wights, orcs and wargs. The fall of the city was possibly hastened by traitors who were bewitched by <a href="/wiki/Amarthiel" title="Amarthiel">Amarthiel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2011: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_War_in_the_North" title="The Lord of the Rings: War in the North">The Lord of the Rings: War in the North</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The first chapter of the game is centered around Fornost. Agandaûr has assembled an army of <a href="/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains" title="Orcs of the Misty Mountains">Orcs of the Misty Mountains</a> in Fornost, and prepared to attack the <a href="/wiki/Free_peoples" title="Free peoples">Free peoples</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Eradan_(video_game_character)" title="Eradan (video game character)">Eradan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Andriel" title="Andriel">Andriel</a> and <a href="/wiki/Farin_(video_game_character)" title="Farin (video game character)">Farin</a> infiltrate Fornost on <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a>&#39;s orders. They free the captured the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Eagles" title="Eagles">Eagle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Beleram" title="Beleram">Beleram</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> and work together with <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Elladan" title="Elladan">Elladan</a> and <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Elrohir" title="Elrohir">Elrohir</a> to stop Agandaûr.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> Agandaûr flees and <a href="/wiki/Tharzog" title="Tharzog">Tharzog</a> - leader of the <a href="/wiki/Orcs" title="Orcs">Orcs</a> - is killed, after which the Orcs remain leaderless and inter-factional fighting starts, hereby preventing an attack.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup></dd>
<dd>Visitable locations are the Main Gate, the Battlements, the Inner and Outer wards and the Citadel.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup></dd></dl>
