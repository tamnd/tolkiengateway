**Foxes** were [dog](/wiki/Dogs "Dogs")-like carnivores of woodland and farmland, distinctive for their red-orange coats and their eerie plaintive cries.

In the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age), in [Númenor](/wiki/Elenna "Elenna"), there were foxes in a large number. Their main food were [lopoldi](/wiki/Lopoldi "Lopoldi"), helping the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) to keep them in order. As they were seldom hunted or because they had enough food with the lopoldi, they never attacked the fowls of the Númenóreans.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), a fox became puzzled at finding [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins "Frodo Baggins"), [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee"), and [Pippin](/wiki/Peregrin_Took "Peregrin Took") asleep in a [fir-wood](/wiki/Firs "Firs") on the first night of their journey from [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton), thinking that there was *“something mighty queer behind this”*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Another fox was encountered by [Strider](/wiki/Aragorn "Aragorn") and the hobbits as they hiked through the [Chetwood](/wiki/Chetwood) after leaving [Bree](/wiki/Bree).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) word for "fox" is <i><b>rusco</b></i>, pl. *rusqui*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> The [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word was <i><b>rusc</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>The Red Foxes of <a href="/wiki/Calenardhon" title="Calenardhon">Calenardhon</a> are mentioned as a species with truly red fur, which made them a target for fur hunters. They were smarter than other fox species, rarely stumbling into death and cunningly avoiding traps and <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a>. The Red Foxes had colour vision and were canny thieves, stealing hens, <a href="/wiki/Rabbits" title="Rabbits">rabbits</a> and small, brightly coloured objects.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup></dd></dl>
