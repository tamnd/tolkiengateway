**Lungorthin** was a [Lord of Balrogs](Lord_of_Balrogs.md) according to an early version of the [legendarium](Legendarium.md).

## History {#History}

Lungorthin is mentioned only once, in *[The Lay of the Children of Húrin](../T/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_Húrin.md)*; after [Húrin](../H/Húrin.md) was captured by [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md), Lungorthin smote him on the mouth.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

According to [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), it is likely that Lungorthin is not another name for <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../G/Gothmog.md" title="Gothmog">Gothmog</a>, as the name Gothmog was mentioned in the earliest Middle-earth writings, as well as the final version of Tolkien's mythology.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The <i>thane of Morgoth</i> who smote him on the mouth (version I, 59) now becomes <i>Lungorthin</i>, <i>Lord of Balrogs</i> (96)——which is probably to be interpreted as ‘a Balrog lord’, since Gothmog, reappears in the ‘Silmarillion’ tradition.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="../T/The_Lays_of_Beleriand.md" title="The Lays of Beleriand">The Lays of Beleriand</a></i>, <i><a href="../T/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_Húrin.md" title="The Lay of the Children of Húrin">The Lay of the Children of Húrin</a></i>, <a href="../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien’s</a> <i>Commentary on Part I of the second version</i></cite></p>
</blockquote>

The name <i><b>Lungorthin</b></i> is in [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md), according to *[Eldamo](../E/Eldamo.md)*, but its meaning is unclear. <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=LUNG&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="LUNG (page does not exist)">LUNG</a> in [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) means “Heavy.” <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=GAR&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="GAR (page does not exist)">GAR</a> means “fenced.” [or](../O/Or.md) means “above.”<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In Tolkien's first version of *[The Lay of the Children of Húrin](../T/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_Húrin.md)*, <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=T%C3%BArin_son_of_H%C3%BArin_%26_Glorund_the_Dragon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Túrin son of Húrin &amp; Glorund the Dragon (page does not exist)">Túrin son of Húrin &amp; Glorund the Dragon</a></i>, "Lungorthin, Lord of Balrogs" had no name and race, being referred to instead as an unknown *thane of Morgoth* who smote Húrin on the mouth.

There are no references to Lungorthin in [J.R.R. Tolkien's](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) later [legendarium](Legendarium.md) and as such, Lungorthin may have been abandoned as he never appeared within any of Tolkien's writing after *The Lay of the Children of Húrin*.
