On [22 November](/wiki/22_November) [1956](/wiki/1956), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote **[a letter](/wiki/Letters_not_published_in_The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien "Letters not published in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien") to Miss Stanley-Smith**, librarian at <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deddington" title="wikipedia:Deddington">Deddington</a> Library.<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

- **Subject:** Tolkien accepts an invitation to give a speech at the opening of the new library (on [14 December](/wiki/14_December)).<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Transcription {#Transcription}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Dear Miss Stanley-Smith,<br/><br/>	Fortunately I think I can manage Friday 14 <s>Nove</s> December. I say ‘fortunately’, because though I dislike talking (in this sense), lecturing, or addressing a gathering, I should have been sorry to refuse your invitation: you sugar the central pill so attractively. I should be delighted to visit Deddington and have lunch with you; and I fear that the fee will be more than ample for the performance. Short? Ten minutes? A quarter of an hour?<br/><br/>I will consult you later, if I may, on the sort of thing likely to be most appropriate.<br/><br/>As for my own writings: beside the two you name there is Farmer Giles of Ham (Allen &amp; Unwin), specially appropriate, since it is localized in Oxfordshire. Other poems and stories are, I fear, either scattered in periodicals or o.p.2 and await collection. Sir Gawain and the Green Knight (Clarendon Press), since it is an edition of a mediaeval poem, would, I imagine, be regarded as too technical, specially as I have not yet published the translation which has twice been broadcast.<br/><br/>It would be most kind if you could send a car, as I have none of my own now and have in consequence seldom visited Deddington, in post-war years, though I used often to do so.<br/><br/>Yours sincerely,<br/><br/>J.R.R. Tolkien<br/><br/>P.S.   I wonder if Mrs. Lionel Hitchens is the mother of Miss Phoebe Hitchens, whom I have met, since my daughter knew her. She was, I think, the cousin (?) of the woman novelist of the same name - (Noughts and Crosses …etc.).<br/>JRR T.
</p>
</blockquote>
