**Lewis Carroll** (pen name used by **Charles Lutwidge Dodgson**; 27 January 1832 – 14 January 1898) was an English author and holder of a Mathematical Lectureship at Christ Church College, [University of Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford).

## Tolkien on Carroll {#Tolkien_on_Carroll}

### *The Hobbit* jacket remarks {#The_Hobbit_jacket_remarks}

Carroll published two books now deemed as fantasy classics, *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865) and *Through the Looking-Glass* (1871). Sharing tenure at Oxford and authorship of fantasy, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) was hastily compared with Carroll by some early reviewers of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> The reviewers of *The Hobbit* comparing it to the *Alice* stories, appear to have "relied overmuch on the publisher's blurb",<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> which stated that:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395753">.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider{margin-top:1rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem;color:var(--tg-color-subtle);display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:space-between}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider:not(:empty){gap:1rem;white-space:nowrap}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::before,.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::after{content:"";height:1px;width:100%;background-color:var(--tg-border-color-base)}</style><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The birth of <i>The Hobbit</i> recalls very strongly that of <i>Alice in Wonderland</i>. Here again a professor of an abstruse subject is at play.<sup id="cite_ref-L15_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L15-3">[3]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

Tolkien offered comments about the "abstruse subject" and the comparison with *Alice in Wonderland* in his [Letter 15](/wiki/Letter_15). Concerning the latter point, Tolkien wrote that *The Hobbit* "is more comparable to Dodgson's amateur photography, and his song of Hiawatha's failure<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 1]</a></sup> than to *Alice*". He did, however, concede that "the compliment to *The Hobbit* is rather high" and, if the comparison is to be maintained, that Carroll's *Through the Looking-Glass* "ought to be mentioned" as it "is much closer in every way".<sup id="cite_ref-L15_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L15-3">[3]</a></sup>

### On Fairy-Stories {#On_Fairy-Stories}

At the beginning of his essay *[On Fairy-Stories](/wiki/On_Fairy-Stories)*, Tolkien separates the "genuine fairy-story" from other forms of fantastical tales. Literary genres sometimes (but erroneously or carelessly, according to Tolkien) defined as fairy-stories include travellers' tales (e.g., <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Swift" title="wikipedia:Jonathan Swift">Jonathan Swift</a>'s <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Voyage_to_Lilliput" title="wikipedia:A Voyage to Lilliput">A Voyage to Lilliput</a></i>), <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beast_fable" title="wikipedia:Beast fable">beast-fables</a>, and dream-stories. Pertaining to the latter, Tolkien names Carroll's *Alice* stories, which he describes as "successful and amusing". However, the *Alice* stories are only an illusion found within the frame of a dream, and do thus not touch on the notion of [Faërie](/wiki/Faerie#Inspiration "Faerie") — the primary and defining characteristic of the fairy-story.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

### Other mentions {#Other_mentions}

Working for the *[Oxford English Dictionary](/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary)*, Tolkien suggested "*for future addition ... at least a quotation from* <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvie_and_Bruno" title="wikipedia:Sylvie and Bruno">Sylvie and Bruno</a> *by Lewis Carroll, illustrating the word smirkle*".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

[Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) and [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) have noted that Tolkien:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...was amused by Lewis Carroll&#39;s <i>Alice&#39;s Adventures in Wonderland</i> (1865) and <i>Through the Looking Glass</i> (1872); later in his life he was also fond of Carroll&#39;s <i>Sylvie and Bruno</i> (1889) and <i>Sylvie and Bruno Concluded</i> (1893), and occasionally recited verses from them.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Tolkien's friend [Roger Lancelyn Green](/wiki/Roger_Lancelyn_Green) was founder of the Lewis Carroll Society, and edited and wrote an introduction for *The Works of Lewis Carroll* (1965).

[John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) has suggested that Tolkien's creation of the word [*ishness*](/wiki/The_Book_of_Ishness "The Book of Ishness") might have been inspired by a passage in chapter 7 of *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*: "did you ever see a thing as a drawing of a muchness!".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>

Tolkien once wrote a translation <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabberwocky" title="wikipedia:Jabberwocky">Jabberwocky</a> into [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) which he had entitled as *[Bealuwérig](/wiki/Bealuw%C3%A9rig)*.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-10">[9]</a></sup>
