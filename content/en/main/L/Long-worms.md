**Long-worms** were either a type of [dragon](/wiki/Dragon "Dragon") in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth)<sup id="cite_ref-EoA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EoA-1">[1]</a></sup> or another name that [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) used for at least some of them.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-2">[2]</a></sup>

The only long-worm mentioned by name was [Scatha](/wiki/Scatha), the great dragon of [Ered Mithrin](/wiki/Ered_Mithrin). He<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-3">[3]</a></sup> was slain by [Fram](/wiki/Fram) of the [Éothéod](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od) in the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). For a long time afterwards, the long-worms did not pose any threat to the land.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-2">[2]</a></sup>

It is possible that the term referred to wingless dragons since Scatha was a wingless [cold-drake](/wiki/Cold-drakes "Cold-drakes").<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-3">[3]</a></sup> It is unknown whether Scatha was the last of the long-worms or if some still existed.

Tolkien regularly used "[worm](/wiki/Worms "Worms")" as a nickname for dragons - examples include [Glaurung](/wiki/Glaurung),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Scatha,<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-2">[2]</a></sup> and [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> - but it is uncertain whether this refers to the long-worms as well.
