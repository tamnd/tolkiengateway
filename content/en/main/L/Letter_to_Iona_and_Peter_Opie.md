On [25 November](/wiki/25_November) [1951](/wiki/1951), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote <b><a href="/wiki/Letters_not_published_in_The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien" title="Letters not published in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien">a letter</a> to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iona_and_Peter_Opie" title="wikipedia:Iona and Peter Opie">Iona and Peter Opie</a></b>.

- **Publication:** Excerpts from the letter were published in *[The Lore and Language of Schoolchildren](/wiki/The_Lore_and_Language_of_Schoolchildren)*. A small excerpt was published in *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Companion and Guide](/wiki/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Companion_and_Guide): Chronology*, p. 379.
- **Background and contents:** On the occasion of several letters, concerned with the word 'faynights',<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> having been published in *Sunday Times*, Iona and Peter Opie sent a letter to the newspaper, explaining the use of the word (published on 25 November 1951). Tolkien read their letter, and wrote a long letter in reply, discussing the etymology of 'faynights'. He wrote that he read their letter "*today, just as I had been puzzling over a curious <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="wikipedia:Geoffrey Chaucer">Chaucer</a> usage*" apparently connected with the issue.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[1]</a></sup> In their work *[The Lore and Language of Schoolchildren](/wiki/The_Lore_and_Language_of_Schoolchildren)*, Iona and Peter Opie quoted from Tolkien's letter, that states, amongst other things, that the terms 'fains I', 'fains' and 'fainites' are survivals of [medieval English](/wiki/Middle_English "Middle English") from the 14th Century.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>
- **Archive**: Held at the archive of Tolkien Papers, [Bodleian Library](/wiki/Bodleian_Library).
