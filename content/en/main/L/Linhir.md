**Linhir** was a haven with a ferrybridge over the river [Gilrain](../G/Gilrain.md).<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup> It was located just downstream from the juncture of the river [Gilrain](../G/Gilrain.md) and the river [Serni](../S/Serni.md) on the western side of the river in [Dor-en-Ernil](../D/Dor-en-Ernil.md) in southern [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md). Through it passed the road from [Erech](../E/Erech.md) to [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

On [11 March](../_/11_March.md) [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) arrived with the [Grey Company](../G/Grey_Company.md) and the [Army of the Dead](../O/Oathbreakers.md) at Linhir where men of [Lamedon](Lamedon.md) were defending the fords over the river Gilrain against men from [Umbar](../U/Umbar.md) and from [Harad](../H/Harad.md), which had sailed up the river. Except the stout-hearted [Angbor](../A/Angbor.md), the Lord of Lamedon, both the defenders and the attacking forces fled in terror from the Army of the Dead. Aragorn asked Angbor to gather his troops and to help him to pursue the men from Umbar and from Harad over the river Gilrain to [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md) where the main fleet from Umbar was anchored.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Linhir* is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name,<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-5">[5]</a></sup> which means "fair stream". It is properly a river name for the joint course of the river Gilrain and the river Serni from their junction to the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-UI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UI-1">[1]</a></sup> Paul Strack suggests that it is a compound of *lind* ("fair") and the [lenited](Lenition.md) form of *[sîr](../S/Sîr.md)* ("river").<sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2014: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>As in the book, Linhir was a city in the region of Central <a href="../G/Gondor.md" title="Gondor">Gondor</a>. It straddled the rivers <a href="../S/Serni.md" title="Serni">Serni</a> and <a href="../G/Gilrain.md" title="Gilrain">Gilrain</a> just past their confluence and therefore was built in both <a href="../D/Dor-en-Ernil.md" title="Dor-en-Ernil">Dor-en-Ernil</a> and Lower <a href="Lebennin.md" title="Lebennin">Lebennin</a>, but was mainly considered part of the former. Three brothers, &#34;Erchar&#34;, &#34;Garvir&#34;, and &#34;Herion&#34;, sons of the late &#34;Comeldir&#34;, commanded the soldiers of Linhir following <a href="../A/Aragorn.md" title="Aragorn">Aragorn</a>&#39;s passage. The brothers were commanded to defend the city, but Erchar and Herion instead attacked the enemy forces in Dor-en-Ernil, resulting in the deaths of many of their men. The brothers were later convinced to return, and when both sides recovered from the terror of the <a href="../O/Oathbreakers.md" title="Oathbreakers">Oathbreakers</a> the remaining <a href="../C/Corsairs_of_Umbar.md" title="Corsairs of Umbar">Corsairs</a> and <a href="../H/Haradrim.md" title="Haradrim">Haradrim</a> launched a second attack. The Gondorians were again victorious but suffered heavy losses.</dd></dl>
