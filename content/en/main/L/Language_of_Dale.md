The **language of Dale**<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-1">[1]</a></sup> was the tongue spoken by the [Men of](../M/Men_of_Dale.md) [Dale](../D/Dale.md) and as a [Northern language](../N/Northern_Mannish.md), it was related to [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md) and, more distantly, to [Westron](../W/Westron.md).

## History {#History}

Most of the people of [Dale](../D/Dale.md) like most of the ancestors of the people of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md) were descended from the [Edain](../E/Edain.md) of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) or from a people closely related with them. As a consequence, their language was related to the [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md) of the Edain who had migrated to [Númenor](../N/Númenor.md) at the beginning of the [Second Age](../S/Second_Age.md) and had preserved a likeness to the [Common Speech](../C/Common_Speech.md) who was descended from Adûnaic.<sup id="cite_ref-Men_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-2">[2]</a></sup>

Many of the high-sounding names of old [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) families, such as the [Tooks](../T/Tooks.md) and the [Bolgers](../B/Bolgers.md) closely resembled the names of [Men](../M/Men_of_the_Vales_of_Anduin.md) in the [Vale of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md) where the [ancestors](../_/Éothéod.md) of the Men of Rohan had lived, in Rohan or in Dale, because most of these seem to have been taken from legends of the past of Men and of Hobbits.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup>

The language of Dale was adopted by the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) from that region, such as the [Dwarves of Erebor](../D/Dwarves_of_Erebor.md). In fact they used names from the language of Dale as their "outer" names,<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-1">[1]</a></sup> because it was a taboo to use their true, original names in their own language [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md) when talking with persons from other [races](../R/Races.md).<sup id="cite_ref-L144_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-4">[3]</a></sup>

The writing system used by the Men of Dale was an old and simple form of the [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

In [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s stories names of Dwarves in the language of Dale were represented by names in [Old Norse](../N/North_Germanic_languages.md),<sup id="cite_ref-L144_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-4">[3]</a></sup> a language related to [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) and modern English, because the language of Dale was related to [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md) and [Westron](../W/Westron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Men_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-2">[2]</a></sup> If the language of Dale and the [Long Lake](Long_Lake.md) would have appeared in *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, it would have been represented as "more or less Scandinavian in character".<sup id="cite_ref-L144_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-4">[3]</a></sup> In a note on a page dated 9 February 1942 about the fictitious translation of the languages of the Shire, Dale, and Rohan, he specified that the language of Dale<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 2]</a></sup> was represented by Norse and that it was used by the Dwarves of the region of Dale.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup>

## Name {#Name}

A suggested name for the language of Dale is **Dalian**.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup> Another "invented" name commonly used by [fans](../F/Fandom.md) is **Dalish**.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become Appendix F states that the name Stoor seemed to have originally meant "big" and that this word was common in "the language of Dale".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup> This version also mentions that Scandinavian stor- means "big".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[11]</a></sup>

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become the chapter "[A Knife in the Dark](../A/A_Knife_in_the_Dark.md)" mentions that *Cram* was a word in the language of the men of Dale and the Long Lake.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup> In the final published version of the chapter "[Farewell to Lórien](../F/Farewell_to_Lórien.md)", [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md) only says after trying *[lembas](Lembas.md)* "I thought it was only a kind of *cram*, such as the Dale-men make for journeys in the wild".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>
