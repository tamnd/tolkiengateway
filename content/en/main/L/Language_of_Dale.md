The **language of Dale**<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-1">[1]</a></sup> was the tongue spoken by the [Men of](/wiki/Men_of_Dale "Men of Dale") [Dale](/wiki/Dale) and as a [Northern language](/wiki/Northern_Mannish "Northern Mannish"), it was related to [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) and, more distantly, to [Westron](/wiki/Westron).

## History {#History}

Most of the people of [Dale](/wiki/Dale) like most of the ancestors of the people of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) were descended from the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) or from a people closely related with them. As a consequence, their language was related to the [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic) of the Edain who had migrated to [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) at the beginning of the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) and had preserved a likeness to the [Common Speech](/wiki/Common_Speech) who was descended from Adûnaic.<sup id="cite_ref-Men_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-2">[2]</a></sup>

Many of the high-sounding names of old [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") families, such as the [Tooks](/wiki/Tooks) and the [Bolgers](/wiki/Bolgers) closely resembled the names of [Men](/wiki/Men_of_the_Vales_of_Anduin "Men of the Vales of Anduin") in the [Vale of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin "Vales of Anduin") where the [ancestors](/wiki/%C3%89oth%C3%A9od "Éothéod") of the Men of Rohan had lived, in Rohan or in Dale, because most of these seem to have been taken from legends of the past of Men and of Hobbits.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup>

The language of Dale was adopted by the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) from that region, such as the [Dwarves of Erebor](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Erebor). In fact they used names from the language of Dale as their "outer" names,<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-1">[1]</a></sup> because it was a taboo to use their true, original names in their own language [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) when talking with persons from other [races](/wiki/Races "Races").<sup id="cite_ref-L144_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-4">[3]</a></sup>

The writing system used by the Men of Dale was an old and simple form of the [Cirth](/wiki/Cirth).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

## Translation {#Translation}

In [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien")'s stories names of Dwarves in the language of Dale were represented by names in [Old Norse](/wiki/North_Germanic_languages "North Germanic languages"),<sup id="cite_ref-L144_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-4">[3]</a></sup> a language related to [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) and modern English, because the language of Dale was related to [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) and [Westron](/wiki/Westron).<sup id="cite_ref-Men_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Men-2">[2]</a></sup> If the language of Dale and the [Long Lake](/wiki/Long_Lake) would have appeared in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, it would have been represented as "more or less Scandinavian in character".<sup id="cite_ref-L144_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L144-4">[3]</a></sup> In a note on a page dated 9 February 1942 about the fictitious translation of the languages of the Shire, Dale, and Rohan, he specified that the language of Dale<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 2]</a></sup> was represented by Norse and that it was used by the Dwarves of the region of Dale.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup>

## Name {#Name}

A suggested name for the language of Dale is **Dalian**.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup> Another "invented" name commonly used by [fans](/wiki/Fandom "Fandom") is **Dalish**.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[9]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become Appendix F states that the name Stoor seemed to have originally meant "big" and that this word was common in "the language of Dale".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[10]</a></sup> This version also mentions that Scandinavian stor- means "big".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[11]</a></sup>

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become the chapter "[A Knife in the Dark](/wiki/A_Knife_in_the_Dark)" mentions that *Cram* was a word in the language of the men of Dale and the Long Lake.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup> In the final published version of the chapter "[Farewell to Lórien](/wiki/Farewell_to_L%C3%B3rien)", [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) only says after trying *[lembas](/wiki/Lembas "Lembas")* "I thought it was only a kind of *cram*, such as the Dale-men make for journeys in the wild".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>
