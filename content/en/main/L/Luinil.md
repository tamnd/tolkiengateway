**Luinil** was one of the bright stars placed in the sky by [Varda](/wiki/Varda) at the time of the awakening of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Its identity is uncertain, though the fact that its name contains the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) word *[luin](/wiki/Luin "Luin")*, meaning 'blue', does offer a clue.

The only remote explanation offered by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") was a rough and uncertain note that seems to link Luinil to the planet we call Neptune. As [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes, though, this dark and distant object, too faint to be seen by the naked eye, would hardly qualify as a 'bright star'.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Another alternative would be blue <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigel" title="wikipedia:Rigel">Rigel</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_(constellation)" title="wikipedia:Orion (constellation)">Orion</a>, making Luinil the mate of red [Borgil](/wiki/Borgil). Two other possibilities - based purely on the fact that they are both bright blue stars - would be <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spica" title="wikipedia:Spica">Spica</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgo_(constellation)" title="wikipedia:Virgo (constellation)">Virgo</a> or <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulus" title="wikipedia:Regulus">Regulus</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_(constellation)" title="wikipedia:Leo (constellation)">Leo</a>.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Luinil* is probably [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) for "Blue Star", as Paul Strack explains, being a compound of *[luinë](/wiki/Luin%C3%AB "Luinë")* ("blue") + *[el](/wiki/El "El")* ("star").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> <span></span>
