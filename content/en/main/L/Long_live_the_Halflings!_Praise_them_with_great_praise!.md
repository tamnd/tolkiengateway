"**Long live the Halflings! Praise them with great praise!**" is the first line of a [chorus of praises and thanks](/wiki/Poems_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "Poems in The Lord of the Rings") sung in [Westron](/wiki/Westron), [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), and [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) by the [Men](/wiki/Gondorians "Gondorians") of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) on the [Field of Cormallen](/wiki/Field_of_Cormallen), honoring the [Ring-bearers](/wiki/Ring-bearer "Ring-bearer") for their valiant efforts in the destruction of the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-song_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-song-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Poem {#Poem}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Long live the Halflings! Praise them with great praise!<br/>
Cuio i Pheriain anann! Aglar&#39;ni Pheriannath!<br/>
Praise them with great praise, Frodo and Samwise!<br/>
Daur a Berhael, Conin en Annûn! Eglerio!<br/>
Praise them!<br/>
Eglerio!<br/>
A laita te, laita te! Andave laituvalmet!<br/>
Praise them!<br/>
Cormacolindor, a laita tárienna!<br/>
Praise them! The Ring-bearers, praise them with great praise!
</p>
</div>

## Background {#Background}

In an early draft of the praise, [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote the following:<sup id="cite_ref-early_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-early-2">[2]</a></sup>

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Long live the Halflings! Praise them with great praise! Cuio i Pheriannath anann! Aglar anann! Praise them with great praise! Wilcuman, wilcutnan, Fróda and Samwís! Praise them! Uton herian holbytlan! A laita te, laita te. Andave laituvalmetl Praise them! The Ringbearers, praise them with great praise!<sup id="cite_ref-early_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-early-2">[2]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

It was noted by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) that this version included some [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) words in addition to Westron, Quenya, and Sindarin.<sup id="cite_ref-early_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-early-2">[2]</a></sup>

In a letter to [Rhona Beare](/wiki/Rhona_Beare) on [8 June](/wiki/8_June) in [1961](/wiki/1961), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) explained that lines 2, 4, and 6 were in Sindarin while lines 7 and 9 were in Quenya. Tolkien's translations of the [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) parts are listed below:<sup id="cite_ref-letter_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-3">[3]</a></sup>

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Line 2: &#34;May the Halflings live long, glory to the Halflings&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-letter_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-3">[3]</a></sup><br/>
Line 4: &#34;Frodo and Sam, princes of the west, glorify (them)&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-letter_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-3">[3]</a></sup><br/>
Line 6: &#34;glorify (them)&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-letter_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-3">[3]</a></sup><br/>
Line 7: &#34;Bless them, bless them, long will we praise them&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-letter_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-3">[3]</a></sup><br/>
Line 9: &#34;The Ring bearers, bless (or praise) them to the height&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-letter_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-3">[3]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

According to Tolkien himself, this song marks the point of [eucatastrophe](/wiki/Eucatastrophe "Eucatastrophe") in *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, bringing tears to his eyes as he wrote it.<sup id="cite_ref-miltonwaldman_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miltonwaldman-4">[4]</a></sup>
