During [1955](/wiki/1955), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) drafted **[a letter](/wiki/Letters_not_published_in_The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien "Letters not published in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien") to David Masson** (librarian at the University of Leeds).<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup>

- **Subject:** [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) historical philology.<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **Description:** 8-page draft letter.
- **Publication:** Extensive quotations from the letter appeared in [*Parma Eldalamberon*, volume 17](/wiki/Parma_Eldalamberon_17 "Parma Eldalamberon 17").<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Excerpt {#Excerpt}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The languages have, of course, changed quite as much as the world and its stories to which they belong, and are now almost an <a href="/wiki/Ents" title="Ents">Entish</a> record of my own linguistic-æsthetic history, hardening at last with age. But you are quite right in your perceptions. <a href="/wiki/Quenya" title="Quenya">Quenya</a> still retains the traces of the impact upon me of Finnish. <a href="/wiki/Sindarin" title="Sindarin">Sindarin</a> was deliberately composed to resemble (with a difference) Welsh, by working similar sound-changes on the simple basic <a href="/wiki/Eldarin" title="Eldarin">Eldarin</a> structure to those exhibited by Brythonic, especially Welsh.
</p>

</blockquote>
