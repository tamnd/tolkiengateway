**Longbottom** was a village or town in the [Southfarthing](/wiki/Southfarthing) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It was the home of some of the [Hornblowers](/wiki/Hornblower_Family "Hornblower Family").

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2670](/wiki/Third_Age_2670 "Third Age 2670") (1070 by the [Shire-reckoning](/wiki/Shire-reckoning)), a certain [Tobold Hornblower](/wiki/Tobold_Hornblower) introduced a new herb there.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The valley's sheltered aspect, and the relatively warm climate of the Southfarthing, made the region around Longbottom ideal for growing this new plant, [pipe-weed](/wiki/Pipe-weed "Pipe-weed"), which rapidly became the centre of a thriving industry. [Longbottom Leaf](/wiki/Longbottom_Leaf) remained one of the most famous varieties of the weed.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The element bottom is frequent in English place-names (and their surnames like <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsbottom" title="wikipedia:Ramsbottom">Ramsbottom</a>); it means "valley", especially its head or inner end.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

[David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) suggests that it represents a possible [Old Hobbitish](/wiki/Old_English "Old English") *Langbotm* meaning "Long Valley".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
