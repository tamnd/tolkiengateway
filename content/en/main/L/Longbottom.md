**Longbottom** was a village or town in the [Southfarthing](../S/Southfarthing.md) of [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It was the home of some of the [Hornblowers](../H/Hornblower_Family.md).

In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2670](../T/Third_Age_2670.md) (1070 by the [Shire-reckoning](../S/Shire-reckoning.md)), a certain [Tobold Hornblower](../T/Tobold_Hornblower.md) introduced a new herb there.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> The valley's sheltered aspect, and the relatively warm climate of the Southfarthing, made the region around Longbottom ideal for growing this new plant, [pipe-weed](../P/Pipe-weed.md), which rapidly became the centre of a thriving industry. [Longbottom Leaf](Longbottom_Leaf.md) remained one of the most famous varieties of the weed.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The element bottom is frequent in English place-names (and their surnames like <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsbottom" title="wikipedia:Ramsbottom">Ramsbottom</a>); it means "valley", especially its head or inner end.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

[David Salo](../D/David_Salo.md) suggests that it represents a possible [Old Hobbitish](../O/Old_English.md) *Langbotm* meaning "Long Valley".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
