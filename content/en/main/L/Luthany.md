**Luthany** was located in the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands), and afterwards it became the island of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain" title="wikipedia:Great Britain">Great Britain</a>, according to the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in some of the outlines for the continuation of *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Gre_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gre-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Origin {#Origin}

Luthany was originally connected to the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands),<sup id="cite_ref-Gre_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gre-1">[1]</a></sup> and after the [First Faring Forth](/wiki/First_Faring_Forth) and the war with evil [Men](/wiki/Men) the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) that yet remained in the Great Lands moved to Luthany in large numbers due to the fact that the King of Luthany was [Ing](/wiki/Ing), a Man that was friendly towards the Elves.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Eärendel and Ing {#Eärendel_and_Ing}

At some point in time [Eärendel](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel), fleeing from [Ossë](/wiki/Oss%C3%AB), appeared in Luthany and sought Ing's help. In return, Eärendel gave Ing *[limpë](/wiki/Limp%C3%AB "Limpë")*, a drink that turned him immortal.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup> He also blessed Ing's progeny, and told him of [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa), an island to which the Elves of Luthany go to, kindling Ing's desire to sail there. Presumably due to this, Ing and many of his people embarked on a voyage to Tol Eressëa - however, due to Ossë's wrath at Ing's helping of Eärendel, they were caught up in a storm and blown eastwards, with Ing being the only survivor.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup>

In response to the departure of Ing, and in fear of an invasion by evil Men, the Elves of Luthany cut a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_channel" title="wikipedia:English channel">channel</a> that separated Luthany from the rest of the Great Lands, making it an island.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>

The island of Luthany suffered seven invasions in its history after the arrival of the Elves, each one worse for the Elves than the last one. The penultimate invasion was that of the [Rúmhoth](/wiki/R%C3%BAmhoth) (also known as the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romans" title="wikipedia:Romans">Romans</a>) who did not even believe that the Elves were real, prompting the departure of nearly every elf left on the island.<sup id="cite_ref-Lut_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lut-6">[5]</a></sup>

<h3><span id="England.27s_origins"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="England&#39;s_origins">England&#39;s origins</span></h3>

After 300 years<sup id="cite_ref-Lut_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lut-6">[5]</a></sup> however the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Ingwaiwar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ingwaiwar (page does not exist)">Ingwaiwar</a> (people of Ing) invaded and were friendly to the Elves due to the teachings of Ing - and because of this, what little was left of the Elves on the island temporarily slowed down their exodus to Tol Eressëa, many of whom learned [Old English](/wiki/Old_English), the language of the Ingwaiwar, during that time.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup>

Some time after, a mariner called [Ælfwine](/wiki/%C3%86lfwine), a descendant of Ing, was born in Luthany. He would later manage to find a way to Tol Eressëa, and learn the histories of the Elves. In one of the outlines of the *Book of Lost Tales*, Ælfwine was the author of the *[Golden Book of Tavrobel](/wiki/Parma_Kuluina "Parma Kuluina")*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup>

According to another outline, it was prophesied by the Elves that Ing would eventually lead them from Tol Eressëa back to Luthany<sup id="cite_ref-Lut_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lut-6">[5]</a></sup> in the [Second Faring Forth](/wiki/Second_Faring_Forth).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>

## Description {#Description}

Many of the places in Tol Eressëa, such as [Kortirion](/wiki/Kortirion) or [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel) (the New), were named after older places in Luthany (such as [Kortirion the Old](/wiki/Kortirion_the_Old) or [Tavrobel the Old](/wiki/Tavrobel_the_Old)).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Luthany* is in [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> meaning "friendship".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup> <i><b>Albion</b></i> also appears as a name of Luthany in one of the outlines.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[12]</a></sup>

An earlier form of the name was <i><b>Lúthien</b></i> (in most other texts from this time period its meaning is "friend", appearing in conjunction with *Luthany* = "friendship"). An alternative form, <i><b>Leithian</b></i>, appears written above *Lúthien* in one of the texts.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[13]</a></sup>

In a somewhat later version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") from the [1930](/wiki/1930) (*[Quenta Noldorinwa](/wiki/Quenta_Noldorinwa)*), **Leithien** was the name of one of the islands that were formed during the destruction of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) in the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath). It later became known as Britain or England.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[14]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

*Luthany* appears as a name of a country in a poem called *The Mistress of Vision* by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Thompson" title="wikipedia:Francis Thompson">Francis Thompson</a>, who was an influence on [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[15]</a></sup>
