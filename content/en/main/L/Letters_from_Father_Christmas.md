<i><b>Letters from Father Christmas</b></i>, also called <i><b>The Father Christmas Letters</b></i> in earlier editions, is a collection of letters written and illustrated by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) between [1920](/wiki/1920) and [1943](/wiki/1943) for his children, from [Father Christmas](/wiki/Father_Christmas).

It was posthumously released on [2 September](/wiki/2_September) [1976](/wiki/1976), the third anniversary of Tolkien's death, and was edited by [Baillie Tolkien](/wiki/Baillie_Tolkien), the wife of [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien).

Here it transcribes these letters and reproduces them, along with their envelopes, in facsimile. Many of these envelopes have Tolkien's hand-drawn stamps on them, and many letters are accompanied with full-page artwork. The work is warmly received by critics, and it has been suggested that some elements were carried over to the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium").

## Overview {#Overview}

The stories are told in the form of letters, primarily written by Father Christmas, with letters or parts of letters written by the [North Polar Bear](/wiki/North_Polar_Bear) and his Elven secretary [Ilbereth](/wiki/Ilbereth). The letters document the adventures and misadventures of Father Christmas and his helpers, including the accident-prone North Polar Bear and nephews, [Paksu](/wiki/Paksu) and [Valkotukka](/wiki/Valkotukka). Many letters include illustrations drawn by Father Christmas.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The letters themselves were written over over 20 years to entertain Tolkien's children each Christmas. The first letter was written in [1920](/wiki/1920) when Tolkien's oldest son, [John](/wiki/John_Tolkien "John Tolkien"), was three years old, after he'd asked about Father Christmas and where he lived.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Each letter included including North Pole stamps and postage marks designed by Tolkien.

## Publication {#Publication}

The first edition was published by [Allen &amp; Unwin](/wiki/Allen_%26_Unwin), under the name *The Father Christmas Letters*, on [2 September](/wiki/2_September) [1976](/wiki/1976), three years after Tolkien's death. Artwork from The Letters from *The Father Christmas Letters* were first displayed in [1976](/wiki/1976)-[1976](/wiki/1976) at the Ashmolean Museum to mark the release of the book.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The [Houghton Mifflin](/wiki/Houghton_Mifflin) edition was released later that year on [19 October](/wiki/19_October).

The book was revised in [1999](/wiki/1999) and was retitled *Letters from Father Christmas*, this edition included some more letters and artwork not published previously. So did the [2012](/wiki/2012)'s, [2019](/wiki/2019)'s, and subsequent editions. For a detailed comparison of contents between editions, see <a class="external text" href="https://www.tolkienguide.com/modules/wiwimod/index.php?page=Letters%20From%20Father%20Christmas" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">this review</a>.

## Reception {#Reception}

The reception to the original publication of *The Father Christmas Letters* was received very positively though Judith A. Johnson, in her book *J.R.R. Tolkien: Six Decades of Criticism* has suggested that this may have been partly due to Tolkien's recent death, noticing that the response to *The Father Christmas Letters* was much more measured and balanced.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

Jessica Kemball-Cook suggested in her book *Twentieth Century Children's Writers* that it would become known as a classic of children's literature, while Nancy Willard for *The New York Times Book Review* also received the book positively, saying "Father Christmas lives. And never more merrily than in these pages." In 2002, an article in *The Independent on Sunday* described the work as rivalling "*The Lord of the Rings* for sheer imaginative joy".

## Relation to the legendarium {#Relation_to_the_legendarium}

While the *Letters from Father Christmas* are not part of Tolkien's legendarium, some shared elements can be noted:

- Father Christmas' Elf-secretary [Ilbereth](/wiki/Ilbereth) (reminiscent of [Elbereth](/wiki/Elbereth_Gilthoniel "Elbereth Gilthoniel")?)<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup>
- glimpses of [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) writing and the [Goblin Alphabet](/wiki/Goblin_Alphabet)
- ["Arctic" Quenya](/wiki/Arktik "Arktik")
- [Man in the Moon](/wiki/Man_in_the_Moon)

Kris Swank has discussed similarities between *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and the *Letters from Father Christmas*, noting that they share a couple of story elements and that both works "contain story elements which have common roots in Tolkien's early [Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales "The Book of Lost Tales") and poems".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
