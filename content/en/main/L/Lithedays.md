The **Lithedays** or just **The Lithe** refers to the three (sometimes four) feast days between the months of [Forelithe](../F/Forelithe.md) and [Afterlithe](../A/Afterlithe.md) of the [Shire Calendar](../S/Shire_Calendar.md); in purpose and function the Lithedays were equivalent to the *[enderi](../E/Enderi.md)* of the [Reckoning of Rivendell](../R/Reckoning_of_Rivendell.md).

They were:

- 1 Lithe (midsummer-eve)
- [Mid-year's Day](../M/Mid-year's_Day.md)
    - [Overlithe](../O/Overlithe.md) (observed only in leap-years)
- 2 Lithe

They were called [Summerdays](../S/Summerdays.md) in [Bree](../B/Bree.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Events that occurred on 1 Lithe:

- [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2778](../T/Third_Age_2778.md) (circa)
    - [Thrór](../T/Thrór.md) draws his [Map](../T/Thrór's_Map.md)
- [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2941](../T/Third_Age_2941.md):
    - The [moon](../M/Moon.md) is "a broad silver crescent".
    - [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md) discovers the [moon-letters](../M/Moon-letters.md) on [Thrór's Map](../T/Thrór's_Map.md).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
- [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3019](../T/Third_Age_3019.md):
    - [Elrond](../E/Elrond.md), [Glorfindel](../G/Glorfindel.md), [Erestor](../E/Erestor.md), [Elladan](../E/Elladan.md), [Elrohir](../E/Elrohir.md), [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md) and [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) arrive with [Arwen](../A/Arwen.md) to [Minas Tirith](../M/Minas_Tirith.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Each midsummer-eve the [Old Took](../G/Gerontius_Took.md) held parties, where [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md) impressed the [hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md) with fireworks.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Old English](../O/Old_English.md) <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/li%C3%BEa" title="wiktionary:liþa">líða</a></i>, is the old name for the months June and July.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-5">[5]</a></sup> It probably referred to the first new moon around the summer solstice.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
