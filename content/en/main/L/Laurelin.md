**Laurelin** was the **Golden Tree** of [Aman](/wiki/Aman), the female mate of [Telperion](/wiki/Telperion), and the younger of the [Two Trees of Valinor](/wiki/Two_Trees_of_Valinor). Laurelin had gold-trimmed leaves and her dew was collected by [Varda](/wiki/Varda) in [her Wells](/wiki/Wells_of_Varda "Wells of Varda").<sup id="cite_ref-BegDays_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BegDays-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Laurelin was created by the [Valier](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Yavanna](/wiki/Yavanna) and [Nienna](/wiki/Nienna) on the hill of [Ezellohar](/wiki/Ezellohar), where the Tree shed her light on the realm of [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) in the long years before the making of the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) and [Moon](/wiki/Moon). Laurelin, the younger of the Two Trees, flowered second after [Telperion](/wiki/Telperion), and bloomed during the latter part of each Valian day.<sup id="cite_ref-BegDays_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BegDays-1">[1]</a></sup>

Laurelin was destroyed by [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth"), who used the giant spider spirit [Ungoliant](/wiki/Ungoliant) to his evil purpose during the theft of the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils), which caused the [Darkening of Valinor](/wiki/Darkening_of_Valinor).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> However, through the power of Nienna and Yavanna, Laurelin produced a single fiery fruit before she died. This golden fire was set in a vessel made by [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) and his people, and was steered through [Ilmen](/wiki/Ilmen) by the [Maia](/wiki/Maiar "Maiar") [Arien](/wiki/Arien), making the [Sun](/wiki/Sun).<sup id="cite_ref-OfSun_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OfSun-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) made images of the Two Trees that stood in his court of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin), and the one made in memory of Laurelin was called [Glingal](/wiki/Glingal).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Laurelin* is said to mean "Song of Gold".<sup id="cite_ref-SI_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SI-5">[5]</a></sup> The name *Laurelin* appears to be [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) and contains *[laurë](/wiki/Laur%C3%AB "Laurë")* ("gold") and *[lind](/wiki/Lind "Lind")ë* ("song").<sup id="cite_ref-SI_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SI-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ety_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ety-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

<i><b>Culúrien</b></i>, likely containing the [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) elements *[cul-](/wiki/KUL "KUL")* ("golden-red")<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and *riende* ("garland").<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Robert Ireland suggests it might rather contain *úr* ("fire, heat"), while the suffix is uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

<i><b>Malinalda</b></i> ("Tree of Gold"),<sup id="cite_ref-SI_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SI-5">[5]</a></sup> composed of the words *malina* ("yellow, of golden colour") + *[alda](/wiki/Alda "Alda")* ("tree").<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In [The Later *Quenta Silmarillion*](/wiki/The_Later_Quenta_Silmarillion "The Later Quenta Silmarillion") manuscript, the Sindarin name of Laurelin is <i><b>Galadlóriel</b></i>. In a footnote to the same page three other Sindarin names of Laurelin are given: <i><b>Glewellin</b></i> ("song of gold", the same as Laurelin), <i><b>Lasgalen</b></i> ("green of leaf"<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>), <i><b>Melthinorn</b></i> ("tree of gold").<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

The [Valarin](/wiki/Valarin) name of Laurelin was <i><b>Tulukhedelgorūs</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

On a note referred to Laurelin's *spilth*, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) explains it is meant to indicate that the tree is founded on the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laburnum" title="wikipedia:Laburnum">laburnum</a> as described by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Thompson&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Francis Thompson (page does not exist)">Francis Thompson</a> in a poem:<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Mark yonder, how the long laburnum drips<br/>its jocund spilth of fire, its honey of wild flame!
</p>
