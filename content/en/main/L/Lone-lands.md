The **Lone-lands** was an area in [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), or may even have been synonymous with it.

## History {#History}

The Lone-lands was a name used by [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) (and possibly the [Bree-landers](/wiki/Bree-landers)) for the wilderness east of [Bree-land](/wiki/Bree-land). This area contained, among other features, the [Weather Hills](/wiki/Weather_Hills) and [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop). Roads were considerably worse there than in [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), and no one dwelt there anymore by the end of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). There were many abandoned castles of [Rhudaur](/wiki/Rhudaur) in the hilly region, which gave it a wicked look.<sup id="cite_ref-Mutton_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mutton-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Lone-lands and Eriador {#Lone-lands_and_Eriador}

It is a subject of some debate whether the Lone-lands and Eriador are one and the same. Etymologies given for *Eriador* certainly suggest such a thing.<sup id="cite_ref-vt42_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt42-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-pe17_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pe17-3">[3]</a></sup> The two mentions of "Lone-lands" were not added until the revised, [1966](/wiki/1966) edition of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> well after the introduction of the word "Eriador" in *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> As the latter was spoken by [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), who would know the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name, and the former by [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins"), who would not (yet) know the "foreign" name, it is not inconceivable that they refer to the same land.<sup id="cite_ref-vt42_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vt42-2">[2]</a></sup>

<b>1988: <i><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_War_in_Middle_Earth" title="J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s War in Middle Earth">J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s War in Middle Earth</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>&#34;Lone-lands&#34; is a name given to the area south of <a href="/wiki/Weathertop" title="Weathertop">Weathertop</a>.</dd></dl>

<b><a href="/wiki/1997" title="1997">1997</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Collectible_Card_Game" title="Middle-earth Collectible Card Game">Middle-earth Collectible Card Game</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd><i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth:_The_Lidless_Eye" title="Middle-earth: The Lidless Eye">The Lidless Eye</a></i> features the <i>Lost in the Wilderness</i> card, illustrated by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a>, which features a quote at the bottom from <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a></i>, which refers to the Lone-lands, indicating that is the area depicted in the artwork. The card has the following effects.<blockquote><p>Playable on a moving company. Its hazard limit increases by one for every Wilderness in its site path.</p></blockquote></dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game)" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (2003 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The narrator mentions the Lone-lands when <a href="/wiki/Thorin_and_Company" title="Thorin and Company">Thorin and Company</a> travel from Hobbiton to the <a href="/wiki/Trollshaws" title="Trollshaws">Trollshaws</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Also, the game&#39;s soundtrack contains a piece called &#34;Combat in the Lone-lands&#34;, which accompanies <a href="/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins" title="Bilbo Baggins">Bilbo</a>&#39;s trek to the campfire of the Trolls.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The name &#34;Lone-lands&#34; refers explicitly to the region between <a href="/wiki/Bree-land" title="Bree-land">Bree-land</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Trollshaws" title="Trollshaws">Trollshaws</a>. Its boundaries are <a href="/wiki/The_Forsaken_Inn" title="The Forsaken Inn">The Forsaken Inn</a> in the far west and the <a href="/wiki/Last_Bridge" title="Last Bridge">Last Bridge</a> at the far east. The game goes around the pre-existing mention of the region being uninhabited by making its people, the Eglain (Sindarin for &#34;Foresaken&#34;), shun existing society and avoid attention by travelers.</dd></dl>
