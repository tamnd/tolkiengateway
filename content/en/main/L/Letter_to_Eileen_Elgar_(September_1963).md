Between [22 September](/wiki/22_September) and [3 October](/wiki/3_October) [1963](/wiki/1963) (when it was postmarked), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote **[a letter](/wiki/Letters_not_published_in_The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien "Letters not published in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien") to Mrs Eileen Elgar**.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

- **Note:** A draft for this letter was published in *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien)* as [Letter 246](/wiki/Letter_246).
- **Contents:** Answers to questions about *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, especially about the failure of [Frodo](/wiki/Frodo). The letter varies widely from the draft.
- **Description:** 6 pages, closely-typed, with an autograph postscript, additions and corrections. 4to, 76 Sandfield Road, Headington.<sup id="cite_ref-BB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BB-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **Publication:** An excerpt from the letter has appeared in *[Sotheby's English Literature and English History 6-7 December 1984](/wiki/Sotheby%27s_English_Literature_and_English_History_6-7_December_1984)* and [*Beyond Bree* May 1985](/wiki/Beyond_Bree_May_1985 "Beyond Bree May 1985") (p. 6).

## Excerpt {#Excerpt}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>... The most important point was that of Frodo&#39;s &#34;moral failure&#34;. Only one other correspondent has referred to it; and he abused F. as a scoundrel, and me — for holding him up to admiration ... Frodo failed as a &#39;hero&#39; conceived in abstract ideal terms: he succumbed to the pressure of <a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring" title="The One Ring">the Ring</a>, which at that instant reached its maximum, when starved, utterly exhausted, and after months of increasing fear and torment. But we are all finite creatures, having absolute limits to our powers of soul, mind, and body ... Frodo took the Ring in complete humility, and his motive was entirely selfless ... By his sufferings he provided a situation in which the quest could be achieved, and by his pity (for <a href="/wiki/Gollum" title="Gollum">Gollum</a>) he made the means for this available. We, the readers, and within the book the Great can, I think have no doubt whatever in our praise ...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>J.R.R. Tolkien<sup id="cite_ref-BB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BB-2">[2]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>
