This article concerns the **later Ages**, defined for the purposes of this article as the [Ages](/wiki/Ages) that took place after the ending of the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age).

According to one of the drafts of the *[Appendices](/wiki/Appendices)*, it is said of the Fourth Age:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Of <a href="/wiki/Eldarion" title="Eldarion">Eldarion</a> son of <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">Elessar</a> it was foretold that he should rule a great <a href="/wiki/Reunited_Kingdom" title="Reunited Kingdom">realm</a>, and <b>that it should endure for a hundred generations of <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a> after him, that is until a new age brought in again new things</b>; and from him should come the kings of many realms in long days after.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth" title="The Peoples of Middle-earth">The Peoples of Middle-earth</a></i>, &#34;<a href="/wiki/The_Tale_of_Years_of_the_Third_Age" title="The Tale of Years of the Third Age">The Tale of Years of the Third Age</a>&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

## Development {#Development}

### Letter to Rhona Beare (1958) {#Letter_to_Rhona_Beare_(1958)}

In the *[Letter 211](/wiki/Letter_211)* that [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) wrote on [14 October](/wiki/14_October) [1958](/wiki/1958) to [Rhona Beare](/wiki/Rhona_Beare), he writes that the time gap between the fall of [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr) (in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019")) and the present-day (as in 1958, at the time that Tolkien wrote the said letter) was c. 6,000 years.

Therefore, Tolkien concluded that in 1958, the world was nearer to the end of the **Fifth Age**, if the subsequent Ages were of the same length as the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and the [Third](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") Ages.

However, he ultimately concluded that the Ages have "quickened", and that the world was now (in 1958, that is) at the end of its **Sixth Age**, or maybe even in the Seventh.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

### *The Awaking of the Quendi* (1960) {#The_Awaking_of_the_Quendi_(1960)}

In a [1960](/wiki/1960) text called <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Awaking_of_the_Quendi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Awaking of the Quendi (page does not exist)">The Awaking of the Quendi</a></i>, Tolkien explicitly calls the year 1960 AD (the year in which he was writing the text) as the year 1960 of the **Seventh Age**.

In that text Tolkien connects the beginning of the Seventh Age (i.e. [Se.A.](/wiki/Seventh_Age "Seventh Age") [1](/wiki/Seventh_Age_1 "Seventh Age 1")) with the [incarnation](/wiki/Old_Hope "Old Hope") of [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) (that is, the birth of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus" title="wikipedia:Jesus">Jesus Christ</a> in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AD_1" title="wikipedia:AD 1">1 AD</a> according to the real-world <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini" title="wikipedia:Anno Domini">Anno Domini</a></i> reckoning of the [Catholic Church](/wiki/Christianity "Christianity")).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup>

Tolkien also goes on to say that the time-gap between the year 1960 of the Seventh Age (i.e. 1960 AD) and the [Year of the Sun](/wiki/Years_of_the_Sun "Years of the Sun") [310](/wiki/First_Age_310 "First Age 310") of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[note 2]</a></sup> was 16,000 years.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[3]</a></sup>

This makes it possible to calculate when some of the in-universe Ages happened in relation to the real-world chronology:

1. The end of the First Age ([F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [590](/wiki/First_Age_590 "First Age 590")) corresponds to the year <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_prehistory#Upper_Paleolithic" title="wikipedia:Timeline of prehistory">13,760 BC</a>
2. The end of the Second Age ([S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441")) corresponds to the year <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_prehistory#Upper_Paleolithic" title="wikipedia:Timeline of prehistory">10,319 BC</a>
3. The end of the Third Age ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3021](/wiki/Third_Age_3021 "Third Age 3021")) corresponds to the year <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_millennium_BC" title="wikipedia:8th millennium BC">7,298 BC</a>

Therefore, the average duration of the Fourth through Sixth Ages would be c. 2,430 years<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[4]</a></sup> justifying his previous letter to Beare that the Ages must have quickened. On the other hand, while the letter suggested that the Third Age ended in around 4,000 BC, this calculation pushes it further back.

### 1964 BBC interview {#1964_BBC_interview}

In [November 26](/wiki/November_26) [1964](/wiki/1964) Tolkien was interviewed by [BBC](/wiki/BBC), and during the more informal part of the interview with [Denys Gueroult](/wiki/Denys_Gueroult)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[5]</a></sup> he offhandedly mentioned that the events of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* happened around 7-8,000 years before present (1964 at the time), somehow consistent to *The Awakening of the Quendi* essay:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The moons, I think, finally the moons and the suns have worked out according to what they were in this part of the world in 1942 actually. They must have something where they... I mean one... I couldn’t... I’m not good enough mathematician or astronomer to work out where they might have been <b>seven thousand, eight thousand years ago</b>. But as long as they correspond to some real configuration I thought it was good enough. Moons are much more tricky to deal with than suns of course, but in the whole, I don’t think the moon is full or rises in the wrong place...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="/wiki/1964_BBC_Interview" title="1964 BBC Interview">1964 BBC Interview</a></i>, <i>24:42 to 25:05 video mark</i><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[6]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b><a href="/wiki/1995" title="1995">1995</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Dragon_Magazine" title="Dragon Magazine">Dragon Magazine</a></i>:</b> The French version of the Magazine had an article about the "Fifth Age" of Middle-earth; the map was illustrated by [Olivier Frot](/wiki/Olivier_Frot).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 3]</a></sup>

<b><a href="/wiki/2000" title="2000">2000</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Tolkien:_The_Illustrated_Encyclopedia" title="Tolkien: The Illustrated Encyclopedia">Tolkien: The Illustrated Encyclopedia</a></i>:</b> [David Day](/wiki/David_Day) displays some chronological evolution of [Arda](/wiki/Arda), from the "Age of the Lamps" to the Third Age, while in the later Ages the illustrations show how Middle-earth changed geologically to resemble modern Europe, while and a continent resembling the Americas forming west of [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer).
