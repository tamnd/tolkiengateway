This [Canto](/wiki/Cantos_of_the_Lay_of_Leithian "Cantos of the Lay of Leithian") starts out with the tale of [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol) and [Melian](/wiki/Melian). Then it gives a description of [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) their daughter, and tells how [Beren](/wiki/Beren) watched in amazement. [Dairon](/wiki/Dairon) warns Lúthien, and she hides, but Beren touches her arm by accident. Again Beren searches, and months later catches her again, naming her Tinúviel. Below is recounted the second meeting.

## Concerning the Canto {#Concerning_the_Canto}

This canto, in contrast to the previous one, shows peace and hope, and the beauty of Doriath.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...the rocks were ringing<br/>
the birds of Melian were singing
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 409-410</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

It tells of the meeting and love of Thingol and Melian, mirroring in a way the future meeting and love of Beren and Lúthien; one elf to [maia](/wiki/Maia "Maia"), the other man to elf.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>There after but an hour, him seems,<br/>
he finds her where she lies and dreams<br/>
pale Melian with her dark hair<br/>
upon a bed of leaves. Beware!<br/>
there slumber and a sleep is twined!<br/>
He touched her tresses and his mind<br/>
was drowned in the forgetful deep<br/>
and dark the years rolled o&#39;er his sleep.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 445-452</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

This canto also includes the short paragraph about the minstrels: Tinfang Gelion, Maglor, and Dairon, the first of which has only this place laid aside for him in all the known writings of [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien").

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Tinfang" title="Tinfang">Tinfang Gelion</a> who still the moon<br/>
enchants on summer nights of June<br/>
and kindles the pale firstling star;<br/>
and he who harps upon the far<br/>
forgotten beaches and dark shores<br/>
where western foam for ever roars<br/>
<a href="/wiki/Maglor" title="Maglor">Maglor</a> whose voice is like the sea;<br/>
and Dairon, mightiest of the three.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 503-510</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Beren's mixture of sorrow and bliss and the fleeing of Lúthien is shown, and at the last when he catches her in the vivid description of her dancing it culumnates.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;Tinúviel! Tinúviel!&#39;<br/>
His voice such love and longing filled<br/>
one moment stood she, fear was stilled;<br/>
one moment only; like a flame<br/>
he leaped towards her as she stayed<br/>
and caught and kissed that elfin maid.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 740-745</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Then is a paragraph unlike the rest of the Lay, unless it were Thingol's thoughts of pity on Lúthien; a sort of cry from the poet to the one he writes about, asking her why she took her doom, and left elven immortality.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>A! Lúthien! A! Lúthien!<br/>
more fair than any child of Men;<br/>
O! loveliest maid of Elfinesse<br/>
what madness does thee now possess!
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 748-751</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

On a final note, she slips away "just at the breaking of the day".
