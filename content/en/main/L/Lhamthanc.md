**Lhamthanc**, was the name of a [serpent](../S/Serpents.md), about whom almost nothing is known except that they possess a serpent-name.<sup id="cite_ref-lhamthanc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lhamthanc-1">[1]</a></sup> It is likely that in this case, the word serpent may actually be referring to one of the [dragons](../D/Dragons.md) of [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md) since Tolkien often described dragons as worms and serpents and Lhamthanc is mentioned next to [Gostir](../G/Gostir.md) in *[The Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)*. Lhamthanc is not mentioned in any known story.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Lhamthanc* means "forked tongue" in [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md), from *lham(b)* ("tongue") and *thanc* ("cleft, split"), as well as the root *LAB*, meaning "lick".<sup id="cite_ref-lhamthanc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lhamthanc-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Lhamthanc was originally called <i><b>Iamthanc</b></i>, an early Noldorin name meaning "snake, (lit) forked tongue", from *lham(b)* ("tongue") and *thanc* (" cleft, divided, split, cloven, forked, bifurcated; cleft, break, breach").<sup id="cite_ref-iamthanc_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-iamthanc-2">[2]</a></sup> However, Tolkien eventually settled on Lhamthanc for the name.<sup id="cite_ref-lhamthanc_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lhamthanc-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

### *Middle-earth Role Playing* {#Middle-earth_Role_Playing}

<dl><dd>Lhamthanc appears as a huge gray and white <a href="../C/Cold-drakes.md" title="Cold-drakes">Cold-drake</a> who possessed a forked tongue that was nine-feet long in the <i>Creatures of Middle-earth</i> supplement of <i><a href="../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>. Lhamthanc was said to be the younger brother of <a href="../G/Gostir.md" title="Gostir">Gostir</a>.</dd></dl>
