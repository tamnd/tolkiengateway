"**Where now the horse and the rider? Where is the horn that was blowing?**" is the first line of a [poem](/wiki/Poems_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings "Poems in The Lord of the Rings") written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) about [Eorl](/wiki/Eorl).

## Poem excerpt {#Poem_excerpt}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Where now the horse and the rider? Where is the horn that was blowing? <br/>
Where is the helm and the hauberk, and the bright hair flowing? <br/>
Where is the hand on the harpstring, and the red fire glowing? <br/>
Where is the spring and the harvest and the tall corn growing?<sup id="cite_ref-lotr_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-1">[1]</a></sup>
</p>
</div>

## History {#History}

A forgotten poet of [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan) had originally written it about [Eorl the Young](/wiki/Eorl_the_Young) and his horse [Felaróf](/wiki/Felar%C3%B3f) long before the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-lotr_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"), [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) sang a lament to characterize the [Rohirrim](/wiki/Rohirrim) as he, [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas), and [Gimli](/wiki/Gimli) approached [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras). He originally chanted it in [Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese). After Legolas commented that the sound of the words resembled the land of Rohan, Aragorn recited his translation of it.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The earliest version of the poem, which may have been written "between [February](/wiki/February) and midsummer" of [1942](/wiki/1942), "seems to be" more about "Eorl’s ride from the North" than the passing of time.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> Subsequently in the next manuscript,<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien utilized the style of <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wanderer" title="wikipedia:The Wanderer">The Wanderer</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> which used a convention called <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubi_sunt" title="wikipedia:Ubi sunt">Ubi sunt</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-companion_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-companion-3">[3]</a></sup>

In a revision to the earliest versions of the chapter "[The King of the Golden Hall](/wiki/The_King_of_the_Golden_Hall)", Tolkien wrote the verse that Aragorn utters into the story, though Eorl is referred to as "the Old" rather than "the Young".<sup id="cite_ref-early_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-early-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

In a note to an early draft, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) wrote that the verse echoes line 92 of *The Wanderer*, an [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) poem.<sup id="cite_ref-note_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-note-5">[5]</a></sup>

It is possible that the first two lines of Aragorn's were inspired by the poem because it contains the words "[mathom](/wiki/Mathom "Mathom")-giver" and "[theoden](/wiki/Theoden "Theoden")".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2002: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers">The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Before the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Hornburg" title="Battle of the Hornburg">Battle</a> of <a href="/wiki/Helm%27s_Deep" title="Helm&#39;s Deep">Helm&#39;s Deep</a>, <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden" title="Théoden">Théoden</a> recites some lines of the poem, lamenting how alone the Rohirrim stand. <blockquote><p>Where is the horse and the rider?<br/>Where is the horn that was blowing?<br/>They have passed like rain on the mountains,<br/>like wind in the meadow.<br/>The days have gone down in the West,<br/>behind the hills... into Shadow.</p></blockquote></dd>
<dd>The remaining lines are sung in Old English on the score in the background in the above and other scenes.</dd></dl>
