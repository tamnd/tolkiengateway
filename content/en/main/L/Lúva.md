Each of the twenty-four standard [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) of the [Fëanorian alphabet](/wiki/F%C3%ABanorian_alphabet) was composed of two basic elements: a straight [telco](/wiki/Telco "Telco") or stem, from which curved a rounded 'bow' shape known as a **lúva**. The nature of the bow defined the [téma](/wiki/T%C3%A9mar "Témar"), or sound-series, to which a character belonged. Some curved upwards and leftwards from the stem, while others curved downwards and to the right. The lúva was sometimes 'closed' (with a horizontal bar connecting it back to the telco) or sometimes left 'open'.

For instance (using the conventions of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age)) all of the tengwar with an open lúva curving down and right belonged to the [tincotéma](/wiki/Tincot%C3%A9ma "Tincotéma"), the series of 't' sounds, while all those with a closed, upward, left-curling lúva were part of the [calmatéma](/wiki/Calmat%C3%A9ma "Calmatéma") (sounds related to 'k').

Though the direction and structure of the lúva was always constant within a series, it was often 'doubled' within that series (that is, a second bow would be drawn curving out from the first). For instance, the character *[tinco](/wiki/Tinco "Tinco")* ('t') had a single lúva, while the 'voiced' version of the same sound, *[ando](/wiki/Ando "Ando")* ('d') had a double bow.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
