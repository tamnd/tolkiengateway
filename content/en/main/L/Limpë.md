<i><b>Limpë</b></i> was a magical elven drink that appeared in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)*, an early form of Tolkien's [legendarium](Legendarium.md).

## History {#History}

*Limpë* could heal longing caused by [Tinfang's](../T/Tinfang.md) music. Only queen [Meril](../M/Meril-i-Turinqi.md) could give it. Drinking it was dangerous for a <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../M/Mortal.md" title="Mortal">mortal</a> [Man](../M/Man.md), as [Ilúvatar](../I/Ilúvatar.md) made his [Children](../C/Children_of_Ilúvatar.md) different, and drinking *limpë* would erase his old desires but awake new ones. Sometimes, after teaching and examination of the heart, it was given to children from the [Cottage of Lost Play](../C/Cottage_of_Lost_Play.md), but giving it to an adult was different and the person would suffer. It gave youth, but the man had to die anyway.<sup id="cite_ref-lt1iv_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt1iv-1">[1]</a></sup>

Because [Noldoli](../G/Gnomes.md) had not had it yet during their flight from [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md), the evil soon ensnared them.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> *Limpë* was given to elves by [Tulkas](../T/Tulkas.md) during their great expedition to save enslaved [Gnomes](../G/Gnomes.md).<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup>

*Limpë* (maybe given by [Eärendel](../E/Eärendel.md)) made a [man](../M/Man.md) named [Ingwë](../I/Ingwë.md), king of [Luthany](Luthany.md), immortal.<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup>

### In the story of Eriol {#In_the_story_of_Eriol}

[Eriol](../E/Eriol.md) learnt about *limpë* from [Vairë](<../V/Vairë_(wife_of_Lindo).md>). When he went for it to Meril-i-Turinqi, she refused to give him it immediately, as first he had to learn more. She told him about the [chaining of Melko](../C/Chaining_of_Melkor.md)<sup id="cite_ref-lt1iv_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt1iv-1">[1]</a></sup> and the coming of the elves.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Later [Lindo](Lindo.md) explained that Eriol had to visit [Gilfanon](../G/Gilfanon.md) in his [House of the Hundred Chimneys](../H/House_of_the_Hundred_Chimneys.md).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> As the last condition, Gilfanon bid Eriol to write down all the tales that he had heard in [Tol Eressëa](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md). Then Eriol drank *limpë*. He was forever young and married [Naimi](../N/Naimi.md), an elf, but finally he died in [Tavrobel](../T/Tavrobel.md), homesick.<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Limpë* is an early [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) name meaning "wine, drink of the [Valar](../V/Valar.md)"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>, being derived from the [root](../S/Sundocarmë.md) <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=LIP&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="LIP (page does not exist)">LIP</a> ("wine").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md), the name *limpë* variously meant "fairy-drink"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>, "elfwine"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>, and "drink of the [fairies](../F/Fairies.md)"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-8">[8]</a></sup> It came from the root LIPI, related with "drip, drop". Its [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) cognate was *limp* or *limpelis*.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-8">[8]</a></sup> However, later Gnomish forms were <i><b>limfa</b></i> or <i><b>limfelis</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> It was called *líp* in [Old English](../O/Old_English.md).<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
