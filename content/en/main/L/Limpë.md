<i><b>Limpë</b></i> was a magical elven drink that appeared in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, an early form of Tolkien's [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium").

## History {#History}

*Limpë* could heal longing caused by [Tinfang's](/wiki/Tinfang "Tinfang") music. Only queen [Meril](/wiki/Meril-i-Turinqi "Meril-i-Turinqi") could give it. Drinking it was dangerous for a <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Mortal" title="Mortal">mortal</a> [Man](/wiki/Man), as [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) made his [Children](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar "Children of Ilúvatar") different, and drinking *limpë* would erase his old desires but awake new ones. Sometimes, after teaching and examination of the heart, it was given to children from the [Cottage of Lost Play](/wiki/Cottage_of_Lost_Play), but giving it to an adult was different and the person would suffer. It gave youth, but the man had to die anyway.<sup id="cite_ref-lt1iv_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt1iv-1">[1]</a></sup>

Because [Noldoli](/wiki/Gnomes "Gnomes") had not had it yet during their flight from [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor), the evil soon ensnared them.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> *Limpë* was given to elves by [Tulkas](/wiki/Tulkas) during their great expedition to save enslaved [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes).<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup>

*Limpë* (maybe given by [Eärendel](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendel)) made a [man](/wiki/Man "Man") named [Ingwë](/wiki/Ingw%C3%AB), king of [Luthany](/wiki/Luthany), immortal.<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup>

### In the story of Eriol {#In_the_story_of_Eriol}

[Eriol](/wiki/Eriol) learnt about *limpë* from [Vairë](</wiki/Vair%C3%AB_(wife_of_Lindo)> "Vairë (wife of Lindo)"). When he went for it to Meril-i-Turinqi, she refused to give him it immediately, as first he had to learn more. She told him about the [chaining of Melko](/wiki/Chaining_of_Melkor "Chaining of Melkor")<sup id="cite_ref-lt1iv_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt1iv-1">[1]</a></sup> and the coming of the elves.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Later [Lindo](/wiki/Lindo) explained that Eriol had to visit [Gilfanon](/wiki/Gilfanon) in his [House of the Hundred Chimneys](/wiki/House_of_the_Hundred_Chimneys).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> As the last condition, Gilfanon bid Eriol to write down all the tales that he had heard in [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa). Then Eriol drank *limpë*. He was forever young and married [Naimi](/wiki/Naimi), an elf, but finally he died in [Tavrobel](/wiki/Tavrobel), homesick.<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Limpë* is an early [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name meaning "wine, drink of the [Valar](/wiki/Valar)"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>, being derived from the [root](/wiki/Sundocarm%C3%AB "Sundocarmë") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=LIP&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="LIP (page does not exist)">LIP</a> ("wine").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

In [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya), the name *limpë* variously meant "fairy-drink"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>, "elfwine"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>, and "drink of the [fairies](/wiki/Fairies "Fairies")"<sup id="cite_ref-eldamo_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eldamo-6">[6]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-8">[8]</a></sup> It came from the root LIPI, related with "drip, drop". Its [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) cognate was *limp* or *limpelis*.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-8">[8]</a></sup> However, later Gnomish forms were <i><b>limfa</b></i> or <i><b>limfelis</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> It was called *líp* in [Old English](/wiki/Old_English).<sup id="cite_ref-lt2vi_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lt2vi-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
