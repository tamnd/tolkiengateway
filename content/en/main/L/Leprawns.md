**Leprawns** (or **leprechauns**<sup id="cite_ref-PE18_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE18-1">[1]</a></sup>) were creatures only appearing in some of the earliest versions of the [legendarium](Legendarium.md). In one passage, the leprawns are seemingly described as a subgroup among the [sprites](../S/Sprites.md).<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-2">[2]</a></sup> In early poems, the mysterious character [Tinfang](../T/Tinfang.md) is said to have been a leprawn.<sup id="cite_ref-PE18_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE18-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It has been suggested that the name *leprawn* is "Tolkien's own peculiar adaptation of *leprechaun*".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other writings {#Other_writings}

The poem *[Goblin Feet](../G/Goblin_Feet.md)* speak of "tiny horns" played by "enchanted leprechauns".

## Real-life {#Real-life}

Authors Philip and Carol Zaleski recount that:<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 1]</a></sup>

> *Tolkien, in response to an undergraduate's query about the truth of dragons and other legends, declared that behind such tales invariably lies something real; he then dug into his pockets and pulled out, along with a ball of string and other detritus, a small green shoe, thin and pointed in the toe, made of leathery substance that felt like reptile skin, and declared it, "stoutly and with apparent sincerity", to be a leprechaun's shoe.*
