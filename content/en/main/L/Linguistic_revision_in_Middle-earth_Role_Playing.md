**Linguistic revision in *[Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing)*** refers to the efforts of [Iron Crown Enterprises](/wiki/Iron_Crown_Enterprises) to correct or provide in-universe rationales for naming inconsistencies in their sourcebooks.

As ICE's writers developed a deeper understanding of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s invented languages, and responded to fan and editorial feedback, they frequently retrofitted earlier, linguistically inconsistent names with more accurate [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), [Adûnaic](/wiki/Adûnaic), or external language analogues. Real world analogues were carelessly chosen, or Quenya names were given to colonies of [Black Númenóreans](/wiki/Black_Númenóreans), who historically eschewed the Elven tongues in favour of Adûnaic.

When it came to correcting incorrect Quenya or Sindarin, or mixtures thereof, [Findegil](/wiki/Findegil) became the authors' mouthpiece. He was quoted in *[Hands of the Healer](/wiki/Hands_of_the_Healer)* and *[Southern Gondor: The Land](/wiki/Southern_Gondor:_The_Land)*, explaining faulty names for places in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) as corrupted forms by locals or overly fancy names by cartographers, while Quenya names for Black Númenórean colonies were contextualized as names used by Gondorians, with new Adûnaic originals being provided.

[Chris Seeman](/wiki/Chris_Seeman) and [Pete Fenlon](/wiki/Pete_Fenlon) found that the linguistic commentary made the newly corrected maps feel even more plausible, reflecting historical analogues.

> Reference: {{Cite web|articleurl=https://www.invasivedesigns.com/otherhands/archives/articles/12/fenlon.html|articlename=Mapping Middle-earth: An Interview with Pete Fenlon|author=Chris Seeman|website=[Other Hands 12](/wiki/Other_Hands_12)|dated=1996}}

In *[Arnor: The People](/wiki/Arnor:_The_People)*, the distinction between [Dunael](/wiki/Dunlendish) and [Blarm](/wiki/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing#Blarm) was established as each language being represented by Gaelic and [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh), respectively. Some remaining iconic yet discrepant names were explained as cross-cultural interaction or contamination between Dunlendings and [Hillmen](/wiki/Hillmen).

> Reference: {{ICE|2022}}, p. 8
