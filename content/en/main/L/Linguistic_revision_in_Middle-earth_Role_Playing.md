**Linguistic revision in [Middle-earth Role Playing](../M/Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md)** refers to the efforts of [Iron Crown Enterprises](../I/Iron_Crown_Enterprises.md) to correct or provide in-universe rationales for naming inconsistencies in their sourcebooks.

As ICE's writers developed a deeper understanding of [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s invented languages, and responded to fan and editorial feedback, they frequently retrofitted earlier, linguistically inconsistent names with more accurate [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md), [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md), [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md), or external language analogues. Real world analogues were carelessly chosen, or Quenya names were given to colonies of [Black Númenóreans](../B/Black_Númenóreans.md), who historically eschewed the Elven tongues in favour of Adûnaic.

When it came to correcting incorrect Quenya or Sindarin, or mixtures thereof, [Findegil](../F/Findegil.md) became the authors' mouthpiece. He was quoted in [Hands of the Healer](../H/Hands_of_the_Healer.md) and [Southern Gondor: The Land](../S/Southern_Gondor%253A_The_Land.md), explaining faulty names for places in [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md) as corrupted forms by locals or overly fancy names by cartographers, while Quenya names for Black Númenórean colonies were contextualized as names used by Gondorians, with new Adûnaic originals being provided.<sup id="cite_ref-annogwain_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annogwain-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-mengalen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mengalen-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rastrhaen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rastrhaen-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-healer_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healer-4">[4]</a></sup> [Chris Seeman](../C/Chris_Seeman.md) and [Pete Fenlon](../P/Pete_Fenlon.md) found that the linguistic commentary made the newly corrected maps feel even more plausible, reflecting historical analogues.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [Arnor: The People](../A/Arnor%253A_The_People.md), the distinction between [Dunael](../D/Dunlendish.md) and [Blarm](Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing.md#Blarm) was established as each language being represented by Gaelic and [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md), respectively. Some remaining iconic yet discrepant names were explained as cross-cultural interaction or contamination between Dunlendings and [Hillmen](../H/Hillmen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## List of revisions {#List_of_revisions}

The Black Númenórean revisions are:<sup id="cite_ref-healer_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healer-4">[4]</a></sup>

- [Mirëdor](../D/Dominions_of_the_Seven.md) to Mírenor (Q.) and Zimrathâni (A.)
- [Ciryatandor](../C/Ciryatanor.md) to Ciryatanor (Q.) and Anbalukkhôr (A.)
- [Hyarn](../K/Khâradûne.md) to Hyarnustar (Q.) and Khâradûne (A.)

The Gondorian revisions include:

- Annogwain (mixed) to Annon Wain<sup id="cite_ref-annogwain_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annogwain-1">[1]</a></sup>
- Aron Úvanimin (Q.) to Eryn-in-Úanhoth<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
- Cabed Arheledh to Imlad Arheledh<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
- Eärost (mixed) to Aerost<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
- Enqui (mixed) to Engui<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
- Men Iaur (mixed) to Men Galen<sup id="cite_ref-mengalen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mengalen-2">[2]</a></sup>
- Rast Hwarin (mixed) to Rast Rhaen<sup id="cite_ref-rastrhaen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rastrhaen-3">[3]</a></sup>
- Sárathondost (mixed) to Saerthondost<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
