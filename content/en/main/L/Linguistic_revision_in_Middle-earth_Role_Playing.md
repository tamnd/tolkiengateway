**Linguistic revision in [Middle-earth Role Playing](/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing)** refers to the efforts of [Iron Crown Enterprises](/wiki/Iron_Crown_Enterprises) to correct or provide in-universe rationales for naming inconsistencies in their sourcebooks.

As ICE's writers developed a deeper understanding of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s invented languages, and responded to fan and editorial feedback, they frequently retrofitted earlier, linguistically inconsistent names with more accurate [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya), [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic), or external language analogues. Real world analogues were carelessly chosen, or Quenya names were given to colonies of [Black Númenóreans](/wiki/Black_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), who historically eschewed the Elven tongues in favour of Adûnaic.

When it came to correcting incorrect Quenya or Sindarin, or mixtures thereof, [Findegil](/wiki/Findegil) became the authors' mouthpiece. He was quoted in [Hands of the Healer](/wiki/Hands_of_the_Healer) and [Southern Gondor: The Land](/wiki/Southern_Gondor:_The_Land), explaining faulty names for places in [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) as corrupted forms by locals or overly fancy names by cartographers, while Quenya names for Black Númenórean colonies were contextualized as names used by Gondorians, with new Adûnaic originals being provided.<sup id="cite_ref-annogwain_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annogwain-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-mengalen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mengalen-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rastrhaen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rastrhaen-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-healer_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healer-4">[4]</a></sup> [Chris Seeman](/wiki/Chris_Seeman) and [Pete Fenlon](/wiki/Pete_Fenlon) found that the linguistic commentary made the newly corrected maps feel even more plausible, reflecting historical analogues.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [Arnor: The People](/wiki/Arnor:_The_People), the distinction between [Dunael](/wiki/Dunlendish "Dunlendish") and [Blarm](/wiki/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing#Blarm "Languages in Middle-earth Role Playing") was established as each language being represented by Gaelic and [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh), respectively. Some remaining iconic yet discrepant names were explained as cross-cultural interaction or contamination between Dunlendings and [Hillmen](/wiki/Hillmen).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## List of revisions {#List_of_revisions}

The Black Númenórean revisions are:<sup id="cite_ref-healer_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healer-4">[4]</a></sup>

- [Mirëdor](/wiki/Dominions_of_the_Seven "Dominions of the Seven") to Mírenor (Q.) and Zimrathâni (A.)
- [Ciryatandor](/wiki/Ciryatanor "Ciryatanor") to Ciryatanor (Q.) and Anbalukkhôr (A.)
- [Hyarn](/wiki/Kh%C3%A2rad%C3%BBne "Khâradûne") to Hyarnustar (Q.) and Khâradûne (A.)

The Gondorian revisions include:

- Annogwain (mixed) to Annon Wain<sup id="cite_ref-annogwain_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-annogwain-1">[1]</a></sup>
- Aron Úvanimin (Q.) to Eryn-in-Úanhoth<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
- Cabed Arheledh to Imlad Arheledh<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
- Eärost (mixed) to Aerost<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
- Enqui (mixed) to Engui<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
- Men Iaur (mixed) to Men Galen<sup id="cite_ref-mengalen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mengalen-2">[2]</a></sup>
- Rast Hwarin (mixed) to Rast Rhaen<sup id="cite_ref-rastrhaen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rastrhaen-3">[3]</a></sup>
- Sárathondost (mixed) to Saerthondost<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
