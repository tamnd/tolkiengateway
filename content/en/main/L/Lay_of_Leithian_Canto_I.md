This first [Canto](/wiki/Cantos_of_the_Lay_of_the_Leithian "Cantos of the Lay of the Leithian") of the [Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian) tells of [Elu Thingol](/wiki/Thingol "Thingol"), and [Lúthien Tinúviel](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien "Lúthien") and [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath). It runs to 98 lines, the shortest canto save the last, unfinished one.

## Concerning the Canto {#Concerning_the_Canto}

This canto starts out with one of the more popular paragraphs, concerning Thingol.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>A king there was in days of old:<br/>
ere <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a> yet walked upon the mould...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 1-2</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395759">.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container{margin:1rem 0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider{margin:0}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-section-divider>span{line-height:0;font-size:2rem;margin-top:1rem}.mw-parser-output .tg-quote-container>.tg-quote{padding-left:0;border-left:none}</style>

The descriptions of jewels is contrasted with the love of his daughter, whose description soon follows.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>There beryl, pearl, and opal pale<br/>
and metal wrought like fishes&#39; mail<br/>
...<br/>
all these he had and loved them less<br/>
than a maiden once in Elfinesse;<br/>
for fairer than are born to Men<br/>
a daughter had he, Lúthien.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 15-6, 19-22</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Then it introduces [Endor](/wiki/Endor) and orients the reader.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>To North there lay the Land of Dread<br/>
whence only evil pathways led<br/>
...<br/>
to South the wide earth unexplored<br/>
to West the ancient <a href="/wiki/Belegaer" title="Belegaer">Ocean</a> roared,<br/>
unsailed and shoreless, wide and wild<br/>
to East in peaks of blue were piled...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 49-50, 55-58</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Note the reference below to fairies, not uncommon in older works. One thing about the Lay is that it commonly connects Beleriand and Faërie.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p><i><a href="/wiki/Esgalduin" title="Esgalduin">Esgalduin</a> that fairies call<br/></i>
in many a tall and torchlit hall
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 67-68</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Note also that the name of **Dairon** is spelt here with an "i", as opposed to **[Daeron](/wiki/Daeron)** of later works.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>When leaves were long and grass was green<br/>
then Dairon with his fingers lean,<br/>
as daylight melted into shade,<br/>
a wandering music sweetly made...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 79-82</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

It ends on a note of change, signifying the end of the introduction and the beginning of the tale.

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...until a day beneath the <a href="/wiki/Sun" title="Sun">sun</a>,<br/>
when many marvels were begun.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>vv. 97-98</cite></p>
</blockquote>
