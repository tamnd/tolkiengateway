<br> **Lórinand** was one of the many old names for the land usually called [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In a manuscript composed by [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") sometime after *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, *Lórinand* is said to be a [Nandorin](/wiki/Nandorin) name, meaning "valley of gold" (containing the "Elvish word meaning 'golden light'").<sup id="cite_ref-UTG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTG-1">[1]</a></sup>

However, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) notes that in a later manuscript the name *Lórinand* was reconceptualized as "a transformation, after the introduction of the [mallorns](/wiki/Mallorn "Mallorn"), of a yet older name *[Lindórinand](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien#Names "Lothlórien")*".<sup id="cite_ref-UTG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTG-1">[1]</a></sup>
