**Lithlad** was a plain in [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Lithlad_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lithlad-4">[4]</a></sup>

The mountain range of the [Ephel Duath](../E/Ephel_Duath.md) and the mountain range of the [Ered Lithui](../E/Ered_Lithui.md) formed a great wall around the mournful plains of Lithlad and [Gorgoroth](../P/Plateau_of_Gorgoroth.md) and the inland sea of [Nurnen](../N/Nurnen.md) in the middle.<sup id="cite_ref-Lithlad_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lithlad-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Lithlad* is a [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) name. It means "plain of ashes".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> It is a compound of *[lith](Lith.md)* ("ash") and *[lad](Lad.md)* ("plain").<sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The label "Lithlad" appears on the [First Map of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_First_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) south of the Ered Lithui and east of [Barad-dûr](../B/Barad-dûr.md). The mountain spur that branches off from the Ered Lithui to the east of Barad-dûr, longer than the short spur on whose end Barad-dûr is located, does not exist on the first map.<sup id="cite_ref-FirstMapWeb_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FirstMapWeb-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FirstMap_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FirstMap-2">[2]</a></sup> The label "Lithlad" appears on the [Second Map of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Second_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) in approximately the same location south of the Ered Lithui and east of Barad-dûr and the longer mountain spur.<sup id="cite_ref-SecondMap_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SecondMap-3">[3]</a></sup> The label "Lithlad" does not appear on the [Third Map of The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Third_Map_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) that was drawn by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> It also does not appear on either the [General Map of Middle-earth](../G/General_Map_of_Middle-earth.md) or [The West of Middle-earth at the End of the Third Age](../T/The_West_of_Middle-earth_at_the_End_of_the_Third_Age.md) maps that were drawn by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) and published with [The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md) and [Unfinished Tales of Númenor and Middle-earth](../U/Unfinished_Tales_of_Númenor_and_Middle-earth.md) respectively.

In draft 'A' of the chapter [The Tower of Cirith Ungol](../T/The_Tower_of_Cirith_Ungol.md) it is mentioned that the eastern face of the tower stood up from a shelf in the mountain wall with four pointed bastions with sides facing north-east and north-west and that the windows of the tower stared over the plains of Gorgoroth and Lithlad.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

[Wayne G. Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Christina Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) state that Lithlad is at the feet of the Ered Lithui.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup>

Mark Fisher places Lithlad between the mountain spur on whose end Barad-dûr is located and the longer mountain spur that branches off from the Ered Lithui to the east of Barad-dûr and incorrectly claims that this position is based on *The First Map of The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Although Karen Wynn Fonstad was aware of the location of Lithlad on *The First Map of The Lord of the Rings*, she places Lithlad on the north-western shore of the [Sea of Núrnen](../S/Sea_of_Núrnen.md) in [Nurn](../N/Nurn.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Fonstad_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fonstad-10">[10]</a></sup>

Robert Foster places Lithlad either in the southwest or the east of Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
