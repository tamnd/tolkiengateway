The **Lhûn**, or [Westronized](/wiki/Westron "Westron") **Lune**,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was a river in the north-west of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador). It gave its name to the [Gulf of Lune](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lune) and the [Mountains of Lune](/wiki/Blue_Mountains "Blue Mountains") (also known as the Blue Mountains). Of old, the Lhûn had been a line of defence: first against Sauron, and later against the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king).

## Geography {#Geography}

The Lhûn flowed from its source on the eastern side of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) near their northern end southward for about 400 miles into the [Gulf of Lune](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lune). Its first tributary, the [Little Lune](/wiki/Little_Lune)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> flowed from its source on the eastern side of the central part of the Blue Mountains eastward into the Lhûn about 150 miles downstream from the source of the Lhûn. Its secondary tributary, an unnamed river flowed from the western side of the central part of the [Emyn Uial](/wiki/Hills_of_Evendim "Hills of Evendim") southwestward into the Lhûn over 200 miles downstream from the source of the Lhûn.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

The course of the Lhûn during the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) is no longer recorded in history. In the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath) at the end of the First Age a great gap appeared between Blue Mountains towards their southern end and a gulf of the sea flowed into the gap. The river Lhûn fell by a new course into the gulf, which was since then called the [Gulf of Lhûn](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lune "Gulf of Lune").<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[5]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

After [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) landed with his ships in [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon)<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[5]</a></sup> in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3319](/wiki/Second_Age_3319 "Second Age 3319"), he travelled up the river Lhûn and established his [realm](/wiki/Arnor "Arnor")<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[5]</a></sup> of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) east of the river Lhûn<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>. His [people](/wiki/Arnorians "Arnorians") lived in many places in Eriador about the course of the Lhûn.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-5">[5]</a></sup>

During the [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron) [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) and [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) that had been stationed in [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) managed to hold the Lhûn against the forces of Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

During the [War with Angmar](/wiki/Angmar_War "Angmar War"), it formed the end of the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king)'s influence: many of the Dúnedain were driven by the forces of Angmar across the Lhûn.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> When Prince [Eärnur](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnur), the son of King [Eärnil II](/wiki/E%C3%A4rnil_II) of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) came with a fleet to the aid of Lindon and Arnor, the combined forces of Lindon, Arnor and Gondor crossed the Lune and defeated the forces of Angmar.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of *Lhûn* is not known. In connection with its first appearance, in a manuscript dating from ca. 1940, the translation "Blue River" is given.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VT48a5_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT48a5-11">[11]</a></sup> Apparently, Tolkien originally envisioned it as [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) for "blue" (cf. [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) *[luin](/wiki/Luin "Luin")*).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> *Lhûn*, and especially the initial */lh/*, may have been valid in Noldorin, it was not so in [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin), so Tolkien had to rewrite the etymology. He considered the following:<sup id="cite_ref-VT48a5_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VT48a5-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

- [CE](/wiki/Common_Eldarin "Common Eldarin") Slōna, "floody"
- CE Slōnā, "in flood, full of water" (during melting season)
- CE Slounā, "flow freely"
- CE Slōno, "deep of water, applied originally to the Gulf!"
- A renaming to *[Sîr](/wiki/S%C3%AEr) [Luin](/wiki/Luin)*, "Blue River"
- CE Slōn, "sound"
- A [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) origin, <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Sal%C3%B4n&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Salôn (page does not exist)">salôn</a></i> or <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Sul%C3%BBn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sulûn (page does not exist)">sulûn</a></i>, "fall, descend swiftly"

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[John Garth](/wiki/John_Garth) points out that both the river <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Lhûn</a> and the river <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Severn" title="wikipedia:River Severn">Severn</a> flow from mountains (the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) and the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_Mountains" title="wikipedia:Cambrian Mountains">Cambrian Mountains</a>, respectively) in a rough semicircle east, south, and then west into a wide gulf (the [Gulf of Lune](/wiki/Gulf_of_Lune)) and the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Channel" title="wikipedia:Bristol Channel">Bristol Channel</a>, respectively).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
