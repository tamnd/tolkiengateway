The **Lord of Moria** ([Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin): <i><b>Aran Moria</b></i>) is the title of the ruler of [Moria](/wiki/Moria) such as the [King of Khazad-dûm](/wiki/Kings_of_Khazad-d%C3%BBm "Kings of Khazad-dûm") who was typically the [King of Durin's Folk](/wiki/Kings_of_Durin%27s_Folk "Kings of Durin's Folk").

The title *Aran Moria*, translated as **Lord of Moria**, refers to [Durin III](/wiki/Durin_III) and appears in the [Doors of Durin](/wiki/Doors_of_Durin).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The kingdom had been abandoned in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1980](/wiki/Third_Age_1980 "Third Age 1980") but [Balin](/wiki/Balin), a [Dwarf](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") of the [House of Durin](/wiki/House_of_Durin), led [a small colony](/wiki/Balin%27s_Colony "Balin's Colony") to reclaim it. His rule lasted only briefly ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2989](/wiki/Third_Age_2989 "Third Age 2989") - [2995](/wiki/Third_Age_2995 "Third Age 2995")) until his death. The [Khuzdul](/wiki/Khuzdul) phrase <i><b>Uzbad Khazaddûmu</b></i> meaning **Lord of Moria** was inscribed on [his tomb](/wiki/Balin%27s_Tomb "Balin's Tomb").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The phrase is translated as "Lord of Moria"; *uzbad* seems to be translated as "Lord", however since Khuzdul words of three consonants normally don't begin with a vowel, [Magnus Åberg](/wiki/Magnus_%C3%85berg) proposes that the word is enclitic, and \**u-* is actually a prefix meaning "and".<sup id="cite_ref-magnus_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnus-3">[3]</a></sup>

If this is so, *uzbad* means "...and lord", the full phrase being "...son of [Fundin](/wiki/Fundin) *and* lord...". Åberg suggests that the basic form of "Lord" would be \**zâbad* and "Lord of Moria" would be \**zəbad Khazaddûmu*.<sup id="cite_ref-magnus_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnus-3">[3]</a></sup>

The ending <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=-u&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="-u (page does not exist)">-u</a></i> in *Khazaddûmu* is probably "an ending that gives the noun an objective or locative meaning".<sup id="cite_ref-magnus_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnus-3">[3]</a></sup>
