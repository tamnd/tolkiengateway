According to [Pengolodh](../P/Pengolodh.md), the sage of [Gondolin](../G/Gondolin.md), the making of a **language** is the chief character of an [Incarnate](../I/Incarnate.md). The speaking peoples used languages to communicate. The first of those races were the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) who were taught by [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md) and then the [Elves](../E/Elves.md), who called themselves [Kwendî](../Q/Quendi.md), the "Speakers".

In the history of [Arda](../A/Arda.md), the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) tongues were separated as part of the peoples emigrating while others stay behind, leading to a split of [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) (High-Elvish, or Elf-Latin) and [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md).

## Linguistic lore {#Linguistic_lore}

In Quenya, *[lambë](Lambë.md)* is the term for spoken language or verbal communiation while *tengwesta* is a more abstract term for a system or a code of signs and may be translated as "grammar". As [Pengolodh](../P/Pengolodh.md) noted, "The making of a lambe is the chief character of an Incarnate," including the [Valar](../V/Valar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md), the Elves began to be interested in the nature of their languages. These loremasters were called [lambengolmor](Lambengolmor.md). Usually they would compare [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) proper against [Telerin](../T/Telerin.md) and point out the original [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md).

The invention of writing is attributed to [Rúmil](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Tirion).md>), who first invented an alphabet: the [Sarati](../S/Sarati.md) (literally "letters"). [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) later enhanced and further developed this alphabet into the [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md), which were spread to Middle-earth by the [Ñoldor](../N/Noldor.md) and remained in use ever after.

[Daeron](../D/Daeron.md) of [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md) indepently of Rúmil and Fëanor had invented the [Cirth](../C/Cirth.md) [Runes](../R/Runes.md), but these were only used for inscriptions, and otherwise replaced by the Tengwar, except among the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md).

An important source of Middle-earth linguistic scholarship is [Pengolodh](../P/Pengolodh.md) of Gondolin who wrote in Quenya. He is the author of *[Quendi and Eldar](../Q/Quendi_and_Eldar.md)*, the *[Lhammas](Lhammas.md)* and *[Ósanwe-kenta](../_/Ósanwe-kenta.md)*.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Tolkien's conceptions of the languages, like his mythology itself, changed through time. For older linguistic concepts see [The Tree of Tongues](../T/The_Tree_of_Tongues.md), [Oromean](../O/Oromean.md), [Aulean](../A/Aulean.md), [Goldogrin](../G/Goldogrin.md), [Kornoldorin](../K/Kornoldorin.md), [Noldorin](../N/Noldorin.md), [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md), [Ilkorin](../I/Ilkorin.md), [Doriathrin](../D/Doriathrin.md), [Nandorin](../N/Nandorin.md) and [Taliska](../T/Taliska.md).

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>What I think is a primary &#39;fact&#39; about my work, that it is all of a piece, and fundamentally linguistic in inspiration. [...] The invention of languages is the foundation. The &#39;stories&#39; were made rather to provide a world for the languages than the reverse. To me a name comes first and the story follows [...] It is to me, anyway,  largely an essay in &#39;linguistic aesthetic&#39;, as I sometimes say to people who ask me &#39;what is it all about&#39;.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Language-making was Tolkien's hobby for most of his life. He is known to have constructed his first languages ([Animalic](../A/Animalic.md) and [Nevbosh](../N/Nevbosh.md)) at a little over thirteen and he continued to ponder upon his creations up until his death more than sixty-five years later. Language invention had always been tightly connected to the mythology that Tolkien developed, as he found that a language could not be complete without the history of the people who spoke it, just as these people could never be fully realistic if imagined only through the English and as speaking English. Tolkien therefore took the stance of a translator and adaptor rather than that of the original author of his works.

Tolkien was a professional philologist and a specialist in the Old English language. He was also interested in many languages outside his field and developed a particular love for the Finnish language (he described the finding of a Finnish grammar book as "entering a complete wine-cellar filled with bottles of an amazing wine of a kind and flavour never tasted before".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Finnish morphology (particularly its rich system of inflection) in part gave rise to [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md). Another of Tolkien's favorites was [Welsh](../W/Welsh.md) — and features of Welsh phonology found their way to [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md). Numerous words were borrowed from existing languages, but less and less obviously as Tolkien progressed. Words that are an exact match with existing Welsh words can be found in the early drafts of Tolkien’s manuscripts published as *[The History of Middle-earth](../T/The_History_of_Middle-earth.md)*,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> but attempts to match a source to a particular Elvish word or name in works published during his lifetime are often very dubious.

In discussing the languages [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) invented, it is necessary to consider two aspects: their "primary world" history, namely their literal development by Tolkien as a linguist, and their "[secondary world](../S/Secondary_world.md)" history, namely their imagined historical development in the history of Middle-earth.

Although the [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) languages [Sindarin](../S/Sindarin.md) and [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) are the most famous and the most mature languages of those that Tolkien invented for his mythology, they are by no means the only ones. They belong to a family of Elvish dialects, that originate in [Common Eldarin](../C/Common_Eldarin.md), the language common to all [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md), which in turn originates in [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md), the common root of [Eldarin](../E/Eldar.md) and [Avarin](../A/Avari.md) languages. In addition to that, there is a separate language family that is spoken by Men, the most prominent member of which was [Westron](../W/Westron.md) (derived from the [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) speech [Adûnaic](../A/Adûnaic.md)) or the "Common speech" of the peoples of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*. Most Mannish tongues showed influences by Elvish, as well as some [Dwarvish](../D/Dwarvish.md) influences. Several independent languages were drafted as well, for example the [Khuzdul](../K/Khuzdul.md) language of the Dwarves. Other languages are [Valarin](../V/Valarin.md) (the tongue of the [Valar](../V/Valar.md)), and the [Black Speech](../B/Black_Speech.md) created by [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md) during the Second Age.

## Elvish scholarship {#Elvish_scholarship}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Indeed it would be the only fitting monument to a man who had to invent a whole world just to have a place where people could greet one another with the words <i><a href="../E/Elen_síla_lúmenn&#39;_omentielvo.md" title="Elen síla lúmenn&#39; omentielvo">Elen síla lúmenn&#39; omentielvo</a></i>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="../H/Helge_Fauskanger.md" title="Helge Fauskanger">Helge Fauskanger</a>, <a class="external text" href="http://folk.uib.no/hnohf/vice.htm" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Tolkien&#39;s Not-So-Secret Vice</a></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Although the study of Tolkien's languages is as a rule not taken seriously by mainstream linguistics, a number of serious scholars have worked on compiling all that can be recovered about their histories and grammars.

An early book dedicated to Tolkien's languages is *[An Introduction to Elvish](../A/An_Introduction_to_Elvish.md)* by [Jim Allan](../J/Jim_Allan.md) (published by Bran's Head Books), written before the publication of *[The Silmarillion](../T/The_Silmarillion.md)* in 1977 and therefore mostly outdated.

There are several journals dedicated to the subject:

- *[Quettar](../Q/Quettar.md)*, was the Bulletin of the Linguistic Fellowship of [The Tolkien Society](../T/The_Tolkien_Society.md), the oldest Eldarin publication.
- *[Vinyar Tengwar](../V/Vinyar_Tengwar.md)* and *[Parma Eldalamberon](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon.md)* are published by the [Elvish Linguistic Fellowship](../E/Elvish_Linguistic_Fellowship.md).
- *[Tyalië Tyelelliéva](../T/Tyalië_Tyelelliéva.md)* was published by [Lisa Star](Lisa_Star.md).
- *[Mythlore](../M/Mythlore.md)* is published by the [Mythopoeic Society](../M/Mythopoeic_Society.md).
- *[Beyond Bree](../B/Beyond_Bree.md)* is published by [Nancy Martsch](../N/Nancy_Martsch.md).
- *[Tengwestië](<../T/Tengwestië_(journal).md>)* published via online by the Elvish Linguistic Fellowship.

With the rise of the Internet, mailing lists dedicated to Tolkien linguistics spread, being the most famous [Tolklang](../T/Tolklang.md), [Elfling](../E/Elfling.md) and [Lambengolmor](<Lambengolmor_(mailing_list).md>). Many scholars and editors were involved there, but they are currently dissolved. Spiritual heirs are the Discord server <a class="external text" href="http://discord.gg/Dhmyzrf" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Vinyë Lambengolmor</a> and websites like <a class="external text" href="http://www.elfdict.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Parf Edhellen</a> and <a class="external text" href="http://eldamo.org/index.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Eldamo</a>.
