**Lámatyávë** (pl. **lámatyáver**) is a noun in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) which refers to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonaesthetics" title="wikipedia:Phonaesthetics">phonaesthesia</a> of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves). It means "sound-taste" and refers to individual pleasure in the sounds and forms of words.

Lámatyávë was practiced when Elves [named their children](/wiki/Ess%C3%AB "Essë").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

Sound-taste was an important factor in [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien)'s created names and languages and by extension, his [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"). His first contact with [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) "pierced his linguistic heart"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [Finnish](/wiki/Finnish) language was to him like "bottles of an amazing wine of a kind and flavour never tasted before" that "quite intoxicated" him.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> These two languages have been the basis for his major [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) languages, [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya).

Throughout his work, Tolkien emphasizes on how the [Elven languages](/wiki/Elvish "Elvish") are heard as beautiful by the characters; he intended them to be particularly "pleasant".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
