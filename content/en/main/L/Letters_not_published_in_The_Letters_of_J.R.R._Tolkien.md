### Correspondence between Tolkien and R.W. Burchfield {#Correspondence_between_Tolkien_and_R.W._Burchfield}

During the years [1953](/wiki/1953)-[72](/wiki/1972 "1972"), Tolkien corresponded with <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=R.W._Burchfield&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="R.W. Burchfield (page does not exist)">R.W. Burchfield</a>. The collection of letters is kept at the [Bodleian Library](/wiki/Bodleian_Library):<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Correspondence between J.R.R. Tolkien and R.W. Burchfield, 1953-72. / <b>Shelfmark:</b> MS. Eng. c. 7284, fols. 1-16. / <b>Extent:</b> 16 leaves. / Comprises ten manuscript, and typescript letters, and one copy letter from Tolkien to Burchfield, relating to Burchfield&#39;s attendance at tutorials, his search for housing in Oxford, his application for funding for his research, his search for an academic post, his congratulations on the publication of The Fellowship of the Ring, an extension for his work on &#39;Ormulum&#39;, and Tolkien&#39;s edition of Ancrene Wisse: The English Text of the Ancrene Riwle, and one letter from Burchfield to Tolkien relating to the printing of Ancrene Wisse. Also a description of the letters by Elizabeth Burchfield, widow of R.W. Burchfield. / <b>Acquisition:</b> Bequest of R.W. Burchfield, per Elizabeth Burchfield, Dec. 2004.
</p>
</blockquote>

### Correspondence between Tolkien and Pablo Martínez del Río {#Correspondence_between_Tolkien_and_Pablo_Martínez_del_Río}

[José Manuel Ferrández Bru](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Ferr%C3%A1ndez_Bru) has noted that a collection of Tolkien's letters to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADnez_del_R%C3%ADo" title="wikipedia:Martínez del Río">Pablo Martínez del Río</a>, whose younger cousins Tolkien took to vacation in Paris in [1913](/wiki/1913), has been donated to the <a class="external text" href="http://www.cehm.com.mx/BaseCatalogo/catalogo.htm" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Centro de Estudios de Historia de México of Condumex</a>. As of 2011, Ferrández Bru wrote that Tolkien's letters unfortunately "are mixed with huge numbers of other documents" and that it will take years before they have been catalogued and available for research.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

### Correspondence between Tolkien and Sterling E. Lanier {#Correspondence_between_Tolkien_and_Sterling_E._Lanier}

In an article in [*Locus* #149](/wiki/Locus_149 "Locus 149") ([1973](/wiki/1973)), the science fiction author <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_E._Lanier" title="wikipedia:Sterling E. <p>His last great legacy to the world, the <i>Silmarillion</i>, has been saved. He wrote me years ago, that it was done in verse! </p>
</blockquote>

### Correspondence between Tolkien and Joni Mitchell {#Correspondence_between_Tolkien_and_Joni_Mitchell}

In the mid-sixties, singer-songwriter <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell" title="wikipedia:Joni Mitchell">Joni Mitchell</a> became a "[fan](/wiki/Tolkien_fandom "Tolkien fandom")" of Tolkien,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> after being introduced to his works by her husband Chuck. The couple set up a publishing and recording business for their material related to Middle-earth, and they wrote Tolkien "letters and got his permission to use names from his books for our bits of business". The correspdondence between Tolkien and Joni and Chuck Mitchell apparently took place no sooner than [1965](/wiki/1965) and not later than [1969](/wiki/1969).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> It is unknown if Tolkien answered their letters in person or if the replies were written by a representative. A letter of admiration from Joni Mitchell to Tolkien will be published in *[Tolkien: Maker of Middle-earth](/wiki/Tolkien:_Maker_of_Middle-earth)* ([2018](/wiki/2018)).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

### Letters to The Society {#Letters_to_The_Society}

<h3><span id="Letters_to_Simonne_d.27Ardenne"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Letters_to_Simonne_d&#39;Ardenne">Letters to Simonne d&#39;Ardenne</span></h3>

[John](/wiki/John_Tolkien "John Tolkien") and [Priscilla Tolkien](/wiki/Priscilla_Tolkien) mention briefly in *[The Tolkien Family Album](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Family_Album)* that [Simonne d'Ardenne](/wiki/Simonne_d%27Ardenne): *"was especially close to Priscilla, up until her death in 1986. She entrusted to Priscilla a great bundle of letters she had received from J.R.R.T. over a period of forty years."* (p. 68). This large collection of letters seems never to have reached a publication.

### Letters to the Hogans {#Letters_to_the_Hogans}

In the article "Tolkien's time in 'Erin'" (in anticipation of the publication of the Irish translation of *The Hobbit*), published in *The Irish Times* on 17 March 2012, Dr. Tom Hogan mentions that Tolkien:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>stayed many times in our house on visits to Ireland. These visits started in the 1950s. He came to Ireland for a couple of weeks each year acting as an external examiner of English in UCD. He worked alongside my late father Jeremiah who was professor of English there at the time. A number of letters from Tolkien to us are in my possession.
</p>
</blockquote>

No dates of individual letters are provided, but Hogan quotes from several letters in the article.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

### The Tea Towel Letter {#The_Tea_Towel_Letter}

The web site *Tolkien Collector's Guide* has an article (on a rare tea towel) with a reference from a supposedly unpublished letter. The background is that George Allen and Unwin in 1971 commissioned the towel, featuring a map by Pauline Baynes, on the occasion of a celebration. The author of the article says: "\[...\] it is clear Tolkien was there, since I read in an (unpublished) letter he himself had received such a teatowel at the 'party' or 'meeting' and was very happy with it."<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

### Wheelbarrows at Dawn {#Wheelbarrows_at_Dawn}

Similarly to what was done in the *[Black &amp; White Ogre Country: The Lost Tales of Hilary Tolkien](/wiki/Black_%26_White_Ogre_Country "Black & White Ogre Country")*, glimpses of the correspondence between J.R.R. Tolkien and his brother [Hilary](/wiki/Hilary_Tolkien "Hilary Tolkien") were supposed to be published in late 2010 in *[Wheelbarrows at Dawn: Memories of Hilary Tolkien](/wiki/Wheelbarrows_at_Dawn "Wheelbarrows at Dawn")*. However, the publication of the latter book was cancelled, and the letters remain unpublished (as of November 2010).

In early 2015, Hilary Tolkien's "archive of family letters, family photographs &amp; ephemera" was put up for sale, "including 15 unseen long letters by JRR Tolkien himself".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup>

### Dr. Higgins and possible eBay frauds {#Dr._Higgins_and_possible_eBay_frauds}

The forum of the website *Tolkien Collector's Guide* contains discussions (in which [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) have taken part) on a circulation on eBay of fraudulent Tolkien letters. Among these are said to be letters adressed to a "Dr. Higgins". Many of these contested letters are typewritten, with a signature by J.R.R. Tolkien in blue ink. The forger, Alan Formhals, was found guilty at Southampton Crown Court in 2012.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Alan_Formhals_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alan_Formhals-19">[19]</a></sup>
