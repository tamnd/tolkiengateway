**Lēle** is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word meaning "to will with conscious purpose, immediate or remote".<sup id="cite_ref-nm_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nm-1">[1]</a></sup>

One of it's verbal form is *lelya*, which means "go, proceed (in any direction), travel", with a past tense of *lende*.<sup id="cite_ref-wj_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wj-2">[2]</a></sup> Another verbal form is *lelinye*.<sup id="cite_ref-nm_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nm-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is distinct from "to be willing, to assent, consent, agree". For example, though a man could say “I agree, I will” to some proposition of another, it may or may not align with his own purpose or design.<sup id="cite_ref-nm_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nm-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It is derived from the [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) [root](../R/Root.md) <b><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=DEL&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="DEL (page does not exist)">DEL</a></b> meaning “walk, go, proceed, travel”.<sup id="cite_ref-wj_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wj-2">[2]</a></sup>

In Quenya there was an early change in of initial **d** &gt; **l** which was regular in both [Vanyarin](../V/Vanyarin.md) and [Ñoldorin](../_/Ñoldorin.md) dialects. Further, <b>*del-</b> seems to have become <b>*led</b>, by dissimilation. The past form clearly shows "\*led", while *lelya* may also be derived from <i>*ledja</i>, since **dj** became **ly** medially in Quenya.<sup id="cite_ref-wj_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wj-2">[2]</a></sup>
