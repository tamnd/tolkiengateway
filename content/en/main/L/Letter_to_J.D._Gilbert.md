On [17 April](../_/17_April.md) [1967](../_/1967.md), [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) wrote **[a letter](Letters_not_published_in_The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md) to J.D. Gilbert**.<sup id="cite_ref-C_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-C-1">[1]</a></sup>

- **Subject:** Tolkien suggests Gilbert to read *[The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth](../T/The_Homecoming_of_Beorhtnoth.md)*, commenting that "*I think one of the reasons why books read in childhood lose their charm later is that many of them are very ill-written, and this fault is not (consciously) noted by the uncritical and inexperienced reader*". Tolkien also explains that he is "*under contract to publish the legends and stories of the eras before the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md)*" but that he is finding that time is going too quickly.
- **Description:** Typed letter signed with autograph additions in black fountain pen ink (five words), on his Professor J.R.R. Tolkien letterhead, Oxford, 76 Sandfield Road, April 17, 1967. 7" x 9", 1 page (recto only).
- **Publication:** A description of the letter was published in [*The J.R.R. Tolkien Companion and Guide: Chronology*](../T/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Companion_and_Guide.md) (p. 696).
