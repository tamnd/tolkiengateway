**Lothlórien** is the sixth chapter of the second book in *[The Fellowship of the Ring](../T/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md)*. The main characters are the remaining Company that comprised the [Fellowship of the Ring](../F/Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md) after the fall of [Gandalf](../G/Gandalf.md): [Frodo Baggins](../F/Frodo_Baggins.md), [Samwise Gamgee](../S/Samwise_Gamgee.md), [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md), [Legolas](Legolas.md), [Gimli](../G/Gimli.md), [Peregrin Took](../P/Peregrin_Took.md), [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md), and [Boromir](../B/Boromir.md). The chapter introduces the [Elven](../E/Elven.md) realm of [Lothlórien](Lothlórien.md) and its people, the [Galadhrim](../G/Galadhrim.md).

Central to the chapter is the Company's passage into Lothlórien - the Golden Wood - revealing the contrast between the peril of Moria and the serenity, antiquity, and guarded power of the Elves. The chapter begins to explore shifting dynamics within the Fellowship, as grief over Gandalf’s fall deepens and the members respond differently to the wonder of Lórien.

## Summary {#Summary}

The chapter begins immediately following the end of the [previous chapter](../A/A_Journey_in_the_Dark.md), with the Fellowship stood in the [Dimrill Dale](../D/Dimrill_Dale.md).

With Gandalf gone, Aragorn assumed leadership of the Company, urging them onward despite their sorrow. On their way, the Company came to [Durin's Stone](../D/Durin's_Stone.md), and Gimli was moved to take Frodo (followed by Sam) to look into the waters of [Mirrormere](../M/Mirrormere.md).

The Company continued toward Lothlórien, land of the [Silvan Elves](../S/Silvan_Elves.md). Sam and Frodo, who had been wounded from the battle in [Moria](../M/Moria.md), were carried on the backs of [Boromir](../B/Boromir.md) and [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md). The Company rested for a while and when Aragorn examined Sam's wound, he found that it would heal quickly. But when he examined Frodo, Aragorn found his wounds had been minimized by [Bilbo's](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) hidden [Mithril-coat](../M/Mithril-coat.md).

As the Company approached the Golden Wood, Frodo stated to Gimli he felt he had heard the sound of feet and the reflections of eyes, not for the first time since leaving Moria. Later, Boromir voiced his unease about entering the land of the Galadhrim, while Legolas and Aragorn offered reassurance.

At the forest’s edge, the Company rested and was there met by [Haldir](../H/Haldir.md) and his brothers [Orophin](../O/Orophin.md) and [Rúmil](<../R/Rúmil_(elf_of_Lórien).md>), who invited Legolas and Frodo to climb into the trees and join them on a [flet](../T/Telain.md) to discuss what would happen next. The Elves decided that Gimli could pass through the forest if blindfolded, since the Elves of Lothlórien did not trust [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) or permit them in their lands.

The rest of the Company climbed up into the trees and spent the night there with the three Silvan ELves. Late at night, a company of [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md) passed by, as well as [Gollum](../G/Gollum.md), who was searching for the [Ring](../O/One_Ring.md).

The next day, Aragorn decided that if Gimli must be blindfolded, they should all proceed with blindfolds, against [Legolas](Legolas.md)'s protest. Later in the day, a message came from the [Lord and Lady of the Galadhrim](Lord_and_Lady_of_Galadhrim.md), welcoming them all, and the blindfolds were removed.

As they travelled deeper into Lórien, the Company marvelled at the beauty of the [mallorn](../M/Mallorn.md) forest, and Frodo sensed that time seemed to flow differently in the Golden Wood, the heart of [Elvendom](../E/Elvendom.md).

## Composition {#Composition}

The chapters taking place in Lórien were first written probably towards the end of [1941](../_/1941.md). During this stage, and while writing this/these chapter(s), Tolkien decided to change Aragorn/[Trotter](../T/Trotter.md)'s name, replacing it with *[Elfstone](../E/Elfstone.md)*, and temporarily *[Ingold](../I/Ingold.md)* before choosing *Elfstone* again.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> <span></span>
