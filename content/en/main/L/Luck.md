**Luck** is a concept recognizing success or failure apparently brought about by chance rather than through direct action.

<h2><span id="Usage_in_Tolkien.27s_legendarium"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Usage_in_Tolkien&#39;s_legendarium">Usage in Tolkien&#39;s legendarium</span></h2>

[Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md) was born with a fair share of it, far exceeding the usual allowance, as [Thorin](../T/Thorin.md) remarked.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

[Elrond](../E/Elrond.md) noted that he did not think it was by chance that many strangers came at the same time to his [council](../C/Council_of_Elrond.md), but "rather that it is so ordered that we, who sit here, and none others, must now find counsel for the peril of the world."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien scholar Joseph Pierce, referencing Tolkien's [Catholicism](../C/Christianity.md), argues that "luck" is a euphemism for "a supernatural dimension to the unfolding of events in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), in which Tolkien shows the mystical balance that exists between the promptings of grace or of demonic temptation and the response of the will to such promptings and temptations. This mystical relationship plays itself out in the form of transcendent Providence, which is much more than 'luck' or chance."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
