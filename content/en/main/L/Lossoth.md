The **Lossoth**, also called the **Snowmen of Forochel**,<sup id="cite_ref-SApp_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SApp-1">[1]</a></sup> were [Men](/wiki/Men), remnant of the [Forodwaith](</wiki/Forodwaith_(people)> "Forodwaith (people)"), the ancient people from the northern lands of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).

## History {#History}

The Lossoth are a remnant of the ancient people of Forodwaith, about whom not much can be known. It can be assumed that the Lossoth flourished well outside the Númenórean sphere of influence, and they suffered from [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar), causing their remnants to retreat.<sup id="cite_ref-lalaith_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lalaith-2">[2]</a></sup> They were afraid of the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king), and they believed he could control elements like frost and thaw. At some point, the Lossoth had retreated to the [Cape of Forochel](/wiki/Forochel "Forochel"), inaccessible to hostile peoples who harassed them.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup>

They entered history near the end of the second millennium of the Third Age, when [Arvedui](/wiki/Arvedui), last [King of Arthedain](/wiki/King_of_Arthedain), fled into the north to escape his kingdom's destruction and the Lossoth gave him their aid. In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1975](/wiki/Third_Age_1975 "Third Age 1975"),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> when an elven-ship came to rescue him, the exiled King rewarded the Lossoth for their friendship with the [Ring of Barahir](/wiki/Ring_of_Barahir). His hosts sensed approaching disaster, and warned Arvedui not to board the ship. Against their advice he did so, and indeed a great storm overwhelmed the vessel and the last King of Arthedain was lost in the cold waters of the far north.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup>

In the later Third Age, the [Rangers of the North](/wiki/Rangers_of_the_North) learned from the Lossoth the fate of the ship, and also paid to buy back the Ring of Barahir.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Culture {#Culture}

The Lossoth lived mainly on the Cape of Forochel that enclosed the [Icebay of Forochel](/wiki/Icebay_of_Forochel), but in winter the Bay was frozen over, and they could camp around its southern rim at the feet of the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains). They had a culture well adapted to their icy existence, constructing houses in the snow (possibly similar to igloos), and travelling on sleds and bone skates. Despite this comfortable existence, the Lossoth had a very primitive culture. They were afraid of the weapons that Arvedui and his men had, did not value jewels and could not understand sailing boats.<sup id="cite_ref-Eriador_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eriador-3">[3]</a></sup>

An isolated and unfriendly people, they must have had some limited contact with other peoples of the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands), as they apparently knew some [Westron](/wiki/Westron).<sup id="cite_ref-lalaith_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lalaith-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Loss(h)oth* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) and consists of *[loss](/wiki/Loss "Loss")* ("snow") + *[hoth](/wiki/Hoth "Hoth")*.<sup id="cite_ref-SApp_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SApp-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Andreas Moehn](/wiki/Andreas_Moehn) suggests that the word is a collective name, from an outsider's point of view, without knowledge of any tribes or variance of cultures. It seems to be derogatory, as highlighted by the (usually pejorative) ending *hoth*.<sup id="cite_ref-SApp_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SApp-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lalaith_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lalaith-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

<b>1997: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a>—<a href="/wiki/The_Northern_Waste" title="The Northern Waste">The Northern Waste</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>The supplement details the <i>Ystävät Talven</i> (<a href="/wiki/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing#Labba" title="Languages in Middle-earth Role Playing">L.</a> “Friends of Winter”) around <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1640" title="Third Age 1640">1640</a>, a related group of northern cultures comprising the <i>Lumimiehet</i>, <i>Jäämiehet</i>, and <i>Merimetsästäjät</i>. The <i>Lumimiehet</i> were the original bearers of the name Lossoth, though outsiders sometimes used it for the other peoples as well. The <i>Lumimiehet</i> were primarily nomadic hunters, the <i>Jäämiehet</i> inhabited the Cape of Forochel and combined tundra hunting with fishing and whaling, while the <i>Merimetsästäjät</i>, who had diverged from the <i>Jäämiehet</i>, lived in permanent settlements carved into the icebergs of the bay.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The <i>Ystävät Talven</i> spoke <a href="/wiki/Languages_in_Middle-earth_Role_Playing#Labba" title="Languages in Middle-earth Role Playing">Labba</a>. Their inherited lore was principally oral, though they also had a runic tradition known as <i>riimut</i>. Society was strongly based upon extended kinship, with <i>perheet</i> (families), <i>suku</i> (septs), and <i>heimot</i> (clans), lacking central rulers. Disputes were instead settled through custom, contests, or the <i>laulutautelu</i> (&#34;song duel&#34;).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2008: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online:_Shadows_of_Angmar" title="The Lord of the Rings Online: Shadows of Angmar">The Lord of the Rings Online: Shadows of Angmar</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>The Lossoth appear in the zone of the Ice-Bay of Forochel introduced in April <a href="/wiki/2008" title="2008">2008</a>. The Lossoth skate on ice and push sledges, and have the ability to tame and ride to war on mammoths. Many aspects of their language, culture, and appearance seem to be derived from or inspired by that of the Finnish, Sami, and Inuit peoples. They call themselves the <i>Lumi-väki</i>. Their chieftain is Yrjänä, who rules from the Great Lodge of Sûri-kylä.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup></dd></dl>
