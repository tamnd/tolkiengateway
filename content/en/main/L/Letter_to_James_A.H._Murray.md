On [5 June](/wiki/5_June) [1973](/wiki/1973), [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote **[a letter](/wiki/Letters_not_published_in_The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien "Letters not published in The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien") to James A.H. Murray** (grandson of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Murray_(lexicographer)" title="wikipedia:James Murray (lexicographer)">Sir James A.H. Murray</a> of the *[Oxford English Dictionary](/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary)*).<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup>

- **Description:** 2 pages, Merton College Oxford (on headed paper of 19 Lakeside Road, Dorset), headed "Private and confidential".<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **Publication:** *Sotheby's English Literature, History, Private Press &amp; Children's Books 12 December 2002* (p. 239).<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Excerpt {#Excerpt}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:James_A.H._Murray_5_June_1973_(2).jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/b/ba/James_A.H._Murray_5_June_1973_%282%29.jpg/300px-James_A.H._Murray_5_June_1973_%282%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="370" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/b/ba/James_A.H._Murray_5_June_1973_%282%29.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="304" data-file-height="375"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

"*if you would like to visit me in my guarded rooms in 21 Merton Street... I should love to see you*" and notes "*...I could show you some pictures &amp; a tapestry associated with 'The Lord of the Rings' and some other things which might interest you...*". Tolkien explains that his rooms in Merton Street are guarded since "*...it was arranged that my living there was not to be known; but since it became known, I have been assailed by hosts of people, and, worse, have been invaded by criminals, so that I live behind locked doors, and under the eye of the local C.I.D. Which all sounds romantic, but is in fact an infernal nuisance. But I should of course arrange for you to pass the barrier ... the college to protect me!*"<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">[2]</a></sup>
