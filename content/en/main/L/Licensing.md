This is the timeline of the history of the adaptations, in copyright level, generally, of works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), and specifically, of two of his books, *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* and *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*:

## History {#History}

- **[1936](/wiki/1936)**
    - [Stanley Unwin](/wiki/Stanley_Unwin) mails [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) a signed duplicate contract for *The Hobbit* to be published.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
- **[1958](/wiki/1958)**
    - [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) and [Rayner Unwin](/wiki/Rayner_Unwin) agree to grant American businessmen, Forrest J. Ackerman and Morton Grady Zimmerman an option on the film rights to *The Lord of the Rings* for six months to allow an agreeable script to be developed. A script was not agreed upon and the option expired in early 1959.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **[1962](/wiki/1962)**
    - J.R.R. Tolkien and Allen &amp; Unwin enter a contract with [William Lawrence Snyder](/wiki/William_Lawrence_Snyder) of <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Rembrandt_Films&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Rembrandt Films (page does not exist)">Rembrandt Films</a> to make a film adaptation of *The Hobbit*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
- **[1967](/wiki/1967)**
    - In a last minute attempt to avoid Snyder's licensing rights to expire, *[The Hobbit](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(1967_film)> "The Hobbit (1967 film)")* short film is released.
- **[1969](/wiki/1969)**
    - [United Artists](/wiki/United_Artists) acquires the film, television, stage, and merchandising rights to *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings* from author J.R.R. Tolkien, who retains book publishing rights, as well as television rights.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **[1976](/wiki/1976)**
    - The [Saul Zaentz](/wiki/Saul_Zaentz) Production Company acquires film and other rights for *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings* from United Artists.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **[1977](/wiki/1977)**
    - The Saul Zaentz Company forms a d/b/a [Tolkien Enterprises](/wiki/Tolkien_Enterprises).<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
    - The first animated film by [Rankin/Bass](/wiki/Rankin/Bass), *[The Hobbit](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(1977_film)> "The Hobbit (1977 film)")* is released.
- **[1978](/wiki/1978)**
    - Saul Zaentz produces the animated film *[The Lord of the Rings](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)> "The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)")*, directed by [Ralph Bakshi](/wiki/Ralph_Bakshi).
- **[1980](/wiki/1980)**
    - The second and the last animated film by [Rankin/Bass](/wiki/Rankin/Bass), *[The Return of the King](</wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)> "The Return of the King (1980 film)")* is released.
- **[1981](/wiki/1981)**
    - [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](/wiki/MGM "MGM") (MGM) acquires United Artists and through this way it obtains distribution rights to film adaptations of *The Hobbit*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
- **[1982](/wiki/1982)**
    - [Melbourne House](/wiki/Melbourne_House) publishes the first video game based on works by Tolkien, *[The Hobbit](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(1982_video_game)> "The Hobbit (1982 video game)")*.
- **[1986](/wiki/1986)**
    - George Allen &amp; Unwin merge with Bell &amp; Hyman in to form Unwin Hyman.<sup id="cite_ref-fob_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fob-7">[7]</a></sup>
- **[1987](/wiki/1987)**
    - [Mithril Miniatures](/wiki/Mithril_Miniatures) is founded, creating licensed metal miniature figures inspired by J.R.R. Tolkien's *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>
- **[1990](/wiki/1990)**
    - Unwin Hyman is purchased by [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins).<sup id="cite_ref-fob_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fob-7">[7]</a></sup>
    - [Interplay Productions](/wiki/Interplay_Productions) aquires the video game rights to *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* and it publishes the videogame *[J.R.R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings](</wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien%27s_The_Lord_of_the_Rings,_Vol._I_(1990_video_game)> "J.R.R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings, Vol. I (1990 video game)")*.
- **[1997](/wiki/1997)**
    - The Saul Zaentz Company options their film rights to *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* to Miramax Films.
- **[1998](/wiki/1998)**
    - [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema) purchases the film options for *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* from Miramax.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **[2000](/wiki/2000)**
    - [Vivendi](/wiki/Vivendi) acquires the book rights for video games to *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit*, while [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts) acquires the film rights for video games only of [*The Lord of the Rings* film series](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)").
- **[2001](/wiki/2001)**
    - New Line Cinema releases *[The Fellowship of the Ring](</wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(2001_film)> "The Fellowship of the Ring (2001 film)")*, co-produced and directed by [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson).
- **[2002](/wiki/2002)**
    - New Line Cinema releases *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers "The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers")*.
- **[2003](/wiki/2003)**
    - New Line Cinema releases *[The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King "The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King")*.
- **[2004](/wiki/2004)**
    - Electronic Arts releases *[The Battle for Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth "The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth")*, a real time strategy game under a joint license with New Line Cinema and Tolkien Enterprises.
- **[2005](/wiki/2005)**
    - Tolkien Enterprises enters a renewed era of licensing with the worldwide popularity of the Peter Jackson films. New Line and its parent company, Warner Brothers, release a flood of merchandise based upon film assets, under license from Tolkien Enterprises, which enters into new license agreements for merchandise based exclusively upon the Tolkien literary property, including for board games, videogames, jewelry, figurines and other collectibles.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **[2006](/wiki/2006)**
    - Electronic Arts acquires and the book rights and it releases *[The Battle for Middle-earth II](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Battle_for_Middle-earth_II "The Lord of the Rings: The Battle for Middle-earth II")* video game.
    - *[The Lord of the Rings (musical)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(musical)>)* stage play premieres in Toronto, Canada. A Kevin Wallace Ltd. production under license from Tolkien Enterprises.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **[2007](/wiki/2007)**
    - 27 March - Electronic Arts extends its publishing license for video games with New Line and Tolkien Enterprises.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
    - [Turbine](/wiki/Turbine) releases an MMO videogame based on the literary property only, *[The Lord of the Rings Online](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online)* (LOTRO) under license from Tolkien Enterprises.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
    - 18 December - New Line and MGM announce the development of a two-film adaptation of J.R.R. Tolkien's *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
- **[2008](/wiki/2008)**
    - New Line Cinema merges with [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) and through this way it obtains distribution rights of [*The Lord of the Rings* film series](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)") and co-production rights of [*The Hobbit* film series](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)> "The Hobbit (film series)").<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
    - 8 May - Electronic Arts announces *[The Lord of the Rings: Conquest](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Conquest)*, developed by Pandemic Studios.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
- **[2009](/wiki/2009)**
    - January - Electronic Arts' publishing licence for video games based on *The Lord of the Rings* expires, returns to [Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Interactive_Entertainment).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
    - 13 January - *The Lord of the Rings: Conquest* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>
    - 28 May - Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment announces a long-term partnership with The Saul Zaentz Company and New Line Productions, Inc. for literary and film rights to develop and publish games based on *The Lord of the Rings* trilogy. *[The Lord of the Rings: Aragorn's Quest](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Aragorn%27s_Quest)* is announced, developed by TT Fusion and Headstrong Games.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>
- **[2010](/wiki/2010)**
    - Tolkien Enterprises changes its name to [Middle-earth Enterprises](/wiki/Middle-earth_Enterprises).<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
    - Turbine is purchased by [WB Games](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Interactive_Entertainment "Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment"), and thus WB Games obtains access to LOTRO rights.<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-warner_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-warner-17">[17]</a></sup>
    - 18 March - Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment announces *[The Lord of the Rings: War in the North](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_War_in_the_North)*, developed by Snowblind Studios in association with Middle-earth Enterprises.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup>
    - 14 December - *The Lord of the Rings: Aragorn's Quest* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>
- **[2011](/wiki/2011)**
    - 30 May - New Line Cinema, Warner Bros. Pictures and MGM announces the titles of Peter Jackson's two-film adaptation of *The Hobbit*. The first film is titled *[The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey](/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey)* and the second film is titled *The Hobbit: There and Back Again*.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[20]</a></sup>
    - 1 November - *The Lord of the Rings: War in the North* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>
- **[2012](/wiki/2012)**
    - 30 July - New Line, MGM and Warner Bros. Pictures announce that Peter Jackson's two-film adaptation of *The Hobbit* will now become a trilogy.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup>
    - 31 August - New Line, MGM and Warner Bros. announce the second film of Peter Jackson's *The Hobbit* trilogy to be retitled as *[The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug](/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Desolation_of_Smaug)*. The third film is titled *The Hobbit: There and Back Again*.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup>
    - 4 December - *[Guardians of Middle-earth](/wiki/Guardians_of_Middle-earth)*, developed by Monolith Productions is released.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>
    - 28 November - Premiere of *The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey*.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[26]</a></sup>
- **[2013](/wiki/2013)**
    - 12 November - Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment announces *[Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor](/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor)*. A new game set between *The Hobbit* and *The Lord of the Rings*, developed by Monolith Productions.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[27]</a></sup>
    - 25 November - Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment, TT Games and The LEGO Group announces *[LEGO The Hobbit](/wiki/Lego_The_Hobbit:_The_Video_Game "Lego The Hobbit: The Video Game")*, based on the first two films of Peter Jackson’s *The Hobbit* Trilogy, *The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey* and *The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug*.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup>
    - 2 December - Premiere of *The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug*.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[29]</a></sup>
- **[2014](/wiki/2014)**
    - 8 April - *LEGO The Hobbit* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup>
    - 24 April - Warner Bros Pictures, MGM and New Line Cinema announce that the title of the third film of Peter Jackson's *The Hobbit* trilogy to be renamed as *[The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies](/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies)*.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup>
    - 30 September - *Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor* is released<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup>
    - 1 December - Premiere of *The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies*.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup>
- **[2016](/wiki/2016)**
    - The rights to LOTRO are sold to [Standing Stone Games](/wiki/Standing_Stone_Games).<sup id="cite_ref-middle-earth-timeline_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-middle-earth-timeline-4">[4]</a></sup>
- **[2017](/wiki/2017)**
    - 27 February - Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment and developer Monolith Productions announces *[Middle-earth: Shadow of War](/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_War)*, a sequel to *Shadow of Mordor*.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[34]</a></sup>
    - 10 October - *Middle-earth: Shadow of War* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">[35]</a></sup>
    - 13 November - [Amazon](/wiki/Amazon.com_Incorporated "Amazon.com Incorporated") purchases global television rights to *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* from [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate) and announces the production of a series through a partnership with the Tolkien Estate, [Tolkien Trust](/wiki/Tolkien_Trust), HarperCollins, and New Line Cinema. This includes multiple seasons and potential spinoff series.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">[36]</a></sup>
- **[2019](/wiki/2019)**
    - 25 March - *[The Lord of the Rings: Gollum](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Gollum)* is announced by Daedalic Entertainment, partnered with Middle-earth Enterprises.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">[37]</a></sup>
- **[2020](/wiki/2020)**
    - 15 June - NetEase and Warner Bros. Interactive Entertainment announce the development of a new officially licensed mobile strategy game *[The Lord of the Rings: Rise to War](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Rise_to_War)*.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup>
- **[2021](/wiki/2021)**
    - 10 June - New Line Cinema partners with Warner Bros. Animation on an original anime theatrical feature *[The Lord of the Rings: The War of the Rohirrim](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_War_of_the_Rohirrim)*.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[39]</a></sup>
    - 24 August - [Wizards of the Coast](/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast) announces *The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth*. A set for the *Magic: The Gathering Universes Beyond* series.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">[40]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[41]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[42]</a></sup>
    - 23 September - *The Lord of the Rings: Rise to War* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">[43]</a></sup>
- **[2022](/wiki/2022)**
    - 9 Feb - The Saul Zaentz Company puts their Tolkien property up for sale. These include rights to motion pictures, video games, merchandising, theme parks and stage productions based on *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit*.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">[44]</a></sup>
    - 17 May - Amazon acquires MGM for $8.5 billion, which includes distribution rights to *The Hobbit*.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">[45]</a></sup>
    - 18 August - [Embracer Group](/wiki/Embracer_Group) acquires Middle-earth Enterprises from The Saul Zaentz Company.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">[46]</a></sup>
    - The first season of the first licenced television series, *[The Rings of Power](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power "The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power")*, is released.
- **[2023](/wiki/2023)**
    - 23 February - Embracer Group, New Line Cinema and Warner Bros. Pictures forge a multi-year agreement to collaborate on features based on *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit*.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[47]</a></sup>
    - 2 March - The Watermill Theatre announces *The Lord of the Rings: A Musical Tale*, a musical stage adaptation of *The Lord of the Rings*. In association with Kevin Wallace for KWL (Kevin Wallace Limited) and Middle-earth Enterprises.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[48]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-watermill_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-watermill-49">[49]</a></sup>
    - 10 May - *[The Lord of the Rings: Heroes of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Heroes_of_Middle-earth)*, a turn-based RPG published by Electronic Arts and developed by Capital Games is released.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[50]</a></sup>
    - 15 May - Amazon Games reaches an agreement with Embracer Group to develop and publish a new massively multiplayer online (MMO) game based on *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">[51]</a></sup>
    - 25 May - *The Lord of the Rings: Gollum* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[52]</a></sup>
    - 8 June - *[The Lord of the Rings: Return to Moria](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Return_to_Moria)*, a survival crafting video game set in the Fourth Age of Middle-earth is announced. Developed by Free Range Games and published by North Beach Games.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">[53]</a></sup>
    - 23 June - *The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[54]</a></sup>
    - 25 July - Premiere of *The Lord of the Rings: A Musical Tale*.<sup id="cite_ref-watermill_49-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-watermill-49">[49]</a></sup>
    - 21 September - *[Tales of the Shire](/wiki/Tales_of_the_Shire)* is announced, developed by Wētā Workshop and published by Private Division.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">[55]</a></sup>
    - 24 October - *The Lord of the Rings: Return to Moria* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[56]</a></sup>
- **[2024](/wiki/2024)**
    - 9 May - Warner Bros. Pictures and New Line Cinema announces two new feature films from Tolkien's Middle-earth. The first film is *[The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Hunt_for_Gollum)* (working title).<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">[57]</a></sup>
    - 24 May - *The Lord of the Rings: Heroes of Middle-earth* discontinues development.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">[58]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">[59]</a></sup>
    - 13 December - Premiere of *The Lord of the Rings: The War of the Rohirrim*.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">[60]</a></sup>
- **[2025](/wiki/2025)**
    - 29 July - *Tales of the Shire: A The Lord of the Rings Game* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">[61]</a></sup>
- **[2026](/wiki/2026)**
    - 24 March - Warner Bros. announce the development of <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_Shadows_of_the_Past&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Lord of the Rings: Shadows of the Past (page does not exist)">The Lord of the Rings: Shadows of the Past</a></i>, the next film after *The Hunt for Gollum*.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">[62]</a></sup>
    - 20 May - Warhorse Studios announces the development of an open-world Middle-earth RPG.<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">[63]</a></sup>
    - 14 August - *Magic: The Gathering \| The Hobbit* is released.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">[64]</a></sup>
