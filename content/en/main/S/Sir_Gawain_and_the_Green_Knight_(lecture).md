**Sir Gawain and the Green Knight** is a lecture on the [medieval English poem](</wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight_(poem)> "Sir Gawain and the Green Knight (poem)"), given by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) as the [W.P. Ker Memorial Lecture](/wiki/W.P._Ker_Lecture "W.P. Ker Lecture") at the University of Glasgow on [15 April](/wiki/15_April) [1953](/wiki/1953).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It has been published in *[The Monsters and the Critics, and Other Essays](/wiki/The_Monsters_and_the_Critics,_and_Other_Essays)* and in some editions of Tolkien's translations of *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Pearl, and Sir Orfeo](/wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight,_Pearl,_and_Sir_Orfeo)*.

The lecture focuses on the Third Fit (the third of four major divisions of the poem), which Tolkien says expresses the major themes of the work. Tolkien considers especially the temptation of Gawain (when the Lady of the Castle, Sir Bertilak's wife, invites him to commit adultery) and his later confession and absolution.
