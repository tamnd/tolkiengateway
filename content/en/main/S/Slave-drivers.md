The **slave-drivers** were the two large and fierce *[uruks](/wiki/Uruk-hai "Uruk-hai")* that led the [Durthang line](/wiki/Durthang_line)<sup id="cite_ref-journey_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-2">[2]</a></sup> from the [Orc-hold](/wiki/Orc-holds "Orc-holds") of [Durthang](/wiki/Durthang) to the [Morannon](/wiki/Morannon) during the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

On Sunday,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> [18 March](/wiki/18_March) of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"),<sup id="cite_ref-march_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-march-5">[5]</a></sup> [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) caught a glimpse of Durthang when they came to the road that led away from "that orc-tower" to the Isenmouthe.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-6">[6]</a></sup> After resting "under overhanging stone in sight of Durthang"<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> until that evening,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> Frodo and Sam began to head down the road, which they found was not very broad.<sup id="cite_ref-begin_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-begin-9">[9]</a></sup> After traveling for twelve miles and deciding to take a rest for a few minutes,<sup id="cite_ref-begin_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-begin-9">[9]</a></sup> a company of Orcs "going at a great pace" from Durthang<sup id="cite_ref-journey_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-2">[2]</a></sup> passed them by.<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup> The Orcs in the front of the line "bore torches" and "were a gang of smaller breeds" who only wanted to finish the march and "escape the whip", being forced to fight for Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup> To prevent the company from seeing their feet, Sam and Frodo lowered their orc-shields.<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup> When one of the two "large fierce *uruks*" that lead them mistook the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) for deserters from the garrison at [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol), the slave-driver forced them to march along with the company to the Black Gate.<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup> In the "early hours"<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup> "before dawn"<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> on [19 March](/wiki/19_March),<sup id="cite_ref-march_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-march-5">[5]</a></sup> after marching for "some miles" eastward near [Udûn](</wiki/Ud%C3%BBn_(valley)> "Udûn (valley)") in Mordor, they came to a "road-meeting" where "several companies came together" and clashed, each trying to get to the Black Gate first.<sup id="cite_ref-journey_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-2">[2]</a></sup> When "a troop of heavy-armed *uruks* from Barad-dûr charged into the [Durthang line](/wiki/Durthang_line)", Sam saw the chance for him and Frodo to slip away in the confusion.<sup id="cite_ref-journey_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-2">[2]</a></sup> It is unknown what became of the slave-drivers that led the Durthang line.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In *[Sauron Defeated](/wiki/Sauron_Defeated)*, [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) noted that, apart from "minor details", the Orc-band from Durthang that Frodo and Sam encounter at the end of "[The Land of Shadow](/wiki/The_Land_of_Shadow)" chapter remain much the same in [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s original draft.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> In this draft, the two "large fierce *uruks*" are further specified as "the fighting-orcs", which Christopher Tolkien suggested might have been the first usage of the word *uruk* (not *Uruk-hai*).<sup id="cite_ref-notes_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-notes-13">[13]</a></sup> The draft also notes that the slave-driver who spotted Frodo and Sam did so "with night-sighted eyes" and told them they should have been "inside the Narch-line" instead of saying that they "should have been inside Udûn before yesterday evening".<sup id="cite_ref-notes_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-notes-13">[13]</a></sup> The Orc also originally claimed, after saying "Don't you know we're at war?", that "the elvish folk" wouldn't treat them well if they won the battle.<sup id="cite_ref-notes_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-notes-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

On [17 October](/wiki/17_October) of [2005](/wiki/2005), [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) commentated on the slave-driver's line "Where there's a whip, there's a will"<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup> in *[The Lord of the Rings: A Reader's Companion](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_A_Reader%27s_Companion)*, suggesting that it is a parody of "Where there's a will there's a way", a proverb.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> They also suggest that the slave-driver's line "Don't you know we're at war?" echoes the catch-phrase "Don't you know there's a war on?" from the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II" title="wikipedia:World War II">Second World War</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b><a href="/wiki/1980" title="1980">1980</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King_(1980_film)" title="The Return of the King (1980 film)">The Return of the King: A Story of the Hobbits</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The slave-driver&#39;s comment &#34;Where there&#39;s a whip, there&#39;s a will&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-built_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-built-3">[3]</a></sup> was adapted into a song named &#34;<i><a href="/wiki/Where_There%27s_a_Whip,_There%27s_a_Way" title="Where There&#39;s a Whip, There&#39;s a Way">Where There&#39;s a Whip, There&#39;s a Way</a></i>&#34;, which was written by <a href="/wiki/Glenn_Yarbrough" title="Glenn Yarbrough">Glenn Yarbrough</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> In the seventeenth scene, &#34;There&#39;s a Whip&#34;, Frodo and Sam are sleeping beneath an overhanging rock in Mordor when &#34;the Durthang line&#34;<sup id="cite_ref-journey_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-journey-2">[2]</a></sup> approaches singing at the behest of an Orc known as the <b>Lord of the Lash</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> an adaptation of the two slave-drivers.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>The song wakes up Sam, who wakes up Frodo.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> When Frodo asks Sam why he woke him up, Sam tells him they slept too long and the overhanging rock was the only thing stopping the Orcs from seeing them.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> It is at that point when the Lord of the Lash catches them.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> He mistakes them for two deserters and orders them into the line from Durthang with a crack of his whip.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> When Frodo states it is lucky that they were thought to be Orcs, Sam refutes him that it isn&#39;t luck to have to march with a huge army back to Cirith Ungol where they started.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> The Lord of the Lash interrupts their debate again to order them into the line.<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup> As the Hobbits get into the line, the Orcs begin singing again, with occasional interruptions from the Lord of the Lash:<sup id="cite_ref-animated_16-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-animated-16">[16]</a></sup></dd></dl>
