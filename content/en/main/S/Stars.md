The **stars** are lights created and set by the [Valie](/wiki/Valie) [Varda](/wiki/Varda), upon [Ilmen](/wiki/Ilmen) (the upper airs) long before the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) and [Moon](/wiki/Moon).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

The stars are associated with the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), who revere the stars and Varda. They give her the names *[Tintallë](/wiki/Tintall%C3%AB)* ("Kindler") or *[Elentári](/wiki/Elent%C3%A1ri)*/*Elbereth* ("Star-queen").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

While the evil of [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) [corrupted](/wiki/Arda_Marred "Arda Marred") the whole of [Arda](/wiki/Arda) the ancient stars contain the unsullied light of creation.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Varda created the first stars in the earliest ages of the creation of [Arda](/wiki/Arda).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/b/be/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg/250px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="250" height="202" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/b/be/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg/375px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/b/be/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg/500px-Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann_-_Stargazing.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="841" data-file-height="680"/></a><figcaption><i>Stargazing</i> by <a href="/wiki/Anke_Ei%C3%9Fmann" title="Anke Eißmann">Anke Eißmann</a></figcaption></figure>

Ages later, prior to the [awakening of the Elves](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves "Awakening of the Elves"), Varda grouped together many of the ancient stars into [constellations](/wiki/Constellations "Constellations"), including [Menelmacar](/wiki/Menelmacar) (Orion), and the [Valacirca](/wiki/Valacirca) (the Sickle of the Valar, the Big Dipper), as signs in the heavens of [Arda](/wiki/Arda)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> "that the gods may read".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> She also created new ones using collected light from the Silver Tree, [Telperion](/wiki/Telperion). These included [Carnil](/wiki/Carnil), [Luinil](/wiki/Luinil), [Nénar](/wiki/N%C3%A9nar), [Lumbar](/wiki/Lumbar), [Alcarinquë](/wiki/Alcarinqu%C3%AB), and [Elemmírë](/wiki/Elemm%C3%ADr%C3%AB)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[note 1]</a></sup>.

The Elves first awoke under the stars and, according to tradition, the stars were the first thing they saw, and the first word spoken by them was *ele!*, a primitive exclamation meaning "behold"! From this origin derived the ancient words *el* and *elen* for "Star".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> Moreover, when [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB) first met the Elves, he too associated them with the stars and named them the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar), the "People of the Stars".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[8]</a></sup>

The [Star of Eärendil](/wiki/Star_of_E%C3%A4rendil) is a unique case. Following his successful mission to persuade the Valar to intervene in the wars against [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) and his ship [Vingilot](/wiki/Vingilot), along with the last remaining [Silmaril](/wiki/Silmarils "Silmarils"), were placed in the sky as a sign of hope to the faithful people of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). After the war against Morgoth was concluded, a group of the [Edain](/wiki/Edain) followed Eärendil's star to the newly created island that would become [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor). Consequently, one of the names of that island was [Elenna](/wiki/Elenna), or "Starwards". <sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 2]</a></sup>

### In culture {#In_culture}

Emblems also featured stars. An eight-rayed star represented the [House of Fëanor](/wiki/House_of_F%C3%ABanor), and seven eight-rayed stars above a crown and anvil were the emblems of <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Durin" title="Durin">Durin</a>. Both of these were depicted on the [West-gate of Moria](/wiki/Doors_of_Durin "Doors of Durin") (the former because [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor), one of the makers of the doors, was a descendant of Fëanor). Seven stars appeared on the banner of Gondor. Certain jewels are also described as stars, notably the [Star of Elendil](/wiki/Star_of_Elendil) that Aragorn wore into the Battle of Pelennor Fields. In the [Lay of Nimrodel](/wiki/Lay_of_Nimrodel), it is said that "*A star was bound upon her brow*," clearly describing some kind of jewel.

Other prominent references to stars include:

- The [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) greeting, *[Elen síla lúmenn' omentielvo](/wiki/Elen_s%C3%ADla_l%C3%BAmenn%27_omentielvo)* (Quenya: "A star shines on the hour of our meeting.")
- Frodo, from the window of his room in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), observed a red star foreshadowing the [Eye of Sauron](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron): "*But low in the South one star shone red ... deep in the heavens burning like a watchful eye that glared above the trees on the brink of the valley*."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[note 3]</a></sup>
- [Sam](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee "Samwise Gamgee")'s song in [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol) concludes "...above all shadows rides the Sun, and Stars for ever dwell. I will not say the Day is done, nor bid the Stars farewell."
- Travelling through [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), Sam saw a star, leading him to conclude that "*In the end the Shadow was only a small and passing thing: there was light and high beauty for ever beyond its reach.*"

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

So important was this identification of the Elves with the stars to [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") that when he considered revising his creation myths so that the Sun and Moon existed from an earlier time (see *[Myths Transformed](/wiki/Myths_Transformed)*), he still insisted that the Elves must have awoken under the stars.
