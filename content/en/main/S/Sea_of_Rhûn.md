The **Sea of Rhûn** or **Inland Sea**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was a large lake or sea in northern [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) that lay east of [Rhovanion](../R/Rhovanion.md) on the western borders of [Rhûn](../R/Rhûn.md). The [Kine of Araw](../K/Kine_of_Araw.md) were found in the fields of Rhûn near the Inland Sea.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

The [Celduin](../R/River_Running.md) flowed from the north-west into an arm of the sea. West of the Sea of Rhûn was the land of [Dorwinion](../D/Dorwinion.md), and roughly 200 miles (300 km) to the south was the eastern end of the [Ered Lithui](../E/Ered_Lithui.md).

A forest stood at the north-eastern shore of the sea, and near the south-western shore there were many hills. The south-eastern part of the Sea seems to be occupied by a small wooded island.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) during the time of the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md), the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md) reached the Sea of Rhûn after marching 450 miles west or northwest from [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md).<sup id="cite_ref-NM_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-7">[7]</a></sup> The craft of ship-making practised by the [Teleri](../T/Teleri.md) reached new heights in their efforts to traverse the Sea of Rhûn on their westward journey.<sup id="cite_ref-Last_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Last-8">[8]</a></sup>

In the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md), the shores of the Inland Sea were populated by tribes of [Men](../M/Men.md) who were migrating to the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../W/West.md" title="West">West</a>. The [Lesser Folk](../H/House_of_Bëor.md) arrived there first and dwelt at the feet of the nearby hills. The [Greater Folk](../H/House_of_Hador.md) came later in the north-east woods near the shores. The Men crafted boats and could sail the sea, but they did not meet often, and their languages soon diverged<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> before they resumed their journey to [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md).

In the early [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md), the [Kings of Gondor](../K/Kings_of_Gondor.md) such as [Rómendacil I](../R/Rómendacil_I.md) campaigned to those lands, and [Turambar](<../T/Turambar_(King_of_Gondor).md>) expanded the kingdom to the East. By the time of King [Hyarmendacil I](../H/Hyarmendacil_I.md), the Inland Sea formed one of the boundaries of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Vorondil](../V/Vorondil.md) hunted the Kine of Araw near the shores of the Sea.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-10">[10]</a></sup>

During the time of [Rómendacil II](../R/Rómendacil_II.md), the Inland Sea was apparently within the borders of the power of [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md).

*[Rhûn](../R/Rhûn.md)* is simply [Sindarin](Sindarin.md) for "East".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In the drafts for *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, [Tolkien](../T/Tolkien.md) considered calling the Sea *Rhûnaer*<sup id="cite_ref-MII_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MII-12">[12]</a></sup> or *Rúnaeluin*.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the drafts of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, the sea was called the **Sea of Rhûnaer**. In the earlier maps, part of the Sea was more clearly occupied by a heavily wooded island.<sup id="cite_ref-MII_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MII-12">[12]</a></sup> In the published maps by [Christopher Tolkien](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md), the island is represented by a dotted pattern.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> The reason for this change and what it signifies was never specified by C. Tolkien. There is no trace of the unnamed island in [Pauline Baynes](../P/Pauline_Baynes.md)'s *[A Map of Middle-earth](../A/A_Map_of_Middle-earth.md)*.

In a [1948](../_/1948.md) note on his *[General Map of Middle-earth](../G/General_Map_of_Middle-earth.md)*, [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) drew an arrow from the [River Running](../R/River_Running.md) with the direction to the end of the map, and carries the note: "<i>To <b>Sea of Rûnaer</b></i>. [Hammond](../W/Wayne_G._Hammond.md) and [Scull](../C/Christina_Scull.md) suggest that *Rûnaer* is likely an alternative name of *Rhûn*.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

Also in these early draft of the maps for *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, Tolkien indicated that *[Neldoreth](<../N/Neldoreth_(Rhûn).md>)* was the name of the forest bordering the Sea of Rhûn's north-eastern shore. This forest went unnamed in later maps.<sup id="cite_ref-MII_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MII-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Theories {#Theories}

Christopher Tolkien once questioned whether the Sea of Rhûn could "...be identified with the [Sea of Helcar](Sea_of_Helcar.md), vastly shrunken".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> [Karen Wynn Fonstad](../K/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad.md) adopted this position in making *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](../T/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth.md)*. However, in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](../T/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth.md)* and *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)*, which were published after Fonstad's *Atlas*, the Sea of Rhûn and its surrounding geographical landmarks exist as far back as the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md) at the time of the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md), hundreds of miles west of where the Elves awoke near the Sea of Helcar.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Last_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Last-8">[8]</a></sup>
