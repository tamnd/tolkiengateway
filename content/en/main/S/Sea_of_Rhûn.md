The **Sea of Rhûn** or **Inland Sea**<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was a large lake or sea in northern [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) that lay east of [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion) on the western borders of [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn). The [Kine of Araw](/wiki/Kine_of_Araw) were found in the fields of Rhûn near the Inland Sea.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Geography {#Geography}

The [Celduin](/wiki/River_Running "River Running") flowed from the north-west into an arm of the sea. West of the Sea of Rhûn was the land of [Dorwinion](/wiki/Dorwinion), and roughly 200 miles (300 km) to the south was the eastern end of the [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui).

A forest stood at the north-eastern shore of the sea, and near the south-western shore there were many hills. The south-eastern part of the Sea seems to be occupied by a small wooded island.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees) during the time of the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey), the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) reached the Sea of Rhûn after marching 450 miles west or northwest from [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen).<sup id="cite_ref-NM_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-7">[7]</a></sup> The craft of ship-making practised by the [Teleri](/wiki/Teleri) reached new heights in their efforts to traverse the Sea of Rhûn on their westward journey.<sup id="cite_ref-Last_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Last-8">[8]</a></sup>

In the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), the shores of the Inland Sea were populated by tribes of [Men](/wiki/Men) who were migrating to the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/West" title="West">West</a>. The [Lesser Folk](/wiki/House_of_B%C3%ABor "House of Bëor") arrived there first and dwelt at the feet of the nearby hills. The [Greater Folk](/wiki/House_of_Hador "House of Hador") came later in the north-east woods near the shores. The Men crafted boats and could sail the sea, but they did not meet often, and their languages soon diverged<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> before they resumed their journey to [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand).

In the early [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), the [Kings of Gondor](/wiki/Kings_of_Gondor) such as [Rómendacil I](/wiki/R%C3%B3mendacil_I) campaigned to those lands, and [Turambar](</wiki/Turambar_(King_of_Gondor)> "Turambar (King of Gondor)") expanded the kingdom to the East. By the time of King [Hyarmendacil I](/wiki/Hyarmendacil_I), the Inland Sea formed one of the boundaries of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Vorondil](/wiki/Vorondil) hunted the Kine of Araw near the shores of the Sea.<sup id="cite_ref-gondor_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-10">[10]</a></sup>

During the time of [Rómendacil II](/wiki/R%C3%B3mendacil_II), the Inland Sea was apparently within the borders of the power of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor).

*[Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn)* is simply [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "East".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

In the drafts for *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) considered calling the Sea *Rhûnaer*<sup id="cite_ref-MII_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MII-12">[12]</a></sup> or *Rúnaeluin*.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the drafts of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, the sea was called the **Sea of Rhûnaer**. In the earlier maps, part of the Sea was more clearly occupied by a heavily wooded island.<sup id="cite_ref-MII_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MII-12">[12]</a></sup> In the published maps by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien), the island is represented by a dotted pattern.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup> The reason for this change and what it signifies was never specified by C. Tolkien. There is no trace of the unnamed island in [Pauline Baynes](/wiki/Pauline_Baynes)'s *[A Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/A_Map_of_Middle-earth)*.

In a [1948](/wiki/1948) note on his *[General Map of Middle-earth](/wiki/General_Map_of_Middle-earth)*, [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") drew an arrow from the [River Running](/wiki/River_Running) with the direction to the end of the map, and carries the note: "<i>To <b>Sea of Rûnaer</b></i>. [Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond "Wayne G. Hammond") and [Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull "Christina Scull") suggest that *Rûnaer* is likely an alternative name of *Rhûn*.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup>

Also in these early draft of the maps for *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Tolkien indicated that *[Neldoreth](</wiki/Neldoreth_(Rh%C3%BBn)> "Neldoreth (Rhûn)")* was the name of the forest bordering the Sea of Rhûn's north-eastern shore. This forest went unnamed in later maps.<sup id="cite_ref-MII_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MII-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Theories {#Theories}

Christopher Tolkien once questioned whether the Sea of Rhûn could "...be identified with the [Sea of Helcar](/wiki/Sea_of_Helcar), vastly shrunken".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup> [Karen Wynn Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad) adopted this position in making *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth)*. However, in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)* and *[The Nature of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth)*, which were published after Fonstad's *Atlas*, the Sea of Rhûn and its surrounding geographical landmarks exist as far back as the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees) of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) at the time of the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey), hundreds of miles west of where the Elves awoke near the Sea of Helcar.<sup id="cite_ref-NM_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NM-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Last_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Last-8">[8]</a></sup>
