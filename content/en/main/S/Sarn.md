<i><b>sarn</b></i> is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word. As a noun it means "(small) stone" or "stony place" (outcrop of rock in softer ground, or in a river-bed).<sup id="cite_ref-silmap_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silmap-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC163_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC163-2">[2]</a></sup> It is also glossed as "small stone, pebble".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

As an adjective, *sarn* means "stony".<sup id="cite_ref-RC163_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC163-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

In *[The Etymologies](/wiki/The_Etymologies)*, [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) *sarn* ("stone as a material, or as adj.") derives from [root](/wiki/Root "Root") <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=SAR&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="SAR (page does not exist)">SAR</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

*Sarn* is a [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) word that means a causeway, a pavement or stepping-stone.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
