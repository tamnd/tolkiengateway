The **Sea of Helcar** (also spelled **Helkar**) was a great lake or inland sea which existed in the central region of northern [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) during the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees).<sup id="cite_ref-S_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the beginning of [Arda](/wiki/Arda), the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) created the [Two Lamps](/wiki/Two_Lamps) and two great towers on which to place them. [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) destroyed the Two Lamps, and where [Illuin](/wiki/Illuin) (the northern tower) had stood, the inland Sea of Helcar was formed. Many rivers flowed into the Sea of Helcar from the east. [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen), where the first [Elves](/wiki/Elves) [awoke](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves "Awakening of the Elves"), was a bay on the eastern side of the Sea of Helcar, and it lay under the shadows of the [Orocarni](/wiki/Orocarni) (Red Mountains). During the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey), the Elves travelled north around the Sea of Helcar before turning westward.<sup id="cite_ref-S_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-1">[1]</a></sup> After this, the Sea of Helcar passed out of history.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the *[Ambarkanta](/wiki/Ambarkanta)*, the Sea of Helcar was depicted as an enormous body of water that stretched from the Red Mountains in the east to the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) in the west.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> After Middle-earth's landscape changed in the [Battle of the Powers](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Powers), the western end of the Sea was depicted as being separated from the [Great Gulf](/wiki/Great_Gulf) only by a narrow strip of mountainous land called the [Straits of the World](/wiki/Straits_of_the_World).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> However, these early depictions predate Tolkien's conception of the wide lands of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), and [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) that lay east of the Great Gulf.

Christopher Tolkien questioned whether or not the [Sea of Rhûn](/wiki/Sea_of_Rh%C3%BBn) could "...be identified with the Sea of Helkar, vastly shrunken".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth)*, [Karen Wynn Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad) assumed that Mordor, [Khand](/wiki/Khand), and parts of [Rhûn](/wiki/Rh%C3%BBn) in the Second and Third Ages lay where the Sea of Helcar had been in the First Age, and that the Sea of Rhûn and [Sea of Núrnen](/wiki/Sea_of_N%C3%BArnen) were its remnants. However, in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)* (which was published after Fontstad's *Atlas*), there are references to the Sea of Rhûn and its surrounding geographical landmarks existing as far back as the [Years of the Trees](/wiki/Years_of_the_Trees) at the time of the [Great Journey](/wiki/Great_Journey),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) was said to have created [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) in Mordor during the "long [First Age](/wiki/First_Age)".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Additionally, it is told in *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)* that the migration of the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) from [Hildórien](/wiki/Hild%C3%B3rien) brought them westward through lands south of Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In *[The Nature of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth)*, [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen) (located on the eastern shores of the Sea of Helcar) is roughly at 200 miles from the Sea of Rhûn,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> or in a more detailed annalistic text on the March of the Quendi [Cuiviénen](/wiki/Cuivi%C3%A9nen) is at around 450 miles eastward from the Sea of Rhûn.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
