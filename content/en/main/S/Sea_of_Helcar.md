The **Sea of Helcar** (also spelled **Helkar**) was a great lake or inland sea which existed in the central region of northern [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) during the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md).<sup id="cite_ref-S_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

In the beginning of [Arda](../A/Arda.md), the [Valar](../V/Valar.md) created the [Two Lamps](../T/Two_Lamps.md) and two great towers on which to place them. [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md) destroyed the Two Lamps, and where [Illuin](../I/Illuin.md) (the northern tower) had stood, the inland Sea of Helcar was formed. Many rivers flowed into the Sea of Helcar from the east. [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md), where the first [Elves](../E/Elves.md) [awoke](../A/Awakening_of_the_Elves.md), was a bay on the eastern side of the Sea of Helcar, and it lay under the shadows of the [Orocarni](../O/Orocarni.md) (Red Mountains). During the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md), the Elves travelled north around the Sea of Helcar before turning westward.<sup id="cite_ref-S_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S-1">[1]</a></sup> After this, the Sea of Helcar passed out of history.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the *[Ambarkanta](../A/Ambarkanta.md)*, the Sea of Helcar was depicted as an enormous body of water that stretched from the Red Mountains in the east to the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) in the west.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> After Middle-earth's landscape changed in the [Battle of the Powers](../B/Battle_of_the_Powers.md), the western end of the Sea was depicted as being separated from the [Great Gulf](../G/Great_Gulf.md) only by a narrow strip of mountainous land called the [Straits of the World](Straits_of_the_World.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> However, these early depictions predate Tolkien's conception of the wide lands of [Eriador](../E/Eriador.md), [Gondor](../G/Gondor.md), and [Mordor](../M/Mordor.md) that lay east of the Great Gulf.

Christopher Tolkien questioned whether or not the [Sea of Rhûn](Sea_of_Rhûn.md) could "...be identified with the Sea of Helkar, vastly shrunken".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](../T/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth.md)*, [Karen Wynn Fonstad](../K/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad.md) assumed that Mordor, [Khand](../K/Khand.md), and parts of [Rhûn](../R/Rhûn.md) in the Second and Third Ages lay where the Sea of Helcar had been in the First Age, and that the Sea of Rhûn and [Sea of Núrnen](Sea_of_Núrnen.md) were its remnants. However, in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](../T/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth.md)* (which was published after Fontstad's *Atlas*), there are references to the Sea of Rhûn and its surrounding geographical landmarks existing as far back as the [Years of the Trees](../Y/Years_of_the_Trees.md) at the time of the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and [Melkor](../M/Melkor.md) was said to have created [Mount Doom](../M/Mount_Doom.md) in Mordor during the "long [First Age](../F/First_Age.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> Additionally, it is told in *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)* that the migration of the [Drúedain](../D/Drúedain.md) from [Hildórien](../H/Hildórien.md) brought them westward through lands south of Mordor.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> In *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)*, [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md) (located on the eastern shores of the Sea of Helcar) is roughly at 200 miles from the Sea of Rhûn,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> or in a more detailed annalistic text on the March of the Quendi [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md) is at around 450 miles eastward from the Sea of Rhûn.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
