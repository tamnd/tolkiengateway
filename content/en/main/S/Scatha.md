**Scatha**, known as the **Worm**, was a blind<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup> and silent<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems1-3">[3]</a></sup> [Long-worm](../L/Long-worms.md) of the [Grey Mountains](../G/Grey_Mountains.md) and one of the greatest [Dragons](../D/Dragons.md) to infest that range of the north.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Little is known of Scatha's life, except that he had a vast<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems2_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems2-4">[4]</a></sup> den in the Grey Mountains<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-1">[1]</a></sup> that "stank like a dark tomb" and contained a hoard of treasure which included "sharp bright stones" and a horn<sup id="cite_ref-partings_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-partings-5">[5]</a></sup> made by the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md)<sup id="cite_ref-partings_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-partings-5">[5]</a></sup>. He also collected bones as "toys", particularly "hands of dwarves and skulls of men" which he "piled in his den" as a "bed"<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems2_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems2-4">[4]</a></sup> and licked them clean.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-2">[2]</a></sup>

After Scatha was slain by [Fram](../F/Fram.md) son of [Frumgar](../F/Frumgar.md) in the early days of the [Éothéod](../_/Éothéod.md), peace came to the land, though his recovered hoard was the subject of great dispute between the Men of Éothéod and the [Dwarves](../D/Dwarves.md) of that region, The Dwarves claimed it as their own, but Fram rebuked their claim, refusing to even "yield them a penny" and instead sent them a necklace made from "the teeth of Scatha" along with an insulting message. It was rumoured that the Dwarves killed him for this insult.<sup id="cite_ref-appendix_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendix-1">[1]</a></sup>

It is likely that the Men of Éothéod had retained at least some of the hoard since the horn taken<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoemsnote_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoemsnote-6">[6]</a></sup> by Fram from the hoard was brought south by [Eorl](../E/Eorl.md) when they settled in [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md). Many hundreds of years later, the [Horn of the Mark](../H/Horn_of_the_Mark.md)<sup id="cite_ref-appendixd_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendixd-7">[7]</a></sup> was given to [Meriadoc Brandybuck](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md) by [Éowyn](../_/Éowyn.md) in the final months of the [War of the Ring](../W/War_of_the_Ring.md).<sup id="cite_ref-partings_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-partings-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Scatha*, which represents a name in the language of the Éothéod, is a modernization of [Old English](../O/Old_English.md) *sceaða*, meaning "injurer, enemy, robber".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

The name *scatha* may be related to [Modern English](../M/Modern_English.md) "<a class="external text" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/scathe" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">to scathe</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> According to <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Mark_Fisher&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mark Fisher (page does not exist)">Mark Fisher</a> on the [Encyclopedia of Arda](../E/Encyclopedia_of_Arda.md), the name *scatha* may be derived "from [Old Norse](../O/Old_Norse.md) *skaða*, meaning 'harm' or 'injure'".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In an early draft of the chapter "[Many Partings](../M/Many_Partings.md)", Éowyn claimed that the Horn of the Mark<sup id="cite_ref-appendixd_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-appendixd-7">[7]</a></sup> was made "in the deeps of time…by the [dwarves](../D/Dwarves_of_Erebor.md) of [Dale](../D/Dale.md)" for the forefathers of the Rohirrim. Tolkien subsequently struck out "of Dale" and wrote "on the galley proof" that the horn "came from the hoard of Scatha the Worm".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Speculation {#Speculation}

[Robert Foster](../R/Robert_Foster.md) speculated in *[The Complete Guide to Middle-earth](../T/The_Complete_Guide_to_Middle-earth.md)* that Scatha's hoard was "a large Dwarf-hoard" and he was slain around [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2000](../T/Third_Age_2000.md),<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup> but there is no writing by Tolkien which backs this up. <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Mark_Fisher&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mark Fisher (page does not exist)">Mark Fisher</a> stated in a note on the [Encyclopedia of Arda](../E/Encyclopedia_of_Arda.md) that

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>There is no annal or record of Scatha&#39;s death, so identifying the correct date is problematic. The only known date that&#39;s remotely related is that of Frumgar&#39;s foundation of the Éothéod in III 1977. Frumgar was the father of Fram, who slew Scatha, but these bare facts leave considerable leeway for speculation.<br/>The earliest possible date for Scatha&#39;s death, then, would be III 1977, the year of the Éothéod&#39;s founding (though we might expect the event to be specially remarked if this were the case). At the other end of the scale, we might assume that Frumgar was a young man when he led his people northwards, that Fram was born late in his father&#39;s life, and that he didn&#39;t slay Scatha until advanced in years himself. Under those conditions, the date might extend as far as - at a real stretch - III 2100. As a compromise, we&#39;ve assumed a date here of roughly III 2000, a few decades after the establishment of the Éothéod.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
