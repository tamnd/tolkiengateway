<i><b>Smith of Wootton Major</b></i> is a long essay by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) on *[Smith of Wootton Major](Smith_of_Wootton_Major.md)*. Selected quotes from the essay were published in *[A Question of Time: J.R.R. Tolkien's Road to Faërie](../A/A_Question_of_Time.md)* (1997). The essay was published in full within the extended edition of *[Smith of Wootton Major](Smith_of_Wootton_Major.md)*.

In the essay Tolkien meticulously provides *[Smith of Wootton Major](Smith_of_Wootton_Major.md)*’s "back-story and philosophical underpinning"<sup id="cite_ref-afterword_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-afterword-1">[1]</a></sup> and "the narrative’s invisible but essential supporting structure"<sup id="cite_ref-afterword_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-afterword-1">[1]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-essay_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-essay-2">[2]</a></sup>

After denying that *[Smith of Wootton Major](Smith_of_Wootton_Major.md)* is an allegory, Tolkien describes the story's physical setting and the politics/economy of the three villages ([Wootton Major](../W/Wootton_Major.md), [Wootton Minor](../W/Wootton_Minor.md), and <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Walton&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Walton (page does not exist)">Walton</a>) and their relationship with [Faery](../F/Faery.md).<sup id="cite_ref-essay_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-essay-2">[2]</a></sup>
