<i><b>Smith of Wootton Major</b></i> is a long essay by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) on *[Smith of Wootton Major](/wiki/Smith_of_Wootton_Major)*. Selected quotes from the essay were published in *[A Question of Time: J.R.R. Tolkien's Road to Faërie](/wiki/A_Question_of_Time "A Question of Time")* (1997). The essay was published in full within the extended edition of *[Smith of Wootton Major](/wiki/Smith_of_Wootton_Major)*.

In the essay Tolkien meticulously provides *[Smith of Wootton Major](/wiki/Smith_of_Wootton_Major)*’s "back-story and philosophical underpinning"<sup id="cite_ref-afterword_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-afterword-1">[1]</a></sup> and "the narrative’s invisible but essential supporting structure"<sup id="cite_ref-afterword_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-afterword-1">[1]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-essay_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-essay-2">[2]</a></sup>

After denying that *[Smith of Wootton Major](/wiki/Smith_of_Wootton_Major)* is an allegory, Tolkien describes the story's physical setting and the politics/economy of the three villages ([Wootton Major](/wiki/Wootton_Major), [Wootton Minor](/wiki/Wootton_Minor), and <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Walton&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Walton (page does not exist)">Walton</a>) and their relationship with [Faery](/wiki/Faery).<sup id="cite_ref-essay_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-essay-2">[2]</a></sup>
