**Sylphs** were creatures only mentioned fleetingly in the earliest versions of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md) and nomenclature by the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Characteristics and divisions {#Characteristics_and_divisions}

The sylphs were part of what apparently was a three-fold division of the lesser [Ainu](../A/Ainur.md) spirits: sylphs (spirits of the air), [sprites](Sprites.md) (spirits of the earth), and [water spirits](../M/Mermaids.md).<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup>

[Manwë](../M/Manwë.md) had "*two clans of air-spirits*",<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-3">[3]</a></sup> a basic division of two groups of sylphs which, with their [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) names, are given as follow:

- [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) <i><b>Mánir</b></i> (or *Mānir*<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>): "spirits of the air";<sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-5">[5]</a></sup> [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) *môna* or *môni*<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-3">[3]</a></sup>
- [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) <i><b>Súruli</b></i>/*Sūruli*, also *Suluthrim*) (sing. *Sūru*): "spirits of the winds"; [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) *Sulus* (pl. *Sulussin*)<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-5">[5]</a></sup>

An individual Súru mentioned by name was **[Ilinsor](../I/Ilinsor.md)**, steersman of the [Moon](../M/Moon.md). Ilinsor "*loved the snows and the starlight and aided [Varda](../V/Varda.md) in many of her works*".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Commentary {#Commentary}

It has been suggested that the concept "air spirits" is used "metaphorically \[...\] for the winds that these spirits govern".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Sylphs are not mentioned in the later versions of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md). Many of the classes of beings references in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)* were superseded by the concept of the [Maiar](../M/Maiar.md), which were not introduced until the reworking of the [Annals of Valinor](../A/Annals_of_Valinor.md) into the [Annals of Aman](../A/Annals_of_Aman.md).<sup id="cite_ref-MRP2b_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MRP2b-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Patrick H. Wynne](../P/Patrick_H._Wynne.md) and [Christopher Gilson](../C/Christopher_Gilson.md) have noted that Tolkien's use of sylphs (and related spirits) is likely influenced by <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus" title="wikipedia:Paracelsus">Paracelsus</a>'s chart of four elementals: *sylphs* (air), *pygmies* or *gnomes* (earth), *nymphs* (water), and *salamanders* (fire).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>
