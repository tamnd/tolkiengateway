<br> **Smials** were the [hobbit-holes](/wiki/Hobbit-holes "Hobbit-holes") tunneled into earth mounds and hills.

For generations the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) dug into the earth to live. By the later [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age) the poorest Hobbits still went on living in burrows of the most primitive kind with only one window or none.

For the most well-to-do hobbits, smials were luxurious versions of those primitive diggings of old. Their tunnels had rounded walls and branched to other rooms.<sup id="cite_ref-conc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conc-1">[1]</a></sup> Smials included [Bag End](/wiki/Bag_End) and the smials along [Bagshot Row](/wiki/Bagshot_Row) of [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton), the [Great Smials](/wiki/Great_Smials) of [Tuckborough](/wiki/Tuckborough)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and [Brandy Hall](/wiki/Brandy_Hall).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> The latter two were large enough to have ample room for a hundred Hobbits.

When suitable sites for these large and ramifying tunnels were not everywhere to be found, many Hobbits lived in wood, brick or stone houses.<sup id="cite_ref-conc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conc-1">[1]</a></sup> This was the case with some muddy regions of the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing), such as the [Marish](/wiki/Marish).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *smial* is an invention of Tolkien based on the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) word *smygel*. Smial is a translation of the [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) word <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Tr%C3%A2n&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Trân (page does not exist)">trân</a></i> ([Rohanese](/wiki/Rohanese) <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Trahan&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Trahan (page does not exist)">trahan</a></i> "burrow").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") explained in his [Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/Guide_to_the_Names_in_The_Lord_of_the_Rings) the origin of the name *Sméagol* in the lemma on smials:

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p><b>Smials</b>. A word peculiar to hobbits (not Common Speech), meaning &#39;burrow&#39;; leave unchanged. It is a form that the Old English word <b>smygel</b> &#39;burrow&#39; might have had, if it had survived. The same element appears in Gollum&#39;s real name, <b>Sméagol</b>.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Tolkien <sup id="cite_ref-ATC_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ATC-6">[6]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

The names [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) and [Sméagol](/wiki/Gollum "Gollum") are etymologically related.

## In real world {#In_real_world}

"Smial" is a term used by [Tolkien fans](/wiki/Tolkien_fan "Tolkien fan") to refer to divisions of Tolkien Societies.
