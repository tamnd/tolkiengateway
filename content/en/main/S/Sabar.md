<i><b>sabar</b></i> (or <i><b>habar</b></i>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[note 1]</a></sup> is an unattested [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word likely meaning "delved mine", being reconstructed from an element found in the compound names <i><a href="/wiki/Anghabar" title="Anghabar">Ang<b>habar</b></a></i> and <i><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Nornhabar" title="Nornhabar">Norn<b>habar</b></a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-DW_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DW-2">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[2]</a></sup>

Note that this word is debatable as it is seen only in these compounds. [Didier Willis](/wiki/Didier_Willis) has suggested that [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") perhaps reused the old [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) root SAPA "dig, excavate"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup> as a base for this word.<sup id="cite_ref-DW_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DW-2">[1]</a></sup> This seems likely, since SAP- and a derivative *sapnā* reappear in the 1950s' *Outline of Phonology* as the source for *samna* "delved hole, pit".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[4]</a></sup>
