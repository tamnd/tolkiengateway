**Sprites**, or **fays**, were creatures only mentioned fleetingly in the earliest versions of the tales of [Arda](../A/Arda.md) and linguistic writings of the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The sprites and fays were part of what apparently was a three-fold division of the lesser [Ainu](../A/Ainur.md) spirits: [sylphs](Sylphs.md) (spirits of the air), sprites (spirits of the earth), and [water spirits](../M/Mermaids.md):<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>About them fared a great host who are the sprites of trees and woods, of dale and forest and mountain-side, or those that sing amid the grass at morning and chant among the standing corn at eve.<br/>
<br/>
...their number is very great: yet must they not be confused with the Eldar, for they were born before the world and are older than its oldest, and are not of it, but laugh at it much, for had they not somewhat to do with its making, so that it is for the most part a play for them.
</p>
</blockquote>

The poem *[An Evening in Tavrobel](../A/An_Evening_in_Tavrobel.md)* mentions tiny, gleaming [spirits](Spirits.md) that appear dancing<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> alluding to sprites or fays.

## Names and divisions {#Names_and_divisions}

While "sprites" and "fays" seem to have been the most common names for these creatures, other names were also used: <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownie_(folklore)" title="wikipedia:Brownie (folklore)">brownies</a></b>, <b><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixie" title="wikipedia:Pixie">pixies</a></b>, and **[leprawns](../L/Leprawns.md)**.<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup>

A basic division of four groups of sprites and fays, with their [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) names, are given as follow:

1. <i><b><a href="../N/Nermir.md" title="Nermir">Nermir</a></b></i>: "fays of the meads";<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-4">[4]</a></sup> [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) *nermil*: "a fay that haunts meadows and riverbanks"<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup>
2. <i><b><a href="../T/Tavari.md" title="Tavari">Tavari</a></b></i>: "sprites of trees and woods",<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup> or "fays of the woods";<sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-4">[4]</a></sup> [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) *tavor*: "a wood fay"<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup> ([Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) *tavar*, *tavarni* "dale-sprites", [root](Sundocarme.md) [TAVA](../T/TAW.md);<sup id="cite_ref-Lexicon_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lexicon-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LT1App_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1App-6">[6]</a></sup> the plural noun *tavárin*, nominative of *tavārin* "fay(s) of the woods"<sup id="cite_ref-PE16_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE16-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>)
3. <i><b><a href="../N/Nandini.md" title="Nandini">Nandini</a></b></i>: "fays of the valleys";<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-4">[4]</a></sup> [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md) *nandir*: fay of the country" ([Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) *nandin*)<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup>
4. <i><b><a href="../O/Orossi.md" title="Orossi">Orossi</a></b></i>: "fays of the mountains"<sup id="cite_ref-LT1-66_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-66-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LT1Index_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1Index-4">[4]</a></sup>

In a poem appear the *[oromandi](../O/Oromandi.md)*, "wood-spirits".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Some other mentions of fays seem rather to be referring to individuals:

- **[Ailinónë](../A/Ailinónë.md)**, a fairy who dwelt in a lily on a pool<sup id="cite_ref-Lexicon_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lexicon-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **[Nardi](../N/Nardi.md)**, a flower-fairy<sup id="cite_ref-Lexicon_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lexicon-5">[5]</a></sup>
- **[Sacha](Sacha.md)**, a fire-fay<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **[Tavros](<../T/Tavros_(wood-fay).md>)**, "chief wood-fay, *the Blue Spirit of the Woods*"<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
- **[Tethil](../T/Tethil.md)**, a fay who dwelt in a [poppy](../P/Poppies.md)<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **[Tevildo](../T/Tevildo.md)**, an "evil fay in [beastlike](../C/Cats.md) shape"<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup>
- **[Tinfang](../T/Tinfang.md)**, the flutist (surnamed *Gwarbilin*, Birdward); a fay<sup id="cite_ref-PE11_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE11-2">[2]</a></sup>
- **[Tû](<../T/Tû_(king_of_the_Hisildi).md>)**, a fay, and the king of the [Hisildi](../H/Hisildi.md)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
- **[Uolë](../U/Uolë.md)**, a [Moon](../M/Moon.md)-fay<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup>
- **[Velindo](../V/Velindo.md)**, a fay of [Aulë](../A/Aulë.md)'s folk<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[14]</a></sup>
- **[Wendelin](../W/Wendelin.md)**, a fay,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[15]</a></sup> and the Queen of the [Woodland Elves](../I/Ilkorindi.md)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">[16]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Sprites and fays are not mentioned in the later versions of the [legendarium](../L/Legendarium.md). Many of the classes of beings referred to in *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)* were superseded by the concept of the [Maiar](../M/Maiar.md), which were not introduced until the reworking of the *[Annals of Valinor](../A/Annals_of_Valinor.md)* into the *[Annals of Aman](../A/Annals_of_Aman.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-MRP2b_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MRP2b-17">[17]</a></sup>

Tolkien had said that as a child (and his children in turn) he always disliked "flower-[fairies](../F/Fairies.md) and fluttering sprites with antennae", a tradition that he traced back to <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Drayton" title="wikipedia:Michael Drayton">Michael Drayton</a>'s *Nimphidia, the Court of Faery*, which he considered "one of the worst ever written".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> In his youth he had written the poem *[Goblin Feet](../G/Goblin_Feet.md)* for [*Oxford Poetry* 1915](../O/Oxford_Poetry_1915.md), mentioning "fairy lanterns", gnomes and leprechauns; at some later point Tolkien mentioned that soon after (c. [1915](../_/1915.md)) he "started to fervently dislike" such whimsical elements and denounced his poem as an "unhappy little thing".<sup id="cite_ref-LT1_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-19">[19]</a></sup>
