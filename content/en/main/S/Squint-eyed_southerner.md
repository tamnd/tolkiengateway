The **squint-eyed southerner** was a nameless brigand driven from [Dunland](/wiki/Dunland), rumored to have a strain of [Orc](/wiki/Orcs "Orcs")-blood within his veins. He initially acted as one of the corrupted [Wizard](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards") [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman)'s most trusted agents and spies, before being compelled into the service of the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) as an informant.

## History {#History}

Hailing from Dunland, the squint-eyed southerner was banished by his fellow [hillmen](/wiki/Dunlendings "Dunlendings") due to a rumor that he had Orc-blood coursing through his veins (implying he may have been one of the [Half-orcs](/wiki/Half-orcs) or [Goblin-men](/wiki/Goblin-men)).<sup id="cite_ref-UT10_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT10-2">[2]</a></sup>

Having entered the employ of the Wizard Saruman, the southerner was at one point stationed on the borders of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") to negotiate the purchase of "[leaf](/wiki/Pipe-weed "Pipe-weed")" and other supplies which were stored in [Isengard](/wiki/Isengard) in preparation for the [war](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring "War of the Ring"). The squint-eyed southerner had been instructed by his master to infiltrate the Shire and learn of any [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) who had departed recently in hopes of finding the [Ring-bearer](/wiki/Ring-bearer). The southerner was well supplied with maps, lists of names, and notes concerning the Shire.

In September of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), the Dunlending was traveling back to the Shire when the black-robed [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) came upon him at the [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) crossing. When questioned, the southerner told the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king) everything, betraying the White Wizard; this proved Saruman was withholding information from his own master, [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron). The Lord of [Morgul](/wiki/Minas_Morgul "Minas Morgul") was made aware of the name [Baggins](/wiki/Baggins_Family "Baggins Family") and [Hobbiton](/wiki/Hobbiton) was singled out for immediate visit and inquiry.

The Witch-king put the [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) of Fear on the southerner and sent him to [Bree](/wiki/Bree) as a spy, warning him he now answered to the [Red Eye](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron "Eye of Sauron") and not the [White Hand](/wiki/White_Hand). The Dunlending met up with [Bill Ferny](/wiki/Bill_Ferny) and at the [Prancing Pony](/wiki/Prancing_Pony) on [September 29th](/wiki/September_29 "September 29"), the two of them saw [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) vanish into thin air. After reporting the information to the Nazgûl, the Nine Black Riders came to attack that night.<sup id="cite_ref-UT10_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT10-2">[2]</a></sup>

Aragorn kept the Hobbits safe through the night. The next morning, the squint-eyed southerner was spotted inside Bill Ferny's house when the company set out to [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop).<sup id="cite_ref-FRI11_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRI11-1">[1]</a></sup>

Nothing else is recorded of the squint-eyed southerner. However in [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"), a large number of [Ruffians](/wiki/Ruffians) occupied the Shire and oppressed the Hobbits.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> It is possible the southerner was among them as many of Saruman's agents there resembled him. The Men were driven out of the Shire in the [Battle of Bywater](/wiki/Battle_of_Bywater) on [3 November](/wiki/3_November). He either met his death in the battle, or survived and fled away.

## Description {#Description}

<p>Just what my father meant to convey by the &#39;squint-eyed Southerner&#39; at Bree I&#39;m not sure. I don&#39;t think that he can possibly have meant that the man had &#39;slit-eyes&#39; (goblin-like). He may have meant that he actually had a squint (optical disorder), but that seems unnecessarily particular. So the likeliest meaning, I think, is that the man didn&#39;t look straight, but obliquely, watchfully, sideways, suggesting craftiness and crookedness.
</p>
