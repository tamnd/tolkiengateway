The **Song of Parting** was [Beren Erchamion](/wiki/Beren "Beren")'s song of farewell to [Lúthien Tinúviel](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien "Lúthien"). It was sung by him in the *[Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian)* as he left her for [Angband](/wiki/Angband), even as he looked across [Anfauglith](/wiki/Anfauglith).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> It is arguably among the best parts of the Lay, as a part of it was included in the *[Quenta Silmarillion](/wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion)*.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## In *The Silmarillion* {#In_The_Silmarillion}

A part of the song is given in the Chapter 19 "[Of Beren and Lúthien](/wiki/Of_Beren_and_L%C3%BAthien)" of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*. This is also included in the third edition of the [*The Road Goes Ever On*](</wiki/The_Road_Goes_Ever_On_(book)> "The Road Goes Ever On (book)"), as the 9th song, with music by [Donald Swann](/wiki/Donald_Swann).

<br><br><br><br><br>

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Farewell sweet earth and northern sky,<br/>
for ever blest, since here did lie<br/>
and here with lissom limbs did run<br/>
beneath the <a href="/wiki/Moon" title="Moon">Moon</a>, beneath the <a href="/wiki/Sun" title="Sun">Sun</a>,<br/>
Lúthien Tinúviel<br/>
more fair than mortal tongue can tell.<br/>
Though all to ruin fell the world<br/>
and were dissolved and backward hurled<br/>
unmade into the old abyss,<br/>
yet were its making good, for this—<br/>
the dawn, the dusk, the earth, the sea—<br/>
that Lúthien on a time should be!
</p>
</div>

## In *The Lays of Beleriand* {#In_The_Lays_of_Beleriand}

The full song is given in *[The Lays of Beleriand](/wiki/The_Lays_of_Beleriand)*, part III "[The Lay of Leithian](/wiki/The_Lay_of_Leithian): [Canto XI](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_XI "Lay of Leithian Canto XI")".

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>    Farewell now here, ye leaves of trees,<br/>
your music in the morning breeze!<br/>
Farewell now blade and bloom and grass<br/>
that see the changing seasons pass;<br/>
ye waters murmuring over stone,<br/>
and meres that silent stand alone!<br/>
Farewell now mountain, vale, and plain!<br/>
Farewell now wind and frost and rain,<br/>
and mist and cloud, and heaven&#39;s air;<br/>
ye star and moon so blinding-fair<br/>
that still shall look down from the sky<br/>
on the wide earth, though Beren die—<br/>
though Beren die not, and yet deep,<br/>
deep, whence comes of those that weep<br/>
no dreadful echo, lie and choke<br/>
in everlasting dark and smoke.<br/>
    Farewell sweet earth and northern sky,<br/>
for ever blest, since here did lie,<br/>
and here with lissom limbs did run,<br/>
beneath the moon, beneath the sun,<br/>
Lúthien Tinúviel<br/>
more fair than mortal tongue can tell.<br/>
Though all to ruin fell the world,<br/>
and were dissolved and backward hurled<br/>
unmade into the old abyss,<br/>
yet were its making good, for this—<br/>
the dawn, the dusk, the earth, the sea—<br/>
that Lúthien on a time should be!
</p>
</div>
