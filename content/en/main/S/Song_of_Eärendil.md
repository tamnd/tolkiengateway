"**Eärendil was a mariner**", also known as <i><b>The Short Lay of Eärendel: Eärendillinwë</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-F_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-F-1">[1]</a></sup> or just the <i><b>Eärendillinwë</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Final_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Final-2">[2]</a></sup> was a song written by [Bilbo](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins "Bilbo Baggins") and [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn), and sung by Bilbo when the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) reached [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

It is believed that the song is a reworking of an earlier nonsensical poem *[Errantry](/wiki/Errantry)*, most likely also composed by Bilbo after his return from the [Quest for Erebor](/wiki/Quest_for_Erebor), lightheartedly incorporating Elvish elements. Perhaps because Bilbo was proud of *Errantry'*s metrical devices, he transformed and (incongruously) applied that poem to the legends of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Aragorn's contributions included his request for a [green jewel](/wiki/Elfstone "Elfstone") in the song; Bilbo obeyed, but inaccurately referred to it as an emerald.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Division of the poem {#Division_of_the_poem}

1. An introduction to [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) and the building of his [ship](/wiki/Vingilot "Vingilot")
2. A detailed description of him, the final line of which was supposedly influenced by [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn)
3. A description of his fruitless wanderings in search of [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor)
4. How [Elwing](/wiki/Elwing) his wife flew to him in the form of a bird and bound the jewel to his forehead
5. How he found Valinor
6. Concerning his stay there
7. How they built a new ship for him, and how [Elbereth](/wiki/Varda "Varda") gave him wings
8. How he left Valinor
9. How he passed over [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and became a [star](/wiki/Star_of_E%C3%A4rendil "Star of Eärendil")

## Metre and assonance {#Metre_and_assonance}

The metre is iambic tetrameter.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[note 1]</a></sup> The poem is structured according to trisyllabic assonances, inherited from "Errantry".<sup id="cite_ref-l133_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l133-7">[6]</a></sup> These are units of three syllables in which the first and third syllables of one unit, which are accented, rhyme or off-rhyme with the first and third syllables of another. The off-rhymes are typically assonances, that is, matching vowel sounds, though some only have similar vowels, and often the consonants are similar or the same as well.

For example,

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>The winds of wrath came <span style="color: red;">driving him</span>,<br/>
and <span style="color: red;">blindly in</span> the <span style="color: blue;">foam he fled</span><br/>
from west to east and <span style="color: green;">errandless</span>,<br/>
un<span style="color: green;">heralded</span> he <span style="color: blue;">homeward sped</span>.
</p>
</div>

Here "fled" rhymes with "sped", but in addition, the vowel of "foam" is the same as the first vowel of "homeward". Thus the matching units are "foam he fled" and "homeward sped". Another pair of matching units is "driving him" and "blindly in". Another is "errandless" and "\[un\]heralded".

The pattern is the same throughout the poem. In every set of four lines, the last three syllables of the first line match the second, third and fourth of the second line. The third and fourth lines have the same kind of matching. And the last three syllables of the second line match those of the fourth line, usually with an exact rhyme at the end.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[note 2]</a></sup>

An example where the vowels don't match exactly is "across" and "passed", though the sounds are similar and the "s" is repeated. The first four lines are especially atypical.

## Poem excerpts {#Poem_excerpts}

### First stanza {#First_stanza}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p><a href="/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil" title="Eärendil">Eärendil</a> was a <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Mariners&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Mariners (page does not exist)">mariner</a><br/>
that tarried in <a href="/wiki/Arvernien" title="Arvernien">Arvernien</a>;<br/>
he built a <a href="/wiki/Vingilot" title="Vingilot">boat of timber</a> felled<br/>
in <a href="/wiki/Nimbrethil" title="Nimbrethil">Nimbrethil</a> to journey in;<br/>
her sails he wove of <a href="/wiki/Silver" title="Silver">silver</a> fair,<br/>
of silver were her lanterns made,<br/>
her prow he fashioned like a <a href="/wiki/Swans" title="Swans">swan</a>,<br/>
and light upon her banners laid.
</p>
</div>

### Second stanza {#Second_stanza}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>In panoply of ancient kings,<br/>
in chainéd <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Rings" title="Rings">rings</a> he armoured him;<br/>
his shining shield was scored with <a href="/wiki/Runes" title="Runes">runes</a><br/>
to ward all wounds and harm from him;<br/>
his bow was made of dragon-horn,<br/>
his arrows shorn of ebony,<br/>
of silver was his habergeon,<br/>
his scabbard of chalcedony;<br/>
his sword of steel was valiant,<br/>
of <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Adamant" title="Adamant">adamant</a> his helmet tall,<br/>
an eagle-plume upon his crest,<br/>
upon his breast an <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Emerald&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Emerald (page does not exist)">emerald</a>.
</p>
</div>

### Last stanza {#Last_stanza}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>And over <a href="/wiki/Middle-earth" title="Middle-earth">Middle-earth</a> he passed<br/>
and heard at last the weeping sore<br/>
of women and of elven-maids<br/>
in <a href="/wiki/Elder_Days" title="Elder Days">Elder Days</a>, in years of yore.<br/>
But on him mighty doom was laid,<br/>
till <a href="/wiki/Moon" title="Moon">Moon</a> should fade, an orbéd <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Star" title="Star">star</a><br/>
to pass, and tarry never more<br/>
on <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Hither_Shores" title="Hither Shores">Hither Shores</a> where <a href="/wiki/Mortals" title="Mortals">mortals</a> are;<br/>
for ever still a herald on<br/>
on errand that should never rest<br/>
to bear his shining lamp afar,<br/>
the <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Flammifer" title="Flammifer">Flammifer</a> of <a href="/wiki/Westernesse" title="Westernesse">Westernesse</a>.
</p>
</div>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

*Eärendil* derives from Tolkien's earlier poem *[Errantry](/wiki/Errantry)* (published [1933](/wiki/1933)). In the drafts of *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*, *Errantry* evolved in stages, and reached its final published form after fifteen revisions.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[7]</a></sup> *Errantry* was eventually republished in *[The Adventures of Tom Bombadil](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Bombadil)*. Both *Errantry* and *Eärendil* use a sound pattern of Tolkien's own invention: trisyllabic assonances or near-assonances.<sup id="cite_ref-l133_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l133-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ABcomm_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABcomm-10">[8]</a></sup> This fact passed into the legendarium, as the Preface to *The Adventures of Tom Bombadil* says that Bilbo was probably proud of his metre and used it as a model for *Earendil*.

## Other versions of the Legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_Legendarium}

Among the various versions of the poem, there is one version which [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) believed that his father most likely intended for publication instead of the above version, which was published in *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Final_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Final-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-F_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-F-1">[1]</a></sup>

The title *Eärendillinwë* (sometimes *Earendillínwë* with a long *í*) is a title that appears in various drafts but not in the published text of the *Fellowship*.<sup id="cite_ref-F_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-F-1">[1]</a></sup> The spelling <i>Earend<b>e</b>l</i> belongs to earlier versions of the Legendarium, as when the character first appears in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.
