The **Starkhorn** was one of the largest of the [White Mountains](../W/White_Mountains.md). It was a snow-clad peak that rose in the inner regions of the mountain range. From its feet sprang the [Snowbourn](Snowbourn.md), whose widening valley ran northwards through the mountains to emerge onto the plains of [Rohan](../R/Rohan.md). The [Rohirrim](../R/Rohirrim.md) called that valley [Harrowdale](../H/Harrowdale.md), and built their courts of [Edoras](../E/Edoras.md) at its mouth, from where the lonely white peak of the Starkhorn could be seen along the straight length of the valley. On a cliff above the valley they found the refuge of [Dunharrow](../D/Dunharrow.md) which had been built by [Pre-Númenórean Men](../P/Pre-Númenóreans.md) of the White Mountains.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

It means "horn standing up stiff like a spike" and represents [Rohanese](../R/Rohanese.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>

As [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) notes, to an English reader *stark* now has implications of nakedness and grimness, not originally present in the word.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-2">[2]</a></sup>
