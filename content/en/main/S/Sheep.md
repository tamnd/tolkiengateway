**Sheep** were woolly grazing animals. The [Folk of Marach](../H/House_of_Hador.md) migrated to [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) with flocks of sheep and [goats](../G/Goats.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> They were especially common in the [Númenórean](../N/Númenóreans.md) region of [Emerië](../E/Emerië.md),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and (in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md)) on the [Barrow-downs](../B/Barrow-downs.md) and in the [Vales of Anduin](../V/Vales_of_Anduin.md).

## Other names {#Other_names}

The [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) word for "sheep" is <i><b>máma</b></i>, derived from [Primitive Quendian](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md) *māmā*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) also suggested the [Common Eldarin](../C/Common_Eldarin.md) form *mbā* ("sheep").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s early conceptions of [Elvish](../E/Elvish.md) languages, the words for "sheep" are <i><b>moa</b></i> in [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> and <i><b>moth</b></i> (pl. *mothrim* or *mothin*) in [Gnomish](../G/Gnomish.md).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

There are also words for "lamb": *eulë* ("little lamb" is *eulistsë*) in Qenya,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> and *iol* ("little lamb, lambkin" is *iolinc*) in Gnomish.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>2002: <a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings%253A_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(video_game).md" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring (video game)"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i> (video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Sheep are found on the fields around <a href="../B/Bagshot_Row.md" title="Bagshot Row">Bagshot Row</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online.md" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Sheep are found throughout <a href="../T/The_Shire.md" title="The Shire">the Shire</a> and <a href="../B/Bree-land.md" title="Bree-land">Bree-land</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2012-2014: <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit_(film_series).md" title="The Hobbit (film series)">The Hobbit (film series)</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Though the dwarves do not use horses and occasionally ride bought or rented ponies, they are depicted as keeping a fictional species of large sheep as a form of dwarven livestock. These sheep are depicted briefly in the prologue of the first film, <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_An_Unexpected_Journey.md" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">An Unexpected Journey</a></i>, as part of the refugee caravans of dwarves exiled from <a class="mw-redirect" href="../E/Erebor.md" title="Erebor">Erebor</a>, and then much more prominently in <i><a href="../T/The_Hobbit%253A_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies.md" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i> During the titular battle, specially trained sheep are ridden by some of the dwarven sodiers and warriors from the <a href="../I/Iron_Hills.md" title="Iron Hills">Iron Hills</a>, used as an unusual form of cavalry capable of fighting in mountainous terrain. These sheep are referred to as &#34;war rams&#34; (albeit Dáin Ironfoot also calls them &#34;goats&#34;, perhaps colloquially). <a class="mw-redirect" href="../T/Thorin_Oakenshield.md" title="Thorin Oakenshield">Thorin Oakenshield</a> borrows a war ram for a hasty gallop towards <a href="../R/Ravenhill.md" title="Ravenhill">Ravenhill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 2]</a></sup></dd></dl>
