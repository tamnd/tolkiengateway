**Sharkey** was the name by which [Saruman](Saruman.md) was known among his followers at [Isengard](../I/Isengard.md) and later among the [ruffians](../R/Ruffians.md) who overran [the Shire](../T/The_Shire.md) at his behest. Saruman apparently did not object to its use, regarding it as a "sign of affection".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

A footnote to "[The Scouring of the Shire](../T/The_Scouring_of_the_Shire.md)" states that the name probably derived from the [Orkish](../O/Orkish.md) word *[sharkû](Sharkû.md)*, meaning "old man".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien describes it in "[Nomenclature of *The Lord of the Rings*](../N/Nomenclature_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)" as an "\[Orkish\] nickname modified to fit [CS](../C/Common_Speech.md)", which in the books is represented by English, hence the use of the English diminutive ending <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-ey#Etymology_2" title="wiktionary:-ey">-<i>ey</i></a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Development {#Development}

In an early version of "The Scouring of the Shire", the name Sharkey is borne by one of the ruffians who accost Frodo and his friends at [Bywater](../B/Bywater.md). Later, Tolkien applied the name to a head ruffian residing at [Bag End](../B/Bag_End.md). The head ruffian was replaced in the final version of the chapter by Saruman, who then took on the name.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
