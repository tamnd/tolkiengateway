The **Straits of the World** was a strait of mountainous land south-east of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) that narrowly separated the [Great Gulf](/wiki/Great_Gulf) of [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer) from the [Inland Sea of Helcar](/wiki/Sea_of_Helcar "Sea of Helcar") in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-Ambar_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ambar-1">[1]</a></sup> The Straits of the World appear only in early writings<sup id="cite_ref-Ambar_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ambar-1">[1]</a></sup> before Tolkien envisioned the wide lands of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador), [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor), and [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) that lay east and south of Beleriand.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

[Karen Wynn Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad) speculated in her work *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth)* that the [Sea of Helcar](/wiki/Sea_of_Helcar) drained into the [Great Gulf](/wiki/Great_Gulf) at the end of the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) and that the <a class="mw-selflink selflink">Straits of the World</a> became the river [Poros](/wiki/Poros). Fonstand's interpretation was predicated on the idea that [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) was created in the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) where the Sea of Helcar once lay.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> However, in *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)* (which was published after Fontstad's *Atlas*), [Melkor](/wiki/Melkor) was said to have created [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) in Mordor during the "long First Age".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Additionally, it is told in *[Unfinished Tales](/wiki/Unfinished_Tales)* that the migration of the [Drúedain](/wiki/Dr%C3%BAedain) from [Hildórien](/wiki/Hild%C3%B3rien) brought them westward through lands south of Mordor before they turned northward, entering [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien) and becoming the first [Men](/wiki/Men) to cross the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>
