The [Elves](../E/Elves.md) were a race subjected to many sunderings and divided into many tribes and peoples. The name **[Quendi](../Q/Quendi.md)** refers to *all* Elves, but even from their [beginning](../A/Awakening_of_the_Elves.md), elvenkind was divided.

## Minyar, Tatyar &amp; Nelyar {#Minyar,_Tatyar_&_Nelyar}

The unbegotten Elves who awoke at [Cuiviénen](../C/Cuiviénen.md) were divided into three tribes, distinguished by appearance and temperament.

- The **[Minyar](../M/Minyar.md)** were golden-haired and loved poetry.
- The **[Tatyar](../T/Tatyar.md)** had dark hair and grey eyes and loved craft and knowledge. They were noted as being especially quarrelsome.<sup id="cite_ref-WJ380_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ380-1">[1]</a></sup>
- The **[Nelyar](../N/Nelyar.md)** were dark or silver of hair and loved song (this was the source of their alternate name, **[Lindar](../L/Lindar.md)**, meaning "Singers"), the woods, and the Sea.

## Eldar and Avari {#Eldar_and_Avari}

Some time after the Elves awakened, the [Vala](../V/Valar.md) [Oromë](../O/Oromë.md) discovered them and invited them to come west to live in [Aman](../A/Aman.md). That invitation and the [Great Journey](../G/Great_Journey.md) that followed split the Elves into two main groups and many minor ones, which were never fully reunited.

The **[Avari](../A/Avari.md)** (the "Unwilling") were those who refused the summons of Oromë. Half of the original Avari were of the [Tatyar](../T/Tatyar.md) and the other half were of the [Nelyar](../N/Nelyar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-WJ380_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ380-1">[1]</a></sup>

The **[Eldar](../E/Eldar.md)** were those who accepted the summons. Their name, "People of the Stars", was given to them by Oromë in [their own language](../P/Primitive_Quendian.md). All the Minyar accepted the summons to Valinor and became known as the **[Vanyar](../V/Vanyar.md)**. Those of the Tatyar and Nelyar who embarked on the Great Journey would come to be known as the **[Noldor](../N/Noldor.md)** and **[Teleri](../T/Teleri.md)** respectively.

### Sunderings of the Teleri {#Sunderings_of_the_Teleri}

Unlike the Vanyar and Noldor, the Teleri were subject to multiple sunderings along the Great Journey:

- Those who feared to cross the [Misty Mountains](../M/Misty_Mountains.md) and stayed in the vales of the [River Anduin](../A/Anduin.md) were called the **[Nandor](../N/Nandor.md)** ("Those Who Go Back").<sup id="cite_ref-WJ412_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ412-2">[2]</a></sup>
    - A splinter group of the Nandor left the vales of Anduin under the leadership of [Denethor](../D/Denethor_of_the_Nandor.md) and came to Beleriand before the Sun first rose. These were called the **Laiquendi** ("[Green-elves](../G/Green-elves.md)").<sup id="cite_ref-WJ380_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ380-1">[1]</a></sup>

- Those who reached [Beleriand](../B/Beleriand.md) west of the [Blue Mountains](../B/Blue_Mountains.md) but did not make the crossing to Aman were called the **[Sindar](Sindar.md)** ("Grey-elves").
    - Many chose to remain behind in order to look for their lord [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md), who disappeared near the end of the journey. These were known as the **[Eglath](../E/Eglath.md)** ("Forsaken"), because they were left behind when their kindred crossed the Sea.
    - Those who came to the shores of the Great Sea but decided to stay there or, like [Nowë](../C/Círdan.md), intended to cross to Aman but arrived too late to board the [island ferry](../T/Tol_Eressëa.md), were called the **[Falathrim](../F/Falas.md)** ("People of the Shore").

- The Teleri under the lordship of [Olwë](../O/Olwë.md) who crossed the [Great Sea](../B/Belegaer.md) to reach Aman were known as the **[Falmari](../F/Falmari.md)** ("Wave-folk").

## Calaquendi and Moriquendi {#Calaquendi_and_Moriquendi}

The Elves who completed the journey to Aman – the Vanyar, the Noldor, and the Falmari – referred to themselves as **[Calaquendi](../C/Calaquendi.md)** ("Elves of Light"). They referred to the Avari, Nandor, and Sindar as **[Moriquendi](../M/Moriquendi.md)** ("Elves of Darkness") in recognition of the fact that they never saw the light of the [Two Trees](../T/Two_Trees.md). The largest part of the Moriquendi were Nelyarin, since half of the original Avari were so, as were all of the Nandor and Sindar.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

### Amanyar and Úmanyar {#Amanyar_and_Úmanyar}

After the [Darkening of Valinor](../D/Darkening_of_Valinor.md), most of the Noldor returned to Middle-earth with [Fëanor](../F/Fëanor.md) and some remained there until the end of the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md). These became known as the **[Exiles](../E/Exile_of_the_Noldor.md)**, and they lived near to the Sindar for the rest of their time in Middle-earth. The Sindar were insulted by the appellation "Moriquendi," so the Exiles instead generally referred to them as **[Úmanyar](../_/Úmanyar.md)** ("Those Not of Aman"). Over the ages, use of this term grew to include all those Elves who started on the Great Journey but did not complete it. The Exiles referred to themselves and those who still dwelt in Valinor as **[Amanyar](../A/Amanyar.md)** ("Those of Aman").

### Thingol {#Thingol}

Thingol was an exception to the usual overlap of the categories Calaquendi with Amanyar and Moriquendi with Úmanyar. This is due to the fact that he was one of the original three Elven ambassadors to Valinor and saw the light of the Two Trees, but did not complete the Great Journey and never dwelt in Valinor. As such, he alone was both Calaquendë and Úmanya.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Reunions of the Elves {#Reunions_of_the_Elves}

In the great forests of Middle-earth, several new peoples were born from the unions of tribes that had once been sundered.

### Silvan Elves {#Silvan_Elves}

The **[Silvan Elves](Silvan_Elves.md)** were a composite tribe of Nandor and Avari that emerged in the forests of [Wilderland](../R/Rhovanion.md) (notably [Eryn Galen](../E/Eryn_Galen.md) and [Lindórinand](../L/Lindórinand.md)) before the end of the First Age.

The Nandor had lived in the forests that girdled the upper vales of the Anduin since the time they turned back from the Great Journey. Over time, tribes of Avarin Elves spreading westward from Cuiviénen reached the Anduin as well, where they reunited with their Nandorin kin. These peoples effectively "became merged together."<sup id="cite_ref-WJ380_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ380-1">[1]</a></sup>

By the end of the First Age, some Silvan Elves had migrated as far south as the [Ethir Anduin](../E/Ethir_Anduin.md) and [Edhellond](../E/Edhellond.md).

### Galadhrim {#Galadhrim}

The **Galadhrim** ("Tree-people")<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> were a composite tribe of Silvan Elves, Sindar, and Noldor that emerged in the forest of [Lindórinand](../L/Lindórinand.md) in the [Second Age](Second_Age.md). It is unclear when exactly the residents of Lindórinand began to refer to themselves as "Galadhrim," but this name came to include many elves of diverse origin that arrived in Lindórinand in multiple waves of immigration.

After the [sinking of Beleriand](../W/War_of_Wrath.md), the Elves who had lived there and did not wish to leave for Valinor had to find new homes. Those Sindar and Laiquendi who preferred the forests to the sea shores (or who wished to distance themselves from the remaining Noldorin Exiles) went eastward to reunite with their Silvan kin who dwelt in the great forests of Eryn Galen and Lindórinand. In both forest realms, the native Silvan Elves took Sindar for their kings ([Oropher](../O/Oropher.md) and [Amdír](../A/Amdír.md), respectively) and underwent a process of "Sindarization" as they absorbed the culture of the Beleriandic immigrants. There were also Noldorin immigrants to the forest realms at this time, though evidently lesser in cultural impact than the Sindar.<sup id="cite_ref-Galadriel_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Galadriel-6">[6]</a></sup>

Separately from Eryn Galen, Lindórinand experienced a second great wave of immigration after the [Sack of Eregion](Sack_of_Eregion.md) in [S.A.](Second_Age.md) [1697](Second_Age_1697.md). Many Elves of Eregion, who were primarily Noldor,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> escaped [Sauron](Sauron.md) by fleeing through [Khazad-dûm](../K/Khazad-dûm.md) and emerged from the eastern side of the Misty Mountains onto Amdír's very doorstep. Some traveled down Anduin to Edhellond to depart Middle-earth, but others remained in Amdír's realm and dwelt there.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> By the time [Galadriel](../G/Galadriel.md) and [Celeborn](../C/Celeborn.md) took up the [Lord and Ladyship](../L/Lord_and_Lady_of_Lórien.md) of the realm in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1981](../T/Third_Age_1981.md), the Galadhrim had thoroughly absorbed these Noldorin refugees into their culture; there is no further mention of them as a distinct people within the Elves of Lórien.

## Other versions of the Legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_Legendarium}

The above ratio of the initial population of the Eldar, but without giving exact figures, derives from Tolkien's notes between [1959](../_/1959.md)-[1960](../_/1960.md), eventually published in a collection of essays titled *[Quendi and Eldar](../Q/Quendi_and_Eldar.md)*.

Around the same time, or perhaps earlier (1959) Tolkien wrote several drafts of Elvish proportions and populations for the outset (and in one case, at the conclusion) of the Great March. They were eventually published in *[The Nature of Middle-earth](../T/The_Nature_of_Middle-earth.md)*.
