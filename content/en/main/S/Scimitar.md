A **scimitar** is a type of curved [sword](/wiki/Sword "Sword") used by [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs)<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">[2]</a></sup> and [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim)<sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">[3]</a></sup>.

## History {#History}

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/b/b2/Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg/329px-Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="329" height="262" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/b/b2/Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg/494px-Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/b/b2/Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg/658px-Abe_Papakhian_-_Orcs_and_Uruks.jpg 2x" data-file-width="792" data-file-height="630"/></a><figcaption>Orcs with curved scimitars as depicted in Abe Papakhian&#39;s <i>The Fighting Uruk-hai and Orcs of Lugburz</i></figcaption></figure>

In the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), Orcs of [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) are said to have used "sickle scimitars"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and "reddened scimitars".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Scimitars were also wielded by Orcs during the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin) in [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [510](/wiki/First_Age_510 "First Age 510").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

The bodyguard of [Bolg](/wiki/Bolg) was said to consist of "Goblins" of huge size who wielded scimitars of steel in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

On [15 January](/wiki/15_January) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019")<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>, the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) found "orc-scimitars with blackened blades" in the [Chamber of Mazarbul](/wiki/Chamber_of_Mazarbul) shortly after entering [Moria](/wiki/Moria).<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">[1]</a></sup> With the exception of the broad blades of the [Orcs of Isengard](/wiki/Orcs_of_Isengard), Orcs are said to have usually carried "curved scimitars".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">[2]</a></sup>

Haradrim are said to have wielded scimitars during the [Battle of Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_Pelennor_Fields) on [15 March](/wiki/15_March) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019").<sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Scimitar is an English word that refers to a sword with curved blade which has an edge on the convex side, and is generally to describe curved swords used by Middle Eastern and North African swordsmen. It is borrowed from the Italian *scimitarra* which was likely borrowed, by uncertain origin, from the Persian *šamšer* ("sword, scimitar").<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>The &#34;Orc Scimitar&#34; and &#34;Goblin Scimitar&#34; cards were included in <a href="/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(Decipher)" title="The Fellowship of the Ring (Decipher)"><i>The Fellowship of the Ring</i></a> base set of the card game.</dd></dl>

<b>2001: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring</i></a></b>

<dl><dd>In <a href="/wiki/Peter_Jackson" title="Peter Jackson">Peter Jackson</a>&#39;s film series, the <a href="/wiki/Uruk-hai" title="Uruk-hai">Uruk-hai</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a> carry swords described in merchandise as &#34;Uruk-hai scimitars&#34;. This blade-type is notably carried by the Orc <a href="/wiki/Lurtz" title="Lurtz">Lurtz</a>. However, &#34;scimitar&#34; is a misnomer given that it is a straight blade with a spike at the end, and neither of these features are characteristic of historical scimitars.</dd></dl>

<b>2002: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers</i></a></b>

<dl><dd>The Uruk-hai of Saruman are shown wielding the straight-bladed &#34;Uruk-hai scimitar&#34; throughout the film.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King"><i>The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King</i></a></b>

<dl><dd>Swords closely resembling historical scimitars are clearly seen being used among the <a href="/wiki/Orcs_of_Mordor" title="Orcs of Mordor">Orcs of Mordor</a>, specifically when <a href="/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee" title="Samwise Gamgee">Sam</a> and <a href="/wiki/Frodo_Baggins" title="Frodo Baggins">Frodo</a> disguise themselves as Orcs in <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Mordor</a>. The costumes of the <a href="/wiki/Corsairs_of_Umbar" title="Corsairs of Umbar">Corsairs of Umbar</a> include curved swords, but they are not clearly visible in the film.</dd></dl>

<b>2003: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game">The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>The &#34;Isengard Scimitar&#34; card was included in the <i>Battle of Helms Deep</i> set of the card game.</dd></dl>

<b>2007: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Trading_Card_Game" title="The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game">The Lord of the Rings Trading Card Game</a></i></b>

<dl><dd>The &#34;Corsair Scimitar&#34; card was included in the <i>Treachery and Deceit</i> set of the card game.</dd></dl>
