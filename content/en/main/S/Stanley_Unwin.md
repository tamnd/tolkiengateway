Sir **Stanley Unwin** ([19 December](/wiki/19_December) [1884](/wiki/1884) – [13 October](/wiki/13_October) [1968](/wiki/1968)) was a British publisher who co-founded the [George Allen and Unwin](/wiki/Allen_and_Unwin "Allen and Unwin") house on [4 August](/wiki/4_August) [1914](/wiki/1914).

In [1936](/wiki/1936) [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) submitted *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* for publication, and Unwin paid his ten-year-old son [Rayner](/wiki/Rayner_Unwin "Rayner Unwin") a few pence to write a report on the manuscript. Rayner's favourable response prompted Unwin to publish the book. Once the book became a success Unwin asked Tolkien for a sequel, which eventually became *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*.

Tolkien wished to publish his mythology ([The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)) after *The Hobbit*. In late [1937](/wiki/1937) Unwin examined the Quenta Silmarillion, the [Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian), and other manuscripts, but he and critic Edward Crankshaw thought it was unsuitable for publication, especially in its current form.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup> Unwin didn't reject the *Silmarillion*, but instead of being published as a mythology, he proposed that it should rather be used as source to write "further books like *The Hobbit*".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-comp_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comp-5">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. lxxi</sup>

Tolkien liked Rayner but, as he said, didn't like much his father, despite their friendly relations. He resented the rejection of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)* and considered giving *The Lord of the Rings* to [another publisher](/wiki/Milton_Waldman "Milton Waldman") who would accept to publish both works; but he felt morally obliged to Unwin because of his promise for a sequel, and their personal friendship, and proceeded with him.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup> On the matter of publishing both works, Rayner suggested to his father to have one of Tolkien's sons to edit some parts of the *Silmarillion* for publication with the *Lord of the Rings* and later drop it. This infuriated Tolkien who put an ultimatum to Unwin, to which he replied "no" as he didn't have enough time.<sup id="cite_ref-comp_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comp-5">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxx</sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup> This started a feud between Tolkien and Allen &amp; Unwin.

Ultimately Tolkien wasn't able to reach an agreement with Waldman either, so he compromised.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[7]</a></sup> In November [1952](/wiki/1952) Unwin was on a business trip in the Far East when his son telegraphed him saying that publication of the *Rings* was the work of a genius but would be risky; to which Stanley replied "If you believe it is a work of genius, *then* you may lose a thousand pounds".<sup id="cite_ref-comp_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comp-5">[4]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:p. xxxii</sup>
