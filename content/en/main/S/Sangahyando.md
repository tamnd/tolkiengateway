**Sangahyando** was one of the great-grandsons of [Castamir](../C/Castamir.md) the Usurper.<sup id="cite_ref-Telemnar_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Telemnar-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

He and [Angamaitë](../A/Angamaitë.md), another great-grandson of Castamir, were leaders of the [Corsairs of Umbar](../C/Corsairs_of_Umbar.md).<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-2">[2]</a></sup> The name Sangahyando was possibly not his birth name, but a personal warrior name (or nickname). There was no need to assert his royal descent with this name, because his royal descent was clear.<sup id="cite_ref-L347_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L347-3">[3]</a></sup>

Angamaitë and Sangayando learned through spies that [Minardil](../M/Minardil.md), the [King of Gondor](../K/King_of_Gondor.md), was in the [Gondorian](../G/Gondor.md) port of [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md) and that he suspected no peril since his father had crushed Harad and Umbar.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-2">[2]</a></sup> In [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [1634](../T/Third_Age_1634.md)<sup id="cite_ref-TA1634_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1634-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-T4_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-T4-5">[5]</a></sup> the Corsairs of Umbar, led by Angamaitë and Sangayando, made a raid up the river [Anduin](../A/Anduin.md), killed king Minardil in Pelargir,<sup id="cite_ref-Telemnar_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Telemnar-1">[1]</a></sup> ravaged Pelargir<sup id="cite_ref-TA1634_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA1634-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-T4_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-T4-5">[5]</a></sup> and the coasts and escaped with great booty.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Sangahyando* is a [Quenya](../Q/Quenya.md) name. It literally means "Throng-cleaver"<sup id="cite_ref-L347_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L347-3">[3]</a></sup> (said to be interpretable as "hewer of hostile ranks"). The first element in the name is *sanga* ("press" or "throng") and the second element is *hyando* ("cleaver").<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-6">[6]</a></sup> A throng is a closely formed body of enemy soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In the manuscripts for what would later become appendix A and appendix B and in the first edition of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)* his name was *Sangahyanda*.<sup id="cite_ref-ElendilC_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ElendilC-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-T4_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-T4-5">[5]</a></sup> His name was changed to *Sangahyando* in the second edition of *The Lord of the Rings*.<sup id="cite_ref-PE17_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

In the *[Etymologies](../T/The_Etymologies.md)*, [Qenya](../Q/Qenya.md) *sangahyando* ("throng-cleaver") is said to be the name of a sword.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>
