**Lake Núrnen**<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Steward_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Steward-3">[3]</a></sup> also known as the **Sea of Núrnen** was an inland sea in the middle of [Nurn](/wiki/Nurn) in [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor).<sup id="cite_ref-Map_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-4">[4]</a></sup>

The lake was fed by four unnamed major rivers, of which two flowed from the [Ephel Dúath](/wiki/Ephel_D%C3%BAath) and two from a mountain spur that branched off from the [Ered Lithui](/wiki/Ered_Lithui) into Lake Núrnen<sup id="cite_ref-Map_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Map-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

It was referred to as a bitter sea<sup id="cite_ref-Gate_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gate-1">[1]</a></sup> and held dark sad water, but the area around it was fertile enough to support great fields that were cultivated by slaves.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-2">[2]</a></sup> An old meaning of sad is dark-coloured, in particular an unpleasant colour. Bitter is perhaps by analogy with sad or in the sense of unpalatable, referring to poisonous water from the rivers.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup>

After the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) King [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) freed the slaves of Mordor and gave them ownership of all the lands around Lake Núrnen.<sup id="cite_ref-Steward_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Steward-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Núrnen* is [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) for "sad-water"<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> or "dead water".<sup id="cite_ref-WPP_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WPP-9">[9]</a></sup> [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull), and [Paul Strack](/wiki/Paul_Strack) suggest that it is a compound of *[Nurn](/wiki/Nurn#Etymology)* (the name of the region in which the inland sea is located) and *[nen](/wiki/Nen "Nen")* ("water").<sup id="cite_ref-RC_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Eldamo_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eldamo-10">[10]</a></sup> [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") also noted the related [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) word *[guru](/wiki/Gurth "Gurth")* ("death").<sup id="cite_ref-WPP_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WPP-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Commentary {#Commentary}

In *[The Atlas of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Atlas_of_Middle-earth)*, [Karen Wynn Fonstad](/wiki/Karen_Wynn_Fonstad) assumed that the [Sea of Rhûn](/wiki/Sea_of_Rh%C3%BBn) and Sea of Núrnen were the remnants of the inland [Sea of Helcar](/wiki/Sea_of_Helcar). The atlas was however published before *[The Peoples of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Peoples_of_Middle-earth)*, where it was revealed that the [Sea of Rhûn](/wiki/Sea_of_Rh%C3%BBn) existed already in the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), as an apparently different body of water than the Sea of Helcar.

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1982-1997: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The river that flows northeastwards from the southern part of the western <i>Ephel Dúath</i> is called <i>Dúaduin</i> on a map of the plateau of Gorgoroth and the western part of <i>Nurn</i>. A smaller river that flows southeast in the direction of the western part of Lake Núrnen from a source in the mountain spur that branches off to the northeast in the southern part of the western <i>Ephel Dúath</i>, a source in the mountain spur that branches off to the southwest from the eastern part of the <i>Ered Litui</i> and a source roughly in the middle of the gap in between those mountain spurs is called <i>Ruinen</i> on that map.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup> The longest river in <i>Mordor</i> that flows (southeastwards and then) southwestwards from the northern part of the mountain spur that branches off to the southwest from the eastern part of the <i>Ered Litui</i> to Lake Núrnen is called <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Sirilith&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sirilith (page does not exist)">Sirilith</a></i> in the Geographic Features section of the Mordor Gazetteer that was made by MERP fans. A river that flows (southeastards) from the southern part of the mountain spur that branches off to the southwest from the eastern part of the <i>Ered Litui</i> to Lake Núrnen is called <i><a href="/wiki/Caranduin" title="Caranduin">Caranduin</a></i> in that Gazetteer. A river that flows (northwestwards and then) northeastwards from the eastern part of the southern <i>Ephel Dúath</i> to Lake Núrnen is called <i><a href="/wiki/Culduin" title="Culduin">Culduin</a></i> in that Gazetteer. A river that flows northeast from the <a href="/wiki/Nargil_Pass" title="Nargil Pass">Nargil Pass</a> (in the central part of the southern part of the Ephel Dúath) to Lake Núrnen was called <i>Nargil River</i> in that Gazetteer. The Gazetteer also includes <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gurthrant_River&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gurthrant River (page does not exist)">Gurthrant River</a></i> as an alternative name of the <i>Dúaduin</i> and states that the <i>Dúaduin</i> and the <i>Ruinen</i> flowed into Lake Núrnen.</dd></dl>

**2014: [Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor](/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor):**

<dl><dd>Queen <a href="/wiki/Marwen" title="Marwen">Marwen</a> is the queen of the Tribesmen of Núrnen and rules over the Sea of Núrnen.</dd></dl>

<b>2022: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power" title="The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power">The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>In the second <a href="/wiki/Adrift" title="Adrift">episode</a>, when <a href="/wiki/Bronwyn" title="Bronwyn">Bronwyn</a> tries to warn the <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Southlanders</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tirharad" title="Tirharad">Tirharad</a> in <a href="/wiki/Waldreg" title="Waldreg">Waldreg&#39;s</a> tavern of the ruin of <a href="/wiki/Hordern" title="Hordern">Hordern</a>, Waldreg notes that <b>Crookfinger Lake</b> has always been spewing vapors, possibly referring to Lake Núrnen as it is the only and the most well known body of water nearby. The dark and damp forested lake that <a href="/wiki/Arondir" title="Arondir">Arondir</a> was suddenly seized from may possibly be a sub lake of Crookfinger Lake.</dd></dl>
