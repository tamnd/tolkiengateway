"**Sing now, ye people of the Tower of Anor**" is a proclamation uttered by a great [Eagle](/wiki/Eagle) to the people of [Minas Tirith](/wiki/Minas_Tirith), announcing the defeat of [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron) and the victory of the [King](/wiki/King_Elessar "King Elessar"). The Eagle came from the [Lords of the West](/wiki/Lords_of_the_West) right after a wind had cleased the [Shadow](/wiki/Dawnless_Day "Dawnless Day") in the sky. The people in Minas Tirith was already filled with joy because of this, and answered the Eagle's words with songs in the streets.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Poem {#Poem}

<div style="font-style:italic; margin-left:20px;" class="poem">
<p>Sing now, ye people of the Tower of Anor,<br/>
for the Realm of Sauron is ended for ever,<br/>
and the <a href="/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr" title="Barad-dûr">Dark Tower</a> is thrown down.<br/>
<br/>
Sing and rejoice, ye people of the Tower of Guard,<br/>
for your watch hath not been in vain,<br/>
and the <a href="/wiki/Black_Gate" title="Black Gate">Black Gate</a> is broken,<br/>
and your <a href="/wiki/Aragorn" title="Aragorn">King</a> hath passed through,<br/>
and he is victorious.<br/>
<br/>
Sing and be glad, all ye children of the West,<br/>
for your King shall come again,<br/>
and he shall dwell among you<br/>
all the days of your life.<br/>
<br/>
And the <a href="/wiki/White_Tree_of_Gondor" title="White Tree of Gondor">Tree</a> that was withered shall be renewed, <br/>
and he shall plant it in the high places,<br/>
and the City shall be blessed.<br/>
<br/>
Sing all ye people!
</p>
</div>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

[Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) explains the clear inspiration of the poem from the Biblical style and language, specially the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms" title="wikipedia:Psalms">Psalms</a>. "*Sing and rejoice*" resembles the "*Rejoice in the Lord*" from <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_33" title="wikipedia:Psalm 33">Psalm 33</a>, while the whole poem resembles <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_24" title="wikipedia:Psalm 24">Psalm 24</a>: "*Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in.*" The imagery is also reminiscent of the [Christian](/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") defeat upon the gates of hell and [Christ](/wiki/Christ)'s coming.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>
