The **Sea-ward Tower** or **Seaward Tower**<sup id="cite_ref-poem_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poem-1">[1]</a></sup>, also called **Tirith Aear**, was a tower situated in or near [Dol Amroth](/wiki/Dol_Amroth).<sup id="cite_ref-preface_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-preface-2">[2]</a></sup>

In a poem recorded in the [Red Book of Westmarch](/wiki/Red_Book_of_Westmarch),<sup id="cite_ref-preface_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-preface-2">[2]</a></sup> a single "slow bell" that was "high in the Seaward Tower" rang solemnly at an "unseemly hour" to announce "the news of" the [Man in the Moon](/wiki/Man_in_the_Moon)'s "[moonsick](/wiki/Moon "Moon") cruise" aboard "a fisherman's boat" in the [Bay of Belfalas](/wiki/Bay_of_Belfalas).<sup id="cite_ref-poem_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poem-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Tirith Aear* is a [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) name possibly meaning "sea watch". It consists of the word *[tirith](/wiki/Tirith "Tirith")* ("watching, guarding;<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> watch, ward, guard")<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and *[aear](/wiki/Aear "Aear")* ("sea")<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

In the first version of *[The Man in the Moon Came Down Too Soon](/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Moon_Came_Down_Too_Soon)*, The Man in the Moon's arrival in Norwich town was announced by "the city's **fifty towers**", referring to the many churches in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-earlyversion_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-earlyversion-7">[7]</a></sup> This was changed in the "second professional typescript" to "the city's **ringing towers**".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>. These "ringing towers" are identified by [Wayne G. Hammond](/wiki/Wayne_G._Hammond) and [Christina Scull](/wiki/Christina_Scull) as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter_Mancroft" title="wikipedia:St Peter Mancroft">St Peter Mancroft</a>, a parish church in the center of Norwich. In a subsequent typescript, Tolkien changed the line to "in tall **St. Peter’s tower**". In [1961](/wiki/1961), Tolkien changed the line to "high in the Seaward Tower" and wrote the names Tirith Aear and the Sea-ward Tower in the preface.<sup id="cite_ref-collectedpoems_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collectedpoems-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Portrayal in adaptations {#Portrayal_in_adaptations}

<b>1996: <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Tirith Aear is conceived to have been constructed as a refuge for the <a href="/wiki/Elves" title="Elves">Elves</a> of <a href="/wiki/Edhellond" title="Edhellond">Edhellond</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Sindar" title="Sindar">Sinda</a> <a href="/wiki/King_of_L%C3%B3rien" title="King of Lórien">lord</a> <a href="/wiki/Amroth" title="Amroth">Amroth</a>, between <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Age_1600" title="Second Age 1600">1600</a> and <a href="/wiki/Second_Age" title="Second Age">S.A. 1620</a>. It is also said to have been used by Galadriel, who installed &#34;a huge bronze bell in its highest chamber&#34; in <a href="/wiki/Third_Age" title="Third Age">T.A.</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Age_1" title="Third Age 1">1</a>. A full layout of the tower is provided.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup></dd></dl>
