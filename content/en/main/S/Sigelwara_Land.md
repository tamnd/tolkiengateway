**Sigelwara Land** is the title of an essay by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), in which is examined the etymology of *Sigelwaran* (and the more usual form *Sigelhearwan*) — the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) word for <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia" title="wikipedia:Ethiopia">Ethiopians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TB1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TB1-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Contents {#Contents}

Tolkien discusses the elements of the word *Sigelhearwan* and related forms, as found in Old English manuscripts. *Sigel* can mean both "sun" and "jewel", and while the meaning of *hearwa* is unknown, he suggests that it is connected to devils and blackness.<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup> Tolkien writes that his tentative analysis shows that:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>[g]limpses are caught, if dim and confused, of the background of English and northern tradition and imagination [...] <i>Sigel</i> may be taken as a symbol of the intricate blending of the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and Northern which makes the study of Old English peculiarly interesting and controversial.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

## Relation to the legendarium {#Relation_to_the_legendarium}

In early drafts of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, anglicized names for the [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim) in the text, were *Silharrows* and *Harwan*, which according to [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) are derived from the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) name for the Aethiopians. [Haradwaith](/wiki/Haradwaith) has the anglicized name *(Sun)Harrowland*.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) has suggested that Tolkien's discussion of *Sigelhearwan* "helped to naturalise the '[Balrog](/wiki/Balrogs "Balrogs")' in the traditions of the North, and \[...\] to create (or corroborate) the image of the *[silmaril](/wiki/Silmarils "Silmarils")*, that fusion of 'sun' and 'jewel' in physical form".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> (In [Aelfwine](/wiki/Aelfwine)'s [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) translations, the name *[Silmaril](/wiki/Silmaril)* is rendered phonetically as *Sigelmaerels* containing *sigel* = sun, jewel).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

## Publication history {#Publication_history}

The essay was originally published in two parts: part one appeared in <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Medium_%C3%86vum_(Vol._1,_No._3)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Medium Ævum (Vol. 1, No. 3) (page does not exist)"><i>Medium Ævum</i> (Vol. 1, No. 3)</a> in [1932](/wiki/1932), and part two appeared in <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Medium_%C3%86vum_(Vol._3,_No._2)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Medium Ævum (Vol. 3, No. 2) (page does not exist)"><i>Medium Ævum</i> (Vol. 3, No. 2)</a> in [1934](/wiki/1934).<sup id="cite_ref-CG_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CG-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TB1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TB1-2">[2]</a></sup> Both parts of the essay also appeared as separate offprints.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
