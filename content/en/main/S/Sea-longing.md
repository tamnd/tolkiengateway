The **Unquiet of [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo)**<sup id="cite_ref-WJ-TY_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ-TY-1">[1]</a></sup> was a strong sea-longing, or desire to return to the sea, that typically affected the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar).

The [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) who lingered in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) had an unquietable yearning for the Sea, coupled with their nostalgia of [their home](/wiki/Eldamar "Eldamar"). It was slumbering in the hearts of the [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar), until it awoke<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> after seeing [Belegaer](/wiki/Belegaer), the Great Sea to the west of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), for the first time. The cry of the [gulls](/wiki/Gulls "Gulls") awakened the sea-longing in [Legolas](/wiki/Legolas)' heart when he heard them at [Pelargir](/wiki/Pelargir).<sup id="cite_ref-LR-TLB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-TLB-3">[3]</a></sup>

[Tuor](/wiki/Tuor) first felt the Unquiet of Ulmo when he looked upon Belegaer, and he felt it ever afterwards. This longing was also passed on to his son, [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In addition, those that heard the music of the [Ulumúri](/wiki/Ulum%C3%BAri), the horns of Ulmo, would always feel a longing for the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The concept of the lust for the sea is already present in the drafts for *[The Tale of Eärendel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_E%C3%A4rendel)*; in the several versions, [Tuor](/wiki/Tuor) or Tûr, hears the conches of Ulmo or 'groweth sea-hungry', takes a boat to disapear in the sea leaving [Idril](/wiki/Idril) and Earendil behind.

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

In the summer of [1914](/wiki/1914) Tolkien visited the Lizard Peninsula in Cornwall where the sea left a great impression on him, and inspired him to write a poem.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

According to Norma Roche, the pull of the Sea in Tolkien's works is not for the sake of the sea and sailing itself, but about what lies beyond the sea: the paradise of the West.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
