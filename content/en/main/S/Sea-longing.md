<p>The Sea! Alas! I have not yet beheld it. But deep in the hearts of all my kindred lies the sea-longing, which it is perilous to stir. Alas! for the gulls. No peace shall I have again under beech or under elm.
</p>

The **Unquiet of [Ulmo](../U/Ulmo.md)**<sup id="cite_ref-WJ-TY_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJ-TY-1">[1]</a></sup> was a strong sea-longing, or desire to return to the sea, that typically affected the [Eldar](../E/Eldar.md).

The [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md) who lingered in [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md) had an unquietable yearning for the Sea, coupled with their nostalgia of [their home](../E/Eldamar.md). It was slumbering in the hearts of the [Sindar](Sindar.md), until it awoke<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> after seeing [Belegaer](../B/Belegaer.md), the Great Sea to the west of [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md), for the first time. The cry of the [gulls](../G/Gulls.md) awakened the sea-longing in [Legolas](../L/Legolas.md)' heart when he heard them at [Pelargir](../P/Pelargir.md).<sup id="cite_ref-LR-TLB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LR-TLB-3">[3]</a></sup>

[Tuor](../T/Tuor.md) first felt the Unquiet of Ulmo when he looked upon Belegaer, and he felt it ever afterwards. This longing was also passed on to his son, [Eärendil](../E/Eärendil.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> In addition, those that heard the music of the [Ulumúri](../U/Ulumúri.md), the horns of Ulmo, would always feel a longing for the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

The concept of the lust for the sea is already present in the drafts for *[The Tale of Eärendel](../T/The_Tale_of_Eärendel.md)*; in the several versions, [Tuor](../T/Tuor.md) or Tûr, hears the conches of Ulmo or 'groweth sea-hungry', takes a boat to disapear in the sea leaving [Idril](../I/Idril.md) and Earendil behind.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>&#39;Twas in the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../L/Land_of_Willows.md" title="Land of Willows">Land of Willows</a> that I heard th&#39; unfathomed breath<br/>Of the <a href="../U/Ulumúri.md" title="Ulumúri">Horns of Ylmir</a> calling—and shall hear them till my death.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><i><a href="../T/The_Horns_of_Ylmir.md" title="The Horns of Ylmir">The Horns of Ylmir</a></i></cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

In the summer of [1914](../_/1914.md) Tolkien visited the Lizard Peninsula in Cornwall where the sea left a great impression on him, and inspired him to write a poem.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

According to Norma Roche, the pull of the Sea in Tolkien's works is not for the sake of the sea and sailing itself, but about what lies beyond the sea: the paradise of the West.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
