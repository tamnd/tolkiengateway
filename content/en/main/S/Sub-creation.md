**Sub-creation** was a term used by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) for a philosophical concept that he applied to all aspects of his life, including his work on the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"). It described the inclination of all living beings to create things within the [Primary World](/wiki/Primary_World) (i.e. the physical world in which they inhabit), using materials and experiences derived from the primary world. The primary world in this framework came to exist through an act of true creation - that is to say the creation of something from nothing.

In real life, Tolkien, as a Catholic, credited this act of true creation to the Christian God. Within the legendarium, however, the Creator is known as [Eru Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar"). When describing the relationship between the legendarium and the real world, Tolkien would often refer to [Arda](/wiki/Arda) as his [secondary world](/wiki/Secondary_world "Secondary world"), constructed within a real primary world.

## In Arda {#In_Arda}

"Sub-creation" was used to describe the creative efforts of created beings, the [Ainur](/wiki/Ainur) and [Incarnates](/wiki/Incarnate "Incarnate"), such as [Elves](/wiki/Elves), [Men](/wiki/Men), [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves), and [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits), all of whom are themselves either creations of [Eru Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar") or, in the case of the Dwarves, created by the [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) and later given life by Ilúvatar.<sup id="cite_ref-aule_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aule-1">[1]</a></sup>

Tolkien used sub-creation in middle earth to demonstrate sin and free will. In order for beings to have true free will no limits can be put on it. Ilúvatar created an unflawed Eä but his created beings were capable of erring and doing evil,<sup id="cite_ref-l212_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l212-2">[2]</a></sup> (Though never being wholly evil<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>). All evil that existed in Arda was of sub-creative origin.<sup id="cite_ref-l212_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l212-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Ainur {#Ainur}

Ilúvatar gave special sub-creative powers to the Ainur. The Ainur (along with all other sub-creators) are unable to change any of the fundaments aspects of Arda, hence why the Valar called upon Ilúvatar when the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) broke the [Ban of the Valar](/wiki/Ban_of_the_Valar).<sup id="cite_ref-l153_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l153-4">[4]</a></sup>

When Aulë created the Dwarves they did not have [souls](/wiki/F%C3%ABa "Fëa") and lacked free will,<sup id="cite_ref-aule_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aule-1">[1]</a></sup> this was because the creation of souls was not a power given by Ilúvatar to sub-creators.<sup id="cite_ref-l153_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l153-4">[4]</a></sup> Ilúvatar viewed this action as an mockery of him and stated that Aulë had tried to usurp the Creator's powers.<sup id="cite_ref-l212_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l212-2">[2]</a></sup> Similarly when [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) created races such as [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs) and [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls) he was unable to duplicate true creation and instead corrupted or emulated beings already in existence: Orcs were produced either from corrupted Elves or Men,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup> while Trolls were said to have been made in mockery of the [Ents](/wiki/Ents) using the element of stone. Morgoth was hence unable to master true creation and his created beings lacked real souls.<sup id="cite_ref-l153_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l153-4">[4]</a></sup>

Ilúvatar gave the Ainur sub-creative powers with some limitations of how they should you use the Ainur being created beings had free will and could use sub-creation against the will of Ilúvatar.<sup id="cite_ref-l153_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l153-4">[4]</a></sup> Ilúvatar tolerated this,<sup id="cite_ref-l153_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l153-4">[4]</a></sup> and ultimately all that he did would in the end serve only to further the plan which Ilúvatar had set into motion at the beginning of [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[5]</a></sup>

## In reality {#In_reality}

"Sub-creation" was also used by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) to refer to process of worldbuilding and creating myths.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[6]</a></sup> In this context, a human author is a "little maker", creating his own world as a subset within God's primary creation. Like the beings of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), Tolkien saw his works as mere emulation of the true creation performed by God.<sup id="cite_ref-l153_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-l153-4">[4]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>...liberation &#39;from the channels the creator is known to have used already&#39; is the fundamental function of &#39;sub-creation&#39;, a tribute to the infinity of His potential variety, one of the ways in which indeed it is exhibited...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, <a href="/wiki/Letter_153" title="Letter 153">Letter 153</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>
