**Simbelmynë** was a small white flower that grew in particular abundance on graves and tombs, like the [Barrowfield](/wiki/Barrowfield) of the [Kings of Rohan](/wiki/Kings_of_Rohan) beneath the walls of [Edoras](/wiki/Edoras);<sup id="cite_ref-TTIII6_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TTIII6-1">[1]</a></sup> and most thickly on the grave of [Helm Hammerhand](/wiki/Helm "Helm").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> It was referred to as <i><b><a href="/wiki/Uilos" title="Uilos">uilos</a></b></i> when Tuor saw these flowers in the great ravine between the Fourth Gate, the [Gate of Writhen Iron](/wiki/Gate_of_Writhen_Iron), and the Fifth Gate that was made of white marble, when he was making his way to [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Another Elven name for this flower was <i><b><a href="/wiki/Alfirin" title="Alfirin">alfirin</a></b></i>, which grew on the low mound that was the tomb of [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) upon the summit of [Amon Anwar](/wiki/Halifirien "Halifirien").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

It grew in turf but small and white like the wood anemone. Though the plant bloomed at all seasons, its flowers were not 'immortelles'.<sup id="cite_ref-N_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N-5">[5]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Simbelmynë* was the name of the flower in [Rohan](/wiki/Rohan), meaning "[Evermind](/wiki/Evermind)",<sup id="cite_ref-TTIII6_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TTIII6-1">[1]</a></sup> a reference to the memories of the dead on whose tombs the flower grew. The name thus resembles 'forget-me-not', but a quite different kind of flower is intended. The [Elves](/wiki/Elves) called them *uilos* and *alfirin*.

The spelling does not appear to be completely fixed. Within *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, and in most other sources, its name is spelt *simbelmynë*. However, there is at least one recorded instance where Tolkien prefers *symbelmynë*.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> These variations do not affect the meaning of its name.

It has been suggested that Tolkien derived *simbelmynë* from the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) words *simbel* ("ever") and *myne* ("mind").<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

Tolkien mentioned that Simbelmyne is an imagined variety of anemone. In appearance, [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") compares Simbelmynë to the <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemone_nemorosa" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">European anemone</a>, a small white flower growing among the grass or <a class="external text" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsatilla_vulgaris" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Anemona pulsatilla</a>, the "pasque flower".<sup id="cite_ref-N_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N-5">[5]</a></sup> <span></span>
