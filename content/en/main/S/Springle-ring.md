The **Springle-ring** was a dance of the [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits). It was described as "a pretty dance, but rather vigorous" where dancers leaped up often.<sup id="cite_ref-Party_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-1">[1]</a></sup>

At [Bilbo's Farewell Party](/wiki/Bilbo%27s_Farewell_Party), while [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) was giving his speech, many thought that he had just finished and the children started playing with their toy instruments; [Everard Took](/wiki/Everard_Took) and [Melilot Brandybuck](/wiki/Melilot_Brandybuck) got on a table and started performing the dance shaking bells on their hands.<sup id="cite_ref-Party_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-1">[1]</a></sup>

The dance is also mentioned in the [Bucklandish](/wiki/Buckland "Buckland") poem *[Bombadil Goes Boating](/wiki/Bombadil_Goes_Boating)*, as the daughters of [Farmer Maggot](/wiki/Farmer_Maggot) sing and dance to honor their guest [Tom Bombadil](/wiki/Tom_Bombadil).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/springle#English" title="wiktionary:springle">springle</a></i> has several meanings in English, but is also a dialect word for "nimble, active".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> [Tolkien](/wiki/Tolkien) commented that the name is made-up and ought to evoke the style of the dance.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Robert Foster](/wiki/Robert_Foster) gives the erroneous translation "war-horse ring".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> <span></span>
