The **Sackville-Baggins Family** was a branch of the [Baggins Family](../B/Baggins_Family~66343.md).

## History {#History}

[Camellia Sackville](../C/Camellia_Sackville.md) was the daughter of the head of the [Sackville Family](Sackville_Family.md).<sup id="cite_ref-L214_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-1">[1]</a></sup> She married [Longo Baggins](../L/Longo_Baggins.md), the son of [Mungo Baggins](../M/Mungo_Baggins.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Bag_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bag-2">[2]</a></sup> Some time prior to the birth of their first son, [Otho](../O/Otho_Sackville-Baggins.md), Camelia's father died. As was the custom in the "younger" families when there was no male heir, the headship passed to the daughter's eldest son. He would then use his mother's name, and add his father's to it. Thus, the Sackville-Baggins branch was created.<sup id="cite_ref-L214_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-1">[1]</a></sup>

Otho married [Lobelia Bracegirdle](../L/Lobelia_Sackville-Baggins.md) of [Hardbottle](../H/Hardbottle.md), and the two had one child, [Lotho](../L/Lotho_Sackville-Baggins.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Bag_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bag-2">[2]</a></sup> Despite being the heir of the wealthy<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Sackville Family, the Sackville-Bagginses yearned for the headship of the Baggins Family, and more specifically, their residence, [Bag End](../B/Bag_End.md), the residence of [Bilbo Baggins](../B/Bilbo_Baggins.md). They became hopeful when Bilbo [disappeared](../Q/Quest_for_Erebor.md) and were disappointed when he returned.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Party_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-5">[5]</a></sup> Their hopes were finally dashed in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [2989](../T/Third_Age_2989.md) when Bilbo adopted an heir, [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md).<sup id="cite_ref-Party_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-5">[5]</a></sup>

The Sackville-Bagginses were the only of Bilbo Baggins's relatives who didn't pay visits. However he did invite them to [his birthday party](../B/Bilbo's_Farewell_Party.md) and they decided to come, thanks to the luxurious invitation card (written with golden ink) and the reputation of Bilbo's tables. During his speech he made a special mention to them, thanking and welcoming them, but they scowled hearing that Frodo (whom they detested) came into Bilbo's inheritance; they also felt insulted when he revealed that the guests were supposed to fill a [Gross](../O/One_Gross.md), as if they had been invited only for this reason. When Bilbo said farewell and disappeared at the end of his speech, they left enraged.<sup id="cite_ref-Party_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-5">[5]</a></sup>

The [next day](../_/23_September.md) they heard that Bilbo's household is giving out for free, and demanded to see Frodo and offensively offered him bad bargain-prices for various valuable things, but Frodo replied that the things given away were parting gifts directed by Bilbo, and only a case of silver spoons was for them. Otho accused Frodo for "doing exceedingly well out of it" and demanded to see Bilbo's will (without which Otho would be Bilbo's heir).<sup id="cite_ref-Party_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-5">[5]</a></sup>

After Frodo's departure, Bag End was sold to Lobelia, but Otho had died.<sup id="cite_ref-Party_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Party-5">[5]</a></sup>

<br>

## Genealogy {#Genealogy}

The Sackville-Bagginses were descendants of Mungo Baggins, like Bilbo. They considered themselves the heir of the Baggins Family headship because Frodo Baggins was the descendant of [Largo Baggins](../L/Largo_Baggins.md), Mungo's younger brother.<sup id="cite_ref-Bag_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bag-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

**Sackville** was the name of a relatively young Hobbit family.<sup id="cite_ref-L214_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-1">[1]</a></sup> Their name had an association with **Baggins** in that both contained an element for "bag/sack"; Sackville was a slightly more aristocratic version.<sup id="cite_ref-Nomen_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nomen-6">[6]</a></sup> [Tom Shippey](../T/Tom_Shippey.md) argued that this "similarity" also provoked an antonymy: [Bag End](../B/Bag_End.md) was used around England as a replacement of French *cul-de-sac*, "dead end street" - even Tolkien's own aunt [Jane Neave](../J/Jane_Neave.md) lived in a house of that name. Tolkien did not like the Norman conquest of Britain, and made the Bagginses English. The name *Sackville*, however, is very Norman, as one of the few, if not the only, Hobbit family name.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s manuscript of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* the Sackville-Baggins were called the **Allibone Baggins**.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> [John D. Rateliff](../J/John_D._Rateliff.md) stated that the change to "Sackville" was penciled in about the time the story was being prepared for publication in [1936](../_/1936.md). The significance of "Allibone" is unknown although Mr. Rateliff thought it might relate to *[Alboin](../A/Alboin.md)*, a character in *[The Lost Road](../T/The_Lost_Road.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> <span></span>
