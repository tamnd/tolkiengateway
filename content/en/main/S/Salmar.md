**Salmar** was a [Maia](/wiki/Maia)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> who entered [Arda](/wiki/Arda) with [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo). He was the maker of the [Ulumúri](/wiki/Ulum%C3%BAri), the great horns of Ulmo which produce the music of the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of the name <i><b>Salmar</b></i> in [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) is unclear. But, see <a class="mw-selflink-fragment" href="#Early_etymology">below</a> for the meaning of his name in the early mythology.

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

### *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)* and associated writings {#The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_and_associated_writings}

**Salmar**, also called **Noldorin**, was a [Vala](/wiki/Valar "Valar") in the early version of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium") in *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*.

He entered the world with [Ulmo](/wiki/Ulmo),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and afterwards dwelt in [Valmar](/wiki/Valmar). There he would often play music on his harp and lyre, sitting beneath [Laurelin](/wiki/Laurelin), and [Ómar-Amillo](/wiki/%C3%93mar "Ómar") his twin brother would sing as he played.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

When the Valar decided to oust [Melko](/wiki/Melko) from his fortress in [Utumna](/wiki/Utumna), he joined the rest of them in their attack.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> Some time later when the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) [awoke](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves "Awakening of the Elves"), he established a friendship with the [Noldoli](/wiki/Noldoli), whom he loved.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

After the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees) were destroyed, it was Salmar who first noticed that in [Vána](/wiki/V%C3%A1na)'s lamentation for them, there was still joy in her voice. When he pointed it out to them, the rest of the Valar gathered there heard the words: *I-kal'antúlien* (meaning "Light hath returned"), since Vána's tears caused a final shoot of Laurelin to spring forth.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

Much later, after the [Fall of Gondolin](/wiki/Fall_of_Gondolin), when the Valar finally decided to wage war on Melko in the [Great Lands](/wiki/Great_Lands) and free the enslaved [Gnomes](/wiki/Gnomes), Salmar came with them. In the ensuing [Battle of Tasarinan](/wiki/Battle_of_Tasarinan), Salmar barely escaped;<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> following his escape he went to the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Iron_Mountains_(The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Iron Mountains (The Book of Lost Tales) (page does not exist)">Iron Mountains</a> looking for the Gnomes and to find their place of imprisonment.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Nothing else is known about his future, except that he eventually returned to Valinor.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[note 2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">[12]</a></sup>

In one text though, it is said that he led the Gnomes back to [Tol Eressëa](/wiki/Tol_Eress%C3%ABa) after the war with Melko.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">[13]</a></sup>

### Early etymology {#Early_etymology}

*Salmar* is a [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) name, derived from *salma* ("lyre, small harp").<sup id="cite_ref-Pie_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pie-16">[14]</a></sup>

### Other names {#Other_names}

The [Gnomish](/wiki/Gnomish) cognate of *Salmar* is <i><b>Salvor</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pie_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pie-16">[14]</a></sup>

Likewise, both <i><b>Noldorin</b></i> and <i><b>Lirillo</b></i> (another name by which he was known by) are also Qenya names: the first probably arising from his association with the [Noldoli](/wiki/Noldoli);<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[15]</a></sup> the second meaning "Valu of the Song", containing either *glîr* ("a song, poem") or *lir-* ("sing") + masculine ending <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=-illo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="-illo (page does not exist)">-illo</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pie_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pie-16">[14]</a></sup>

The Gnomish equivalent of *Noldorin* is <i><b>Goldriel</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[note 3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[16]</a></sup> and that of *Lirillo* is <i><b>Glirlos</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pie_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pie-16">[14]</a></sup>

Another name for the character was <i><b>Avlon</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[17]</a></sup> It is in Gnomish, but its meaning is unclear.

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

The characters of Salmar (Lirillo), Ómar (Amillo), and [Erinti](/wiki/Erinti) were likely inspired by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) himself, his brother [Hilary Tolkien](/wiki/Hilary_Tolkien), and Tolkien's wife [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Bratt), respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[18]</a></sup>

For more information, see: <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Lirillion&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lirillion (page does not exist)">Lirillion</a></i>.
