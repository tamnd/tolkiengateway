**Scary** was a small settlement that lay in the hilly region of caves and rock holes that formed the northern parts of the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing), which also gave its name to the surrounding [stony hills](/wiki/Hills_of_Scary "Hills of Scary").<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

From among the rocky lands around Scary much of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")'s stone was quarried.

During the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), the Scary quarries were used by [Sharkey's Men](/wiki/Ruffians "Ruffians") to store their loot, and after Sharkey was defeated, much of this was recovered by the [Shire-hobbits](/wiki/Shire-hobbits). It was around these hills that [Fredegar Bolger](/wiki/Fredegar_Bolger) organized his band of rebels against the Ruffians, until they were caught.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") notes that the name was meaningless in [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) of the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring). However he notes that since it was in a rocky region and of a stone-quarry, it may be supposed to contain English dialectal *scar*, "rocky cliff".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Based on the above, [David Salo](/wiki/David_Salo) has suggested that Scary represents a speculative [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) \**Skerig* "precipitous (place)". Salo also noted that the form of this dialectal word, specifically the existence of sk-, reveals a [Norse](/wiki/Norse) origin.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

[Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey) suggests an etymology from Old English *scearu* "a boundary", marking the Shire's northern boundary; the retaining (or reversal) of /sk/ might reflect the peculiarity of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_English" title="wikipedia:Northern English">Northern English</a> (in such names as <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skipton" title="wikipedia:Skipton">Skipton</a>, <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire" title="wikipedia:Scarborough, North Yorkshire">Scarborough</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
