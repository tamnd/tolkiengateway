The **Stoors** were one of the three breeds of [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits).

In their earliest recorded history the Stoors, like the other Hobbits, lived in the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin). They were a riverside people that dwelt in the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields), and were fishermen.<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

They were heavier and broader in build than the other Hobbits, and had large hands and feet. Among the Hobbits, the Stoors most resembled [Men](/wiki/Men) and were most friendly to them. Stoors were the only Hobbits who normally grew facial hair.<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup>

While few Hobbits could swim and they were known to be afraid of rivers and boats (let alone of the Sea, a token of fear and death for them which few had ever seen), the Stoors preferred flat lands and riversides.<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup>

Stoorish characteristics and appearance (large build, heavy feet and a down on the chin), could still be seen among the Hobbits of the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing), [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland) (such as the [Brandybucks](/wiki/Brandybuck_Family "Brandybuck Family")) and the [Bree-hobbits](/wiki/Bree-hobbits). The Hobbits of the Eastfarthing wore dwarf-boots in muddy weather.<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup>

The Stoors also had their [own dialect](/wiki/Stoorish "Stoorish") of [Hobbit-speech](/wiki/Hobbitish "Hobbitish"), owing to the fact that they spent some time in [Dunland](/wiki/Dunland) and adopted many strange words and names which they took to [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire"), and retained even until the late Third Age.

## History {#History}

The Stoors are believed to have lived longer in the southern vales of Anduin.<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup> During the Hobbit [Wandering Days](/wiki/Wandering_Days), after the [Harfoots](/wiki/Harfoots) had migrated westward in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050"), and the [Fallohides](/wiki/Fallohides) flew north first and then followed them [about a century later](/wiki/Third_Age_1150 "Third Age 1150"), the Stoors long remained back in their dwelling in the Vale of Anduin, but in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1150](/wiki/Third_Age_1150 "Third Age 1150"), they, too, migrated west to [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador).<sup id="cite_ref-ThirdAge_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ThirdAge-3">[3]</a></sup>

They took the [Redhorn Pass](/wiki/Redhorn_Pass) and followed a southern route along the course of the [Loudwater](/wiki/Loudwater), where some Stoors branched off and moved to the [Angle](/wiki/Angle) of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador) south of [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell), and many settled long between [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) and [Dunland](/wiki/Dunland); most went to Dunland ([Swanfleet](/wiki/Swanfleet) near Tharbad)<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ThirdAge_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ThirdAge-3">[3]</a></sup> which most resembled their old lands. There they came into contact with the [Dunlendings](/wiki/Dunlendings). This contact altered their speech slightly, mostly by picking up a few Dunlending words.

A hundred years later [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) began to threaten Eriador and many Stoors of the Angle fled ([T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1356](/wiki/Third_Age_1356 "Third Age 1356")<sup id="cite_ref-ThirdAge_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ThirdAge-3">[3]</a></sup>–[T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1409](/wiki/Third_Age_1409 "Third Age 1409")<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup>) because of the [wars](/wiki/Angmar_War "Angmar War") and the evil climate of Eriador; however a few of them returned to the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Stoor-country&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Stoor-country (page does not exist)">Stoor-country</a><sup id="cite_ref-letter_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-1">[1]</a></sup> in [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion).<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-4">[4]</a></sup>

The Stoors of Dunland moved back north to join the other Hobbits in colonizing [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire") about [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1630](/wiki/Third_Age_1630 "Third Age 1630")<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ThirdAge_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ThirdAge-3">[3]</a></sup> and settled mostly in the [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing) and [Southfarthing](/wiki/Southfarthing). Some of these villages might have survived until the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring), when they were sought out by the [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl").<sup id="cite_ref-UT|Hunt_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT|Hunt-6">[5]</a></sup> One result of the Stoorish influx was that places that were settled by Stoors had some slight linguistic oddities, due to their time of separation and contact with the Dunlendings. The three original Hobbit-kinds merged and blended in the centuries since the settlement of the Shire, but regional variations remained with Stoorish characteristics seen in [Eastfarthing](/wiki/Eastfarthing) and [Buckland](/wiki/Buckland).<sup id="cite_ref-Hobbits_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hobbits-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Stoors of Wilderland {#Stoors_of_Wilderland}

The Stoors who returned to Rhovanion in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1356](/wiki/Third_Age_1356 "Third Age 1356") settled the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields) by the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin), becoming a riverland people of fishers.<sup id="cite_ref-eriador_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eriador-4">[4]</a></sup> They lost contact with all other Hobbits and nothing is known about their culture<sup id="cite_ref-L214_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-7">[6]</a></sup> other than what Gandalf inferred from his interrogation with [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum).<sup id="cite_ref-shadows_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadows-8">[7]</a></sup> While their cousins in the Shire developed a more settled and elaborate social life, it is possible that they reverted to a more wild and primitive lifestyle.<sup id="cite_ref-L214_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-7">[6]</a></sup> They made little boats of [reeds](/wiki/Reeds "Reeds") with which they sailed the River, they fished, and they even swam<sup id="cite_ref-shadows_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadows-8">[7]</a></sup> (something not common among the other hobbits).

By [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2430](/wiki/Third_Age_2430 "Third Age 2430") their community was ruled by a "matriarch" of high repute; possibly a dominant grandmother who had outlived her husband, as she belonged to a large and wealthy family. Among those Stoors were [Déagol](/wiki/D%C3%A9agol) and [Sméagol](/wiki/Sm%C3%A9agol), who rediscovered the [One Ring](/wiki/One_Ring), lost since the dawn of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age).<sup id="cite_ref-L214_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L214-7">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-shadows_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadows-8">[7]</a></sup> Sméagol caused mischief becoming invisible, learning secrets and later stealing, and eventually his grandmother expelled him from the family and their [hobbit-hole](/wiki/Hobbit-hole "Hobbit-hole").<sup id="cite_ref-shadows_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shadows-8">[7]</a></sup>

By the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) the settlements of the Stoors had been deserted.<sup id="cite_ref-UT|Hunt_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UT|Hunt-6">[5]</a></sup> What became of those Stoors and whether they rejoined their folk in Eriador, no history tells.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Stoor* is supposed to be a special [Hobbitish](/wiki/Hobbitish) word that did not exist in [Westron](/wiki/Westron) during the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring).<sup id="cite_ref-N_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N-9">[8]</a></sup>

[J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote in his guide for translators for the translation of *The Lord of the Rings* into other languages that the name Stoors for the kind of [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) was based on the early English word *stor*, *stoor* ("large", "strong"), which is now obsolete. He explained that it was a hobbit-word that was not current in Westron.<sup id="cite_ref-N_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N-9">[8]</a></sup>

An earlier manuscript version of what would later become Appendix F states that the name Stoor seemed to have originally meant "big" and that this word was common in the language of Dale.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[9]</a></sup> This version also mentions that Scandinavian stor- means "big".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup>
