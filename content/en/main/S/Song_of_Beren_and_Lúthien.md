"**The leaves were long, the grass was green,**" is the first line of an excerpt from the [Lay of Lúthien](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian "Lay of Leithian"),<sup id="cite_ref-name_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-1">[1]</a></sup> a lengthy sad tale made in the *[ann-thennath](/wiki/Ann-thennath "Ann-thennath")* [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) verse mode telling the story of [Beren](/wiki/Beren), [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien), and the [Quest for the Silmaril](/wiki/Quest_for_the_Silmaril).<sup id="cite_ref-excerpt_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-excerpt-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Sometime between the years [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [503](/wiki/First_Age_503 "First Age 503")<sup id="cite_ref-first_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-first-3">[3]</a></sup> and [F.A.](/wiki/First_Age "First Age") [538](/wiki/First_Age_538 "First Age 538")<sup id="cite_ref-second_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-4">[4]</a></sup> of the late [First Age](/wiki/First_Age), the *Lay of Lúthien* was likely among many tales and songs that were made in the [Havens of Sirion](/wiki/Havens_of_Sirion).<sup id="cite_ref-second_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-second-4">[4]</a></sup> It was said to be the second longest lay; only being beaten in length by [Dírhaval](/wiki/D%C3%ADrhaval)'s <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Narn_i_Ch%C3%AEn_H%C3%BArin" title="Narn i Chîn Húrin">Narn i Chîn Húrin</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-long_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-long-5">[5]</a></sup>

By the time of the late [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age), the ending of the lay was forgotten and only [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) could remember it "as it was told of old".<sup id="cite_ref-excerpt_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-excerpt-2">[2]</a></sup>

When [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn) saw [Arwen](/wiki/Arwen) in [Rivendell](/wiki/Rivendell) for the first time, he sang an excerpt from the Lay of Lúthien telling of Beren and Lúthien's first meeting in the [Forest of Neldoreth](/wiki/Forest_of_Neldoreth).<sup id="cite_ref-name_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-1">[1]</a></sup>

In the year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), Aragorn sang the same excerpt on [Weathertop](/wiki/Weathertop) in the [Weather Hills](/wiki/Weather_Hills) to distract [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins), [Meriadoc Brandybuck](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck), [Peregrin Took](/wiki/Peregrin_Took), and [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) from their fears. Afterwards, Aragorn summarized the lay and explained its history and form to the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits). He also commented that his [Westron](/wiki/Westron)-translated excerpt was "but a rough echo of" the original since the lay was difficult to translate.<sup id="cite_ref-excerpt_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-excerpt-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In [June](/wiki/June) of [1925](/wiki/1925), a poem that [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) wrote that year,<sup id="cite_ref-canto_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canto-6">[6]</a></sup> **Light as Leaf on Lindentree**, appeared in a volume of *The Gryphon Magazine*, a Leeds University publication.<sup id="cite_ref-poem_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poem-7">[7]</a></sup> The poem was revised and incorporated into the second version of the <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Narn_i_Ch%C3%AEn_H%C3%BArin" title="Narn i Chîn Húrin">Narn i Chîn Húrin</a></i> where one of [Morwen](/wiki/Morwen)'s servants sang the poem as they entered [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath) with [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin). In this version, Tolkien gave the poem many variations of names including **As Light as Leaf on Lindentree**, **Light as Leaf on Linden-tree**, **As Light as Leaf on Linden-tree**, and **Light as Leaf on Linden**. This scene in [The Lay of the Children of Húrin](/wiki/The_Lay_of_the_Children_of_H%C3%BArin) was deduced by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) as being the predecessor of the scene of Aragorn singing the excerpt "before the attack of the Ringwraiths" on Weathertop.<sup id="cite_ref-early_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-early-8">[8]</a></sup>

Tolkien later reworked the poem into Canto III of *[The Lay of Leithian](/wiki/The_Lay_of_Leithian)*.<sup id="cite_ref-luthien_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-luthien-9">[9]</a></sup>

While writing *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, Tolkien made another variation of the poem's name, **Light on Linden Tree**, and revised it until it came close to the published version.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-10">[10]</a></sup>

In an early version of the Rivendell chapters, [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) was to sing **Tinúviel**, an excerpt from the Lay of Lúthien which may possibly have been the same as Light as Leaf on Lindentree. Tolkien decided against this, having Bilbo sing an early version of the *[Eärendillinwë](/wiki/Song_of_E%C3%A4rendil "Song of Eärendil")* instead.<sup id="cite_ref-bilbo_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bilbo-11">[11]</a></sup>

## Analysis {#Analysis}

The excerpt consists of nine stanzas of eight lines each, rhymed *ABAC BABC*. The C rhyme in each stanza is triple: the sixth syllable of the fourth line rhymes with the sixth syllable of the eighth line, and the seventh and eighth syllables of the fourth line are identical (or nearly) to those of the eighth. The poem's metre is <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iambic_tetrameter" title="wikipedia:Iambic tetrameter">iambic tetrameter</a> (four pairs of unstressed and stressed syllables).

Following Aragorn's words, [Patrick Wynne](/wiki/Patrick_Wynne) and [Carl F. Hostetter](/wiki/Carl_F._Hostetter) explain that the English metre tries to imitate what the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) metre (*[ann-thennath](/wiki/Ann-thennath "Ann-thennath")*) would have been in the original poem.

The song would be an extract translated from the *[Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian)*, specifically [Canto III](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian_Canto_III "Lay of Leithian Canto III").<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup>
