**Sauron** was a highly gifted [Maia](/wiki/Maiar "Maiar"), originally an apprentice of [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB), who became skilled at crafting and making. Coveting the power through which he would coordinate all things according to his own will, he joined with [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth"). As "Gorthaur" he became the most trusted lieutenant of Morgoth, being reckoned as the greatest of his allies and servants in the [Wars of Beleriand](/wiki/Battles_of_Beleriand "Battles of Beleriand"). From his fortress of [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](/wiki/Tol-in-Gaurhoth), Sauron was directly responsible for the death of [Barahir](/wiki/Barahir) and later the [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldor "Noldor") king [Finrod](/wiki/Finrod) during the [Quest for the Silmaril](/wiki/Quest_for_the_Silmaril). He demonstrated the ability to take the form of a wolf, a serpent, and a vampire.

After the downfall of Morgoth, Sauron continually strove to conquer [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) throughout the [Second](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") and [Third](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") Ages. In the Second Age, under the guise of **Annatar**, he deceived the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) of [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion), who under his guidance had created the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power), whilst he secretly forged [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") in [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom). Thus Sauron became "**[the Lord of the Rings](/wiki/Lord_of_the_Ring "Lord of the Ring")**". Failing to corrupt the Elves, he assaulted the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands), beginning a period called the [Dark Years](/wiki/Dark_Years), the first time he became known as the **[Dark Lord](/wiki/Dark_Lord)**. The arrogant [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), contesting their rule on Middle-earth, challenged him, and Sauron accepted to be brought to [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) as a captive; however his influence corrupted the Númenóreans further - leading to the [Downfall of Númenor](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor). His spirit escaped, as did [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and his sons, founding the Realms in Exile of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). Elves and the [Dúnedain](/wiki/D%C3%BAnedain), formed the [Last Alliance](/wiki/Last_Alliance) and, in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441"), Elendil and Elven [High King](/wiki/High_King_of_the_Noldor "High King of the Noldor") [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) died fighting Sauron. Following Sauron's defeat, Elendil's son, [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur), severed his Ringed finger and took the One Ring for himself.

In the Third Age, Sauron the Enemy returned to Middle-earth and, as the **Necromancer**, took the hill of [Amon Lanc](/wiki/Amon_Lanc) as his fortress of [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur); one of his foremost servants, the [Witch-king](/wiki/Witch-king), formed the realm of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar) in the north of [Eriador](/wiki/Eriador). Following an attack by the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), the Dark Lord Sauron returned to his fortress of [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr) in [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor), marshalling his armies and using his two-faced ally, the corrupted [Wizard](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards") [Saruman](/wiki/Saruman). By [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018"), [Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins) was in possession of the Ring, and he was led by [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) as a member of the [Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring) in the [Quest of the Ring](/wiki/Quest_of_the_Ring). Whilst Sauron waged the [War of the Ring](/wiki/War_of_the_Ring) against the [Free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples) of Middle-earth, Frodo Baggins, [Samwise Gamgee](/wiki/Samwise_Gamgee) and [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum) reached Mount Doom on [25 March](/wiki/25_March) [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3019](/wiki/Third_Age_3019 "Third Age 3019"), and the Ring was destroyed. The breaking of the Ring caused Sauron's fall, and his stepping on Morgoth's path down into the [Void](/wiki/Void), resulting in the start of the [Fourth Age](/wiki/Fourth_Age) and the [Dominion of Men](/wiki/Dominion_of_Men).

As one of the most powerful [Maiar](/wiki/Maiar), Sauron was [created](/wiki/Creation_of_the_Ainur "Creation of the Ainur") by [Ilúvatar](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar) before the [Music of the Ainur](/wiki/Music_of_the_Ainur).<sup id="cite_ref-Ainu_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ainu-2">[2]</a></sup> At the beginning of [Time](/wiki/Time), he was amongst the [Ainur](/wiki/Ainur) who entered into [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4).<sup id="cite_ref-SVala_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SVala-3">[3]</a></sup> Here he became one of the Maiar of [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB), among whose people he was deemed mighty and surpassed only by the Smith himself,<sup id="cite_ref-SVala_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SVala-3">[3]</a></sup> and was known as <i><b>Mairon</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-PE17.1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17.1-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[note 1]</a></sup>

Mairon's virtue was his love for order, planning and coordination, disliking confusion and chaos. But his obsession with order gradually overshadowed his love for the other intelligent beings of Arda, who would benefit from his planning; it became the sole object of his will, the end in itself. He started admiring [Melkor](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth")'s power to realize his designs quickly and masterfully.<sup id="cite_ref-myths_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-myths-6">[5]</a></sup>

Early on, already by the blissful [Spring of Arda](/wiki/Spring_of_Arda), he was ensnared by Melkor, becoming his spy on the isle of [Almaren](/wiki/Almaren), the dwelling-place of the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-Annals_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Annals-7">[6]</a></sup> While [Tulkas](/wiki/Tulkas) was asleep, Melkor ruined the [Two Lamps](/wiki/Two_Lamps), and when Almaren was also destroyed, the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) moved to the [Blessed Realm of Aman](/wiki/Aman "Aman"), still not perceiving Sauron's treachery.<sup id="cite_ref-Valinor_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Valinor-8">[7]</a></sup> Soon Mairon left the Blessed Realm for [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), [in a dark time](/wiki/Sleep_of_Yavanna "Sleep of Yavanna") where Melkor dominated limitlessly, and bent the knee before him as his greatest and most trusted servant.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup> Thus he came to be known as <i><b>Gorthaur</b></i> by the [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar) of [Beleriand](/wiki/Beleriand) and <i><b>Sauron</b></i> by others. Sauron initially was not as evil as Morgoth, as he was serving someone and not himself;<sup id="cite_ref-SVala_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SVala-3">[3]</a></sup> and unlike Melkor, who wanted to unmake and corrupt the world, Sauron wished to rule it and do what he wanted with it.<sup id="cite_ref-myths_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-myths-6">[5]</a></sup>

After [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) made his great fortress of [Angband](/wiki/Angband) in the north-west of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth), he appointed Sauron to be its commander.<sup id="cite_ref-SCaptivity_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SCaptivity-10">[9]</a></sup>

At some point after the [Awakening of the Elves](/wiki/Awakening_of_the_Elves), it was Sauron who first discovered them and their dwellings.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[10]</a></sup> Following their subsequent discovery by [Oromë](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB), the Valar [made war against Melkor](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Powers "Battle of the Powers") to protect them and though they stormed Angband and Utumno, they failed to find Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-SCaptivity_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SCaptivity-10">[9]</a></sup> Thus Sauron continued his evil operations; during the [Great March](/wiki/Great_Journey "Great Journey") of the [Eldar](/wiki/Eldar) to Valinor, it is said that he may have been responsible for the affliction upon the [land of the Elves](/wiki/Atyamar "Atyamar") in the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin), forcing the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) to resume their journey.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>

With the unchaining of Morgoth and his subsequent destruction of the [Two Trees of Valinor](/wiki/Two_Trees_of_Valinor), the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) first rose and ushered in the awakening of [Men](/wiki/Men). Leaving Sauron in command of the war, Morgoth left Angband in secret to find the second-born kindred of the [Children of Ilúvatar](/wiki/Children_of_Il%C3%BAvatar) and to corrupt them to his will.<sup id="cite_ref-SWest_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SWest-13">[12]</a></sup>

After the [Fall of Fingolfin](/wiki/Fall_of_Fingolfin), Sauron launched an attack on [Tol Sirion](/wiki/Tol_Sirion). Utter fear descended upon [Orodreth](/wiki/Orodreth) and those who defended the isle. Sauron assailed [Minas Tirith](</wiki/Minas_Tirith_(Beleriand)> "Minas Tirith (Beleriand)") and turned it into a watch tower for Morgoth. Therein Sauron sat and Tol Sirion the fair became [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](/wiki/Tol-in-Gaurhoth), the Isle of Werewolves.<sup id="cite_ref-SFingolfin_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SFingolfin-14">[13]</a></sup>

Upon hearing of the deeds of [Barahir](/wiki/Barahir) and his companions, Morgoth ordered Sauron to find and kill them. [Gorlim](/wiki/Gorlim), one of Barahir's companions, was captured and brought before Sauron. Sauron promised that he would free Gorlim and his wife [Eilinel](/wiki/Eilinel) in return for information. Under the terror of Sauron's eyes, Gorlim revealed everything he knew, and thus the hiding place of Barahir was betrayed to the enemy. Subsequently, Sauron revealed Eilinel was dead and had Gorlim put to death.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-15">[14]</a></sup>

[Beren](/wiki/Beren), son of [Barahir](/wiki/Barahir), promised to avenge his father's death. He wandered throughout [Dorthonion](/wiki/Dorthonion) as an outlaw and achieved great deeds that were heard far and wide. Morgoth set a high price on his head and Sauron, commanding a great army of [werewolves](/wiki/Werewolves "Werewolves") and [fell beasts](/wiki/Fell_beasts "Fell beasts"), sought for Beren.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-15">[14]</a></sup>

Later, [Finrod Felagund](/wiki/Finrod "Finrod"), Beren, and their ten companions left [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond) in search of the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils). Despite their being disguised as Orcs, Sauron espied them as they entered into the vale between [Ered Wethrin](/wiki/Ered_Wethrin) and [Taur-nu-Fuin](</wiki/Taur-nu-Fuin_(Dorthonion)> "Taur-nu-Fuin (Dorthonion)") and was suspicious, as Orcs passing were supposed to report to him. He had them captured and they were brought to him. There Finrod and Sauron fought in songs of power; the strength of both was great, but Sauron was more powerful. He then stripped them of their Orc disguises but failed to discern who they were. He had them thrown into a dark pit where one by one they were devoured by a werewolf. Withstanding this horror, they refused to betray one another.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-15">[14]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_Huan_Subdues_Sauron.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/4/4c/Ted_Nasmith_-_Huan_Subdues_Sauron.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_Huan_Subdues_Sauron.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/4/4c/Ted_Nasmith_-_Huan_Subdues_Sauron.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="300"/></a><figcaption><i>Huan subdues Sauron</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

When all of their companions were dead, Finrod and Beren were the last who remained alive in Sauron's pit. When a werewolf went to attack Beren, Finrod Felagund used all his power to defeat it. In this he was successful. However, he was critically wounded and soon passed away. In that dark moment, [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) came to the bridge of [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](/wiki/Tol-in-Gaurhoth) and sang. From his tower of Minas Tirith, Sauron saw Lúthien and knew that it was the famous daughter of [Melian](/wiki/Melian) and [Thingol](/wiki/Thingol). He desired to capture her and hand her over to Morgoth. Therefore, he sent a wolf to the bridge, but it was quickly and silently slain by [Huan](/wiki/Huan). He sent many more, and each one Huan killed. Finally, he sent [Draugluin](/wiki/Draugluin), sire of the werewolves of Angband. The fight between Huan and Draugluin was fierce. Eventually Draugluin fled and, before dying, he told his master that Huan was there. According to prophecy, Huan would not die until he had encountered the greatest of all wolves, so Sauron contrived to take on that role and defeat the hound. Therefore Sauron took on the form of a werewolf, the greatest the world had ever seen then, and went towards the bridge. So great was the terror of his approach that even Huan momentarily recoiled. Sauron leaped to attack Lúthien, but she drew her magic veil over his eyes, afflicting him with fatigue and blindness. Then Huan sprang upon Sauron and there they fought. The force of Sauron's malice alone left Lúthien weak and nearly unconscious, and the fighting was brutal and prolonged; however, he could not subdue the hound of [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor). He was trapped within Huan's jaws and could not break free, even when he took the form of a serpent and finally his own shape. Rather than leave his physical form, he yielded to Lúthien, giving her control of the isle in return for his release. He then took the form of a vampire and fled to [Taur-nu-Fuin](</wiki/Taur-nu-Fuin_(Dorthonion)> "Taur-nu-Fuin (Dorthonion)"), filling the forest with horror.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-15">[14]</a></sup>

After the [War of Wrath](/wiki/War_of_Wrath), with the downfall of [Morgoth](/wiki/Morgoth) and the destruction of [Thangorodrim](/wiki/Thangorodrim), Sauron adopted a fair form and repented his evil deeds in fear of the wrath of the Valar. [Eönwë](/wiki/E%C3%B6nw%C3%AB) ordered Sauron to return to [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor) in order to receive the judgement of Manwë. Sauron was not willing to suffer such humiliation, and he instead fled and hid himself in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup>

### Second Age {#Second_Age}

It was [500](/wiki/Second_Age_500 "Second Age 500") years into the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age) when Sauron started to stir again.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> Sauron decided that the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) had forgotten about [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth) and he once again turned to evil; many Men in [East](/wiki/East) and <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/South" title="South">South</a>, already corrupted by Melkor, fell under the [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) by following him.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup> By [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [882](/wiki/Second_Age_882 "Second Age 882"), [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) sensed a shadow arising in the East and sent a [warning](/wiki/Gil-galad%27s_letter "Gil-galad's letter") to [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">[16]</a></sup> Around [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1000](/wiki/Second_Age_1000 "Second Age 1000") Sauron was alarmed by the growing power of the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans), and chose [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) as a land to make into a stronghold. He began the building of [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr), the Dark Tower, near Mount Doom.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup>

Although Sauron long knew that [Men](/wiki/Men) were easier to sway, he sought to bring the [Elves](/wiki/Elves) into his service, as they were far more powerful.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup> After lying hidden and increasing his power in secret, in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1200](/wiki/Second_Age_1200 "Second Age 1200") Sauron put on a fair visage, calling himself **Annatar**, the Lord of Gifts, an emissary from the Valar.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> He was never welcome in [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon) as [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) and [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) did not trust him and refused to treat with him, although they did not perceive who he truly was.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup>

Elsewhere Annatar was gladly received, especially in [Eregion](/wiki/Eregion), where only [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel) distrusted him. The [Noldorin](/wiki/Noldorin) smiths there learned much from him in art and magic, as their thirst for knowledge was great.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-coe_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-coe-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> Under the tutelage of Annatar and the leadership of [Celebrimbor](/wiki/Celebrimbor), grandson of [Fëanor](/wiki/F%C3%ABanor), the [Gwaith-i-Mírdain](/wiki/Gwaith-i-M%C3%ADrdain), became more skilled than anyone save Fëanor himself. In the year [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1500](/wiki/Second_Age_1500 "Second Age 1500"), when they reached the very height of their power, the Elves began the forging of the [Rings of Power](/wiki/Rings_of_Power), which according to Annatar, would help them preserve their powers over Middle-earth.

But Sauron was ready to begin his own plans, and in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1600](/wiki/Second_Age_1600 "Second Age 1600") - ten years after the completion of the Rings of Power - he created [The One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring) to control the bearers of the other Rings. He invested most of his own power into the Ring as he forged it, so that it would be more powerful than the others. With its power he completed the building of the Dark Tower.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup>

But the Elves were not so easily ensnared, and as soon as Sauron put on the One Ring they and Celebrimbor were aware of him, and realised they were betrayed.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-coe_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-coe-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> They hid their Rings from Sauron and did not use them. Sauron demanded that the other Rings be given to him, for they would not have been made without his knowledge. The Elves refused, and the War was inevitable.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup>

#### [Black Years](/wiki/Black_Years) {#Black_Years}

During this time, Sauron constructed the [Black Gate](/wiki/Black_Gate) of Mordor to prevent invasion, and raised massive armies of [Orcs](/wiki/Orcs), [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls), and Men, chiefly [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) and [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim) whom he dominated as a king and god. He had them make many fortified towns, and armed those under him with iron.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dm_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dm-19">[18]</a></sup>

The [War of the Elves and Sauron](/wiki/War_of_the_Elves_and_Sauron) began in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1693](/wiki/Second_Age_1693 "Second Age 1693") and was a bloody conflict which destroyed Eregion and devastated much of Eriador.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> Celebrimbor was slain and his body tied to a pole paraded at the head of Sauron's legions.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">[19]</a></sup> The Elves were pushed back almost to the Blue Mountains, while their Dwarven allies (who had also rejected Sauron) retreated behind the walls of [Moria](/wiki/Moria) where Sauron could not assail them.

Sauron was the [Dark Lord](/wiki/Dark_Lord) of almost all of Middle-earth beyond the coasts, but the [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) responded to the Elves' call for aid and sent a relief force. With united forces, Sauron's army was driven back and defeated near the [Sarn Ford](/wiki/Sarn_Ford) and withdrew to [Tharbad](/wiki/Tharbad) where he was reinforced. But the Númenórean admiral [Ciryatur](/wiki/Ciryatur) had sent a fleet up the river Gwathló and Sauron's army was [attacked in the rear and utterly defeated](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Gwathl%C3%B3 "Battle of the Gwathló"). The Dark Lord fled back to Mordor with little more than his own bodyguard and a handful of Orcs.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup>

Nonetheless, while Sauron's subsequent power never quite matched the height it had during the War with the Elves, many of his most powerful enemies' homelands had been devastated. As the Númenóreans established dominions around the [Westlands](/wiki/Westlands) (c. [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [1800](/wiki/Second_Age_1800 "Second Age 1800")),<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> Sauron's empire continued to expand to dominate barbarian Men as servants and worshippers to the far south and east.<sup id="cite_ref-dm_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dm-19">[18]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup>

As the Elves had failed him, he had decided to distribute the Rings of Power to three corrupted lords of [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor)<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-23">[22]</a></sup> and [an Easterling king](/wiki/Kham%C3%BBl "Khamûl") and other five Men, as well as lords of the Dwarves. The Dwarves proved too hardy and resistant to their effects; but the Men eventually faded and in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [2251](/wiki/Second_Age_2251 "Second Age 2251") they appeared as [Ringwraiths](/wiki/Ringwraiths), his greatest slaves.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup>

Believing he would dominate all of Middle-earth, Sauron assumed many glorious titles: **King of Kings**,<sup id="cite_ref-L131_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-24">[23]</a></sup> **King of Men**,<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-23">[22]</a></sup> **Lord of the Earth**,<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup> and even **Lord of the World**.<sup id="cite_ref-L131_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-24">[23]</a></sup>

#### On Númenor {#On_Númenor}

<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg/200px-Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="282" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/c/c0/Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg/300px-Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/c/c0/Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg/400px-Elena_Kukanova_-_The_Temple_of_Melkor.jpg 2x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="635"/></a><figcaption><i>The Temple of Melkor</i> by <a href="/wiki/Elena_Kukanova" title="Elena Kukanova">Elena Kukanova</a></figcaption></figure>

This offended the arrogant [Númenóreans](/wiki/N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3reans) who had already started to fall under the [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow). The proud Númenóreans came to Middle-earth with great force of arms, and Sauron's forces fled. Realising he could not defeat the Númenóreans with military strength, Sauron allowed himself to be taken as a hostage to Númenor by King [Ar-Pharazôn](/wiki/Ar-Pharaz%C3%B4n) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3262](/wiki/Second_Age_3262 "Second Age 3262").<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup>

There, he quickly grew from captive to adviser and was known as **Zigûr**, the Wizard;<sup id="cite_ref-Defeated_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Defeated-25">[24]</a></sup> he seduced the King and further corrupted the people.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> He converted many Númenóreans to the worship of Darkness, becoming High Priest of a Melkor cult. He had the [White Tree](</wiki/Nimloth_(tree)> "Nimloth (tree)") cut down and in its place raised a great [Temple](/wiki/Temple) in which he performed human sacrifices, persecuting those who were still [Faithful](/wiki/Faithful). Finally, he convinced the king to rebel against the Valar and attack Valinor itself, claiming they would gain immortality. [Eru](/wiki/Il%C3%BAvatar "Ilúvatar"), the supreme God, then directly intervened: Númenor was drowned under the sea, and the great navy of Númenor was destroyed.

Sauron was on Númenor in the Temple of Melkor and was caught in the ensuing [flood](/wiki/Downfall_of_N%C3%BAmenor "Downfall of Númenor"). However, his spirit survived, although severely weakened by the destruction, and it fled back to Middle-earth.

#### Against the Faithful {#Against_the_Faithful}

Sauron's spirit returned to Mordor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3320](/wiki/Second_Age_3320 "Second Age 3320"),<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> where he slowly rebuilt his strength, although he was unable to assume a fair shape. From this point on he started to rule through terror and force, largely filling the fearsome role left vacant by his former master.

Meanwhile, a few faithful Númenóreans, led by [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil), were saved from the flood, and they founded [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) and [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) in Middle-earth. Sauron still considered them his hated enemies and he launched a pre-emptive attack on Gondor in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3429](/wiki/Second_Age_3429 "Second Age 3429").<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup>

In response, the Númenóreans formed the [Last Alliance](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men "Last Alliance of Elves and Men") with the Elven-king [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad). Learning of them, Sauron dispatched some [Orcs of Mordor](/wiki/Orcs_of_Mordor) to the [Misty Mountains](/wiki/Misty_Mountains) to ambush them.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">[25]</a></sup> He also burned the [gardens](/wiki/Brown_Lands "Brown Lands") of the [Entwives](/wiki/Entwives) against the advance of the Allies down the [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin).<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">[26]</a></sup> Nonetheless, the Allies reached Mordor and defeated Sauron's forces in the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad) in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3434](/wiki/Second_Age_3434 "Second Age 3434") and finally laid siege before [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr).<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup>

The siege lasted for seven years until [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441"), when Sauron left his fortress, engaging in direct combat. Elendil and Gil-galad fought Sauron and vanquished him, but both were killed.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-toy_16-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-toy-16">[15]</a></sup> [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur), son of Elendil, cut the One Ring from Sauron's finger and claimed it. Later, the Ring betrayed him and was lost for more than two thousand years. After his defeat in the War of the Last Alliance, Sauron lost his ability to form a physical body for a great while.

### Third Age {#Third_Age}

Sauron's defeat released his subjects, like the [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings), from his tyranny, but they fell into chaos. Their tribes and kingdoms battled against each other and some withdrew to the hated west.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">[27]</a></sup> Weakened by his defeat and the loss of the One Ring, it is thought that he fled to the far east to regain his power and strength before returning.

It was not until c. [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1000](/wiki/Third_Age_1000 "Third Age 1000") that Sauron could again begin to take shape in a physical living body. Worried by this prospect, the [Valar](/wiki/Valar) sent [five](/wiki/Wizards "Wizards") [Maiar](/wiki/Maiar) from the West to assist the peoples of Middle-earth against Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup>

His power was enough that he began again to throw a shadow across portions of [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth). Apparently, Sauron's spirit managed to move some Easterlings, who invaded [Rhovanion](/wiki/Rhovanion) and came to the [Vales of Anduin](/wiki/Vales_of_Anduin).<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup>

#### The Necromancer {#The_Necromancer}

These moves coincided with the coming of the [Shadow](/wiki/Shadow) to [Greenwood the Great](/wiki/Mirkwood "Mirkwood").<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">[30]</a></sup> Around [1050](/wiki/Third_Age_1050 "Third Age 1050") he chose [a hill](/wiki/Amon_Lanc "Amon Lanc") in southern Greenwood as a place to build the fortress of [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur). At first, the [Wise](/wiki/Wise) thought that this "Necromancer" was one of the [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) who had returned and taken up residence in southern Greenwood.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

The year [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [1300](/wiki/Third_Age_1300 "Third Age 1300") marked Sauron's increased power, evidenced by "evil things" who multiplied and grew bold again, like the [Orcs of the Misty Mountains](/wiki/Orcs_of_the_Misty_Mountains) and some of the [Dragons](/wiki/Dragons), who attacked the [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves); and the return of the Nazgûl, with the founding of the evil realm of [Angmar](/wiki/Angmar).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> In the following centuries, Sauron's subjects in Angmar, the East and the South concentrated against his ancient enemies. Kings [Araphant](/wiki/Araphant) of [Arnor](/wiki/Arnor) and [Ondoher](/wiki/Ondoher) of [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor) realised that a single force was coordinating the attacks on both of their kingdoms and that they needed to work together to combat this evil. However, Angmar was successful in destroying Arnor, and soon after the Nazgûl gathered in Mordor and [conquered](/wiki/Second_Fall_of_Minas_Ithil "Second Fall of Minas Ithil") [Minas Ithil](/wiki/Minas_Ithil) to prepare for Sauron's return; their final success was ending the [royal line](/wiki/King_of_Gondor "King of Gondor") of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gondor_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gondor-33">[32]</a></sup> As his shadow deepened, [a balrog](/wiki/Durin%27s_Bane "Durin's Bane") awoke, causing the desertion of [Moria](/wiki/Moria).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

By [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2060](/wiki/Third_Age_2060 "Third Age 2060") the power of Dol Guldur grew so much that [the Wise](/wiki/The_Wise "The Wise") were alerted that Sauron was returning. [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) entered the fortress in [2063](/wiki/Third_Age_2063 "Third Age 2063") but the shadow fled before him. Returning to the [East](/wiki/East),<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> Sauron started corrupting the [Easterlings](/wiki/Easterlings) and forging a strong alliance between their tribes.<sup class="noprint" style="line-height: 0.4em;">[<a href="/wiki/Help:References" title="Help:References"><i>source?</i></a>]</sup> The period of his absence was known as the *[Watchful Peace](/wiki/Watchful_Peace)*, because the Shadow on Mirkwood had lessened and the Nazgûl stayed quiet in Minas Morgul,<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> using this period to prepare for Sauron's return.

#### Return to Dol Guldur {#Return_to_Dol_Guldur}

The Necromancer returned in [2460](/wiki/Third_Age_2460 "Third Age 2460") more powerful, with many Men in his service, and again took up residence in [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> His return coincided with the One Ring revealing itself [three years later](/wiki/Third_Age_2463 "Third Age 2463"), falling in the hands of [a Stoor](/wiki/Gollum "Gollum"). Sensing the danger, the [Wise](/wiki/Wise) formed the [White Council](/wiki/White_Council).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

The Necromancer's aims remained to gather the Rings of Power, find news about the One Ring, and eliminate the [Heir of Isildur](/wiki/Heir_of_Isildur), if one remained in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth).<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">[33]</a></sup> In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2475](/wiki/Third_Age_2475 "Third Age 2475") the [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai) exited Mordor and briefly conquered [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien). [Some years later](/wiki/Third_Age_2480 "Third Age 2480") the Orcs of the Mountains organised themselves, blocking the passes to the West; Moria had been depopulated centuries earlier by the Balrog, and Sauron sent Orcs and [Trolls](/wiki/Trolls) there.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> Also under his shadow were the [Balchoth](/wiki/Balchoth), who invaded Mirkwood and cooperated with Orcs to attack Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-stew_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-35">[34]</a></sup> Sauron's servants captured the [Dwarf King](/wiki/King_of_Durin%27s_Folk "King of Durin's Folk") [Thráin II](/wiki/Thr%C3%A1in "Thráin") and took [one](/wiki/Ring_of_Thr%C3%B3r "Ring of Thrór") of the [Seven Dwarf rings](/wiki/Seven_Rings "Seven Rings") from him.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

Still investigating the Necromancer, [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) snuck into Dol Guldur in [2850](/wiki/Third_Age_2850 "Third Age 2850") and met the dying Dwarf King, learning that the Necromancer was none other than Sauron. [The next year](/wiki/Third_Age_2851 "Third Age 2851"), Gandalf informed the White Council and urged an immediate attack upon the fortress, but [Saruman the White](/wiki/Saruman "Saruman") had learned of the presence of the [Ruling Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") near the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields), and he thought it best to allow Sauron to build up his strength in order to reveal its location so that Saruman could seize it himself. Following this strategy, Saruman opposed Gandalf.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

As his power was growing and his arising came closer, his minions moved again against Gondor; his agents stirred the [Haradrim](/wiki/Haradrim) to resume attacking Gondor, while [Uruk-hai](/wiki/Uruk-hai) and [Orcs of Mordor](/wiki/Orcs_of_Mordor) infested [Ithilien](/wiki/Ithilien)<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-stew_35-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-35">[34]</a></sup> but never managed to pass beyond Anduin, further into Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-hunt_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunt-36">[35]</a></sup> All this time Sauron apparently had learned about the [Disaster of the Gladden Fields](/wiki/Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields) where his old enemy Isildur was killed, shortly after his own demise, almost three millennia ago. He sent his minions to look for the One Ring around [Anduin](/wiki/Anduin) near the [Gladden Fields](/wiki/Gladden_Fields), not knowing that it had been already in the possession of [a Stoor](/wiki/Gollum "Gollum").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

#### Attack of the White Council {#Attack_of_the_White_Council}

Considering the situations, and the occupation of the dragon [Smaug](/wiki/Smaug) of [Erebor](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain "Lonely Mountain"), Gandalf was worried that Sauron's military assault against the <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/West" title="West">West</a> was a matter of time, and that he would use Smaug in his force; Gandalf started considering a simultaneous attack both against Dol Guldur and against Smaug, to weaken Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-erebor_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erebor-37">[36]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/7/7a/Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg/300px-Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="289" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/7/7a/Angus_McBride_-_Dol_Guldur.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="385"/></a><figcaption><i>Dol Guldur</i> by <a href="/wiki/Angus_McBride" title="Angus McBride">Angus McBride</a></figcaption></figure>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2939](/wiki/Third_Age_2939 "Third Age 2939"), Saruman learned that Sauron was searching for the Ring, and worried that he would find it sooner than he. In [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), he conceded with Gandalf to [attack Dol Guldur](/wiki/Attack_on_Dol_Guldur "Attack on Dol Guldur").<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> Indeed, in the meantime Gandalf managed to eliminate Smaug, and the Orcs of the Mountains were decimated in the [ensuing battle](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies "Battle of Five Armies"), allowing the [Kingdom under the Mountain](/wiki/Kingdom_under_the_Mountain) and [Dale](/wiki/Dale) to flourish again.<sup id="cite_ref-erebor_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erebor-37">[36]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">[37]</a></sup>

Although Gandalf was weakening Sauron's potential grasp in the [North](/wiki/Wilderland "Wilderland"),<sup id="cite_ref-erebor_37-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erebor-37">[36]</a></sup> Sauron had been expecting the White Council's attack against Dol Guldur and fled from there.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup>

#### Return to Mordor {#Return_to_Mordor}

The [Nazgûl](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl) had been preparing [Barad-dûr](/wiki/Barad-d%C3%BBr) for Sauron's return, so it was easy for Sauron to return secretly to his old stronghold [a year later](/wiki/Third_Age_2942 "Third Age 2942"). Sauron declared himself openly in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2951](/wiki/Third_Age_2951 "Third Age 2951"), sent three Nazgûl back to Dol Guldur and started rebuilding the Dark Tower, and once it was completed, the [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom) erupted.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-stew_35-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-35">[34]</a></sup>

From then on, Sauron stayed in Barad-dûr to conduct his war on the [Free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples). The shadow of Mordor caused despair and sickness to the Gondorians, like [Finduilas](</wiki/Finduilas_(wife_of_Denethor)> "Finduilas (wife of Denethor)"), and in his desperation, the Steward [Denethor](/wiki/Denethor), Sauron's most immediate enemy, used the [Anor-stone](/wiki/Anor-stone) to gain knowledge. That *[palantír](/wiki/Palant%C3%ADr "Palantír")* was directly linked to the [Ithil-stone](/wiki/Ithil-stone) that the Nazgûl had taken from Minas Morgul, and when Sauron used it, he discovered Denethor was using his. Sauron attempted to wrench the Anor-stone to his will, but failed due to Denethor's strength of will and birth-right to the stone. But this stressed and wore out the steward, who lost his hope.<sup id="cite_ref-stew_35-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stew-35">[34]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">[38]</a></sup>

By [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3000](/wiki/Third_Age_3000 "Third Age 3000"), the shadow lengthened, and Saruman, who was residing in [Orthanc](/wiki/Orthanc), had secretly found and decided to use the [Orthanc-stone](/wiki/Orthanc-stone). As happened with Denethor, Sauron linked with his mind, managing to corrupt him;<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> from one of his wisest enemies, Saruman became one of his greatest allies.

Around [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3009](/wiki/Third_Age_3009 "Third Age 3009"), [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum), who formerly bore the One Ring and now pursued its thief, ventured into Mordor and was captured by Sauron's minions. Gollum was tortured and interrogated for the following years,<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> and before releasing him, Sauron learned that the One Ring had been found by [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) of [the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire").

Sauron bred immense armies of Orcs and allied with or enslaved Men from the east and south. He adopted the symbol of a lidless eye, and he was able at that time to send out his will over Middle-earth, so that the [Eye of Sauron](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron) became a symbol of power and fear.

#### The War of the Ring {#The_War_of_the_Ring}

Sauron was alarmed when the [Elves of Mirkwood](/wiki/Elves_of_Mirkwood) captured Gollum,<sup id="cite_ref-TA_32-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-32">[31]</a></sup> so in June of [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [3018](/wiki/Third_Age_3018 "Third Age 3018") he dispatched the Nazgûl to hunt for [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring") in secrecy. To disguise the crossing of the Nine over the Great River, Sauron staged an [attack on Osgiliath](/wiki/Sauron%27s_attack_on_Osgiliath "Sauron's attack on Osgiliath"). This feint served two purposes: first, to test the strength and preparedness of Denethor, and second and more importantly, to present the Nazgûl as merely a military asset, concealing from the [Wise](/wiki/Wise) their true mission.<sup id="cite_ref-hunt_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunt-36">[35]</a></sup> Denethor's forces were stronger than Sauron hoped, but Sauron took the eastern half of the city nonetheless. This allowed the Nazgûl to cross the Great River and begin their hunt.<sup id="cite_ref-hunt_36-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunt-36">[35]</a></sup> Now knowing the measure of his enemy, Sauron continued to amass his forces in Minas Morgul and Mordor in preparation for the siege of Gondor.<sup id="cite_ref-TT|IV4_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TT|IV4-40">[39]</a></sup>

By September, the Nazgûl had failed to discover the Shire, and Sauron's wrath and fear were mounting. Via messenger, Sauron ordered the Nine to discard secrecy in favour of speed and go to Isengard to confront Saruman about his knowledge of the whereabouts of the Ring. At this time, Sauron also conveyed to the Nine such dire threats as to dismay even the Lord of Morgul.<sup id="cite_ref-hunt_36-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunt-36">[35]</a></sup> The Nine ultimately failed in their mission, being defeated and unhorsed by the flood of the Bruinen. The Witch-king returned alone to Mordor in December of 3018 to Sauron's great fear, for Sauron perceived that his foes still had strength left in them, and luck seemed to be on their side.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">[40]</a></sup>

Sauron's next glimpse of the Ring (or so he believed) would come in the late hours of [5 March](/wiki/5_March) T.A. 3019, when Pippin looked into the *palantír* of Orthanc that he had recovered from the flood of Isengard. Sauron, unaware of Saruman's defeat at hands of the [Ents](/wiki/Ents), assumed Pippin was the Wizard's prisoner, and was being forced to look into the *palantír* as a form of cruel sport. Rather than interrogate Pippin immediately, Sauron chose to torment him, and bid him tell Saruman that he would send for the prisoner immediately.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">[41]</a></sup> He was therefore deceived into thinking the Ring was finally within his grasp.

A few hours later, on the morning of [6 March](/wiki/6_March), Sauron encountered one of his foes in the *palantír* of Orthanc: Aragorn looked into the Stone, revealing himself to Sauron as the Heir of Isildur and wielder of [Andúril](/wiki/And%C3%BAril), the Blade Reforged. Aragorn spoke no word to Sauron and with a great effort of will wrested control of the Stone from him. As Aragorn intended, this played on Sauron's gnawing doubts and filled him with fear, believing that Aragorn might have overthrown Saruman, seizing Pippin and therefore the Ring. Thus, he ordered the Witch-king to launch his long-planned assault on Gondor immediately, rather than wait for all preparations to be made, hoping to destroy his enemies before they had a chance to make full use of the Ring's power.<sup id="cite_ref-RKV2_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKV2-43">[42]</a></sup>

The death of the Witch-king and the defeat of his army in the [Battle of the Pelennor Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Pelennor_Fields) were a setback to Sauron, but he still had a great force of arms held in reserve in Mordor, more than enough to eventually secure military victory against all of his enemies combined. After his encounter with Aragorn in the *palantír*, Sauron believed that one of his foes in Minas Tirith held the One Ring and would soon attempt to use it. As such, Gandalf was able to exploit Sauron's false beliefs with an audacious plan: the army of the Free Peoples would march on the Black Gate as a feint, keeping Sauron's Eye fixed upon them and blind to all else that moved, as the true Ringbearer made his way to Mount Doom.<sup id="cite_ref-RKV9_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKV9-44">[43]</a></sup>

For the next nine days, Gandalf's plan worked - as the Host of the West made its way east and north, Sauron kept his entire focus upon it and diverted the whole strength of Mordor to the Black Gate to meet them. Even when Shagrat brought news of an "Elvish" intruder in [Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Cirith_Ungol), Sauron's belief that the Ring was with the Host of the West remained unshaken and he made no attempt to investigate further; he could certainly not imagine that his enemies were trying to sneak the Ring into Mordor. Frodo and Sam thus made it to the [Sammath Naur](/wiki/Cracks_of_Doom "Cracks of Doom") undetected, and Sauron did not become aware of them until the very moment that Frodo claimed the Ring for himself.<sup id="cite_ref-RKVI3_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKVI3-45">[44]</a></sup>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/d/d1/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg/300px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="228" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/d/d1/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg/450px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/d/d1/Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg/600px-Ted_Nasmith_-_The_Shadow_of_Sauron.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1221" data-file-height="929"/></a><figcaption><i>The Shadow of Sauron</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ted_Nasmith" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

Only then did Sauron finally perceive Frodo and, realising he had been tricked, was overwhelmed with wrath and fear. He instantly lost all interest in the ongoing [Battle of the Morannon](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Morannon) and dispatched the Nazgûl from the skies above the battle to Mount Doom with all haste. It was too late; Gollum fell into the Crack of Doom with the Ring, destroying it and himself. In the moment of the Ring's destruction, Barad-dûr and many other of Sauron's fortresses crumbled to ruin. Sauron's spirit emerged and rose above Mordor like a black cloud - he stretched out his hand towards the army of the West, but even as he did so, he was blown away by a great wind and vanished.<sup id="cite_ref-RKVI4_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKVI4-46">[45]</a></sup>

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Thus Sauron's power was unmade, and his dominion in Middle-earth came to an end. According to Gandalf, Sauron was "maimed for ever, becoming a mere spirit of malice that gnaws itself in the shadows, but cannot again grow or take shape."<sup id="cite_ref-RKV9_44-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKV9-44">[43]</a></sup> Sauron's corporeal body that was in the Dark Tower died, and without the Ring, his spirit no longer had the power to create a new one. Though his indestructible mind and being were bound forever to [Eä](/wiki/E%C3%A4), Sauron had lost all power to effect his will upon the world<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">[46]</a></sup> and could never again grow in strength.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">[47]</a></sup>

It is said of Sauron, in summary of his long career of wickedness in Arda, that "he rose like a shadow of Morgoth and a ghost of his malice, and walked behind him on the same ruinous path down into the Void."<sup id="cite_ref-SVala_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SVala-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

Sauron was a being of exceptional power, both in personal might and in sorcery, yet his greatest strength lie in his cunning, intellect, and charisma; through persuasion, deception, and coercion, he brought ruin to many mortal realms and united others unto himself, often without ever having to physically intervene, and he nearly cast Middle-earth into darkness several times throughout his existence.

Much like his [master](/wiki/Morgoth "Morgoth"), pride in his own abilities was his greatest personal weakness, and this arrogance, coupled with an underestimation of his enemies, would ultimately doom him.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">[48]</a></sup>

### Physical form {#Physical_form}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/5/55/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg/300px-The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="184" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/5/55/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg/450px-The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/5/55/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg/600px-The_Brothers_Hildebrandt_-_The_Dark_Lord_-_Sauron.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1257"/></a><figcaption><i>The Dark Lord: Sauron</i> by <a href="/wiki/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt" title="The Brothers Hildebrandt">the Brothers Hildebrandt</a></figcaption></figure>

At first Sauron appeared as a royal and commanding figure in a strong body. He was also able to veil his power and change his shape. Later, however, he could take only a terrible form, of a stature greater than a [Man](/wiki/Men "Men")'s, though not giant,<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">[49]</a></sup> "an image of malice and hatred made visible; and the Eye of Sauron the Terrible few could endure."<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-23">[22]</a></sup> [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) recounted that at the [Siege of Barad-dûr](/wiki/Siege_of_Barad-d%C3%BBr), Sauron's hand was black with a deadly burning touch.<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-coe_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-coe-1">[1]</a></sup> Gollum would also call him "Black Hand", noticing he only had four fingers after Isildur cut one off.<sup id="cite_ref-Closed_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Closed-51">[50]</a></sup>

### Eye of Sauron {#Eye_of_Sauron}

The **[Eye of Sauron](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron)**, called by various names, was the symbol of Sauron the Dark Lord following the loss of [the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"). This symbol was adopted to show his unceasing vigilance and piercing perception, and was displayed on the weaponry of his servants, or at least the [orcs](/wiki/Orcs "Orcs").

Sauron's Eye as Frodo sees it in the [Mirror of Galadriel](/wiki/Mirror_of_Galadriel) is the only feature of his later form described in detail. It is yellow and rimmed with fire, with a slit pupil, "a window into nothing."<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">[51]</a></sup> The colour is compared to that of a cat's eye, but because of the references to Sauron's Lidless Eye, it may also resemble that of a snake, such as an adder. Readers differ as to whether Sauron's eyes were literally lidless and resembled what Frodo saw, or instead the Eye was only a symbol that Frodo saw in the Mirror in an elaborate form.

Sauron's gaze, even indirectly, was known to wither, exhaust, and weaken his foes, with [Denethor](/wiki/Denethor_II "Denethor II"), [Aragorn](/wiki/Aragorn_II "Aragorn II"), and [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) all rendered weary and dreadful after confronting him.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">[52]</a></sup> The sheer malevolence Sauron exuded through his presence alone was enough to render Lúthien frail and nearly unconscious.<sup id="cite_ref-Beren_15-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beren-15">[14]</a></sup>

<i><b>Sauron</b></i> (pron. <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈsaʊron]</span>)<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">[note 2]</a></sup> is a [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) name, said to mean "the Abhorred".<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">[53]</a></sup>

Several accounts of the origin of the name *Sauron* were suggested in different linguistic manuscripts:

- deriving from Quenya *[saura](/wiki/Saura "Saura")* ("foul, evil-smelling, putrid", from the [root](/wiki/Sundocarme "Sundocarme") [THUS](/wiki/THUS#Other_versions)).<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">[54]</a></sup>
- deriving from Quenya *[saura](/wiki/Saura "Saura")* ("foul, vile"; from root [SAWA](/wiki/SAWA)). The manuscript continues saying that *Sauron* "could be a genuine [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) formation from *[saur](/wiki/Saur "Saur")*; but is probably from Quenya". However, this origin appears to have been rejected, as it is followed by the comment "No. [THAW-](/wiki/THAW "THAW"), cruel. *[Saura](/wiki/Saura)*, cruel" in the manuscript.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">[55]</a></sup>
- deriving from the [Primitive Quendian](/wiki/Primitive_Quendian) form *þaurond-* (formed from the adjective *þaurā* "detestable", from root [THAW](/wiki/THAW)).<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">[56]</a></sup>
- deriving from <i><b>Thauron</b></i>, which includes the [Sindarin](/wiki/Sindarin) element *[thaur](/wiki/Thaur "Thaur")* ("abominable, abhorrent"; also found in <a class="mw-selflink-fragment" href="#Other_names"><i>Gor<b>thaur</b></i></a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-59">[57]</a></sup>

<i><b>Gorthaur</b></i> was a name used of Sauron by the [Sindar](/wiki/Sindar) during the [First Age](/wiki/First_Age),<sup id="cite_ref-SVala_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SVala-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">[58]</a></sup> which was composed of the elements <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Gor&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gor (page does not exist)">gor</a></i> ("horror, dread") and *[thaur](/wiki/Thaur "Thaur")* ("abominable, abhorrent"), thus meaning "Terrible Dread";<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_59-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-59">[57]</a></sup> an alternate variation of this name was <i><b>Gorsodh</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">[59]</a></sup> The Quenya equivalent was <i><b>Ñorsus</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-PE17.1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17.1-4">[4]</a></sup>

In some of Tolkien's notes from the 1950s, it is said that Sauron's original name was <i><b>Mairon</b></i> ([Q](/wiki/Quenya "Quenya"), "the Admirable"), but this was altered after he was suborned by Melkor. He continued to call himself Mairon the Admirable, or **Tar-mairon** ("King Excellent"), until after Númenor's downfall,<sup id="cite_ref-PE17.1_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PE17.1-4">[4]</a></sup> although he could not use that name in Númenor, as it was a Quenya name with royal implications. There he was called <i><b>Zigûr</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Defeated_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Defeated-25">[24]</a></sup> meaning "Wizard" in [Adûnaic](/wiki/Ad%C3%BBnaic).<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">[60]</a></sup>

<i><b>Annatar</b></i> is Quenya for "Lord of Gifts", from *[anna](/wiki/Anna "Anna")* + *[tar](/wiki/Tar "Tar")*.<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">[61]</a></sup> It can be noticed that Morgoth used a similar name when he seduced the first [Men](/wiki/Men): "Giver of Gifts".<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">[62]</a></sup> In an isolated note, Tolkien gives other names used by Sauron when he seduced the Elves in the Second Age: <i><b>Artano</b></i> ("High-smith") and <i><b>Aulendil</b></i> ("Devoted to Aulë").<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">[63]</a></sup>

Among his many titles and epithets were:

- The **Base Master of Treachery**, used once by Gandalf to explain why the [Mouth of Sauron](/wiki/Mouth_of_Sauron) should not be trusted.<sup id="cite_ref-Mouth_66-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mouth-66">[64]</a></sup>
- The **[Black Hand](/wiki/Black_Hand)**, used twice by Gollum.<sup id="cite_ref-Closed_51-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Closed-51">[50]</a></sup>
- The **[Black Master](/wiki/Master "Master")**, used by [Isildur](/wiki/Isildur) when cursing Sauron's former servants, the [Oathbreakers](/wiki/Oathbreakers).<sup id="cite_ref-RKV2_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RKV2-43">[42]</a></sup>
- The **Black One**, used once by [Gollum](/wiki/Gollum).<sup id="cite_ref-Closed_51-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Closed-51">[50]</a></sup>
- The **[Dark Lord](/wiki/Dark_Lord)**, given by the [Free Peoples](/wiki/Free_Peoples) after he rose in the Second Age.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup>
- The **Dark Power**, used by Gandalf.<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_67-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-67">[65]</a></sup>
- The **Deceiver**, given by [Amandil](/wiki/Amandil) before departing to ask the Valar deliverance from Sauron's corruption.<sup id="cite_ref-Akallabeth_23-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akallabeth-23">[22]</a></sup>
- The **Enemy**, given by the [Free Peoples](/wiki/Free_Peoples) after he rose in the Second Age.<sup id="cite_ref-SRings_9-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SRings-9">[8]</a></sup>
- The **[Eye](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron "Eye of Sauron")**, with its variants, mainly referred to his mental form and his [emblem](/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron "Eye of Sauron"), but also applied to Sauron himself.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">[66]</a></sup>
- The **Great**, following the name of *Sauron*, it is only used once by Gandalf,<sup id="cite_ref-Shadow_67-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shadow-67">[65]</a></sup> once by [Glóin](/wiki/Gl%C3%B3in),<sup id="cite_ref-lotr-coe_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lotr-coe-1">[1]</a></sup> and once by the Mouth of Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-Mouth_66-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mouth-66">[64]</a></sup>
- The **[King of Kings](/wiki/King_of_Kings)**, given by himself when he rose in the Second Age, enraging Ar-Pharazôn.<sup id="cite_ref-L131_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-L131-24">[23]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

Since the earliest versions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Sauron has undergone many changes. The prototype of this character was **[Tevildo](/wiki/Tevildo)**, lord of the cats, who played the role later taken by Sauron in the earliest version of the story of [Beren](/wiki/Beren) and [Lúthien](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien) in *[The Book of Lost Tales: Part Two](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales:_Part_Two)*, "[The Tale of Tinúviel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_Tin%C3%BAviel)". Morgoth also had another servant named **[Fankil](/wiki/Fankil)** who led evil [Dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves) and notably acted as his lieutenant, a role assumed by Sauron in the final works.

Tevildo later was transformed into **[Thû](/wiki/Th%C3%BB)**, the Necromancer - the first proper iteration of the later Sauron. The name was then changed to *Gorthû*, *Sûr*, and finally to Sauron. *Gorthû*, in the form *Gorthaur*, remained in *The Silmarillion*.

In the first version of the Fall of Númenor, Sûr (Thû) originally came to Númenor in the likeness of a great bird and preached a message of deliverance, and he prophesied the second coming of Morgoth. He spoke with the king (Angor) and queen (Istar) and promised them undying life and lordship of the Earth. They believed him and fell under the shadow, and the greatest part of the people of Númenor followed them. Angor raised a great temple to Morgoth in the midst of the land, and Sûr dwelt there.<sup id="cite_ref-downfall1_72-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-downfall1-72">[70]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§5</sup> As Angor felt the oncoming of old age, Sûr convinced the king that that the gifts of Morgoth were withheld by the Gods, and that to obtain plenitude of power and undying life he must be master of the West. Sûr then aided the Númenóreans in making their great armament.<sup id="cite_ref-downfall1_72-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-downfall1-72">[70]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§6</sup> Thû survived the destruction of the island and retreated to Middle-earth where he fought wars against the surviving Númenóreans, whose hearts were not crooked and hated Thû, that assailed his temples and servants.<sup id="cite_ref-downfall1_72-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-downfall1-72">[70]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§13</sup> Eventually Amroth,<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">[note 3]</a></sup> the King of Beleriand, took council with Elrond, and with what Elves remained they crossed over the mountains and assailed the fortress of Thû; Amroth himself was slain, but Thû was brought to his knees, with his servants dispelled and his dwellings destroyed, and he fled to a dark forest and hid himself.<sup id="cite_ref-downfall1_72-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-downfall1-72">[70]</a></sup><sup class="references" style="margin: 0;line-height: 0.4em; padding-top: 0px !important; font-size: 70%;">:§14</sup>

Prior to the publication of *The Silmarillion*, Sauron's origins and true identity were unclear to those without full access to Tolkien's notes. For example, in early editions of *[The Guide to Middle Earth](/wiki/The_Complete_Guide_to_Middle-earth "The Complete Guide to Middle-earth")*, Sauron was described as "probably of the Eldar elves".

### The Necromancer {#The_Necromancer_2}

In *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, the Necromancer is an obscure villainous entity mentioned fleetingly by [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) as one of the dangers of the wider world. He is peripheral to the plot of the book: explaining why the company takes the dangerous road through Mirkwood rather than going around, and providing a reason for Gandalf's absence for that section of the journey. Thematically the Necromancer, a truly 'terrible' force beyond the power of the main protagonists, gives the world of *The Hobbit* a greater level of reality which Tolkien felt was necessary for a "fairy-tale" to ring true.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter17_74-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter17-74">[71]</a></sup>

Despite the alias, it would appear that the Necromancer was always intended to stand for Sauron, a figure from the very earliest phases of his legendarium (as [Tevildo](/wiki/Tevildo) in "[The Tale of Tinúviel](/wiki/The_Tale_of_Tin%C3%BAviel)"). Shortly after the publication of *The Hobbit* Tolkien wrote:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Mr Baggins began as a comic tale among conventional and inconsistent Grimm&#39;s fairy-tale dwarves, and got drawn into the edge of it – so that even Sauron the terrible peeped over the edge.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Letter_19" title="Letter 19">Letter 19</a></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Indeed, in the *[Lay of Leithian](/wiki/Lay_of_Leithian)*, [Thû](/wiki/Th%C3%BB) is called a "necromancer" who "held his hosts of [phantoms](/wiki/Phantoms "Phantoms") and of wandering [ghosts](/wiki/Ghosts "Ghosts")" (l. 2075) in [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](/wiki/Tol-in-Gaurhoth).<sup id="cite_ref-75" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-75">[72]</a></sup>

However, as *The Hobbit* was not originally intended to be integrated with Tolkien's wider mythology the Necromancer did not necessarily need to be consistent with his [First Age](/wiki/First_Age) counterpart Sauron, rather the two were loosely linked to add an 'impression of depth' to the narrative of *The Hobbit*. With Tolkien's decision to merge the two 'worlds' and make Sauron the central antagonist of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)* came the need to reconcile the two figures and account for his whereabouts in the millennia between the end of the First Age and his dwelling in Bilbo's Mirkwood. This was largely achieved in the *[Tale of Years](/wiki/Appendix_B "Appendix B")*, with Sauron becoming a much greater figure after the fall of his master, one who arguably drove the history of the entire Second and Third Ages of Middle-earth.

Fans have noted that the alias "Necromancer" for Sauron is obscure, as in the context of neither *The Hobbit* nor *The Lord of the Rings* Sauron is ever specifically mentioned to use <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/necromancy" title="wikipedia:necromancy">necromancy</a>, i.e. controlling the [spirits](/wiki/Spirits "Spirits") of the dead.<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">[73]</a></sup>

A later essay mentions the practice of necromancy concerning the *[fëar](/wiki/F%C3%ABar "Fëar")* of the [Unbodied](/wiki/Unbodied) Elves, mentioning that Sauron possibly did so and also taught his followers,<sup id="cite_ref-77" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-77">[74]</a></sup> although this reference is not given in the context of the story.

### Films {#Films}

<b>1978: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)" title="The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (1978 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Sauron is briefly shown in the prologue sequence as a shadowy figure in a horned helmet in a non-speaking role.</dd></dl>

<b>2001-03: <a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)" title="The Lord of the Rings (film series)"><i>The Lord of the Rings</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Sauron is played by <a href="/wiki/Sala_Baker" title="Sala Baker">Sala Baker</a> and voiced by <a href="/wiki/Alan_Howard" title="Alan Howard">Alan Howard</a>. In these films, he is depicted as a tall -yet not gigantic - armored warlord wielding a huge mace (a blend between his vague description by Tolkien and the more detailed appearance of <a href="/wiki/Morgoth" title="Morgoth">Morgoth</a> as described in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Silmarillion" title="The Silmarillion">The Silmarillion</a></i>). In the first film, he is depicted dispatching a number of Elves and Men with his mace, before killing <a href="/wiki/Gil-galad" title="Gil-galad">Gil-galad</a> (offscreen; the scene figured in the concept art done for the prologue, however) and then <a href="/wiki/Elendil" title="Elendil">Elendil</a> before being defeated by <a href="/wiki/Isildur" title="Isildur">Isildur</a> using his father&#39;s <a href="/wiki/Narsil" title="Narsil">broken sword</a> to cut off the finger wearing <a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring" title="The One Ring">the One Ring</a>, as well as three others on the same hand. This strangely causes his body to explode, producing a shockwave that knocks everyone on the battlefield off their feet.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Later on, <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a> implies to <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> that Sauron was unable to retain his physical form and that the Eye was his astral form, a detail which is never brought up in the novel. It is unknown if Saruman was truthful with this statement, or if he was either misinformed or lying (since he was already corrupted by his yearning for power and Sauron&#39;s influence).</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>For the portrayal of Sauron as a giant eye of fire in these movies, see <a href="/wiki/Eye_of_Sauron#Portrayal_in_adaptations" title="Eye of Sauron">Eye of Sauron#Portrayal in adaptations</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2012-14: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)" title="The Hobbit (film series)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (film series)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Sauron is played and voiced by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch" title="Benedict Cumberbatch">Benedict Cumberbatch</a>, under his assumed identity as the Necromancer. In these films, it is stated that the White Council does not discover he is Sauron until much later, during the events of these films, previously believing him to be a human with skills in magic. In these films, Sauron initially appears as a shadowy figure before assuming his armoured form from the previous films. With him as the slit black pupil, he projects flames around him, creating the visage of the Great Eye.</dd></dl>

**2017: <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lego_Batman_Movie" title="wikipedia:The Lego Batman Movie">The Lego Batman Movie</a></i>:**

<dl><dd>In the film, Sauron, appearing as a flaming eye from atop his tower, is among the villains recruited by The Joker to aid in his conquest of Gotham City. His powers of vision prove to be a hindrance to Batman and his companions, but he is eventually inadvertently destroyed by the Kraken&#39;s mouth projectile. He is voiced by Jemaine Clement.</dd></dl>
