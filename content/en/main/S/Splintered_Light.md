<i><b>Splintered Light: Logos and Language in Tolkien&#39;s World</b></i> is a scholarly book on [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)'s works, written by [Verlyn Flieger](/wiki/Verlyn_Flieger), first published in [1983](/wiki/1983).

A second edition was published in [2002](/wiki/2002) after the appearance of *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)* series, which gave much light on the matter.

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>J.R.R. Tolkien is perhaps best known for The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, but it is in The Silmarillion that the true depth of Tolkien&#39;s Middle-earth can be understood. The Silmarillion was written before, during, and after Tolkien wrote The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings. A collection of stories, it provides information alluded to in Tolkien&#39;s better known works and, in doing so, turns The Lord of the Rings into much more than a sequel to The Hobbit, making it instead a continuation of the mythology of Middle-earth.
</p><p><br/><br/>Verlyn Flieger&#39;s expanded and updated edition of Splintered Light, a classic study of Tolkien&#39;s fiction first published in 1983, examines The Silmarillion and The Lord of the Rings in light of Owen Barfield&#39;s linguistic theory of the fragmentation of meaning. Flieger demonstrates Tolkien&#39;s use of Barfield&#39;s concept throughout the fiction, showing how his central image of primary light splintered and refracted acts as a metaphor for the languages, peoples, and history of Middle-earth.
</p>
</blockquote>
