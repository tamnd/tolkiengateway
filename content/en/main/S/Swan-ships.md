The **Swan-ships** were [Elven](/wiki/Elves "Elves") ships crafted to resemble [swans](/wiki/Swans "Swans").

## The Teleri {#The_Teleri}

When the [Teleri](/wiki/Falmari "Falmari") came to [Aman](/wiki/Aman) and dwelt at [Alqualondë](/wiki/Alqualond%C3%AB), they built white ships in the likeness of swans. These ships had eyes of gold and jet, and their beaks also were golden.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> Fëanor later demanded the ships from the Teleri, purposing to use them to carry himself and the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) [back to Middle-earth](/wiki/Flight_of_the_Noldor "Flight of the Noldor"). [Olwë](/wiki/Olw%C3%AB) refused to surrender the ships, however, saying,

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>We learned not that craft from the Noldor, but from the <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Lords_of_the_Sea&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lords of the Sea (page does not exist)">Lords of the Sea</a>; and the white timbers we wrought with our own hands, and the white sails were woven by our wives and our daughters…these are to us as are the gems of the Noldor: the work of our hearts, whose like we shall not make again.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien</a> (ed.), <i><a href="/wiki/The_Silmarillion" title="The Silmarillion">The Silmarillion</a></i>, &#34;<i><a href="/wiki/Quenta_Silmarillion" title="Quenta Silmarillion">Quenta Silmarillion</a></i>: <a href="/wiki/Of_the_Flight_of_the_Noldor" title="Of the Flight of the Noldor">Of the Flight of the Noldor</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Fëanor then led the Noldor in an [assault](/wiki/Kinslaying_at_Alqualond%C3%AB "Kinslaying at Alqualondë") on the Teleri, taking the ships by force. Fëanor later burned the ships at [Losgar](/wiki/Losgar), destroying the fairest vessels ever to sail the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

## Lothlórien {#Lothlórien}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg/300px-Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="204" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/0d/Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg/450px-Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/0d/Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg/600px-Henning_Janssen_-_Elven_Swan_Gondola.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="545"/></a><figcaption><i>Elven Swan Gondola</i> by Henning Janssen</figcaption></figure>

The Elves of [Lothlórien](/wiki/Lothl%C3%B3rien) built a "swan-shaped barge", having a "curved neck, and jewelled eyes, and half-raised wings".<sup id="cite_ref-TI_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TI-3">[3]</a></sup>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>They turned a sharp bend in the river, and there, sailing proudly down the stream toward them, they saw a swan of great size. The water rippled on either side of the white breast beneath its curving neck. Its beak shone like burnished gold, and its eyes glinted like jet set in yellow stones; its huge white wings were half lifted. A music came down the river as it drew nearer; and suddenly they perceived that it was a ship, wrought and carved with elven-skill in the likeness of a bird. Two elves clad in white steered it with black paddles. In the midst of the vessel sat Celeborn, and behind him stood Galadriel, tall and white; a circlet of golden flowers was in her hair, and in her hand she held a harp, and she sang.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Fellowship of the Ring">The Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>, &#34;<a href="/wiki/Farewell_to_L%C3%B3rien" title="Farewell to Lórien">Farewell to Lórien</a>&#34;</cite></p>
</blockquote>

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:M%C3%A5ns_Bj%C3%B6rkman_-_Dol_Amroth_device.gif"><img src="/w/images/c/cd/M%C3%A5ns_Bj%C3%B6rkman_-_Dol_Amroth_device.gif" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="101" height="101" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="101" data-file-height="101"/></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The emblem of the [Prince of Dol Amroth](/wiki/Prince_of_Dol_Amroth) was a silver-upon-blue banner, bearing a ship with a swan-prow upon the sea.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the Legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_Legendarium}

In the *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)*, [Aulë](/wiki/Aul%C3%AB) seems to have been the first to make "great vessels like to the bodies of swans".<sup id="cite_ref-LT1_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LT1-5">[5]</a></sup>

A detail left out from the description of the swan-boat of Lothlórien in *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* (either out of space constraints, literary considerations, or because the idea was abandoned), has been provided by [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien):

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Two elves, clad in white, steered it with black paddles so contrived that the blades folded back, as a swan&#39;s foot does, when they were thrust forward in the water.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>
<a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien" title="Christopher Tolkien">Christopher Tolkien</a> (ed.), <i><a href="/wiki/The_Treason_of_Isengard" title="The Treason of Isengard">The Treason of Isengard</a></i>, &#34;XIV. Farewell to Lórien&#34;, p. 279<sup id="cite_ref-TI_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TI-3">[3]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>
