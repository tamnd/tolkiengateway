The **Siege of Barad-dûr** was the armed conflict that would end the [War of the Last Alliance](/wiki/War_of_the_Last_Alliance) and the [Second Age](/wiki/Second_Age). It was the direct result of the [Battle of Dagorlad](/wiki/Battle_of_Dagorlad), where passage into [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor) was won by the [Last Alliance](/wiki/Last_Alliance_of_Elves_and_Men "Last Alliance of Elves and Men") at a heavy cost.

## History {#History}

### The Siege {#The_Siege}

In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3434](/wiki/Second_Age_3434 "Second Age 3434") the Last Alliance entered [Mordor](/wiki/Mordor). The Orcs that survived the slaughter at Dagorlad were surrounded in Barad-dûr, Sauron's dark stronghold. There, the forces of [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad), [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and [Thranduil](/wiki/Thranduil) laid siege to the tower, but could not breach its gates.

Sauron put together a strong defence with a seemingly inexhaustible supply of projectile and sorties throughout seven years, during which the Allies suffered heavy casualties. In [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3440](/wiki/Second_Age_3440 "Second Age 3440"), Anárion's helmet was crushed by a thrown rock resulting in his death.

[A year later](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441"), however, Sauron went out with a sortie himself and broke the leaguer. He came to [Mount Doom](/wiki/Mount_Doom), where the two kings, Gil-galad and Elendil, fought with him in single combat. Sauron struck down Elendil, and his sword [Narsil](/wiki/Narsil) broke in two beneath him as he fell. [Gil-galad](/wiki/Gil-galad) was scorched by the heat of Sauron's hand, killing him. Nonetheless, Sauron was defeated in the fight with the two kings, and Isildur took up the broken remnant of his father's blade and cut the Ring from Sauron's hand.

### Aftermath {#Aftermath}

The battle marked the (temporary) passing of Sauron and the beginning of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). Gil-galad's heralds [Círdan](/wiki/C%C3%ADrdan) and [Elrond](/wiki/Elrond) advised Isildur to destroy the Ring by throwing it in the fires of Mount Doom. But instead, Isildur replied: *This I will have as weregild for my father's death, and my brother's. Was it not I who dealt the Enemy his death-blow?*<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-1">[1]</a></sup>

Gondor prospered, and built fortresses on all the entrances to Mordor: the [Morannon](/wiki/Morannon), [Durthang](/wiki/Durthang) and the [Tower of Cirith Ungol](/wiki/Tower_of_Cirith_Ungol). Isildur wrote an account of the battle, describing his father and Gil-galad's duel with Sauron in detail and the lengthy siege itself.

With the death of Gil-galad, the [Noldor](/wiki/Noldor) were without a King as he left no heir. Elrond and Círdan returned to [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon). Relations between Elves and Men worsened due to the deaths of Gil-galad and Elendil, and also Isildur's taking of the ring. The Last Alliance as it came to be known, would be the last time Elves would go to open war in Middle-earth ever again. The relationship between Men and Elves wouldn't be as close as they were in the first and second ages and never wholly repaired because the Elves were leaving Middle-earth for Aman.

Over the course of the war, which ended with the Siege of Barad-dûr in [S.A.](/wiki/Second_Age "Second Age") [3441](/wiki/Second_Age_3441 "Second Age 3441"), most of the Silvan army had been lost. Thranduil led the remaining third of his army back home to the Greenwood.<sup id="cite_ref-Silvan_Princes_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Silvan_Princes-2">[2]</a></sup>

Isildur wrote a [scroll](/wiki/Scroll_of_Isildur "Scroll of Isildur") detailing his account of the Ring and travelled to [Minas Anor](/wiki/Minas_Anor), where he dwelt awhile.<sup id="cite_ref-Council_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-3">[3]</a></sup> When he did return North, he and his sons were [ambushed](/wiki/Disaster_of_the_Gladden_Fields "Disaster of the Gladden Fields"). The Ring was lost in the tumult.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-1">[1]</a></sup>

Since the Ring was not unmade, Sauron was not completely destroyed: his spirit was able to live on. In the Third Age, he reassumed physical shape and regained most of his old realm and allies. Ever after Sauron hunted for the Ring, dispatching his servants across Middle-earth to locate it.<sup id="cite_ref-Rings_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rings-1">[1]</a></sup> The Ring would come to be known as [Isildur's Bane](/wiki/Isildur%27s_Bane), as its corruption afflicted him.<sup id="cite_ref-Council_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Council-3">[3]</a></sup>
