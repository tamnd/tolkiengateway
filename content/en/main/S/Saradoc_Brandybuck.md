**Saradoc "Scattergold" Brandybuck**<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup> was a [Hobbit](../H/Hobbits.md) and [Master of Buckland](../M/Master_of_Buckland.md).

## History {#History}

Saradoc was the son of [Rorimac Brandybuck](../R/Rorimac_Brandybuck.md) and [Menegilda Goold](../M/Menegilda_Goold.md). He married [Esmeralda Took](../E/Esmeralda_Took.md), and had one child, [Meriadoc](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md),<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup> who joined the [Company of the Ring](../F/Fellowship_of_the_Ring.md). With his father, wife and son, he was present at [Bilbo's Farewell Party](../B/Bilbo's_Farewell_Party.md).<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup>

Saradoc became [Master of Buckland](../M/Master_of_Buckland.md) after his father's death in [S.R.](Shire_Reckoning.md) [1408](../T/Third_Age_3008.md).<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup>

He died twenty-four years later in [S.R.](Shire_Reckoning.md) [1432](../F/Fourth_Age_11.md) at the age of 92, and the [Mastership](../M/Master_of_Buckland.md) was inherited by his son and heir Meriadoc.<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of the name *Saradoc* is unknown. [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) noted that the names of the Bucklanders had a vaguely [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md) style.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-2">[2]</a></sup>

In an earlier version of the family tree for the [Brandybucks](../B/Brandybucks.md), the name of [Merry](../M/Meriadoc_Brandybuck.md)'s father was <i><b>Caradoc</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Caradoc is a [Celtic](../C/Celtic.md) name, which means "kind" or "amiable".<sup id="cite_ref-Allan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allan-4">[4]</a></sup>

In using this name, J.R.R. Tolkien may have been inspired by how in about 1130 AD, a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caradoc_of_Llancarfan" title="wikipedia:Caradoc of Llancarfan">Caradoc of Llancarfan</a> wrote about the life of St. Gildas, the first known text that associated <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../K/King_Arthur.md" title="King Arthur">King Arthur</a> with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury" title="wikipedia:Glastonbury">Glastonbury</a>. A character called Caradoc is the hero in the *Livre de Caradoc*, which is part of the First Continuation of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chr%C3%A9tien_de_Troyes" title="wikipedia:Chrétien de Troyes">Chretien de Troyes&#39;</a> <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perceval,_the_Story_of_the_Grail" title="wikipedia:Perceval, the Story of the Grail">Perceval</a></i> from about 1200 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Caradoc is the name of different characters in Arthurian romance. A *Caradoc Brecbras* was apparently a sixth century British ruler with lands in Britain and Brittany. *Saradoc des Sept Fontaines* is the name of one of the Knights of the Round Table listed in the 1501 edition of the romance of *Giron le Courtois*.<sup id="cite_ref-Allan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allan-4">[4]</a></sup>
