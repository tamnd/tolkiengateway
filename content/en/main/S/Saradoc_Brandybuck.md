**Saradoc "Scattergold" Brandybuck**<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup> was a [Hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") and [Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland).

## History {#History}

Saradoc was the son of [Rorimac Brandybuck](/wiki/Rorimac_Brandybuck) and [Menegilda Goold](/wiki/Menegilda_Goold). He married [Esmeralda Took](/wiki/Esmeralda_Took), and had one child, [Meriadoc](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck"),<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup> who joined the [Company of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring "Fellowship of the Ring"). With his father, wife and son, he was present at [Bilbo's Farewell Party](/wiki/Bilbo%27s_Farewell_Party).<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup>

Saradoc became [Master of Buckland](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland) after his father's death in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1408](/wiki/Third_Age_3008 "Third Age 3008").<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup>

He died twenty-four years later in [S.R.](/wiki/Shire_Reckoning "Shire Reckoning") [1432](/wiki/Fourth_Age_11 "Fourth Age 11") at the age of 92, and the [Mastership](/wiki/Master_of_Buckland "Master of Buckland") was inherited by his son and heir Meriadoc.<sup id="cite_ref-App_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-App-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The meaning of the name *Saradoc* is unknown. [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) noted that the names of the Bucklanders had a vaguely [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) style.<sup id="cite_ref-Translation_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Translation-2">[2]</a></sup>

In an earlier version of the family tree for the [Brandybucks](/wiki/Brandybucks), the name of [Merry](/wiki/Meriadoc_Brandybuck "Meriadoc Brandybuck")'s father was <i><b>Caradoc</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> Caradoc is a [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic) name, which means "kind" or "amiable".<sup id="cite_ref-Allan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allan-4">[4]</a></sup>

In using this name, J.R.R. Tolkien may have been inspired by how in about 1130 AD, a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caradoc_of_Llancarfan" title="wikipedia:Caradoc of Llancarfan">Caradoc of Llancarfan</a> wrote about the life of St. Gildas, the first known text that associated <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">King Arthur</a> with <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury" title="wikipedia:Glastonbury">Glastonbury</a>. A character called Caradoc is the hero in the *Livre de Caradoc*, which is part of the First Continuation of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chr%C3%A9tien_de_Troyes" title="wikipedia:Chrétien de Troyes">Chretien de Troyes&#39;</a> <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perceval,_the_Story_of_the_Grail" title="wikipedia:Perceval, the Story of the Grail">Perceval</a></i> from about 1200 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

Caradoc is the name of different characters in Arthurian romance. A *Caradoc Brecbras* was apparently a sixth century British ruler with lands in Britain and Brittany. *Saradoc des Sept Fontaines* is the name of one of the Knights of the Round Table listed in the 1501 edition of the romance of *Giron le Courtois*.<sup id="cite_ref-Allan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allan-4">[4]</a></sup>
