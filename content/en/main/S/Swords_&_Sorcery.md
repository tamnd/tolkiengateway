<i><b>Swords &amp; Sorcery</b></i> is a collection of short stories of heroic fantasy.

The editor of the anthology, [L. Sprague de Camp](/wiki/L._Sprague_de_Camp), sent a copy to [Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien "J.R.R. Tolkien") in [1964](/wiki/1964). Tolkien "*found \[the anthology\] interesting but did not much like the stories in it*".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien sent [a letter in 1964](/wiki/Letter_to_L._Sprague_de_Camp "Letter to L. Sprague de Camp") about the anthology to de Camp,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> and also wrote a [critique of "Distressing Tale of Thangobrind the Jeweler"](/wiki/Critique_of_%22Distressing_Tale_of_Thangobrind_the_Jeweler%22 "Critique of \"Distressing Tale of Thangobrind the Jeweler\""), one of the contributions to the collection.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

De Camp mentions Tolkien in his Introduction: "..let us give credit to \[William\] Morris for reviving the genre. Echoes of his novel \[*The Well at the World's End*\] are still heard in J.R.R. Tolkien's *Fellowship of the Ring* trilogy and in Fletcher Pratt's *The Well of the Unicorn*".

- [Poul Anderson](/wiki/Poul_Anderson): "The Valor of Cappen Varra"

- Robert E. Howard: "Shadows in the Moonlight"
- Henry Kuttner: "The Citadel of Darkness"
- Fritz Leiber: "When the Sea King's Away"
- H.P. Lovecraft: "The Doom that came to Sarnath"
- C.L. Moore: "Hellsgarde"
- Clark Ashton Smith: "The Testament of Athammaus"

## From the back {#From_the_back}

Here are tales of heroes of old - brawny adventurers ready to storm the gates of Hell with their valor and ready blades for a purse of gold ... or a wench. And here are a legion of wizards, ghouls, demons - and worse - casting dire enchantments and summoning all the powers of darkness to their aid. ...

In this anthology of heroic fantasy, SPRAGUE DE CAMP has collected stories which resound with the clash of battle in the eerie night ... which reek the unholy smoke of the magician's cell ... which draw the reader into a strange, thrilling, weird world of SWORDS AND SORCERY
