**Smaug** was the greatest [fire-breathing](/wiki/Fire-drakes "Fire-drakes") [dragon](/wiki/Dragons "Dragons") of the [Third Age](/wiki/Third_Age). In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2770](/wiki/Third_Age_2770 "Third Age 2770") he attacked the [Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain) and the town of [Dale](/wiki/Dale). He claimed the treasure of the mountain for himself and forced [Thrór](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r), [King under the Mountain](/wiki/King_under_the_Mountain), and [his people](/wiki/Durin%27s_Folk "Durin's Folk") into exile. [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin)'s [quest](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor "Quest of Erebor") - with the help of his burglar [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) - to reclaim the treasure ended in success when Smaug was slain by [Bard](/wiki/Bard) in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941").<sup id="cite_ref-AppA3_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA3-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-H14_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H14-3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-H1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H1-6">[6]</a></sup>

His flames are green and scarlet.<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Sack of Erebor {#Sack_of_Erebor}

Smaug was perhaps one of the dragons that infested the [Grey Mountains](/wiki/Grey_Mountains).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup> Details of his origin are unknown, but he apparently was considered still "young" by himself and [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) when he first appeared in [Middle-earth](/wiki/Middle-earth)'s history in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2770](/wiki/Third_Age_2770 "Third Age 2770"),<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-H1_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H1-6">[6]</a></sup> and as having transitioned to become "old and strong" by [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"). This implies that he was young during the [War of the Dwarves and Dragons](/wiki/War_of_the_Dwarves_and_Dragons), or else not even alive at the time. However, he, like many others would hear of the wealth and splendour of [Erebor](/wiki/Erebor). Hearing about the wealth of the [Dwarves of Erebor](/wiki/Dwarves_of_Erebor), Smaug flew south "*like a hurricane coming from the North*" and attacked the wealthy [Dwarven](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") kingdom of the [Lonely Mountain](/wiki/Lonely_Mountain) and its adjacent lands: he first landed upon the mountain before going down the slopes and setting the woods on fire. When the [dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") came running out of the front gate Smaug killed them all before turning his attention to the men of [Dale](/wiki/Dale), also killing most of their warriors including [Girion](/wiki/Girion), [Lord of Dale](/wiki/Lord_of_Dale) (but his wife and child safely fled to [Lake-town](/wiki/Lake-town)<sup id="cite_ref-H14_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H14-3">[3]</a></sup>). Returning to the mountain, he crawled into the [front gate](/wiki/Front_Gate "Front Gate") and left no dwarf he found alive.

Only [Thrór](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r), [King under the Mountain](/wiki/King_under_the_Mountain), and his son [Thráin II](/wiki/Thr%C3%A1in "Thráin") managed to escape by using the [secret side-door](/wiki/Back_Door "Back Door") (Thrór later gave the key to the secret door and [a map](/wiki/Thr%C3%B3r%27s_Map "Thrór's Map") of the Lonely Mountain to Thráin), while [Thorin Oakenshield](/wiki/Thorin "Thorin") was one of the few dwarves who were not inside the mountain at the time of the Attack. Smaug claimed the treasure (which included the [Arkenstone](/wiki/Arkenstone) and a [Mithril-coat](/wiki/Mithril-coat)) for himself and laid there upon a bed of gold, for the next two centuries only occasionally leaving the mountain to carry away people (especially maidens) and continue the destruction of Dale.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA3_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA3-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-H1_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H1-6">[6]</a></sup> The area surrounding the mountain became known as the [Desolation of the Dragon](/wiki/Desolation_of_the_Dragon) as "*there was neither bush nor tree, and only broken and blackened stumps to speak of ones long vanished*".<sup id="cite_ref-H11_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H11-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Quest for Erebor {#Quest_for_Erebor}

<div class="tg-hatnote metadata noprint"><div class="tg-hatnote-icon"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img src="/w/images/c/c6/CdxIconArticleDisambiguation.svg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20"/></span></span></div><div class="tg-hatnote-content">See also: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit#Synopsis" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit#Synopsis</a></div></div>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r398211"/>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/f/fd/Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg/300px-Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="215" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/f/fd/Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg/450px-Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/f/fd/Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg/600px-Henning_Janssen_-_Magnificent_Guardian.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="573"/></a><figcaption><i>Magnificient Guardian</i> by <a href="/wiki/Henning_Janssen" title="Henning Janssen">Henning Janssen</a></figcaption></figure>

In [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2850](/wiki/Third_Age_2850 "Third Age 2850"), [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf), whilst spying on the [Necromancer](/wiki/Necromancer) in [Dol Guldur](/wiki/Dol_Guldur), found a Dwarf imprisoned; near death, the dwarf gave Gandalf a key and a map. Unbeknownst to Gandalf at the time, this dwarf was [Thráin](/wiki/Thr%C3%A1in), [King of Durin's Folk](/wiki/Kings_of_Durin%27s_Folk "Kings of Durin's Folk"), who had been captured by the Necromancer in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2845](/wiki/Third_Age_2845 "Third Age 2845"). Having discovered that the Necromancer was indeed [Sauron](/wiki/Sauron), Gandalf was very concerned about the Weakened State of the North, but he was most concerned about Sauron and Smaug. He knew that if Sauron could win Smaug's allegiance, Sauron could use him to terrible effect. It is for this reason that the Grey Pilgrim sought a plan to neutralise the threat of the Dragon, thus simultaneously depriving the [Dark Lord](/wiki/Dark_Lord) of a potential minion and limit his growing power in the northern lands of Middle-earth.<sup id="cite_ref-UTErebor_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTErebor-9">[9]</a></sup>

By chance on [15 March](/wiki/15_March) [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), Gandalf met [Thorin Oakenshield](/wiki/Thorin "Thorin") in [Bree](/wiki/Bree) (although another source states that they met when Thorin overtook but started to talk to Gandalf on the road<sup id="cite_ref-UTErebor_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTErebor-9">[9]</a></sup>). They discussed their desire to destroy Smaug and retake the Lonely Mountain; they later met in [Thorin's Halls](/wiki/Thorin%27s_Halls) in the [Blue Mountains](/wiki/Blue_Mountains) to develop a plan. Gandalf wanted Thorin to take the [hobbit](/wiki/Hobbits "Hobbits") [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins) as a *burglar* on their adventure to retake Erebor; this took considerable persuasion.<sup id="cite_ref-AppA3_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA3-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AppB2_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppB2-2">[2]</a></sup>

Later in [T.A.](/wiki/Third_Age "Third Age") [2941](/wiki/Third_Age_2941 "Third Age 2941"), at the home of [Bilbo Baggins](/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins), Gandalf presented Thorin with the map and key, and accompanied them on part of [their quest](/wiki/Quest_of_Erebor "Quest of Erebor").<sup id="cite_ref-H1_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H1-6">[6]</a></sup> The party reached the mountain later that year on [Durin's Day](/wiki/Durin%27s_Day).<sup id="cite_ref-H11_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H11-8">[8]</a></sup> They sent Bilbo in through the secret door to carry out his duty as their burglar; as Smaug lay dreaming of greed and violence, Bilbo stole a heavy two-handed cup from the dragon's vast hoard of treasure (which he used as a bed) and went back to the dwarves. Meanwhile Smaug had an uneasy dream:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>a warrior, altogether insignificant in size but provided with a bitter sword and great courage, figured most unpleasantly
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Narrator<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[note 1]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Upon awaking Smaug felt the draught from the tunnel which led to the secret door (from which he had previously heard untoward knocking). Already suspicious, Smaug noticed that the cup was missing. This enraged Smaug beyond measure, causing him to leave his chamber and scour the mountainside for the intruder, even though he had no real use for the cup; remembering hearing strange noises from the passageway he failed to find the entrance, only eating their ponies.<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup>

Remembering a saying of [his father](/wiki/Bungo_Baggins "Bungo Baggins")'s, "*every worm has a weak spot*", Bilbo offered to return to the dragon's lair - the Dwarves ardently accepted and, putting on [the ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring"), off he went. Bilbo believed that the dragon was fast asleep and that his presence would remain unknown to the dragon, however, Smaug was pretending to be asleep. He then spoke to Bilbo:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Well thief! I smell you and I feel your air. I hear your breath. Come along! Help yourself again, there is plenty and to spare!
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Smaug to <a href="/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins" title="Bilbo Baggins">Bilbo Baggins</a>.</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:David_Wyatt_-_Smaug.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/6/6e/David_Wyatt_-_Smaug.jpg/300px-David_Wyatt_-_Smaug.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="426" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/6/6e/David_Wyatt_-_Smaug.jpg/450px-David_Wyatt_-_Smaug.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/6/6e/David_Wyatt_-_Smaug.jpg 2x" data-file-width="593" data-file-height="843"/></a><figcaption><i>Smaug</i> by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=David_Wyatt&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="David Wyatt (page does not exist)">David Wyatt</a></figcaption></figure>

But Bilbo was more shrewd than Smaug gave him credit: Bilbo praised the dragon and made sure that he did not reveal his real name, speaking only in [riddles](/wiki/Riddle-game "Riddle-game"). Smaug could not resist the fascination of what Bilbo had said (although he did remind him that flattery would not save his life) and needed to understand it, so they continued to talk; he was also intrigued to smell something new being puzzled by the never-before-encountered "hobbit-smell". Bilbo grew more and more uncomfortable in the presence of Smaug, but plucking up courage he revealed that he had not come for the treasure alone: Smaug laughed and mocked the notion that anyone could steal from him and get away with it. Bilbo eventually revealed the true purpose of his mission to Smaug, that of "*revenge*" - Smaug burst into a devastating laughter, shaking Bilbo to the floor, mocking the suggestion that anyone could achieve revenge, boasting about his achievements and strengths. Bilbo then suggested that dragon's were softer underneath, particularly in the chest: in response, and in an act of sheer vanity, Smaug rolled over and claimed that Bilbo's information was false and outdated. But Bilbo, while loudly praising Smaug's jewel-encrusted underside, saw something crucial, thinking:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Old fool! Why there is a large patch in the hollow of his left breast as bare as a snail out of its shell!
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Bilbo, to himself.</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Bilbo had obtained the information that he had needed. He fled up the passage, leaving behind a dragon infuriated by the notion of "revenge", and had the hair on the back of his head and heels singed off. When Bilbo returned to the door-step he regaled the dwarves with the story of his conversation with the dragon whilst the [thrush](/wiki/Thrushes "Thrushes") was listening. Smaug left his lair once more and smashed the mountainside with strikes of his tail, trapping [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) inside the secret passage.<sup id="cite_ref-HInsider_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HInsider-11">[10]</a></sup>

### Death and aftermath {#Death_and_aftermath}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/8/8e/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg/300px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="245" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/8/8e/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg/450px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/8/8e/J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg/600px-J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Death_of_Smaug.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="815"/></a><figcaption><i>Death of Smaug</i> by <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a></figcaption></figure>

Realizing that the [Lake-men](/wiki/Lake-men) of [Esgaroth](/wiki/Lake-town "Lake-town") must have helped the intruders - reinforced by Bilbo calling himself the "*Barrel-rider*," Smaug resolved to attack and destroy the town as a punitive measure. He approached it in a rage. The waters around it turned red but Smaug dared not get too close as the water would quench his fire; as the Lake-men had cut the bridges, Smaug flew above attacking and setting the town aflame, destroying the [Great House](/wiki/Great_House) with a swipe of his tail. The dragon's scales were impervious to the arrows of the defenders, but the thrush had flown to the town and informed [Bard](/wiki/Bard) - a descendant of [Girion](/wiki/Girion), [Lord of Dale](/wiki/Lord_of_Dale) - of the bare spot in Smaug's armour. He was then able to kill Smaug by firing the [Black Arrow](/wiki/Black_Arrow) directly into the vulnerable spot of his left breast. As Smaug fell he crashed into and destroyed Esgaroth.<sup id="cite_ref-H14_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H14-3">[3]</a></sup>

After Smaug's death, [Thorin and Company](/wiki/Thorin_and_Company) claimed the treasure as theirs by birthright. This created a conflict with Bard and the [Elvenking](/wiki/Thranduil "Thranduil") of [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood), who each wanted a portion of the treasure as reimbursement for huge damage that Smaug had inflicted upon them. Thorin refused to share the treasure and had every intention of going to war with the [Elves of Mirkwood](/wiki/Elves_of_Mirkwood) and men of Esgaroth to defend his right to the treasure. However the sudden attack by the armies of [Bolg](/wiki/Bolg) brought the forces of the [free peoples](/wiki/Free_peoples "Free peoples") of Middle-earth together in the [Battle of Five Armies](/wiki/Battle_of_Five_Armies).<sup id="cite_ref-H15_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H15-12">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-H16_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H16-13">[12]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-H17_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H17-14">[13]</a></sup> After the Battle, Bilbo's fourteenth-share of the hoard was given over to Bard who sent some to Lake-town to aid its rebuilding.<sup id="cite_ref-H18_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H18-15">[14]</a></sup>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/a/a2/Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg/200px-Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="200" height="85" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/a/a2/Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg/300px-Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/a/a2/Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg/400px-Alan_Lee_-_There_for_ages_his_huge_bones_could_be_seen_in_calm_weather_amid_the_ruined_piles_of_the_old_town..jpg 2x" data-file-width="844" data-file-height="360"/></a><figcaption>&#34;There for ages his huge bones could be seen in calm weather amid the ruined piles of <a href="/wiki/Lake-town" title="Lake-town">the old town</a>.&#34; by <a href="/wiki/Alan_Lee" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

Following the death of Smaug a new Lake-town was built further north; Smaug's bones could be seen from the shore but the people were always fearful of it and no one dared go in the water to retrieve the gems or gold, showing that even in Death, Smaug still had a fearful presence.<sup id="cite_ref-H14_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H14-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Personality {#Personality}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I kill where I wish and none dare resist. I laid low the warriors of old and their like is not in the world today. Then I was but young and tender. Now I am old and strong, strong strong, Thief in the Shadows!
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>Smaug to Bilbo.</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

Like all dragons, Smaug - described by [Thorin](/wiki/Thorin) as "*a most specially greedy, strong and wicked worm*"<sup id="cite_ref-H1_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H1-6">[6]</a></sup> - loves to hoard gold with a meticulous knowledge of his own collection as evidenced by his immediate spotting of the missing cup.<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup> Whilst being quick to anger in defence of his own wealth, he also exhibits a fierce intellect (as well as a curious fascination) in guessing/deciphering Bilbo's cryptic origins, a pride and vanity in his own armour, an arrogance in his own invulnerability (laughing at the suggestion that the dwarves would be able to have their revenge), and an ability to question Bilbo's loyalty to the dwarves.<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup> These qualities, complemented by his destructive power, raised Gandalf's concerns of Smaug as a potential ally, or servant of Sauron.<sup id="cite_ref-UTErebor_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UTErebor-9">[9]</a></sup>

## Inspiration {#Inspiration}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg"><img src="/w/images/thumb/0/08/John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg/300px-John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="226" class="mw-file-element" srcset="/w/images/thumb/0/08/John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg/450px-John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg 1.5x, /w/images/thumb/0/08/John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg/600px-John_Howe_-_Smaug_Destroys_Lake-town.jpg 2x" data-file-width="825" data-file-height="621"/></a><figcaption><i>Smaug Destroys Lake-town</i> by <a href="/wiki/John_Howe" title="John Howe">John Howe</a></figcaption></figure>

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Fafnir in the late Norse versions of the Sigurd-story is better; and  Smaug and his conversation obviously is in debt there.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a><sup id="cite_ref-Letter122_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter122-16">[15]</a></sup></cite></p>
</blockquote>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

In [Letter 122](/wiki/Letter_122), Tolkien noted his lack of enthusiasm for the dragon in *[Beowulf](</wiki/Beowulf_(poem)> "Beowulf (poem)")*. Instead he stated his preference for the dragon-like creature <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fafnir" title="wikipedia:Fafnir">Fafnir</a> from the late Norse versions of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigurd" title="wikipedia:Sigurd">Sigurd-story</a>. Indeed Tolkien wrote that Smaug's character owed much to Fafnir.<sup id="cite_ref-Letter122_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter122-16">[15]</a></sup>

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Smaug* is "the past tense of the primitive Germanic verb <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/sm%C5%ABgan%C4%85" title="wiktionary:Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/smūganą">Smugan</a></i>, to squeeze through a hole".<sup id="cite_ref-Letter25_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letter25-17">[16]</a></sup> The name is derived from Icelandic *smjúga*.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">[17]</a></sup> It has been suggested that it is derived from the same Old English and Germanic roots as *[smial](/wiki/Smials "Smials")* and *[Smeagol](/wiki/Sm%C3%A9agol "Sméagol")* and that J.R.R. Tolkien likely thought of [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) *smeag*, a word used to describe a "[worm](/wiki/Worms "Worms")".<sup id="cite_ref-RW_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RW-19">[18]</a></sup>

## Other names {#Other_names}

In drafts of the [Appendices](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Appendices "The Lord of the Rings Appendices"), Tolkien wrote that *[Trāgu](/wiki/Tr%C4%81gu)* was the name of Smaug in the [language of Dale](/wiki/Language_of_Dale "Language of Dale").<sup id="cite_ref-PM54_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM54-20">[19]</a></sup>

In the conversation between Smaug and Bilbo, Bilbo calls him "**Smaug the Tremendous**", "**Smaug the Chiefest and Greatest of Calamities**", "**Smaug the Mighty**", "**Smaug the Unassessably Wealthy**", "**Lord Smaug the Impenetrable**" and "**Your Magnificence**",<sup id="cite_ref-H12_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H12-1">[1]</a></sup> and later Bilbo refers to him as "**Smaug the Terrible**" and "**Smaug the Dreadful**" and [Balin](/wiki/Balin) calls him "**Old Worm**".<sup id="cite_ref-HInsider_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HInsider-11">[10]</a></sup> When the [dwarves](/wiki/Dwarves "Dwarves") learn of Smaug's demise they grab their harps and sing, referring to Smaug as the "**Worm of Dread**".<sup id="cite_ref-H15_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-H15-12">[11]</a></sup> [Appendix A](/wiki/Appendix_A) uses the popular name "**Smaug the Golden**",<sup id="cite_ref-AppA3_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AppA3-4">[4]</a></sup> whilst in "[The Quest of Erebor](/wiki/The_Quest_of_Erebor)" he is simply referred to as "**The Dragon**".

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In a very early manuscript of *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)*, Smaug was known as *Pryftan*. Indeed [John D. Rateliff](/wiki/John_D._Rateliff) refers to this manuscript as *The Pryftan Fragment*.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[20]</a></sup> *Pryftan* is a simple [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh) compound that literally means "Worm of Fire."<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">[21]</a></sup>

### Films {#Films}

<b>1967: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1967_film)" title="The Hobbit (1967 film)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1967 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Smaug is named &#34;Slag&#34;, the Ancient Monster.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">[22]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1977: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1977_film)" title="The Hobbit (1977 film)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1977 film)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Smaug was voiced by American actor <a href="/wiki/Richard_Boone" title="Richard Boone">Richard Boone</a>. In this version, Smaug is broadly similar in size and colour but his shape is less elongated due to being more weighty. Smaug&#39;s face is rounder, possessing more mammalian traits - resembling a mix between a cat and a bat - having bat-like ears and fur around the face and down the back. Also, his armour is never mentioned to be strengthened by lying on the treasure, but more resembles an extra padding of skin/scales, minus the bare patch.</dd></dl>

**1985: <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=The_Fabulous_Journey_of_Mr._Bilbo_Baggins_The_Hobbit_Across_The_Wild_Land_Through_The_Dark_Forest_Beyond_The_Misty_Mountains_There_And_Back_Again_(1985_Russian_film)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="The Fabulous Journey of Mr. Bilbo Baggins The Hobbit Across The Wild Land Through The Dark Forest Beyond The Misty Mountains There And Back Again (1985 Russian film) (page does not exist)"><i>The Fabulous Journey of Mr. Bilbo Baggins The Hobbit Across The Wild Land Through The Dark Forest Beyond The Misty Mountains There And Back Again</i> (1985 Russian film)</a>:**

<dl><dd>Original title: Сказочное путешествие мистера Бильбо Беггинса, хоббита, через Дикий край, Черный лес, за Туманные горы туда и обратно.  Smaug was created by a puppet.</dd></dl>

**1991: <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Treasures_Under_the_Mountain,_or_The_Hobbit_(1991_film)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Treasures Under the Mountain, or The Hobbit (1991 film) (page does not exist)"><i>Treasures Under the Mountain, or The Hobbit</i> (1991 film)</a>:**

<dl><dd>Animated unreleased Russian Hobbit short film.  During the destruction of Dale Smaug darkens the sky. Children are flying kites (paper dragons) which are consumed by a dragon of fire.  original posting:   <a class="external free" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2hWwu17udnI" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2hWwu17udnI</a>   and here with English subtitles: <a class="external free" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nzoRd14L7AQ" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nzoRd14L7AQ</a></dd></dl>

<b>2012: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_An_Unexpected_Journey" title="The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey">The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Smaug is a <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery" title="wikipedia:Computer-generated imagery">CGI</a>-motion capture creature produced by <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Weta_Digital" title="Weta Digital">Weta Digital</a><sup id="cite_ref-PJCasting_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PJCasting-24">[23]</a></sup></dd></dl>

During the initial release of An Unexpected Journey, Smaug is shown to have four legs and walk on his front legs. In the sequel, it was decided to depict him with no front legs and walking on the wrists of his wings. In the Extended Edition and later home-media re-releases, a shot of his front feet was digitally altered to the front claws of his wigns to maintain consistency with the sequel. When he reaches inside Erebor, however, the old front leg design is still visible.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">[24]</a></sup>

<dl><dd>Smaug&#39;s <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Erebor" title="Sack of Erebor">sack</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dale" title="Dale">Dale</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lonely_Mountain" title="Lonely Mountain">Erebor</a> is shown briefly during the prologue. During a meeting of the Wise, <a href="/wiki/Gandalf" title="Gandalf">Gandalf</a> reveals his concern regarding a potential alliance between Sauron and Smaug, as a a &#34;dragon could be used to terrible effect&#34;. <a href="/wiki/Saruman" title="Saruman">Saruman</a> dismisses this, stating that Sauron had been vanquished. The film ends with a glimpse of the <a href="/wiki/Dragons" title="Dragons">Dragon</a>&#39;s eye as he wakes, though he is buried under the treasure. In addition, movie Smaug was over 140 metres (462 feet) in length, or “twice the size of a 747”.</dd></dl>

<b>2013: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Desolation_of_Smaug" title="The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug">The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>The Dragon makes his full appearance in the second part and is voiced by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch" title="Benedict Cumberbatch">Benedict Cumberbatch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-PJCasting_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PJCasting-24">[23]</a></sup> <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Thrain" title="Thrain">Thrain</a> confirms Gandalf&#39;s fears of Smaug being in league with the unseen <a href="/wiki/Dark_Lord" title="Dark Lord">Dark Power</a> in <a href="/wiki/Dol_Guldur" title="Dol Guldur">Dol Guldur</a>, later revealed to be Sauron. In fact, Smaug himself confirms this, showing an awareness of <a href="/wiki/The_One_Ring" title="The One Ring">the One Ring</a> during his conversation with <a href="/wiki/Bilbo_Baggins" title="Bilbo Baggins">Bilbo</a>, as well as giving hints on Sauron&#39;s return - the Dragon&#39;s pleased attitude further outlines his intention to throw his backing behind the Dark Lord&#39;s banner for the spoils of war. It is also observed by Bilbo that the <a href="/wiki/Lake-men" title="Lake-men">Lake-men</a>&#39;s legend of <a href="/wiki/Girion" title="Girion">Girion</a> having loosened and removed a scale from the Dragon&#39;s breast was in fact true. When the <a href="/wiki/Dwarves" title="Dwarves">Dwarves</a> enter the mountain, Smaug chases them, finally catching up to them in the forges, where the Dwarves stage a counterattack, tricking Smaug into relighting the forges with his fire. They manage to cover Smaug in hot molten gold, but he sheds it off without difficulty. Smaug leaves them and flies off to <a href="/wiki/Lake-town" title="Lake-town">Lake-town</a> to seek revenge for what he perceives to be their part in Bilbo and the Dwarves&#39; attack.</dd></dl>

<b>2014: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies" title="The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies">The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>After destroying the majority of Lake-town, Smaug sees <a href="/wiki/Bard" title="Bard">Bard</a> attempting to take him on. He taunts the bowman, and, then, while charging him, Bard shoots his <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Black_Arrow" title="Black Arrow">Black Arrow</a> into Smaug&#39;s vulnerable spot, the same spot that his ancestor had shaken a scale off loose centuries earlier. While in the throes of death, the Dragon flies up into the sky, where he takes his last breath, claws at the sky and then falls to his watery grave in <a href="/wiki/Long_Lake" title="Long Lake">Long Lake</a> (taking the greedy <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Lake-town" title="Master of Lake-town">Master of Lake-town</a> in his boat with him along the way).</dd></dl>

### Games {#Games}

<b>1982: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(1982_video_game)" title="The Hobbit (1982 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (1982 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>The goal of this game is to plunder the treasuries of Smaug&#39;s Lair in the Lonely Mountain. While the computer is loading the game it shows a picture of Smaug with the Lonely Mountain on the background.<sup id="cite_ref-Newspaper_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Newspaper-28">[27]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1988: <a href="/wiki/Creatures_of_Middle-earth" title="Creatures of Middle-earth"><i>Creatures of Middle-earth</i> (1st edition)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In this supplement to the 1st edition of <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>, Smaug is put forward as a potential enemy should the gamesmaster wish to include the dragon in his or her story. Another dragon, Utumkodur, is described as Smaug&#39;s elder sister.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">[28]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>1994: <a href="/wiki/Creatures_of_Middle-earth_(2nd_edition)" title="Creatures of Middle-earth (2nd edition)"><i>Creatures of Middle-earth</i> (2nd edition)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>In this supplement to the 2nd edition of <i><a href="/wiki/Middle-earth_Role_Playing" title="Middle-earth Role Playing">Middle-earth Role Playing</a></i>, Smaug is described in some detail and given a brief history before his coming to Erebor. Smaug survived the destruction of <a href="/wiki/Angband" title="Angband">Angband</a> at the end of the <a href="/wiki/First_Age" title="First Age">First Age</a> and settled at Anvilmount in the <a href="/wiki/Grey_Mountains" title="Grey Mountains">Grey Mountains</a>. Here he found and defiled a First Age Adan holy place, destroying tombs and stealing a modest amount of treasure. Smaug was upset by his lack of wealth. Therefore when he heard the tale of the Dwarves of Erebor he knew that the great treasure should be his. <i>Creatures of Middle-earth</i> also describes Smaug as being the son of <a href="/wiki/Ancalagon" title="Ancalagon">Ancalagon</a>, being one of a number of siblings including Throkmaw, Ruingurth, and Utumkodur.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">[29]</a></sup></dd></dl>

<b>2003: <a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit_(2003_video_game)" title="The Hobbit (2003 video game)"><i>The Hobbit</i> (2003 video game)</a>:</b>

<dl><dd>Smaug was voiced by <a href="/wiki/James_Horan" title="James Horan">James Horan</a>.</dd></dl>

<b>2018: <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Online" title="The Lord of the Rings Online">The Lord of the Rings Online</a></i>:</b>

<dl><dd>Smaug the Terrible has a sister, Etterfang Foulmaw. Both of them slept in the cavern called Glimmerdeep in the <a href="/wiki/Grey_Mountains" title="Grey Mountains">Grey Mountains</a> until Etterfang was disturbed by dwarves and fled into the deeper Caverns of Thrumfall; Smaug was unable to find her again when he awoke. It is revealed that a servant of the Dark Lord provoked his rage by slaying many young drakes and placing their heads at the front of the Dragon&#39;s lair alongside dwarven weapons. This, coupled with greed, caused Smaug to attack the Erebor, which was part of Sauron&#39;s design. Following this, the Dark Lord sent emissaries to win him the Dragon&#39;s allegiance; when none returned, Sauron tasked Karazgar, a <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Black_N%C3%BAmen%C3%B3rean" title="Black Númenórean">Black Númenórean</a> agent, with daunting Smaug into submission. Cowed through fear, the Dragon promised to give an answer to the Dark Lord&#39;s offer in fifty years. The Quest for Erebor, put in motion by Gandalf, deprived Sauron of Smaug&#39;s potential fealty, as the latter was slain before he could be summoned to <a href="/wiki/Mordor" title="Mordor">Mordor</a>.</dd></dl>

<dl><dd>Smaug does not make a physical appearance, but his bones are still found around the watery ruins of the old <a href="/wiki/Lake-town" title="Lake-town">Lake-town</a> (known as Esgaroth-of-old). Bandits pillage the ruins and the bones for some unknown purpose.</dd></dl>
