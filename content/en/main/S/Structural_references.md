**Structural references** (also known as **Arda structural references**) is a term coined by [Beregond, Anders Stenström](/wiki/Anders_Stenstr%C3%B6m "Anders Stenström") in 1998, aimed at simplifying the usage of giving references to the works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) by employing abbreviations and some conventions developed in *[Arda](</wiki/Arda_(annual)> "Arda (annual)")*.

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r395753">.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider{margin-top:1rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem;color:var(--tg-color-subtle);display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:space-between}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider:not(:empty){gap:1rem;white-space:nowrap}.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::before,.mw-parser-output .tg-section-divider::after{content:"";height:1px;width:100%;background-color:var(--tg-border-color-base)}</style><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>A structural reference identifies an element of the text by help of its place in the text&#39;s structure (rather than by its place in the layout of the book, which does not work when editions differ in layout).
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite><a href="/wiki/Anders_Stenstr%C3%B6m" title="Anders Stenström">Beregond, Anders Stenström</a></cite></p>
