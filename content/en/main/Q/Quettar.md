<i><b>Quettar</b></i> was the journal of the Linguistic Fellowship of [The Tolkien Society](/wiki/The_Tolkien_Society).

## Publication {#Publication}

Quettar was founded in [1980](/wiki/1980) by [Susan Rule](/wiki/Susan_Rule), and edited since then by Steve Pillinger, Michael Poxon, [David Doughan](/wiki/David_Doughan) and [Julian C. Bradfield](/wiki/Julian_C._Bradfield). In [1995](/wiki/1995), after 49 issues, Bradfield could no longer find time to edit, and due to the rise of the Internet - Bradfields own [Tolklang](/wiki/Tolklang) - submissions became low, and Quettar ceased to exist. Recently, Bradfield has expressed interest in reviving Quettar <a class="external autonumber" href="http://www.quettar.org/revival.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">[1]</a>, and started publishing back issues.

## Aim {#Aim}

On the back of every issue, the aim of *Quettar* was written:

<blockquote>
<p><i> <b>QUETTAR</b> is Quenya, or &#39;High-Elven&#39;. It means &#39;words&#39;, and is the bulletin of the Linguistic Fellowship of the Tolkien Society, whose members are referred to as <b>Quendili</b>, which means &#39;lovers of language&#39; or &#39;lovers of Quenya&#39;, (though there are some <b>Sindarindili</b> among us). Those who describe themselves as philologists tend to say <b>Lambendili</b>. Feanorian calligraphers are known (perhaps inaccurately) as <b>Tengwardili</b>, runemasters as <b>Certatúri</b>. </i>
</p><p><i>The languages which principally interest us are those sub-created by <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>, including: </i>

<p><i>This also involves a degree of interest in Finnish, Welsh, Old English and other &#39;real world&#39; languages. We stress &#39;interest&#39;. While expertise is welcome, in order to become a Quendil all you need is love. We trust that knowledge will follow.</i>

As these publications appeared before most of Tolkien's linguistic papers were published in *[The History of Middle-earth](/wiki/The_History_of_Middle-earth)* and *[Vinyar Tengwar](/wiki/Vinyar_Tengwar)*, much of it relied on speculation. Little original material was published, though the [Tengwar](/wiki/Tengwar) numerals did find their first full appearance in issue 13.
