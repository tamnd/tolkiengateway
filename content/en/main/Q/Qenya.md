<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

**Qenya** (pronounced \[KWEN-ya\])<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> was the original name of the High-Elven tongue which gradually would change into [Quenya](/wiki/Quenya) of the *[Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Lord of the Rings")*.

In the earliest versions of the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), Qenya (as every [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) language) belonged to the [Oromean](/wiki/Orom%C3%ABan "Oromëan") phylum, derived from the language [Orome](/wiki/Orom%C3%AB "Oromë") taught the [Quendi](/wiki/Quendi). It was the language of the First Tribe of the [Elves](/wiki/Elves), the [Lindar](/wiki/Lindar) ([Vanyar](/wiki/Vanyar) in the later legendarium) and for this it was also called **Lindarin**.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> In Gnomish the name of the language was **Cweneglin** or **Cwedhrin**.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> In the [Qenya Lexicon (QL)](/wiki/Qenyaqetsa "Qenyaqetsa"), the name **Eldarissa** appears glossed as "*language of the Eldar*" with no explanation for the etymology, though [Christopher Tolkien](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien) speculated the possibility that the final element -issa may have been derived from the root ISI, which includes the following derivations: *[ista-](/wiki/Ista- "Ista-")* ("know"), *[-issë](/wiki/-iss%C3%AB)* ("knowledge, lore"), or <i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Iswa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Iswa (page does not exist)">iswa</a></i>/<i><a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Isqa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Isqa (page does not exist)">isqa</a></i> ("wise").<sup id="cite_ref-Appendix_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Appendix-4">[4]</a></sup> [Eldamo](/wiki/Eldamo) suggests that the final element means language.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Qenya goes back to at least [1915](/wiki/1915) when Tolkien compiled the "[Qenya Lexicon](/wiki/Qenya_Lexicon)". Qenya thus predates the [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium"), although the Lexicon was used in parallel to *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)* a couple of years later.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup> According to Tolkien, his [secondary world](/wiki/Secondary_world "Secondary world") was created primarily for the need to make his constructed languages usable.<sup id="cite_ref-External_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-External-7">[7]</a></sup>

Qenya was started by Tolkien who wished to emulate the phonoesthetics of the Finnish language. Early Qenya has been reported to have much more Finnish influences, compared to the later stages.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup>

During that time, Tolkien used to write the sound <span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[kʷ]</span> simply as a single *q*. Therefore both spellings Qenya and Quenya are always supposed to be pronounced the same (<span title="Pronunciation in IPA" class="IPA">[ˈkʷeɲa]</span>). While writing the *Lord of the Rings*, Tolkien decided to adopt the romanized spelling *qu* and replace *k* with *c*. These changes were mainly aesthetic in order to accentuate the role of Quenya as an "<a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Elf-latin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Elf-latin (page does not exist)">Elf-latin</a>". This does not alter the pronunciation.

## Difference between Qenya and Quenya {#Difference_between_Qenya_and_Quenya}

Although widely consistent in phonology, the early stage of Qenya displayed some differences, with words such as *ektar* (*kt* is not allowed in later Quenya) and words ending in consonant clusters, such as *nelt, imbilink, mandl, koisimatl* etc. The later phonology rules of Quenya would not allow such words.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

Since the High-Elven language changed throughout Tolkien's lifetime from its conception until late in his life, some [Tolkienists](/wiki/Tolkienist "Tolkienist") and linguists deliberately use the spelling "Qenya" (without "u") to differentiate the early version of this language from the later.

Such Tolkien linguists note "*a dramatic change*" after which the language somehow stabilized after [1940](/wiki/1940). One of such changes was that later Quenya does not display the "*number of borrowings and obvious influences of real-world languages*" that Qenya did.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

[Helge Fauskanger](/wiki/Helge_Fauskanger) used to refer to the early stage(s) of Quenya as "immature Qenya".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[note 1]</a></sup>

The differentiation between Qenya and Quenya has been criticized as artificial, since it neglects the continuity and gradual evolution of the language; although Tolkien replaced old ideas with newer ones, there is no indication that he divided his conception into stages, and no definite point which separates Qenya from Quenya can be traced. Also, the term "immature" was considered controversial and derogatory.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[11]</a></sup>
