The <i><b>Qenyaqetsa</b></i> is a phonology and lexicon of [Qenya](Qenya.md) written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md).

The manuscript was a notebook, and Tolkien included the number '23' in the first page, suggesting it was begun between [1915](../_/1915.md) and [1916](../_/1916.md), when he had that age.<sup id="cite_ref-Lost_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lost-1">[1]</a></sup> In a letter to [Edith Bratt](../E/Edith_Bratt.md) dated [2 March](../_/2_March.md) 1916 he mentioned working on a "nonsense fairy language" during his military training<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> suggesting that he had this notebook with him in Rugeley Camp in Staffordshire.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup>

Many revisions were made until [1919](../_/1919.md) or [1920](../_/1920.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup> Both the description of the phonology and the lexicon were the foundation of the [Qenya](Qenya.md) language, which decades later would appear as "[Quenya](Quenya.md)" in his published works.

His son [Christopher](../C/Christopher_Tolkien.md) published some fragments in the Appendices of *[The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md)* *[Part One](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One.md)* and *[Part Two](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two.md)*, referring to it as <i><b>Qenya Lexicon</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lost_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lost-1">[1]</a></sup> The full text was published in [*Parma Eldalamberon* 12](../P/Parma_Eldalamberon_12.md) ([1998](../_/1998.md)).
