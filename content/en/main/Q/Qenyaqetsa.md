The <i><b>Qenyaqetsa</b></i> is a phonology and lexicon of [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien).

The manuscript was a notebook, and Tolkien included the number '23' in the first page, suggesting it was begun between [1915](/wiki/1915) and [1916](/wiki/1916), when he had that age.<sup id="cite_ref-Lost_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lost-1">[1]</a></sup> In a letter to [Edith Bratt](/wiki/Edith_Bratt) dated [2 March](/wiki/2_March) 1916 he mentioned working on a "nonsense fairy language" during his military training<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> suggesting that he had this notebook with him in Rugeley Camp in Staffordshire.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[note 1]</a></sup>

Many revisions were made until [1919](/wiki/1919) or [1920](/wiki/1920).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[3]</a></sup> Both the description of the phonology and the lexicon were the foundation of the [Qenya](/wiki/Qenya) language, which decades later would appear as "[Quenya](/wiki/Quenya)" in his published works.

His son [Christopher](/wiki/Christopher_Tolkien "Christopher Tolkien") published some fragments in the Appendices of *[The Book of Lost Tales](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales)* *[Part One](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_One "The Book of Lost Tales Part One")* and *[Part Two](/wiki/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales_Part_Two "The Book of Lost Tales Part Two")*, referring to it as <i><b>Qenya Lexicon</b></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lost_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lost-1">[1]</a></sup> The full text was published in [*Parma Eldalamberon* 12](/wiki/Parma_Eldalamberon_12 "Parma Eldalamberon 12") ([1998](/wiki/1998)).
