**Quessetéma** was one of the four series of the [Tengwar](../T/Tengwar.md) writing system specifically for the [Quenya](Quenya.md) language. As with all of the series it consisted of six characters in each of six *[tyeller](../T/Tyellë.md)* or 'grades'. This particular series took its name from the first of its signs, *[quesse](Quesse.md)* (literally meaning 'feather', but here simply the character representing 'kw').

Unlike [Westron](../W/Westron.md) or other languages, Quenya needed a series of characters for labialized consonants. Quenya writers used the third series of Tengwar characters for the [Calmatéma](../C/Calmatéma.md) (the k-series) and the fourth series for the Quessetéma (the kw-series). In the Quessetéma, half of the symbols had one bow or *[lúva](../L/Lúva.md)* while the other half had two bows. All bows in this series curved upwards and to the left from a stem, or *[telco](../T/Telco.md)*, which was either lowered, raised, or shortened, and all of the bows were closed with a bar across their opening.

The names of the letters in the Quessetéma were established in Quenya and had become standardised by the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md). The first four signs in the series were *[quesse](Quesse.md)*, *[ungwe](../U/Ungwe.md)*, *[hwesta](../H/Hwesta.md)* and *[unque](../U/Unque.md)*, representing 'kw', 'gw', 'khw' and 'ghw', respectively. The fifth sign, *[nwalme](../N/Nwalme.md)* (earlier *ngwalme*), represented 'ngw', while the sixth, *[vilya](../V/Vilya.md)* (earlier *wilya*) was used for 'w'.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>
