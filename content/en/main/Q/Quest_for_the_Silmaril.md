The **Quest for the Silmaril** was the pursuit of [Beren Erchamion](../B/Beren.md) for a [Silmaril](../S/Silmaril.md) from the [Iron Crown](../I/Iron_Crown.md) of [Morgoth](../M/Morgoth.md) during the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md). This was the price set upon Beren by [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md), King of [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md), for his daughter [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md)'s hand in marriage. Beren was aided in the Quest by Lúthien herself, King [Finrod Felagund](../F/Finrod_Felagund.md) of [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md), [Edrahil](../E/Edrahil.md), and [Huan the Hound](../H/Huan.md) of [Valinor](../V/Valinor.md).

The Quest was immortalised in the Elvish poetic epic, the *[Lay of Leithian](../L/Lay_of_Leithian.md)*, and is also told in the [nineteenth chapter](../O/Of_Beren_and_Lúthien.md) of the *[Quenta Silmarillion](Quenta_Silmarillion.md)*. Moreover, the Quest, and its conception and evolution by author [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md), is detailed in the book [Beren and Lúthien](../B/Beren_and_Lúthien.md) (2017), with one of the earliest iterations given in [The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md) as [The Tale of Tinúviel](../T/The_Tale_of_Tinúviel.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

### Coming of Beren to Doriath {#Coming_of_Beren_to_Doriath}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Lúthien.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/8/8d/Ted_Nasmith_-_L%C3%BAthien.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="286" height="450" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="286" data-file-height="450"/></a><figcaption><i>Lúthien</i> by <a href="../T/Ted_Nasmith.md" title="Ted Nasmith">Ted Nasmith</a></figcaption></figure>

In [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [464](../F/First_Age_464.md) at mid-summer,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Beren, son of [Barahir](../B/Barahir.md), came into [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md) and chanced upon [Lúthien](../L/Lúthien.md) — daughter of [Thingol](../T/Thingol.md), King of [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md), and [Melian](../M/Melian.md) the [Maia](../M/Maia.md) — dancing in the woods of [Neldoreth](../N/Neldoreth.md). There, a spell of dumbness fell upon Beren due to Lúthien's great beauty and he spent long wandering in Doriath seeking her. At first spring in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [465](../F/First_Age_465.md)<sup id="cite_ref-GA_177_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_177-5">[5]</a></sup> Beren spoke to her, calling her Tinúviel, and doom was written for [Man](../B/Beren.md) and [Elf-maiden](../L/Lúthien.md), and the two fell in love with each other.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_177_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_177-5">[5]</a></sup> But [Daeron](../D/Daeron.md) the minstrel reported this to Thingol and Beren was brought to the King in [Menegroth](../M/Menegroth.md). Before the court of Thingol, Beren declared his love for Lúthien, and in his anger, Thingol set upon Beren a task he deemed impossible: to retrieve a Silmaril from the Iron Crown of Morgoth. Only then would he would allow Beren to receive Lúthien as his bride.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_178_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_178-1">[1]</a></sup>

### The Quest Begins {#The_Quest_Begins}

Departing Doriath and beginning in earnest his Quest, Beren travelled to [Nargothrond](../N/Nargothrond.md), seeking the help of [Finrod Felagund](../F/Finrod_Felagund.md), who had aforetime sworn an oath to Barahir to come to the aid of his kin in need.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_178_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_178-1">[1]</a></sup> Upon hearing Beren's plight, Finrod was dispirited, for he perceived the [Oath of Fëanor](../O/Oath_of_Fëanor.md) to be at work. With ten companions, Beren and Finrod set out for [Angband](../A/Angband.md). By the arts of the Elven-lord, they were disguised as [Orcs](../O/Orcs.md). However, in passing [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](../T/Tol-in-Gaurhoth.md), the company were captured by [Sauron](../S/Sauron.md).<sup id="cite_ref-GA_180_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_180-6">[6]</a></sup> Thus befell the renowned contest of Finrod and Sauron. The two strove against each other in songs of power, but ultimately, Sauron had the mastery, and, stripping them of their disguises, he held them prisoner in [Tol-in-Gaurhoth](../T/Tol-in-Gaurhoth.md).<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup> But being unable to discern their identities or purpose, he sent werewolves to devour them one by one, until only Finrod and Beren were left. When the wolf came for Beren, Finrod put forth all his power, burst his bonds, and slew it with his bare hands, but alas at the cost of his own life. There ended Finrod Felagund, fairest of all the Noldor, his oath to Barahir redeemed at the last.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_180_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_180-6">[6]</a></sup> During their confinement, a darkness fell upon the heart of Lúthien, who learned from Melian that Beren was held as Sauron’s captive. Lúthien resolved to go and free him, but Thingol forbade this, and ordered for her to be guarded in a house built for her. But using her magic, she weaved a cloak of enchantment, and escaped from [Doriath](../D/Doriath.md).<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-GA_181_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_181-8">[8]</a></sup>

### Lúthien meets Huan {#Lúthien_meets_Huan}

On her journey, Lúthien encountered [Huan](../H/Huan.md), the hound of the [Valar](../V/Valar.md), who brought her before his masters, [Celegorm](../C/Celegorm.md) and [Curufin](../C/Curufin.md), two of the [Sons of Fëanor](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md).<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup> And they were filled with wonder upon seeing her and feigned friendship with her. And Lúthien was glad. But through treachery, she was taken captive. Celegorm intended to take Lúthien as his wife, and for this purpose he sent emissaries to Thingol. For through an alliance with Doriath, Celegorm planned to become the mightiest of the princes of the [Noldor](../N/Noldor.md);<sup id="cite_ref-GA_181_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_181-8">[8]</a></sup> he desired to unite all of the [Elves](../E/Elves.md) of Middle-earth under one banner before beginning an assault upon [Angband](../A/Angband.md) in the hope of reclaiming the [Silmarils](../S/Silmarils.md). But Huan took pity on Lúthien, and had grown in friendship with her. Thus, for the first time, Huan spoke, giving her counsel, and freed her from her bondage. He bore her away at speed, and the two came at last to Tol-in-Gaurhoth.

### Sauron defeated {#Sauron_defeated}

Sauron was aware of them, and smiled, for he knew a great reward would await him for the capture of the daughter of Melian. So he sent wolves, including the father of werewolves, [Draugluin](../D/Draugluin.md), to the gate, but Huan slew them all. Then Sauron recalled the fate of Huan, foretold long ago in Valinor; that he would be slain by the mightiest wolf ever to walk the world. And Sauron, in his conceit, imagined that he himself could be the one to end Huan. So he took the form of a terrible wolf, and both Lúthien and Huan quailed before him. But, as Sauron advanced, by the power of Lúthien, he was struck by a blinding enchantment of weariness, and Huan took him by the throat. Lúthien demanded he yield the mastery of the tower, lest Huan destroy his mortal form. Sauron yielded, and fled in the form of a terrible vampire.<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup> Laying waste to the fortress and freeing many from thraldom, Lúthien found Beren and healed him. United once more, they buried the body of Finrod and departed that place, and Huan returned to the service of Celegorm.

### Journey to Angband {#Journey_to_Angband}

Near Doriath, they were ambushed by Celegorm and Curufin, who knew of Beren's intentions to wrest a Silmaril from Morgoth. Curufin swept Lúthien up but [Beren leapt](../L/Leap_of_Beren.md) into his saddle,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> and all fell to the ground. In that hour, Huan forsook the two sons of Fëanor, and with the aid of the hound, Beren defeated them. Being humiliated, Curufin shot an arrow at Lúthien, but Beren came in front of her and was struck. Huan pursued the brothers, who fled, and returned with herbs to aid Lúthien in the healing of Beren. They returned to the borders of Doriath, where Beren left Lúthien sleeping, in the care of Huan, and continued his quest. However, Lúthien found him singing the [Song of Parting](../S/Song_of_Parting.md) at the borders of [Anfauglith](../A/Anfauglith.md).<sup id="cite_ref-GA_183_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_183-10">[10]</a></sup> Beren sought to discourage Lúthien from continuing the Quest with him, but Huan spoke for the second time, counselling that their fates were now bound together, whether Beren willed it or not. And Beren accepted this. Then by the arts of Lúthien, the two were arrayed in the hame of [Draugluin](../D/Draugluin.md), and the bat-like messenger of Sauron, [Thuringwethil](../T/Thuringwethil.md). Together, they passed through untold perils, and, in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [466](../F/First_Age_466.md)<sup id="cite_ref-GA_184_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_184-11">[11]</a></sup> arrived finally at the [Gates of Angband](../G/Gates_of_Angband.md).

### At Angband {#At_Angband}

Guarding the gates was a creature of whom no news had yet reached the Noldor: the werewolf [Carcharoth](../C/Carcharoth.md). And he denied them entry and bade them stand. Suddenly some power, descended from divine race, possessed Lúthien, and casting back her raiment she stood forth, radiant and terrible. Lifting up her hand, she commanded Carcharoth to sleep, and he was felled. Then Beren and Lúthien crossed the threshold of Angband, descended the serpentine stairs, and together wrought the greatest deed that has been dared by Elves or Men.<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup> For they came to the seat of Morgoth himself in his deepest hall, and it was full of fire and horror. Yet Lúthien was undaunted by Morgoth and began to dance and sing for him in the manner of a minstrel. Her song was of such enchantment and power that all his court fell into a deep sleep. The Silmarils in the Iron Crown suddenly blazed forth, and the burden of the jewels became too much for even the will of Morgoth to bear, for he became drowsy. Then Lúthien sprang into the air and cast her cloak before his eyes, setting upon him a dark dream.<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-GA_184_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_184-11">[11]</a></sup> And Morgoth was cast down in unconscious slumber.

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/T/Ted_Nasmith_-_Lúthien_Escapes_upon_Huan_(1998).jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/0/0e/Ted_Nasmith_-_L%C3%BAthien_Escapes_upon_Huan_%281998%29.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="297" height="450" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="297" data-file-height="450"/></a><figcaption><i>Lúthien Escapes Upon Huan (1998)</i> by Ted Nasmith</figcaption></figure>

Then Beren unsheathed [his knife](../A/Angrist.md) and cut a Silmaril from the crown of Morgoth. It did not burn him. It came then to Beren's mind to go beyond his vow and retrieve not one, but *all three* Silmarils from Angband. But such was not his fate, for the knife broke, and a shard struck the face of Morgoth. He and his court stirred. Realising their immediate peril, terror unknown came upon Lúthien and Beren, and they fled, heedless and without disguise. Upon reaching the surface, they were ambushed by Carcharoth. Beren raised the Silmaril before the wolf, but Carcharoth desired it and bit off the right hand of Beren.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_185_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_185-12">[12]</a></sup> The jewel seared the wolf's innards, and Carcharoth fled, in agonising pain. In his madness he passed through the [Girdle of Melian](../G/Girdle_of_Melian.md) into Doriath, for fate, and the power of the Silmaril, drove him.

### Return to Doriath {#Return_to_Doriath}

With the aid of [Thorondor](../T/Thorondor.md), Lúthien and Beren escaped the reach of Morgoth and came to the outer borders of Doriath. There Beren was nursed to health once more by Lúthien. After tarrying there for a time, they returned to Doriath in the spring of [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [466](../F/First_Age_466.md),<sup id="cite_ref-GA_185_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_185-12">[12]</a></sup> and Thingol marvelled to see Beren alive. Then Beren and Lúthien told their tale, and that a Silmaril was yet in his hand. The court of Menegroth was astonished;<sup id="cite_ref-S_Beren_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Beren-7">[7]</a></sup> Thingol's mood was softened, and he perceived the doom of Beren and Lúthien might not be withstood by any power of the world.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_185_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_185-12">[12]</a></sup> And, at last, he yielded Lúthien to him, and the two were married before the throne of Thingol.

### Carcharoth comes to Doriath {#Carcharoth_comes_to_Doriath}

So there may have been peace and joy in Doriath, but Carcharoth was discovered, and a hunting party composed of Thingol, Beren, [Beleg Cúthalion](../B/Beleg_Cúthalion.md), [Mablung](../M/Mablung.md), and Huan<sup id="cite_ref-GA_186_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_186-2">[2]</a></sup> was sent to slay the beast. During the hunt, Thingol was nearly slain, but Beren saved him, at the cost of a mortal wound. Huan then slew Carcharoth, but was himself fatally wounded. Coming to Beren, Huan spoke for the third and final time, bidding him farewell. There died Huan, hound of the Valar. The Silmaril was cut from Carcharoth's burned flesh, and Beren presented it at last to Thingol, saying "Now the Quest is achieved". Lúthien bade Beren await her [beyond the Western Sea](../A/Aman.md).<sup id="cite_ref-GA_186_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_186-2">[2]</a></sup> Looking upon her one last time, Beren died of his wounds, and his spirit found its way into the west, to the shores of the [Outer Sea](../O/Outer_Sea.md). Through grief, the spirit of Lúthien fled its body at the first breaking of Spring in [F.A.](../F/First_Age.md) [467](../F/First_Age_467.md)<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">[13]</a></sup> and came to the [Halls of Mandos](../H/Halls_of_Mandos.md). There she sang a song of such woe, and beauty, and lamentation, that even [Mandos](../M/Mandos.md) was moved to pity. He summoned Beren's spirit, and there the two were reunited. But it was not within the power of Mandos to change the fates of the [Children of Ilúvatar](../C/Children_of_Ilúvatar.md), so he went to [Manwë](../M/Manwë.md), who sought counsel from [Eru](../E/Eru.md) in his innermost thought, where the will of Ilúvatar was revealed to him.

<h3><span id="L.C3.BAthien.27s_Choice"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Lúthien&#39;s_Choice">Lúthien&#39;s Choice</span></h3>

Two choices were put before Lúthien. To remain in Valinor and its eternal bliss, forgetting the griefs of life, but without Beren. For to the Undying Lands, mortals were not permitted to come. The second choice was that she and Beren might return to Middle-earth as mortals, where they would both die a second death. Lúthien chose the latter, forsaking the Undying Lands for the love of Beren. So it came to pass that she and Beren returned to Doriath.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_214_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_214-14">[14]</a></sup>

Many rejoiced at their return, but Melian saw in her daughter's eyes the mortal doom that was there written; she foresaw that a parting beyond the world was now due between mother and daughter. And it is told that no grief has been greater than that of Melian the Maia in that hour.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_214_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_214-14">[14]</a></sup> But the Quest was completed; Thingol had received the Silmaril, and Beren and Lúthien had been wedded. The two went to dwell in [Tol Galen](../T/Tol_Galen.md), in [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md), where their son, [Dior Aranel](../D/Dior.md) was born.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_215_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_215-15">[15]</a></sup> There they lived, to the end of their days. And where their bodies were finally laid to rest, no tale tells.

## Aftermath {#Aftermath}

### Nirnaeth Arnoediad {#Nirnaeth_Arnoediad}

The Silmaril retrieved from Morgoth's crown brought about considerable changes in Beleriand. The deeds of Beren and Lúthien inspired [Maedhros](../M/Maedhros.md) to create a [military Union](../U/Union_of_Maedhros.md) against Morgoth,<sup id="cite_ref-S_Fifth_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S_Fifth-16">[16]</a></sup> resulting in the disastrous [Nirnaeth Arnoediad](../N/Nirnaeth_Arnoediad.md). In this battle, many perished, and [Húrin](../H/Húrin.md), father of [Túrin Turambar](../T/Túrin_Turambar.md) and [Nienor](../N/Nienor.md), was captured by Morgoth.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_240_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_240-17">[17]</a></sup> These events seeded the [tragedies](../O/Of_Túrin_Turambar.md) that eventually befell the two children of Húrin.<sup id="cite_ref-GA_247_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GA_247-18">[18]</a></sup>

### Ruin of Doriath {#Ruin_of_Doriath}

A more direct impact was the [Ruin of Doriath](../R/Ruin_of_Doriath.md). In naming the desire of the Silmaril, Thingol awoke the [Curse of Mandos](../C/Curse_of_Mandos~52081.md) and became ensnared in the [Oath of Fëanor](../O/Oath_of_Fëanor.md). Just as Finrod had cautioned Beren, so it came to be. Before long, the [Sons of Fëanor](../S/Sons_of_Fëanor.md) sent messages demanding the return of the jewel. Melian counselled Thingol to give it up, and save Doriath from its doom. But Thingol was angered by the prideful words of the Sons of Fëanor, and since the jewel had been won by the efforts of Beren and Lúthien, he refused. Indeed, his thought turned unceasingly to that jewel, and it came to his mind to combine together the beauty of the Nauglamir with the majesty of the Silmaril. And, he instructed the [Dwarves of Nogrod](../D/Dwarves_of_Nogrod.md) to reconstruct the [Nauglamir](../N/Nauglamir.md) like so. But the Dwarves desired the Silmaril and the Nauglamir for themselves, and they slew Thingol and tried to escape with the treasure after a [battle in the Thousand Caves](../B/Battle_in_the_Thousand_Caves.md). Most of them were slain by Beren and the Elves of [Ossiriand](../O/Ossiriand.md) in the [Battle of Sarn Athrad](../B/Battle_of_Sarn_Athrad.md) and the Silmaril was returned to Melian. But it did little to soothe her, for she had tasted the bitterness of mortality in full, and she was overcome with grief. So she departed from Middle-earth and returned [into the West](../V/Valinor.md), and the [Girdle of Melian](../G/Girdle_of_Melian.md) which had fenced Doriath from evil for many long years vanished in her absence.

This was the starting point for the eventual destruction of Doriath by the sons of Fëanor, when they tried to reclaim the Silmaril from [Dior](../D/Dior.md), slaying him in the process. They failed however, and the Silmaril was passed to [Elwing](../E/Elwing.md), daughter of Dior. Eventually the sons of Fëanor, compelled by [their oath](../O/Oath_of_Fëanor.md), tracked her down, which led to the [assault](../T/Third_Kinslaying.md) on the [Mouths of Sirion](../M/Mouths_of_Sirion.md). Elwing still managed to escape with the Silmaril.

### War of Wrath {#War_of_Wrath}

Though Morgoth did not count the missing Silmaril as a great loss, it would soon result in his downfall. Elwing and her husband [Eärendil](../E/Eärendil.md) [were guided to Valinor](../O/Of_the_Voyage_of_Eärendil_and_the_War_of_Wrath.md) by the Silmaril, and there Eärendil pleaded with the [Valar](../V/Valar.md) on behalf of [the two kindreds](../C/Children_of_Ilúvatar.md) to forgive the folly of the Noldor and put an end to the darkness that had grown in the East. The Valar granted his plea. The lone Silmaril was set in the sky as a [star](../G/Gil-Estel.md), and the Valar assembled a mighty host in the [War of Wrath](../W/War_of_Wrath.md), which resulted in the conclusive defeat of Morgoth, and the end of the [First Age](../F/First_Age.md).

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The Quest of Beren and Lúthien for the Silmaril became one of the greatest tales of the [Elder Days](../E/Elder_Days.md) by the [Third Age](../T/Third_Age.md). It was indirectly referred to in the [Song of Beren and Lúthien](../S/Song_of_Beren_and_Lúthien.md) sung by [Aragorn](../A/Aragorn.md) to the four [Hobbits](../H/Hobbits.md), [Frodo](../F/Frodo.md), [Sam](../S/Sam.md), [Merry](../M/Merry.md), and [Pippin](../P/Pippin.md) on [Weathertop](../W/Weathertop.md), after leaving [Bree](../B/Bree.md) in [T.A.](../T/Third_Age.md) [3018](../T/Third_Age_3018.md).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/A/Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tinúviel.jpg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/a/ae/Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tin%C3%BAviel.jpg/300px-Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tin%C3%BAviel.jpg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="300" height="516" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/a/ae/Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tin%C3%BAviel.jpg/450px-Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tin%C3%BAviel.jpg 1.5x, https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/a/ae/Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tin%C3%BAviel.jpg/600px-Alan_Lee_-_Tevildo_catches_sight_of_Tin%C3%BAviel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1074" data-file-height="1846"/></a><figcaption>&#34;Tevildo catches sight of Tinúviel&#34; by <a href="../A/Alan_Lee.md" title="Alan Lee">Alan Lee</a></figcaption></figure>

The Quest for the Silmaril forms a key part of [The Tale of Tinúviel](../T/The_Tale_of_Tinúviel.md) as told in the book [Beren and Lúthien](../B/Beren_and_Lúthien.md). This was one of the earliest stories written by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) (circa 1917<sup id="cite_ref-BLUnnamedIntro_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BLUnnamedIntro-20">[20]</a></sup>) in his mythology that would later become [The Book of Lost Tales](../T/The_Book_of_Lost_Tales.md).<sup id="cite_ref-BLUnnamedIntro_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BLUnnamedIntro-20">[20]</a></sup> The main story arc is essentially unchanged when compared to that which appears in the [Quenta Silmarillion](Quenta_Silmarillion.md), in that Beren and [Tinúviel](../T/Tinúviel.md) (Lúthien) attempt to retrieve a Silmaril from [Melko](../M/Melko.md) (Morgoth) in his fortress [Angamandi](../A/Angamandi.md) (Angband). There are however some key differences between the primitive and developed stories. Beren is not a Man in this early tale, but is instead an Elf, more specifically, a *[Gnome](../G/Gnome.md)*; the Gnomes would later become the *[Noldor](../N/Noldor.md)* as Tolkien rewrote the mythology. The mistrust of [Tinwelint](../T/Tinwelint.md) (Thingol) towards Beren is explained by the perception of the woodland Elves towards the Gnomes of [Dor-lómin](../D/Dor-lómin.md) as being treacherous creatures. Further, the minstrel [Dairon](../D/Dairon.md) (Daeron) is the brother of Tinúviel. Beren is set to work in the kitchens of [Tevildo](../T/Tevildo.md), Prince of Cats (later replaced by the Necromancer [Thû](../T/Thû.md), who was an early construction of Sauron), after being brought before Melko by a band of orcs who captured him as prisoner. From here was he rescued by Tinúviel who, after befriending Huan of the Dogs, used her magic to free those in thraldom in the castle of Tevildo.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup>

A notable absence in [this early iteration](../T/The_Tale_of_Tinúviel.md) of the Quest is the character of Felagund. Moreover, there is no early incarnation of the two Sons of Fëanor, Celegorm and Curufin. After developing the mythology in later years, Tolkien tied the tale of the Quest together with the events of Nargothrond, thus maturing the story towards the form it takes in the Quenta Silmarillion.
