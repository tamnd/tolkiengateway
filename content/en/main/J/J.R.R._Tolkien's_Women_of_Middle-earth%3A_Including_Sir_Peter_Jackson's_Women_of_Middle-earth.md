<i><b>J.R.R. Tolkien&#39;s Women of Middle-earth: Including Sir Peter Jackson&#39;s Women of Middle-earth</b></i> by <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=Dr_Chris_Barclay&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Dr Chris Barclay (page does not exist)">Dr Chris Barclay</a> offers an analysis of the female characters in [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien)’s legendarium alongside their reinterpretation in [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson)’s [film adaptations](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)"). Drawing on literary and film-studies approaches, the book examines how key figures such as [Galadriel](/wiki/Galadriel), [Arwen](/wiki/Arwen) &amp; [Éowyn](/wiki/%C3%89owyn) are constructed in Tolkien’s texts and transformed on screen, exploring themes of agency, gender, and narrative significance.

Barclay’s work places Tolkien’s often-slim female cast in their broader mythic, cultural and cinematic contexts, showing how their roles (though relatively few in number) resonate with moral weight, thematic import and visual power.

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>The women of J.R.R. Tolkien’s Middle-earth may have been few, but they more than made up for it by being strong, courageous and influential - they were game changers. Those familiar with The Hobbit and the Lord of the Rings - books and films, will already know something of Arwen, Galadriel, Éowyn and perhaps even Lobelia Sackville-Baggins. But many more amazing women populate The Silmarillion’s tales of the ages before the War of the Ring: Elbereth, Míriel, Morwen, Idril, Elwing and of course, Lúthien to name but a few. J.R.R. Tolkien’s Women of Middle-earth celebrates these amazing women and explores the striking links many have with the women of his own lived experience: particularly Mabel his mother, Edith his wife and, the object of his lifelong devotion, The Virgin Mary - his Women of England.
</p>
</blockquote>
