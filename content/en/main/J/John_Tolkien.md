Father **John Francis Reuel Tolkien** ([November 16](../_/16_November.md), [1917](../_/1917.md) - [January 22](../_/22_January.md), [2003](../_/2003.md)) was the first child and eldest son of [J.R.R.](J.R.R._Tolkien.md) and [Edith Tolkien](../E/Edith_Tolkien.md). His second name comes from his father's guardian, Father [Francis Xavier Morgan](../F/Francis_Xavier_Morgan.md).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

John often had difficulty falling asleep and his father would come to his bedside and tell stories to him. One of these tales was about a boy named [Carrots](../C/Carrots.md), who had red hair and climbed "into a cuckoo clock" to go off on a series of strange adventures, and a tale called "[The Orgog](../T/The_Orgog.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>

In 13 February [1944](../_/1944.md) John Tolkien was made Exorcist and Acolyte (the two highest of the Minor Orders of the Roman Catholic Church) in a ceremony at Stonyhurst. He was given his Orders as a priest on [10 February](../_/10_February.md) [1946](../_/1946.md).

Between 1957 and 1987 John Tolkien lived in Stoke-on-Trent. He wrote the script for a film about the life of Cardinal Newman, which was shown in June 1957 at the Oratory of Birmingham.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup> In September 1973, John Tolkien celebrated a Requiem Mass for his father at the Church of St. Anthony of Padua, in Oxford.

John Tolkien is buried in the same cemetery as his parents, with his grave not far from theirs.

He appears briefly in the biographical drama film *[Tolkien](<../T/Tolkien_(film).md>)*, portrayed by actor Harry Webster.
