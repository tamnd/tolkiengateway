**J.R.R. Tolkien's signature and monogram** are synonymous with Tolkien's works, and have a history of their own. Both are registered trademarks owned by the [Tolkien Estate](../T/Tolkien_Estate.md).

## Signature history {#Signature_history}

Tolkien's signature evolved over time. As early as [1910](../_/1910.md), he is recorded as having signed his name as "Ronald Tolkien" on an art piece titled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Ruins_at_West_End_of_White_Abbey&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Ruins at West End of White Abbey (page does not exist)">Ruins at West End of White Abbey</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> In [1913](../_/1913.md) he signed his name as the more familiar "J.R.R. Tolkien" on his painting entitled <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Foxglove_Year&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Foxglove Year (page does not exist)"><i>Foxglove Year</i></a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup>, which accompanied a poem he wrote by the same name. A further evolution of Tolkien's signature is visible from a [1923](../_/1923.md) signing of a book he owned called *The Latin Poems.*<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Monogram history {#Monogram_history}

### Early monograms {#Early_monograms}

Tolkien's first recorded use of a monogram was on a sketch titled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Lyme_Regis_Harbor_from_the_Drawing_Room_Window_of_the_Cups_Hotel&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Lyme Regis Harbor from the Drawing Room Window of the Cups Hotel (page does not exist)">Lyme Regis Harbor from the Drawing Room Window of the Cups Hotel</a></i> in [1906](../_/1906.md)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> and included just a "J", an "R", and a "T". Another monogram appeared on a [1911](../_/1911.md) illustration titled [Lamb's Farm, Gedling](../L/Lamb's_Farm,_Gedling.md). The first use of the two-"R" motif (with one "R" being flipped) was in [1912](../_/1912.md) on a sheet containing two sketches, <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Keystone_of_Door&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Keystone of Door (page does not exist)">Keystone of Door</a></i> and <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gargoyles,_South_Side,_Lambourn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gargoyles, South Side, Lambourn (page does not exist)">Gargoyles, South Side, Lambourn</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>. [1913](../_/1913.md) saw the first use of the four dot design in a monogram on a piece titled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Grownupishness&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Grownupishness (page does not exist)">Grownupishness</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>. Another 1913 monogram contained the first instance of two uses of the four dots design on a piece titled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Pagent_House_Gardens,_Warwick&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Pagent House Gardens, Warwick (page does not exist)">Pagent House Gardens, Warwick</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>. In [1914](../_/1914.md), a symmetrical monogram design appeared in the first text about [Middle-earth](../M/Middle-earth.md)'s genesis called <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Tanaqui&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Tanaqui (page does not exist)">Tanaqui</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> which depicted an early version of the mountain [Taniquetil](../T/Taniquetil.md). In [1918](../_/1918.md), a monogram closely resembling the final monogram appeared on a piece entitled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Gipsy_Green&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Gipsy Green (page does not exist)">Gipsy Green</a>.</i><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup>

### Monogram usage in *The Hobbit* {#Monogram_usage_in_The_Hobbit}

The publication of *[The Hobbit](../T/The_Hobbit.md)* in [1937](../_/1937.md) saw the use of several unique monograms. An asymmetrical variation showed up in the ink illustration titled *Trolls. Another* new symmetrical monogram showed up in one of Tolkien's illustrations titled *[The Hill: Hobbiton-across-the Water](../T/The_Hill%253A_Hobbiton-across-the_Water.md)*<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>. A similar 1937 monogram appears on the dust jacket of the book. Another 1937 monogram appears on the illustration titled <i><a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Conversation_with_Smaug&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Conversation with Smaug (page does not exist)">Conversation with Smaug</a></i>, and is highly reminiscent of Tolkien's "modern" monogram<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-12">[12]</a></sup>.

<h3><span id="Final_version_of_Tolkien.27s_monogram"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Final_version_of_Tolkien&#39;s_monogram">Final version of Tolkien&#39;s monogram</span></h3>

<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a class="mw-file-description" href="../../file/J/JRRT.jpeg.md"><img src="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/thumb/7/7d/JRRT.jpeg/123px-JRRT.jpeg" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="123" height="184" class="mw-file-element" srcset="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/images/7/7d/JRRT.jpeg 1.5x" data-file-width="159" data-file-height="238"/></a><figcaption>Final monogram.</figcaption></figure>

The final version of Tolkien's monogram appeared in [1976](../_/1976.md) on the title page of <i><a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../D/Drawings_by_Tolkien.md" title="Drawings by Tolkien">Drawings by Tolkien</a></i>, the catalogue of a J.R.R. Tolkien exhibition of the same name. The exhibition was held by Ashmolean Museum, Oxford, in between 1976 and [1977](../_/1977.md). Curiously, the monogram was horizontally inverted, which was likely a mistake of the printer who produced the catalogues.

A correctly oriented version of the monogram later appeared on the spine of *[Unfinished Tales](../U/Unfinished_Tales.md)* in [1980](../_/1980.md). However, this version omitted the two "four-dots" designs.

The two groups of four dots were added but were used inconsistently until [1981](../_/1981.md), when the final version of the monogram was designed and used on the cover of *[The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien](../T/The_Letters_of_J.R.R._Tolkien.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-12">[12]</a></sup>

## Coincidence {#Coincidence}

- It is entirely a coincidence that the monogram closely resembles the Chinese character "<a class="external text" href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9D%9F#Definitions" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">束</a>", which means "bundle" or "to bind".
