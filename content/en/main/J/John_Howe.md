**John Howe** is a Canadian illustrator most known for illustrating many works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) as well as working on both [*The Lord of the Rings*](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)") and [*The Hobbit*](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)> "The Hobbit (film series)") film series. Most recently he has worked as a concept artist for *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power)* television series<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> and *[The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Hunt_for_Gollum)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Hunt_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hunt-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

John Howe was born in Vancouver, Canada.<sup id="cite_ref-bio_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-3">[3]</a></sup> He graduated from the École des Arts Dècoratifs of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg" title="wikipedia:Strasbourg">Strasbourg</a><sup id="cite_ref-bio_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-3">[3]</a></sup> in [1981](/wiki/1981), having obtained a Diploma in Illustration.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> He now lives in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuchatel" title="wikipedia:Neuchatel">Neuchatel</a> with his wife [Fataneh](/wiki/Fataneh_Howe "Fataneh Howe") (a fellow Tolkien illustrator) and their son Dana.<sup id="cite_ref-bio_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-3">[3]</a></sup>

Howe first read [*The Lord of the Rings*](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings "The Lord of the Rings") in high school, having borrowed the books from his school library. He started with [*The Two Towers*](/wiki/The_Two_Towers "The Two Towers") because "*everyone who started the first volume never got any further*" and [*The Fellowship of the Ring*](/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring "The Fellowship of the Ring") was rarely available to borrow.<sup id="cite_ref-myth_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-myth-5">[5]</a></sup> He was inspired by the artwork of [The Brothers Hildebrandt](/wiki/The_Brothers_Hildebrandt) to start creating illustrations of Tolkien's works.<sup id="cite_ref-myth_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-myth-5">[5]</a></sup>

Howe's [version](/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_Boromir_01.jpg "File:John Howe - Boromir 01.jpg") of [Boromir](/wiki/Boromir) is based on his own face.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

<h3><span id="Involvement_in_Peter_Jackson.27s_Middle-earth_film_series"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Involvement_in_Peter_Jackson&#39;s_Middle-earth_film_series">Involvement in Peter Jackson&#39;s Middle-earth film series</span></h3>

John Howe and [Alan Lee](/wiki/Alan_Lee) worked as the chief conceptual designers on [*The Lord of the Rings*](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)> "The Lord of the Rings (film series)") trilogy.<sup id="cite_ref-imdb_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-imdb-7">[7]</a></sup> Howe is also credited as a carpenter in the first two films.<sup id="cite_ref-imdb_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-imdb-7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">[8]</a></sup> He has a cameo as one of the nine [Kings of Men](/wiki/Nazg%C3%BBl "Nazgûl") in the Prologue of *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)*. Howe and Lee returned to work as conceptual designers for [*The Hobbit*](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)> "The Hobbit (film series)") film series. They both make uncredited appearances as men-musicians of [Laketown](/wiki/Lake-town "Lake-town") in *[The Desolation of Smaug](/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Desolation_of_Smaug "The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug")*.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">[9]</a></sup> Howe worked as a concept artist for *[The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Hunt_for_Gollum)*.<sup id="cite_ref-Hunt_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hunt-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Artwork theft {#Artwork_theft}

In the night of 18 May 1997, eleven of Howe's originals were stolen from the Médiathèque of Sedan in [Belgium](/wiki/Category:Belgium "Category:Belgium"). The thief's father contacted the Médiathèque a couple weeks later promising to try to retrieve the stolen works from his son. Eight originals were finally returned, one ([Minas Tirith](/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_Minas_Tirith.jpg "File:John Howe - Minas Tirith.jpg")) was damaged beyond repair. [*Éowyn and the Nazgûl*](/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_Eowyn_and_the_Nazgul.jpg "File:John Howe - Eowyn and the Nazgul.jpg"), [*Gandalf the Grey*](/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_Gandalf_the_Grey_02.jpg "File:John Howe - Gandalf the Grey 02.jpg"), and [*The Uruk-hai*](/wiki/File:John_Howe_-_The_Uruk-hai.jpg "File:John Howe - The Uruk-hai.jpg") were never recovered.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">[10]</a></sup>

### *Lord of the Brush* {#Lord_of_the_Brush}

In 2005 [Lord of the Brush](/wiki/Lord_of_the_Brush), a documentary about Howe's work in the Legendarium was released to DVD.

## Published Artwork {#Published_Artwork}

Howe is one of [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins)' most used artists, and his cover art is frequently featured on translations as well.

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Visualising Tolkien, I truly believe, is a process not unlike his own transcribing of his world, with the obvious advantage of having his texts to work from. It means illustrating between the lines, casting one&#39;s net farther out, searching for the credibility that is inherent in his work, with the disadvantage of not being to always leave elements to the imagination. This said, any illustration should be an invitation, with space for the viewer to bring his or her personal experience, both in relation to the reading experience and in a wider context, to complete the picture.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>John Howe<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">[11]</a></sup></cite></p>

<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395759"/>

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r395753"/><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I see <a href="/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">Tolkien</a>’s artwork as a window into his mind, and a very useful one. They also reinforce the notion that creation is not so neatly divided into writing and painting or drawing, the two skills can contribute by turn to the same universe. I tend to agree with him: his universe is better not illustrated, but painting is my profession, so I will accept the contradiction. This said, while I understand his reluctance, and often talk with readers who would prefer that their imaginations not be “guided” by artwork, I do not think that the text has anything to fear from any picture. If the image does not find a symbiotic relationship with the word, then it is quickly forgotten. If the image persists, then it somehow captures what the text not only says, but more importantly, what it does not say.
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>John Howe<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">[12]</a></sup></cite></p>
