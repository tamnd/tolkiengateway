**J.R. Titmass** was a [Man](/wiki/Man) who lived in <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain" title="wikipedia:Great Britain">Britain</a> during the early <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_century" title="wikipedia:21st century">21st century</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Foreword_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foreword-2">[2]</a></sup>

## History {#History}

Mr. J.R. Titmass was a famous "historian of <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_century" title="wikipedia:20th century">twentieth-century</a> [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford)". When Mr. [Howard Green](/wiki/Howard_Green), the school clerk of the <a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examination_Schools" title="wikipedia:Examination Schools">Examination Schools</a> in Oxford found the disordered bundle of [The Notion Club Papers](/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers) in the basement, Titmass assisted him in deciphering the papers in any way that he could. Indeed, it was Titmass who discovered that the name "[Nicholas Guildford](/wiki/Nicholas_Guildford)", used by the reporter of the [Notion Club](/wiki/Notion_Club), was in origin derived from *[The Owl and the Nightingale](/wiki/The_Owl_and_the_Nightingale)*,<sup id="cite_ref-intro_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-3">[3]</a></sup> a medieval dialogue that was once read in Oxford.<sup id="cite_ref-Foreword_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foreword-2">[2]</a></sup>

After the publication of the first edition of the papers at an unknown date, Titmass informed Green that he couldn't "find any record in the" [1940s](/wiki/1940 "1940") of any of the other names given in the Papers. As a result, Green changed his view, writing in his Second Edition foreword in [2014](/wiki/2014) that he now assumed that if any such club existed at that period, the names would have to be pseudonyms.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

## Other versions of the legendarium {#Other_versions_of_the_legendarium}

In an early version of the Foreword, J.R. Titmass's original surname was **Titmouse** before [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) changed this to Titmass.<sup id="cite_ref-intro_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-intro-3">[3]</a></sup>
