**Joseph Wright** ([31 October](../_/31_October.md) [1855](../_/1855.md) – [27 February](../_/27_February.md) [1930](../_/1930.md)) an English Germanic philologist. He served as Professor of Comparative Philology at the [University of Oxford](../U/University_of_Oxford.md), where he was one of [J.R.R. Tolkien](J.R.R._Tolkien.md)'s tutors.

It was during Tolkien's years as a schoolboy that he first became acquainted with Wright's works, specifically his *Primer of the Gothic Language*.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup> This text provided insight into Tolkien's early studies of Germanic languages.

In a letter dated [26 January](../_/26_January.md) [1925](../_/1925.md), Tolkien expressed gratitude to Wright on the eve of his retirement, saying, "It was your works that came into my hands by chance as a schoolboy, that first revealed to me the philology I love".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien later said of Wright that he "proved a good friend and adviser".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Wright's impact on Tolkien was not limited to German, however, for he learned much of Greek, [Latin](../L/Latin.md), and Welsh from him. The two continued to stay close friends and Tolkien remembered frequently going over to Wright's house for tea and discussion.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup>

In [1925](../_/1925.md) Wright wrote a letter of recommendation for Tolkien's application at [Rawlinson and Bosworth Professorship of Anglo-Saxon](../R/Rawlinson_and_Bosworth_Professorship_of_Anglo-Saxon.md):

<div class="tg-quote-container"><div class="tg-section-divider"><span>”</span></div><blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>I have known Professor Tolkien intimately since the beginning of his undergraduate days at Oxford, and have greatly admired his keen interest in the philological study of Latin, Greek, and more especially the Germanic Languages. He regularly attended my classes and lectures for two years on Comparative Philology in general, and on Latin, Greek, and Gothic, and I formed a high opinion of his attainments in these subjects...
</p>
<p class="tg-quote-footer"><cite>An Application for the Rawlinson and Bosworth Professorship of Anglo-Saxon in the University of Oxford by J.R.R. Tolkien, Professor of the English Language in the University of Leeds, June 25, 1925</cite></p>
</blockquote><div class="tg-section-divider"></div></div>

Tolkien would go on to be an executor of Wright's will.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>
