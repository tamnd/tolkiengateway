<i><b>J.R.R. Tolkien: Author of the Century</b></i> is a scholarly study of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) and his work by Professor [Tom Shippey](/wiki/Tom_Shippey).

The book's content is largely similar to *[The Road to Middle-earth](/wiki/The_Road_to_Middle-earth)*. As in the Professor's words, the two books

<dl><dd><i>&#34;... now complement each other through their different approaches, though they present essentially the same explanations of the central works.&#34;</i> <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup></dd></dl>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Recent polls have consistently declared that J.R.R. Tolkien is &#34;the most influential author of the century&#34; and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a></i> is &#34;the book of the century.&#34; In support of these claims, Tom Shippey, the prominent medievalist and scholar of fantasy, now presents us with a fascinating companion to the works of J.R.R. Tolkien, focusing in particular on <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hobbit" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a></i>, <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>, and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Silmarillion" title="The Silmarillion">The Silmarillion</a></i>.
</p><p>The core of the book consists of three chapters that examine <i>The Lord of the Rings</i> as a linguistic and cultural map, as a twisted web of story, and as a response to the meaning of myth. Shippey presents a unique argument to explain the nature of evil and gives readers a compelling insight into the complicated interweaving of many strands as the narrative moves between characters and into the remarkable skill behind the construction of such a rich and complex story. Other chapters examine <i>The Hobbit</i>, explaining the hobbits&#39; anachronistic relationship to the heroic world of Middle-earth; the fundamental importance of <i>The Silmarillion</i> to Tolkien&#39;s canon; and an illuminating look at <i><a href="/wiki/Farmer_Giles_of_Ham" title="Farmer Giles of Ham">Farmer Giles of Ham</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Leaf_by_Niggle" title="Leaf by Niggle">Leaf by Niggle</a></i>, and other lesser-known works in connection to Tolkien&#39;s life.
</p><p>With a clear and accessible style, Shippey offers a new approach to Tolkien, to fantasy, and to the importance of language in literature. He demonstrates how <i>The Hobbit</i>, <i>The Lord of the Rings</i>, and <i>The Silmarillion</i> form part of a live and continuing tradition of storytelling that can trace its roots back through Grimms&#39; <i>Fairy Tales</i> to <i><a href="/wiki/Beowulf_(poem)" title="Beowulf (poem)">Beowulf</a></i>. <i>J.R.R. Tolkien: Author of the Century</i> not only gives readers a deeper understanding of Tolkien and his work, but also serves as a learned and entertaining introduction to some of the finest and most influential works of fantasy ever written.
</p>
</blockquote>
