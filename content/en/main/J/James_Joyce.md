**James Joyce** ([2 February](/wiki/2_February) 1882 — [13 January](/wiki/13_January) [1941](/wiki/1941)) was an Irish writer.

In the works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien), there are at least three references to Joyce and his works:

1. A brief reference in a draft of the lecture "[A Secret Vice](/wiki/A_Secret_Vice)".<sup id="cite_ref-JDR_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JDR-1">[1]</a></sup>
2. A note on the back of a manuscript page of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, held at the collections of the [Marquette University](/wiki/Marquette_University).<sup id="cite_ref-JDR_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JDR-1">[1]</a></sup>
3. The line "Anna Livia Plurabelle" (from Joyce's <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnegans_Wake" title="wikipedia:Finnegans Wake">Finnegans Wake</a></i>) at the top of a linguistic manuscript.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Works comparing the literature of Joyce and Tolkien include: "Yeats, Joyce and Tolkien, the Artist as Magician" in [*Mythlore* 36](/wiki/Mythlore_36 "Mythlore 36") (1983), *[Interrupted Music: The Making of Tolkien's Mythology](/wiki/Interrupted_Music:_The_Making_of_Tolkien%27s_Mythology)* (2005), and *[The Loss and the Silence: Aspects of Modernism in the Works of C.S. Lewis, J.R.R. Tolkien &amp; Charles Williams](/wiki/The_Loss_and_the_Silence "The Loss and the Silence")* (2011).
