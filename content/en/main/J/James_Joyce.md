**James Joyce** ([2 February](../_/2_February.md) 1882 — [13 January](../_/13_January.md) [1941](../_/1941.md)) was an Irish writer.

In the works of [J.R.R. Tolkien](J.R.R._Tolkien.md), there are at least three references to Joyce and his works:

1. A brief reference in a draft of the lecture "[A Secret Vice](../A/A_Secret_Vice.md)".<sup id="cite_ref-JDR_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JDR-1">[1]</a></sup>
2. A note on the back of a manuscript page of *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*, held at the collections of the [Marquette University](../M/Marquette_University.md).<sup id="cite_ref-JDR_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JDR-1">[1]</a></sup>
3. The line "Anna Livia Plurabelle" (from Joyce's <i><a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnegans_Wake" title="wikipedia:Finnegans Wake">Finnegans Wake</a></i>) at the top of a linguistic manuscript.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

Works comparing the literature of Joyce and Tolkien include: "Yeats, Joyce and Tolkien, the Artist as Magician" in [*Mythlore* 36](../M/Mythlore_36.md) (1983), *[Interrupted Music: The Making of Tolkien's Mythology](../I/Interrupted_Music%253A_The_Making_of_Tolkien's_Mythology.md)* (2005), and *[The Loss and the Silence: Aspects of Modernism in the Works of C.S. Lewis, J.R.R. Tolkien &amp; Charles Williams](../T/The_Loss_and_the_Silence.md)* (2011).
