**Margaret Joy Hill** ([1936](../_/1936.md)-[1991](../_/1991.md)) became the secretary of [J.R.R. Tolkien](J.R.R._Tolkien.md) during the 1960s, working for [George Allen &amp; Unwin](../A/Allen_and_Unwin.md) since her late teens.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

In [1966](../_/1966.md) she suggested to Tolkien to remove his telephone number from the public directory to avoid phonecalls from fans.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> Tolkien also asked her legal opinion about denying some fan to write a sequel to *[The Lord of the Rings](../T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md)*.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

In [1967](../_/1967.md) she offered Tolkien a copy of *[The Children's Treasury of Literature](../T/The_Children's_Treasury_of_Literature.md)* which Tolkien in turn passed to his little grandson, [Simon](../S/Simon_Tolkien.md).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup>

In [1968](../_/1968.md) she discovered in Tolkien's library the poem [Bilbo's Last Song](../B/Bilbo's_Last_Song.md). Tolkien granted to her its copyright as a gift.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>

On [30 November](../_/30_November.md) [1969](../_/1969.md) Joy Hill attended the event "[An Afternoon in Middle-earth](../A/An_Afternoon_in_Middle-earth.md)", delivering the speech "Coping with a cult". The programme booklet for the event also included an article by Hill on [fan letters to Tolkien](../T/Tolkien_fandom.md), in which she writes:

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>They come from all over the world; they come in English, French, German and Elvish... they come three posts a day, five days a week; they have been coming for quite some time and they are still coming; no longer just a few a day but an ever-increasing flow [...] The English are cautious in their approach and often begin &#39;I have hesitated to write and thank you&#39; whereas writers from across the Atlantic are very much bolder. &#39;Dear darling Professor&#39; wrote one hippy from San Fransisco, &#39;I have been on a trip to Middle-earth and it is indeed a beautiful place. I must see you&#39;. From Norfolk, Virginia, one letter ended: &#39;One day I shall corner you on a remote little star and we shall talk&#39;.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
