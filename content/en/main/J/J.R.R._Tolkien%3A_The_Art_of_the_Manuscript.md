<i><b>J.R.R. Tolkien: The Art of the Manuscript</b></i> is an exhibition catalogue which accompanies an [2022](../_/2022.md) exhibition of the same name.

The exhibition was held between [19 August](../_/19_August.md) and [23 December](../_/23_December.md) [2022](../_/2022.md), organized collaboratively by [Marquette University](../M/Marquette_University.md)'s Raynor Memorial Libraries and the Haggerty Museum of Art, curated by Tolkien Archivist [William M. Fliss](../W/William_M._Fliss.md) and Dr. <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=Sarah_C._Schaefer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="Sarah C. Schaefer (page does not exist)">Sarah C. Schaefer</a>.

The book reproduces the complete object list in color, including 147 items both from Marquette's collection and from other repositories, many of which have never been exhibited or published.<sup id="cite_ref-MaFB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MaFB-1">[1]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> It was also nominated for the [Tolkien Society Award](../T/Tolkien_Society_Awards.md) for the best book in [2023](../_/2023.md).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>

<br>

## From the publisher {#From_the_publisher}

<blockquote class="tg-quote noexcerpt">
<p>Marquette University&#39;s Raynor Memorial Libraries and the Haggerty Museum of Art collaboratively present this exhibition focused on the work of celebrated author and artist J.R.R. Tolkien (1892-1973), best known for his literary classics The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings.<br/>
The exhibition considers Tolkien’s work through the lens of manuscripts, in terms of both the materials that Tolkien studied as a medieval philologist and the manuscripts that he created while developing his collected writings on Middle-earth. Professor Tolkien was deeply immersed in the complexities of manuscripts, and this exhibition will illustrate how different aspects of the manuscript tradition found expression within Tolkien’s scholarly life and in his creative writing.<br/>
Founded on Marquette&#39;s <a class="external text" href="https://www.marquette.edu/library/archives/tolkien.php" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">J.R.R. Tolkien Collection</a>, the exhibition also includes items borrowed from other repositories, including a significant number of Tolkien manuscripts and artwork from the <a class="mw-redirect" href="../B/Bodleian_Libraries.md" title="Bodleian Libraries">Bodleian Libraries</a> at the University of Oxford. Many of the 147 items in the exhibition have not previously been exhibited or published.<br/>
The exhibition is accompanied by a 196-page hardbound catalogue that reproduces the complete object list, and includes introductory essays by exhibition co-curators William M. Fliss, PhD and Sarah C. Schaefer, PhD.
</p>
</blockquote>
