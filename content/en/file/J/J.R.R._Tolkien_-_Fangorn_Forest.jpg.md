## Summary {#Summary}

This painting by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) appeared as 'Fangorn Forest' first as the cover illustration of *[The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Two_Towers)* in [1973](/wiki/1973) (© George Allen &amp; Unwin Ltd, 1973). It later appeared in *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Calendar 1974](/wiki/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Calendar_1974)*, and an enlargement of the central area of the picture in *[The Lord of the Rings 1977 Calendar](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1977_Calendar)*, in both Calendars captioned 'Fangorn Forest', as in the title inscription, in the hand of the artist, on the painting itself. In *[The Silmarillion Calendar 1978](/wiki/The_Silmarillion_Calendar_1978)* the same reproduction as in 1977 was used, but this time captioned "Beleg finds Gwindor in Taur-nu-Fuin". The reason for this is that while preparing the 1978 Calendar the original significance of the painting was realized.

J.R.R. Tolkien stated in a letter of [1937](/wiki/1937) that the picture of [Mirkwood](/wiki/Mirkwood) for *[The Hobbit](/wiki/The_Hobbit)* was itself redrawn from a painting made earlier to illustrate the passage in "[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)" ([Chapter 21](/wiki/Of_T%C3%BArin_Turambar "Of Túrin Turambar")) where [Beleg](/wiki/Beleg) finds [Gwindor](/wiki/Gwindor) in the forest of [Taur-nu-Fuin](</wiki/Taur-nu-Fuin_(Dorthonion)> "Taur-nu-Fuin (Dorthonion)"). That painting is beyond question the one reproduced here, despite the title 'Fangorn Forest'. In view of the title the two figures would naturally be taken to be the [Hobbits](/wiki/Hobbits) [Pippin](/wiki/Pippin) and [Merry](/wiki/Merry), straying in <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Fangorn" title="Fangorn">Fangorn</a> before their encounter with [Treebeard](/wiki/Treebeard) (*The Two Towers*, Book III, [Chapter 4](</wiki/Treebeard_(chapter)> "Treebeard (chapter)")). It is clear, however, that this is not so; the figures are [Elves](/wiki/Elves) and not Hobbits; and the Elf climbing over the tree-roots is Beleg Strongbow of [Doriath](/wiki/Doriath), bearing his great sword [Anglachel](/wiki/Anglachel) (which was afterwards reforged for [Túrin](/wiki/T%C3%BArin) as [Gurthang](/wiki/Gurthang)). The other is Gwindor of [Nargothrond](/wiki/Nargothrond), lying exhausted after his escape from the mines of Angband, with his [lamp](/wiki/F%C3%ABanorian_lamps "Fëanorian lamps") beside him.

The only possible explanation is that J.R.R. Tolkien decided that "The Silmarillion" painting could nevertheless be used as the cover illustration of the George Allen &amp; Unwin Ltd, 1973 edition of *The Two Towers*, as an illustration of the Hobbits in Fangorn Forest. It was likely created at the same time as the other "Silmarillion" paintings in the late [1920s](/wiki/1920 "1920").
