## Summary {#Summary}

This painting by [J.R.R. Tolkien](../../main/J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md) appeared as 'Fangorn Forest' first as the cover illustration of *[The Two Towers](../../main/T/The_Two_Towers.md)* in [1973](../../main/_/1973.md) (© George Allen &amp; Unwin Ltd, 1973). It later appeared in *[The J.R.R. Tolkien Calendar 1974](../../main/T/The_J.R.R._Tolkien_Calendar_1974.md)*, and an enlargement of the central area of the picture in *[The Lord of the Rings 1977 Calendar](../../main/T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_1977_Calendar.md)*, in both Calendars captioned 'Fangorn Forest', as in the title inscription, in the hand of the artist, on the painting itself. In *[The Silmarillion Calendar 1978](../../main/T/The_Silmarillion_Calendar_1978.md)* the same reproduction as in 1977 was used, but this time captioned "Beleg finds Gwindor in Taur-nu-Fuin". The reason for this is that while preparing the 1978 Calendar the original significance of the painting was realized.

J.R.R. Tolkien stated in a letter of [1937](../../main/_/1937.md) that the picture of [Mirkwood](../../main/M/Mirkwood.md) for *[The Hobbit](../../main/T/The_Hobbit.md)* was itself redrawn from a painting made earlier to illustrate the passage in "[The Silmarillion](../../main/T/The_Silmarillion.md)" ([Chapter 21](../../main/O/Of_Túrin_Turambar.md)) where [Beleg](../../main/B/Beleg.md) finds [Gwindor](../../main/G/Gwindor.md) in the forest of [Taur-nu-Fuin](<../../main/T/Taur-nu-Fuin_(Dorthonion).md>). That painting is beyond question the one reproduced here, despite the title 'Fangorn Forest'. In view of the title the two figures would naturally be taken to be the [Hobbits](../../main/H/Hobbits.md) [Pippin](../../main/P/Pippin.md) and [Merry](../../main/M/Merry.md), straying in <a class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" href="../../main/F/Fangorn.md" title="Fangorn">Fangorn</a> before their encounter with [Treebeard](../../main/T/Treebeard.md) (*The Two Towers*, Book III, [Chapter 4](<../../main/T/Treebeard_(chapter).md>)). It is clear, however, that this is not so; the figures are [Elves](../../main/E/Elves.md) and not Hobbits; and the Elf climbing over the tree-roots is Beleg Strongbow of [Doriath](../../main/D/Doriath.md), bearing his great sword [Anglachel](../../main/A/Anglachel.md) (which was afterwards reforged for [Túrin](../../main/T/Túrin.md) as [Gurthang](../../main/G/Gurthang.md)). The other is Gwindor of [Nargothrond](../../main/N/Nargothrond.md), lying exhausted after his escape from the mines of Angband, with his [lamp](../../main/F/Fëanorian_lamps~17189.md) beside him.

The only possible explanation is that J.R.R. Tolkien decided that "The Silmarillion" painting could nevertheless be used as the cover illustration of the George Allen &amp; Unwin Ltd, 1973 edition of *The Two Towers*, as an illustration of the Hobbits in Fangorn Forest. It was likely created at the same time as the other "Silmarillion" paintings in the late [1920s](../../main/_/1920.md).
