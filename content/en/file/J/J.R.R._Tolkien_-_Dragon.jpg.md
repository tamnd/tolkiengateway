## Summary {#Summary}

This painting by [J.R.R. Tolkien](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien) dates from September [1927](/wiki/1927). Above the coiled dragon are two flowers. Tolkien inscribed the words "<i><b>hringboga heorte gefýsed</b></i>" beneath the dragon, which are derived from the [Old English](/wiki/Old_English) poem *[Beowulf](</wiki/Beowulf_(poem)> "Beowulf (poem)")* (line 2561), "*ða wæs hringbogan heorte gefysed / saecce tó séceanne*", rendered as "Now was the heart of the coiling beast stirred to come out to fight" in J.R.R. Tolkien's translation of the poem.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">[1]</a></sup>

This drawing was published in the *[J.R.R. Tolkien Calendar 1979](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien_Calendar_1979)* and appears on the cover of the Deluxe edition of *The Hobbit* (1976). It was reproduced as plate nr. 48 in *[J.R.R. Tolkien: Artist and Illustrator](/wiki/J.R.R._Tolkien:_Artist_and_Illustrator)* with its inscription as the title. Also used as the front cover of *[Beowulf: A Translation and Commentary](/wiki/Beowulf:_A_Translation_and_Commentary)* (2014). It was also reproduced on the <a class="external text" href="https://www.tolkienestate.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">website</a> of the [Tolkien Estate](/wiki/Tolkien_Estate).
