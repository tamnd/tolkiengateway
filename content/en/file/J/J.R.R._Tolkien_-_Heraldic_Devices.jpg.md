## Summary {#Summary}

Eight of these devices were used on the back of the cover of *[The J. R. R. Tolkien Calendar 1974](../../main/T/The_J._R._R._Tolkien_Calendar_1974.md)* (those of [Finwë](../../main/F/Finwë.md): and [Eärendil](../../main/E/Eärendil.md) in slightly different forms), and all the sixteen here reproduced appeared in *[The Silmarillion Calendar 1978](../../main/T/The_Silmarillion_Calendar_1978.md)* (the second device of [Lúthien Tinúviel](../../main/L/Lúthien.md) on the cover of the Calendar, and also on the jacket of *[The Silmarillion](../../main/T/The_Silmarillion.md)*), where the following note was given:

<dl><dd>Some details of the emblems cannot now be explained, but the following notes draw attention to notable features. The device of Finwë:, first king of the <a href="../../main/N/Noldor.md" title="Noldor">Noldorin</a> <a href="../../main/E/Elves.md" title="Elves">Elves</a>, is a winged sun, as that of <a class="mw-redirect" href="../../main/E/Elwë.md" title="Elwë">Elwë</a>: (King <a href="../../main/T/Thingol.md" title="Thingol">Thingol</a> of <a href="../../main/D/Doriath.md" title="Doriath">Doriath</a>) is a winged moon with stars. Those of Finwë:&#39;s sons <a href="../../main/F/Fëanor.md" title="Fëanor">Fëanor</a> and <a href="../../main/F/Fingolfin.md" title="Fingolfin">Fingolfin</a> are clearly related to Finwë&#39;s emblem, although in the case of Feanor it is natural to associate the flames with the meaning of his name, Fëanáro &#39;Spirit of Fire&#39;. <a href="../../main/G/Gil-galad.md" title="Gil-galad">Gil-galad</a>&#39;s device of white stars is also associated with his name, which means &#39;Star of Radiance&#39;; but the harp of Finrod Felagund probably derives from the legend (<i>The Silmarillion</i> <a href="../../main/O/Of_the_Coming_of_Men_into_the_West.md" title="Of the Coming of Men into the West">Chapter 17</a>) of his coming upon the first <a href="../../main/M/Men.md" title="Men">Men</a> to enter <a href="../../main/B/Beleriand.md" title="Beleriand">Beleriand</a>, and of his singing to them to the accompaniment of a harp that he found in their camp.</dd></dl>

The white flowers that appear in the devices of Luthien are probably to be connected with the flowers of [niphredil](../../main/N/Niphredil.md) that sprang at her birth in Doriath, as is told in *The Silmarillion* [Chapter 10](../../main/O/Of_the_Sindar.md). The emblem of [Idril Celebrindal](../../main/I/Idril.md) (daughter of [Turgon](../../main/T/Turgon.md) of [Gondolin](../../main/G/Gondolin.md) and mother of [Eärendil](../../main/E/Eärendil.md)) is a cornflower pattern, and was named [Menelluin](../../main/M/Menelluin.md) ('Sky-blue'); this is stated to be an inlaid plaque saved from Gondolin and descending through Eärendil and his son [Elros](../../main/E/Elros.md) to [Númenor](../../main/N/Númenor.md), whence it was saved by [Elendil](../../main/E/Elendil.md) and taken to [Gondor](../../main/G/Gondor.md). Its influence on Númenórean circular designs can be seen in the Númenórean tile <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tile_1.jpg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tile 1.jpg (page does not exist)">Tile 1</a> <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tile_2.jpg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tile 2.jpg (page does not exist)">Tile 2</a> <a class="new" href="https://tolkiengateway.net/w/index.php?title=File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tile_3.jpg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tile 3.jpg (page does not exist)">Tile 3</a>.

The full description of the emblem in the centre of the bottom row is 'Ancient Emblem representing the derivation of the [Silmarils](../../main/S/Silmarils.md) from the Light of the Trees upon Ezellohar', Ezellohar (or Corollairë) being the [Green Mound](../../main/E/Ezellohar.md) on which the [Two Trees](../../main/T/Two_Trees.md) grew in [Valinor](../../main/V/Valinor.md).
