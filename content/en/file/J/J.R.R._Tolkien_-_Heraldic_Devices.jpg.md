## Summary {#Summary}

Eight of these devices were used on the back of the cover of *[The J. R. R. Tolkien Calendar 1974](/wiki/The_J._R._R._Tolkien_Calendar_1974)* (those of [Finwë](/wiki/Finw%C3%AB): and [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil) in slightly different forms), and all the sixteen here reproduced appeared in *[The Silmarillion Calendar 1978](/wiki/The_Silmarillion_Calendar_1978)* (the second device of [Lúthien Tinúviel](/wiki/L%C3%BAthien "Lúthien") on the cover of the Calendar, and also on the jacket of *[The Silmarillion](/wiki/The_Silmarillion)*), where the following note was given:

<dl><dd>Some details of the emblems cannot now be explained, but the following notes draw attention to notable features. The device of Finwë:, first king of the <a href="/wiki/Noldor" title="Noldor">Noldorin</a> <a href="/wiki/Elves" title="Elves">Elves</a>, is a winged sun, as that of <a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Elw%C3%AB" title="Elwë">Elwë</a>: (King <a href="/wiki/Thingol" title="Thingol">Thingol</a> of <a href="/wiki/Doriath" title="Doriath">Doriath</a>) is a winged moon with stars. Those of Finwë:&#39;s sons <a href="/wiki/F%C3%ABanor" title="Fëanor">Fëanor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fingolfin" title="Fingolfin">Fingolfin</a> are clearly related to Finwë&#39;s emblem, although in the case of Feanor it is natural to associate the flames with the meaning of his name, Fëanáro &#39;Spirit of Fire&#39;. <a href="/wiki/Gil-galad" title="Gil-galad">Gil-galad</a>&#39;s device of white stars is also associated with his name, which means &#39;Star of Radiance&#39;; but the harp of Finrod Felagund probably derives from the legend (<i>The Silmarillion</i> <a href="/wiki/Of_the_Coming_of_Men_into_the_West" title="Of the Coming of Men into the West">Chapter 17</a>) of his coming upon the first <a href="/wiki/Men" title="Men">Men</a> to enter <a href="/wiki/Beleriand" title="Beleriand">Beleriand</a>, and of his singing to them to the accompaniment of a harp that he found in their camp.</dd></dl>

The white flowers that appear in the devices of Luthien are probably to be connected with the flowers of [niphredil](/wiki/Niphredil "Niphredil") that sprang at her birth in Doriath, as is told in *The Silmarillion* [Chapter 10](/wiki/Of_the_Sindar "Of the Sindar"). The emblem of [Idril Celebrindal](/wiki/Idril "Idril") (daughter of [Turgon](/wiki/Turgon) of [Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin) and mother of [Eärendil](/wiki/E%C3%A4rendil)) is a cornflower pattern, and was named [Menelluin](/wiki/Menelluin) ('Sky-blue'); this is stated to be an inlaid plaque saved from Gondolin and descending through Eärendil and his son [Elros](/wiki/Elros) to [Númenor](/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor), whence it was saved by [Elendil](/wiki/Elendil) and taken to [Gondor](/wiki/Gondor). Its influence on Númenórean circular designs can be seen in the Númenórean tile <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tile_1.jpg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tile 1.jpg (page does not exist)">Tile 1</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tile_2.jpg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tile 2.jpg (page does not exist)">Tile 2</a> <a class="new" href="/w/index.php?title=File:J.R.R._Tolkien_-_Tile_3.jpg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" title="File:J.R.R. Tolkien - Tile 3.jpg (page does not exist)">Tile 3</a>.

The full description of the emblem in the centre of the bottom row is 'Ancient Emblem representing the derivation of the [Silmarils](/wiki/Silmarils) from the Light of the Trees upon Ezellohar', Ezellohar (or Corollairë) being the [Green Mound](/wiki/Ezellohar "Ezellohar") on which the [Two Trees](/wiki/Two_Trees) grew in [Valinor](/wiki/Valinor).
