<th scope="row" id="fileinfotpl_desc" class="fileinfo-paramfield" style="background:var(--tg-color-surface-5)">Description<span class="summary fn" style="display:none">Miriam Ellis - Bilbo&#39;s Favorite Walks.jpg</span></th>
<td class="description">
<blockquote><p>Come into the breakfast room at <a href="../../main/B/Bag_End.md" title="Bag End">Bag End</a> and peer over <a href="../../main/B/Bilbo_Baggins.md" title="Bilbo Baggins">Bilbo</a>&#39;s shoulder as he is making the famous map on which he marked his favorite walks around the Shire in special red ink. It&#39;s a lovely early afternoon in late spring, and Bilbo has fortified himself with a great mug of tea and a nice piece of toast and jam, but he&#39;s become so absorbed in his project that he scarcely notices the telltale thrush who has hopped in at the window in search of crumbs.</p></blockquote>
<blockquote><p><a href="../../main/H/Hobbits.md" title="Hobbits">Hobbits</a> are keenly observant of the natural world, as is evidenced by their writings that we think of as <a href="../../main/J/J.R.R._Tolkien.md" title="J.R.R. Tolkien">J.R.R. Tolkien</a>&#39;s <a href="../../main/T/The_Hobbit.md" title="The Hobbit">The Hobbit</a> and <a href="../../main/T/The_Lord_of_the_Rings.md" title="The Lord of the Rings">The Lord of the Rings</a>, which are filled with countless passages describing trees, rivers, lakes, flowers, wildlife, and so much more. Bilbo would have known so much about the landscape and life of the four farthings from his many walks. In fact, he has painted a tiny figure of himself setting out from <a href="../../main/H/Hobbiton_Hill.md" title="Hobbiton Hill">The Hill</a>. A travelling <a href="../../main/D/Dwarves.md" title="Dwarves">dwarf</a> is near the capitol, <a href="../../main/M/Michel_Delving.md" title="Michel Delving">Michel Delving</a>, while a tarrying <a href="../../main/E/Elves.md" title="Elves">elf</a> is in the eaves of the <a href="../../main/W/Woody_End.md" title="Woody End">Woody End</a>. Every spot on the map would have been filled with Bilbo&#39;s experiences and memories.</p></blockquote>
<blockquote><p>In the little jug on the table, each of the flowers has a special meaning. Bachelor&#39;s buttons represent Bilbo&#39;s chosen lifestyle. Daisies are reminiscent of his <a href="../../main/R/Riddle-game.md" title="Riddle-game">riddling game</a> beneath <a href="../../main/M/Misty_Mountains.md" title="Misty Mountains">the mountains</a>, and snapdragons foreshadow his encounter with <a href="../../main/S/Smaug.md" title="Smaug">Smaug</a>. Meanwhile, the poppies are my symbol for J.R.R. Tolkien due to his experiences in <a href="../../main/W/World_War_I.md" title="World War I">the Great War</a>, while the white cow parsley symbolizes <a href="../../main/E/Edith_Tolkien.md" title="Edith Tolkien">Edith Bratt Tolkien</a>, who once danced among such flowers.</p></blockquote>
<blockquote><p>Bilbo has splurged on an especially large sheet of handmade paper for this delightful project. He has made his own watercolors out of flowers and leaves, and doubtless his ink would have been made of walnut husks and other local, natural ingredients.</p></blockquote>
