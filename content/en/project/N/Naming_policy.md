The **naming policy** of [Tolkien Gateway](/wiki/Tolkien_Gateway) governs the **article title**, the article title being the large header displayed at the top of the article's content. When considering an article title, an editor should consider the [naming guidelines below](#Naming_guidelines).

Usually, the title is the name of the concept/subject of the article, or it may be a descriptive phrase. However, the limitations of MediaWiki software dictate that no two pages can have the same title. As a result, Tolkien Gateway needs to have a policy in place to regulate the naming of articles in order to differentiate between several different concepts.

## Naming guidelines {#Naming_guidelines}

In naming any article, please take into account the following guidelines (<a class="extiw" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Article_titles" title="wikipedia:Wikipedia:Article titles">similar to those used on Wikipedia</a>):

- **Recognisability** - the title of the subject should be easily recognisable to someone familiar with the subject area. For example, "[Gondolin](/wiki/Gondolin)" is much more recognisable than "Lothengriol";
- **Conciseness** - it should be as short as possible. For example, "[Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)" and not "Gandalf the Grey";
- **Naturalness** - an article title that someone is likely to actually search for. For example, "[Faramir](/wiki/Faramir)" is preferable to "Faramir, son of Denethor".
- **Precision** - it accurately describes the subject without confusing it with another. For example, "[The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(1978_film)>)" distinct from "[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)";
- **Consistency** - so that all articles in the same subject area are similarly named. See [the specific naming conventions](#Specific_naming_conventions) below for more details on some of these;
- **Canonicity** - the article title should be a canonical name for the character. For example "[Frodo Baggins](/wiki/Frodo_Baggins)" as opposed to "Bingo Baggins".

Should there be more than one concept of the same name, please refer to [disambiguation](#Disambiguation) below.

## Specific naming conventions {#Specific_naming_conventions}

- **Definite/indefinite article** - avoid the definite article ("the") and the indefinite article ("a"/"an") unless addressing the titles of books, series, films, etc. For example, that "[Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Ring)" is the article for the group of characters, whereas "<i><a href="/wiki/The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring" title="The Fellowship of the Ring"><b>The</b> Fellowship of the Ring</a></i>" is the article describing the first volume of *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*. Of course, there are some exceptions, like "[the One Ring](/wiki/The_One_Ring "The One Ring")" or "[the Shire](/wiki/The_Shire "The Shire")".
- **Groups** - contrary to Wikipedia policy, the name of groups should be plural rather than singular. For example, "[Elves](/wiki/Elves)". However if plurals are disputed - especially the case with [Elvish](/wiki/Elvish) words - then they should be in the singular, e.g. "[Mallorn](/wiki/Mallorn)".
- **Images** - see [Image Naming Policy](/wiki/Tolkien_Gateway:Image_policy "Tolkien Gateway:Image policy").
- **Lists** - lists can be difficult to maintain. However, should you decide to set up a list they should be named "List of ...";
- **Real-world people** - real people should be listed on the name they are most commonly known, preferably not a pseudonym.
- **Video games** - if the title has been used elsewhere, then the article title should have "(video game)" at the end, for example "[The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (video game)](</wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers_(video_game)>)". If the video game's name isn't used elsewhere then "(video game)" isn't necessary, for example "[The Lord of the Rings: War in the North](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_War_in_the_North)".
- **Years** - real-world years are simply the year, such as [2014](/wiki/2014). For in-universe years, the title should be the age followed by the year, such as "[Year of the Trees 1250](/wiki/Year_of_the_Trees_1250)", "[Second Age 750](/wiki/Second_Age_750)" and "[Third Age 2043](/wiki/Third_Age_2043)".

## Disambiguation {#Disambiguation}

<table class="noprint" style="float: right; border:var(--tg-border-base); background:var(--tg-color-surface-2); margin: .3em .3em .3em 1em; padding: 10px; text-align: center;border-radius:var(--tg-border-radius-large);"><tbody><tr><th style="border: none; background: transparent;" class="plainlist"><small>Shortcut:<br/><a class="mw-redirect" href="/wiki/Tolkien_Gateway:DISAMBIG" title="Tolkien Gateway:DISAMBIG">TG:DISAMBIG</a></small></th></tr></tbody></table>

Disambiguation is the process of resolving the ambiguity between two or more similarly-named concepts. Because certain names may be used more than once we have a disambiguation policy to regulate this.

### Methods of disambiguation {#Methods_of_disambiguation}

There are a number of ways to differentiate between similarly-named articles, these are the most common:

- **Post-nominal numerals** - common with leaders, it is easy to differentiate "[Arathorn I](/wiki/Arathorn_I)" from "[Arathorn II](/wiki/Arathorn_II)";
- **Bracketed descriptions** - this is the most common form of disambiguation and is the *only* form of disambiguation for concepts outside the Middle-earth universe. We do this by by putting a descriptive statement in brackets following the name of the concept. Examples include: "[Calimehtar (son of Calmacil)](</wiki/Calimehtar_(son_of_Calmacil)>)", "[Beregond (Steward of Gondor)](</wiki/Beregond_(Steward_of_Gondor)>)", "[Fastred (rider of Rohan)](</wiki/Fastred_(rider_of_Rohan)>)", "[Angband (game)](</wiki/Angband_(game)>)" and "[The Hobbit (1977 film)](</wiki/The_Hobbit_(1977_film)>)";
- **Epithets and title** - this is not encouraged unless editors feel that there is *no other option*. Examples of this include "[Húrin of Emyn Arnen](/wiki/H%C3%BArin_of_Emyn_Arnen)".

### Disambiguation pages {#Disambiguation_pages}

When there are three or more concepts - or two concepts of equal notoriety - we create a [disambiguation page](/wiki/Category:Disambiguation "Category:Disambiguation"). If one of articles is clearly more notable than the others then that articles gets primacy to the title, if not then it should redirect to the disambiguation page.

For example:

- Gandalf the character is much more notable than Gandalf the fanzine or either of the two "Gandalf" bands. As such, "[Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf)" links to the character whilst "<a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Gandalf_(disambiguation)" title="Gandalf (disambiguation)">Gandalf (disambiguation)</a>" includes links to "[Gandalf (fanzine)](</wiki/Gandalf_(fanzine)>)", "[Gandalf (US band)](</wiki/Gandalf_(US_band)>)", and "[Gandalf (Finnish band)](</wiki/Gandalf_(Finnish_band)>). The [Gandalf](/wiki/Gandalf) article links to the disambiguation page;
- There are two characters named Ponto Baggins are indistinguishable notability. As such "Ponto Baggins" redirects to "<a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Ponto_Baggins_(disambiguation)" title="Ponto Baggins (disambiguation)">Ponto Baggins (disambiguation)</a>" which then links to "[Ponto Baggins I](/wiki/Ponto_Baggins_I)" and "[Ponto Baggins II](/wiki/Ponto_Baggins_II)".

The structure of disambiguation pages is as follows:

- The first line of the page should have the concept name in bold with an arbitrary line about the disambiguation. For example, "**Gandalf** can refer to the following concepts:"
- The list of disambiguated articles. The list should be split up, when necessary, between [legendarium](/wiki/Legendarium "Legendarium")-related links and Real-world links (see examples at <a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Minas_Tirith_(disambiguation)" title="Minas Tirith (disambiguation)">Minas Tirith (disambiguation)</a> and <a class="mw-disambig" href="/wiki/Tom_(disambiguation)" title="Tom (disambiguation)">Tom (disambiguation)</a>). In-universe concepts always take precedence in the list.
- At the end of the article, add **{{disambig}}**
- Further categorization is not necessary.
